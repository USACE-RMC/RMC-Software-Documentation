# Inundation Analysis

The Inundation Analysis page, at **Consequences > Inundation Analysis**, defines and runs the flood simulations. An analysis is a named set of
loading levels, which are reservoir water-surface elevations. Each loading level is simulated in HEC-RAS with the analysis's boundary conditions and
plan settings, and the analysis's results are one flood map per loading level.

The page is a map (see [The map](./401-inundation-geometry.mdx#the-map)) with these regions:

- **Analysis Configuration**, a panel at the upper left, with a tile for each analysis, the boundary conditions of the selected analysis, and its
  simulation status.
- **[Feature] Boundary Conditions**, a panel at the right that opens when a dam, model area, river or coastline is selected on the map or in the
  list. The geometry itself is read-only on this page.
- The **Depth over time at a point** tool in the map toolbar, and the results layers in the legend.

## Analyses

Each analysis is a tile in the Analysis Configuration panel; selecting a tile makes it the active analysis. The icons on a tile are **Edit
analysis**, **Run the simulation**, **Copy analysis** and **Delete analysis**. A status dot shows the last simulation: green "Simulated.", amber
"Inputs have changed since the last simulation.", or red when the last simulation did not complete or could not start.

**Add analysis** creates an analysis named "Set N" and opens its **Edit Analysis** dialog. **Copy analysis** copies the plan settings, loading levels
and boundary conditions into "[name] (Copy)". **Delete analysis** asks for confirmation; it deletes the analysis and all of its loading levels, and
cancels a simulation that is running.

## Edit Analysis

The **Edit Analysis** dialog holds the analysis name, its plan settings and its loading levels, and saves them with **Save**; **Cancel** discards
the changes. <TableReference tableKey="dst-users-guide-table-consequences-plan-settings" /> lists its fields.

The **Loading Level** table lists the analysis's loading levels, one row each, with the columns **Loading Level** (the pool elevation, a positive
number, unique in the analysis) and an optional **Label** of up to 100 characters. Rows sort by loading level and are added, pasted and deleted like
any DST table (see [Tables](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#tables)). A new loading level copies the boundary conditions of the lowest existing level, without
its per-level values, and starts its reservoir at the loading level. Removing a loading level deletes its results and its consequence runs.

## Boundary conditions

The panel below the tiles lists the features that can take a boundary condition in the active analysis: the dam centerline, the model area, the
rivers that enter the model area, and the coastlines. Selecting one opens its boundary conditions at the right, under the heading "[feature]
([analysis])". Settings apply to every loading level of the analysis, except the values entered in tables with one row per loading level. The
boundary conditions save as they are entered; there is no Save button.

### Dam

**Boundary Condition Type** is **None** (the default), **Hydrograph** or **1D Reservoir**:

- **Hydrograph** applies a dam-breach hydrograph directly to the 2D mesh. Its location is set with **Latitude** and **Longitude**, or with the
  crosshair button and a click on the dam centerline, and **Width** is the breach width over which it enters the mesh. **Hydrograph Type** and its
  tables are described in [Hydrographs](#hydrographs).
- **1D Reservoir** routes an inflow through a reservoir and a breach, on three tabs: **Reservoir**, **Inflow** and **Breach**.

The **Reservoir** tab states how the reservoir releases water: through the Stage-Outflow curve on the Operational Data page, or, without one, only
over the dam crest. **Storage & outflow curves** shows the Operational Data curves the reservoir uses, read-only, with a link to edit them there. Its
table gives, for each loading level, the **Initial WSE** (the reservoir level at the start of the simulation, defaulting to the loading level) and,
when the outlet curve is in use, the **Release at start**. A red message explains any problem in the Stage-Outflow curve that stops a run.

The **Inflow** tab's **Inflow Hydrograph** check box routes an inflow hydrograph into the reservoir (see [Hydrographs](#hydrographs)). The **Breach**
tab's **Breach** check box turns the breach on.

### Breach

The breach fields appear on the Breach tab once **Breach** is checked. <TableReference tableKey="dst-users-guide-table-consequences-breach" /> lists
them. **Breach Method** decides where the breach geometry comes from: **User Defined** takes the development time, bottom width and side slopes for
each loading level, and **FEMA Defaults** calculates them from the dam type, height and length (FEMA P-946, Table 9.3) for every loading level.
Values entered under User Defined are kept while FEMA Defaults is selected.

The **Breach Geometry** plot draws the terrain along the dam, the dam crest, the breach location and each loading level's breach trapezoid, and the
initial pipe for a piping failure. Under User Defined, the calculator icon opens the **Breach Calculator**, which estimates the breach with the
**MacDonald-Langridge-Monopolis** or the **Xu & Zhang / Von Thun** method from the failure mode, the top of dam, breach bottom and pool elevations,
the pool volume at failure, and the method's own dam inputs. **Calculate** shows the **Calculator Results**, and **Apply Results** writes them to
every loading level.

### Model area, rivers and coastlines

- **Model area.** **Normal Depth Slope** (default 0.001, greater than 0 and at most 1) is the outflow boundary that lets water leave the model area
  around its perimeter. The table's **Initial water level** for each loading level is left blank; the simulation service cannot yet start a 2D area
  wet, and a run with a value refuses to start.
- **River.** **Upstream Boundary** is **None** or **Hydrograph**, an inflow series for the river. A river that crosses the dam takes its flow from
  the dam, and a river that starts inside the model area cannot take an inflow.
- **Coastline.** **Boundary** is **None** or **Hydrograph**, a stage series applied along the model area edge between the ends of the coastline.
  Stages may be negative.

### Hydrographs

**Hydrograph Type** is **Trapezoidal** (the default) or **Tabular**. A trapezoidal hydrograph has, for each loading level, a **Base** value, a **Base
Time** (hrs), a **Peak** value and a **Peak Time** (hrs): it rises from base to peak, holds the peak and returns to base by the base time, so the Peak
Time must not exceed the Base Time. A tabular hydrograph has a tab for each loading level with **Time (hr)** and **Value** columns and at least two
rows. Values are flows, or stages for a coastline. A chart plots one series per loading level.

## Running an analysis

The flood simulations are one of the two places in DST where the user starts a calculation (see
[Automatic Calculation](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#automatic-calculation)); nothing runs on its own. Each analysis tile has a play button, **Run the
simulation** (**Run the simulation again** once it has run), which simulates every loading level of that analysis. The button is unavailable, with
the reason, when the analysis has no loading levels ("Add a loading level to run the analysis") or when simulations are not available. While the
run is in progress the button becomes a spinner; selecting it cancels the run ("[status]. Click to cancel."). There is no separate retry: a failed
or cancelled analysis is run again with the same button.

Before starting, DST checks the model and refuses to start with the reasons when, for example, the mesh is not computed, there is no terrain or land
cover, no inflow is defined, or a breach is incomplete. Warnings that do not stop the run, such as a breach the reservoir never reaches, are reported
as the run starts.

The **[analysis] Simulation** band under the boundary conditions shows the run's status: Waiting for a model server, Sending files, Queued, Building
model, Running (with a percentage), Complete, Failed, Cancelled, Timed out or Lost. Its rows show each loading level, as Computed, Did not compute,
Not run or Not verified, and its icons open the dam results, open **Simulation details**, and download the HEC-RAS project. When inputs change after
a run, the status shows a warning: "Inputs have changed since this simulation ran. The depth grids show the earlier inputs; run the analysis again to
update them."

**Simulation details** opens a dialog with the run's status, timing and HEC-RAS version; any reasons it could not start, errors, warnings and
values built differently from what was asked; a **Loading levels** table with each level's result, volume error, largest cell error, water that left
the model area and compute time; and folded sections for how the model was built and the console output. **Download HEC-RAS project** saves the
project, results and logs as a zip file.

## Results on the map

The legend's **Inundation Results** group lists, for each analysis and loading level, **Maximum depth**, **Depth over time** and **Water leaving the
model**. Turning on **Depth over time** shows a player with **Play**, **Pause**, the previous and next outputs, a **Simulation time** slider, a speed
in simulation hours per second, **Loop** and a blend toggle. Each frame is the water surface at one mapping output time.

**Depth over time at a point** in the toolbar reads the depths at a point selected on the map. Its panel plots depth over time for each loading
level of the chosen **Analysis**, with an **Arrival depth** (0.5 ft by default) and a table of when each level reaches that depth, its peak depth and
time, and how long it stays that deep. **Download CSV** saves the depths.

## Dam results

The dam results dialog, **[analysis] Results**, opens from the chart icon in the simulation band or the **Results** button on the dam's boundary
conditions. It lists, for each loading level, when overtopping and the breach started, when the breach was fully formed, the peak outflow and its
time, the maximum pool and tailwater, and the water released. A time plot, **Outflow through the dam**, shows the outflow
series the run kept, such as the flow through the breach, and the pool and tailwater, with **Flow**, **Elevation** and **Both**, **Event** and **Whole simulation**, and **Compare levels**;
a cross-section of the breach follows the time bar. The table copies to the clipboard and downloads as CSV.

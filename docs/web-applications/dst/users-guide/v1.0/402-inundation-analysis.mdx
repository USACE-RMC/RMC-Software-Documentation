# Inundation Analysis

The flood simulations are set up, run and reviewed on the Inundation Analysis page (**Consequences > Inundation Analysis**). Each simulation belongs
to an analysis: a named set of loading levels, which are reservoir water-surface elevations. DST simulates each loading level in HEC-RAS with the
analysis's boundary conditions and plan settings, and produces one flood map per loading level.

## Analyses

Selecting a tile in the Analysis Configuration panel makes it the active analysis. The icons on a tile are **Edit analysis**, **Run the
simulation**, **Copy analysis** and **Delete analysis**. The tile's status dot shows the last simulation: green "Simulated.", amber "Inputs have
changed since the last simulation.", or red when the last simulation did not complete or could not start.

- **Add analysis** creates an analysis named "Set N" and opens its **Edit Analysis** dialog.
- **Copy analysis** copies the plan settings, loading levels and boundary conditions into "[name] (Copy)".
- **Delete analysis** asks for confirmation, then deletes the analysis and all of its loading levels, and cancels a simulation that is running.

:::tip

A different breach or inflow can be tested without losing a finished run by copying the analysis and changing the copy.

:::

## Boundary conditions

The boundary-condition list holds the features that can take a boundary condition in the active analysis: the dam centerline, the model area, the
rivers that enter the model area, and the coastlines. Selecting one opens its boundary conditions, headed "[feature] ([analysis])". Settings apply
to every loading level of the analysis, except the values in tables with one row per loading level. Boundary conditions save as they are entered;
there is no Save button.

### Dam

**Boundary Condition Type** is **None** (the default), **Hydrograph** or **1D Reservoir**:

- **Hydrograph** applies a dam-breach hydrograph directly to the 2D mesh. **Latitude** and **Longitude** set its location (or the crosshair button
  and a click on the dam centerline), and **Width** is the breach width over which it enters the mesh. **Hydrograph Type** and its tables are
  described in [Hydrographs](#hydrographs).
- **1D Reservoir** routes an inflow through a reservoir and a breach, on three tabs: **Reservoir**, **Inflow** and **Breach**.

The **Reservoir** tab states how the reservoir releases water: through the Stage-Outflow curve on the Operational Data page, or, without one, only
over the dam crest. **Storage & outflow curves** shows the Operational Data curves the reservoir uses, with a link to edit them there. Its table
gives, for each loading level, the **Initial WSE** (the reservoir level at the start of the simulation, defaulting to the loading level) and, when
the outlet curve is in use, the **Release at start**. A message explains any problem in the Stage-Outflow curve that stops a run.

The **Inflow** tab's **Inflow Hydrograph** check box routes an inflow hydrograph into the reservoir (see [Hydrographs](#hydrographs)). The **Breach**
tab's **Breach** check box turns the breach on.

### Breach

The breach fields appear on the Breach tab once **Breach** is checked. <TableReference tableKey="dst-users-guide-table-consequences-breach" /> lists
them. **Breach Method** sets where the breach geometry comes from: **User Defined** takes the development time, bottom width and side slopes for each
loading level, and **FEMA Defaults** calculates them from the dam type, height and length (FEMA P-946, Table 9.3). Values entered under User Defined
are kept while FEMA Defaults is selected.

The **Breach Geometry** plot draws the terrain along the dam, the dam crest, the breach location and each loading level's breach, and the initial

writes them to every loading level.

### Model area, rivers and coastlines

- **Model area.** **Normal Depth Slope** (default 0.001, greater than 0 and at most 1) allows water to leave the model area around its perimeter. The
  table's **Initial water level** stays blank for every loading level; a run with a value refuses to start.
- **River.** **Upstream Boundary** is **None** or **Hydrograph**, an inflow series for the river. A river that crosses the dam takes its flow from
  the dam, and a river that starts inside the model area cannot take an inflow.
- **Coastline.** **Boundary** is **None** or **Hydrograph**, a stage series applied along the model area edge between the ends of the coastline.
  Stages may be negative.

## Hydrographs

A hydrograph is the water entering the model at a boundary over time: a flow (cfs or cms) against hours from the start of the simulation, or, at a
coastline, a water surface elevation. Four boundary conditions take one, and all use the same editor:

- The **Inflow** tab of a dam's **1D Reservoir** boundary condition, the flood flowing into the reservoir. This is the most common use, and the
  rest of this section describes it.
- A dam's **Hydrograph** boundary condition, a breach outflow applied directly to the 2D mesh (see [Dam](#dam)).
- A river's **Upstream Boundary**, the flow entering the model area along the river.
- A coastline's **Boundary**, a stage series along the model area edge.

### What the inflow hydrograph does

For each loading level, HEC-RAS starts the reservoir at its **Initial WSE** and routes the inflow hydrograph into it. The reservoir rises by the
Stage-Storage curve on the Operational Data page (see [Operational Data](./103-operational-data.mdx)), and releases water through the
Stage-Outflow curve and over the dam crest. The inflow hydrograph therefore sets:

- the highest pool the reservoir reaches, the **Max pool** in the dam results (see [Dam results](#dam-results)), from the hydrograph's peak and
  volume together;
- when, and whether, a breach with an **Elevation** trigger starts;
- the length of the simulation: from time zero to the end of the longest hydrograph (or of the breach, if later), plus the **Run time after inflow
  ends**. **Simulation details** reports the window, for example "Simulation window 01Dec2099 0000 to 02Dec2099 0200 (longest hydrograph or breach
  20 h + travel time 6 h)".

Without an inflow hydrograph the reservoir can only fall from its Initial WSE, so a breach triggered above it never starts. The run then warns that
the reservoir "never rises that far and the dam does not breach in this simulation." With an inflow and a Stage-Outflow curve, DST estimates the
highest pool before the run and gives the same warning when the estimate stays below the trigger. Either way the run goes ahead.

for all the analysis's loading levels at once. The chart plots one line per loading level and follows the values as they are entered.

### Tabular hydrographs

A tabular hydrograph is any shape entered point by point. The loading-level tabs choose the level, and each level has its own **Time (hr)** and
**Value** columns. Times are hours from the start of the simulation and need not be evenly spaced. A hydrograph needs at least two rows, and
neither its times nor its flows may be negative. A tabular hydrograph suits a flood hydrograph from another source, such as HEC-HMS output, an
inflow design flood, a recorded flood or a storm hydrograph scaled to each loading level.

:::tip

Time and flow pairs copied from a spreadsheet can be pasted into the table; the paste adds rows as needed (see
[Tables](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#tables)).

:::

### Shaping an inflow hydrograph

- **Starting flow.** A hydrograph that begins at the **Release at start** on the Reservoir tab holds the pool steady until the flood arrives.
- **Pool and loading level.** After a run, the user compares the **Max pool** in the dam results with the loading level, and scales the hydrograph
  up or down and runs again if they differ.
- **Breach trigger.** For a breach with an Elevation trigger, the pool must reach the trigger value. When DST warns that it will not, the user
  enlarges the inflow or lowers the trigger.
- **Length.** The run time grows with the hydrograph's length. A hydrograph can end once the pool has peaked and the flow has receded; the **Run
  time after inflow ends** carries the flood through the model area.
- **One shape across the levels.** Scaling one shape to each loading level keeps the levels comparable.

## Running an analysis

The flood simulations run only when the user starts them (see [Automatic Calculation](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#automatic-calculation)). **Run the
simulation** on an analysis tile (**Run the simulation again** once it has run) simulates every loading level of that analysis. While the run is in
progress the button is a spinner; selecting it cancels the run. A failed or cancelled analysis is run again with the same button.

Before starting, DST checks the model and refuses to start when an input is missing, with the reason in an error message and in **Simulation
details** (see [Common problems](#common-problems)). Warnings that do not stop the run, such as a breach the reservoir never reaches, appear as the
run starts.

The **[analysis] Simulation** band shows the run's status: **Waiting for a model server**, **Sending files**, **Queued**, **Building model**,
**Running** (with a percentage), **Complete**, **Failed**, **Cancelled**, **Timed out** or **Lost**. Each loading level reads **Computed**, **Did
not compute**, **Not run** or **Not verified**, and then "Preparing map" or "Preparing animation" while its depth results are made. The band's icons
open the dam results, open **Simulation details**, and download the HEC-RAS project; until the project is saved, the download icon reads "The
HEC-RAS project is being saved. It can be downloaded once it is."

When inputs change after a run, the band warns "Inputs have changed since this simulation ran. The depth grids show the earlier inputs; run the
analysis again to update them." The results stay until the analysis runs again.

## Results on the map

The legend's **Inundation Results** group lists, for each analysis and loading level, **Maximum depth**, **Depth over time** and **Water leaving the
model**. When a run has map results, the simulation band points to them: "Turn on depth grids and depth over time under Inundation Results in the
map legend."

### Reading values at the pointer

While **Identify features** is on, a card follows the pointer and reads the value of every visible result layer, each row named "[analysis] ›
[loading level]" and the layer:

- **Maximum depth** gives the deepest water at that point.
- **Depth over time** gives the depth at the player's current time.
- While the terrain is shown, a row gives the ground elevation.

A depth layer reads "Dry" where the ground stayed dry, and the terrain reads "No data" outside its coverage. Values read while zoomed far out are
approximate and marked "≈". An amber dot marks a result that is out of date.

### The depth player

Turning on a **Depth over time** layer shows the player, which plays every Depth over time layer that is on, in step. Its controls are:

- **Previous output** and **Next output**, which pause and step one output time.
- **Play** and **Pause**.
- The **Simulation time** slider. Dragging it pauses the player.
- The speed, in simulation hours per real second: 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 (the default), 4 or 8 h/s.
- **Loop**, which starts again at the end.
- **Blend between outputs**, which blends smoothly between output times. Off (the default), the map steps from one output to the next.

A line under the controls reads "Output [N] of [M]". When an animation cannot be loaded, the player says so with **Retry**. **Maximum depth** is the
deepest water over every computation step, while each frame shows one mapping output time, so a Maximum depth layer can be deeper than any frame.

### Depth over time at a point

**Depth over time at a point** in the map toolbar reports when the flood reaches a location and how deep the water becomes. After a point is selected
on the map, the panel plots depth over time for each loading level of the chosen **Analysis**, and lists, for each level, when it **Reaches** the
**Arrival depth** (0.5 ft by default, 0.15 m in a metric screening), its **Peak depth** and **Peak at** time, how long it stays that deep, and when it
is **Back under** that depth. Another point can be selected without closing the panel.

- Each row's check box puts that level on the plot or takes it off; the heading check box does all levels. A row's own button plots only that level.
- The copy button copies the plotted levels' depths for a spreadsheet, and **Download CSV** saves them; both take only the levels on the plot.
- Depths are read from the mapping outputs, as the note under the plot says: "One value every [interval], the analysis's mapping output interval."

In place of the plot, the panel can read "This point is outside the model area.", "No loading level has a depth to plot here.", "Tick a loading
level to plot it.", "[analysis] has no results yet. Run it to read depths here." or "[analysis] is being simulated. Its depths can be read once the
run finishes." When inputs have changed since the run, a note reads "Inputs changed since this simulation ran. Run the analysis again to update its
results."

levels are cleared, and the arrival depth is raised to 2 ft, which redraws the guide line and recalculates the table.

## Dam results

The dam results record what happened at the dam in each loading level. The **[analysis] Results** dialog opens from the chart icon in the
simulation band or the **Results** button on the dam's boundary conditions. Its table lists, for each loading level, when overtopping and the breach
started, when the breach was fully formed, the peak outflow and its time, the maximum pool and tailwater, and the water released. The table copies to
the clipboard and downloads as CSV.

Selecting a row selects the loading level the **Outflow through the dam** plot shows. The plot's controls are:

- **Event** (the default), which frames the flood at the dam, or **Whole simulation**.
- **Flow**, **Elevation** or **Both**. **Flow** stacks the outflow **Through the breach**, **Overtopping beside the breach**, **Through the
  spillways and outlet works** and **Other outflow**, and labels the peak. **Elevation** draws the **Pool (headwater)**, **Tailwater**, **Breach
  bottom** and **Top of dam**. Markers show when the breach starts and is fully formed.
- **Compare levels**, which draws every computed loading level. It is unavailable when fewer than two levels computed.
- The copy button and **Download CSV**, for the level shown, or every level while levels are compared.

The time bar under the plot plays or drags the plot's cursor and the breach cross-section through the simulation. The line below the plot gives
the level's verification, volume error and the water that left the model area, and **Show where on the map** turns on that level's **Water leaving
the model** layer. A dam with a **Hydrograph** boundary condition has no reservoir or breach, so its results read **Flow entering the model**,
**Stage** and **Max stage at the dam**.

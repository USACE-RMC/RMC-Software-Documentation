# Consequences

The Consequences module estimates what happens downstream when the dam fails, and when it does not. The screening team builds a two-dimensional
hydraulic model of the area below the dam, simulates the flood at each loading level with HEC-RAS, and then simulates life loss and economic damages
in each flood with LifeSim. The results become the consequence functions that the Risk Analysis module combines with the hazard and system
response (see [Chapter 32, Risk Analysis](./32-risk-analysis.mdx)).

The module has six pages, reached from **Consequences** in the module navigation, in the order the work is done:

- **Inundation Geometry** builds the hydraulic model: its area, dam, rivers, terrain, land cover and mesh.
- **Inundation Analysis** sets the loading levels, boundary conditions and breach of each analysis, and runs the flood simulations.
- **Consequence Data** holds the structure inventory, the Emergency Response ratings and an optional non-failure hazard zone.
- **Consequence Analysis** sets when each hazard occurs and how much notice people have, and runs the consequence simulations.
- **Results Map** shows the consequences of one loading level on the map, structure by structure.
- **Results Data** shows each consequence function as graphs and tables and holds functions entered by hand.

None of these pages is verified. Their inputs are read-only to users who are not on the screening team and while the screening's inputs are locked
(see [Input Lock](./04-working-in-dst.mdx#input-lock)). The methods behind the simulations are described in the HEC-RAS and LifeSim documentation
and in the DST Technical Reference Manual.

### The map

Every page of the Consequences module except Consequence Analysis and Results Data is a map. The toolbar at the upper right changes the map style,
identifies the features under the pointer (**Identify features**), measures distances, zooms in, out and to all layers, resets the bearing to north
and exaggerates the terrain. When a feature that can be edited is selected, the toolbar adds **Edit selected feature**, and while editing, **Save
edits**, **Cancel edits (Escape)** and **Add points**. The strip under the left panel shows the pointer's longitude, latitude and zoom, and hints for
the current tool, such as "Click a feature to select".

The legend groups the layers as **Inundation Geometry**, **Consequence Data**, **Inundation Results** and **Reference Layers**, which include USGS
rivers and lakes and the levees of the National Levee Database. DST remembers which layers are on for each page during the session. Below zoom 13 the
map shows a sample of the structures; zooming in shows every one.

### Model Configuration panel

The add buttons, in order, are **Dam**, **Model Area**, **River**, **Coastline**, **Breakline**, **Refinement**, **Terrain** and **Land Cover**,
followed by **Mesh**; resting the pointer on a button gives the feature's full name. A screening has one model area, one dam centerline, one terrain and one land cover; once one
exists, its button replaces it ("Replace the …").

The tree lists **Model Geometry**, with the mesh status, the mesh button and a download button, and under it the Dam Centerline, Model Area,
**River Centerlines**, **Coastlines**, **Breaklines** and **Refinement Regions**, then **Terrains** and **Land Covers**, each with a count. Each row
has a download button. An amber warning icon marks a feature whose settings are incomplete or invalid; resting the pointer on it gives the reason.

### Terrain

The terrain supplies the ground elevations of the mesh. **Terrain** opens the **Add Terrain** dialog, with two tabs:

- **OpenTopography** downloads a terrain for the model geometry with a 660 ft (200 m) buffer. The **Dataset** is one of Copernicus 30m (COP30, the
  default) or 90m (COP90), SRTM 30m (SRTMGL1) or 90m (SRTMGL3), or USGS 1m, 10m or 30m (3DEP, United States only). A **Download Area** preview shows
  the rectangles to be requested. One download covers at most 4° of latitude or longitude, 1.5 billion cells and 40 requests. **Download** starts it,
  and it continues after the dialog closes.
- **Upload File** reads a GeoTIFF (.tif, .tiff) digital elevation model up to 11 GB, chosen with **Select GeoTIFF File**. **Elevation Units**
  (Meters or Feet) is required; meters are converted to feet.

**Name** is optional in both; a blank name is generated. While the terrain processes, its row shows a spinner, and DST reports "Terrain "[name]" is
ready!" when it finishes. Its properties panel shows its name, source, resolution, elevation units, dataset, import date and bounding box, and warns
when the model geometry extends past the terrain.

### Land cover

The land cover sets the roughness (Manning's n) of the floodplain. **Land Cover** opens the **Add Land Cover** dialog, with two tabs:

- **NLCD** downloads the National Land Cover Database for the model geometry with a 660 ft buffer. **NLCD Year** is **Latest available** (the
  default) or a release from 2021 back to 2001.
- **Upload File** reads a GeoTIFF of whole-number land cover classes. NLCD codes keep their NLCD names and Manning's n; any other code becomes
  "Class N" with n = 0.06 until it is set.

The properties panel shows the land cover's source, year, resolution and bounding box, and **Edit Manning's n…** opens the **Manning's n by Land
Cover Class** dialog. It lists each class's **Code**, **Land Cover Class**, **Default n** and **Manning's n**, which is editable, greater than 0 and
at most 1. **Reset to defaults** restores the defaults and **Save** keeps the changes. Changing a value marks existing simulation results as out of
date. Where the model extends past the land cover, the mesh uses a Manning's n of 0.06.

### Computational mesh

**Mesh** computes the computational mesh from the model geometry, and selecting it again while the mesh computes cancels it. The status under
Model Geometry reads "Queued…", a percentage, "[N] cells", "[N] cells · [K] warnings", "Failed", "Cancelled" or "Not computed". A mesh needs a model
area with a drawn boundary.

When the mesh fails or has warnings, the **Mesh failed** or **Mesh warnings** card opens at the top of the map. It lists each message from the
mesher with **Show on Map**, which outlines and zooms to the problem. DST does not recompute the mesh when the geometry changes; the mesh must be
recomputed after the geometry or the dam's settings change.

### Editing, saving and downloading geometry

The fields in a properties panel save when the user leaves the field (see [Saving](./04-working-in-dst.mdx#saving)). Outlines are edited
explicitly: **Edit Geometry** in the properties panel, or **Edit selected feature** in the toolbar, starts an edit, and **Save** or **Cancel** ends
it; Escape cancels. Switching features with unsaved edits asks "Discard Unsaved Changes?" (**Keep Editing** or **Discard Changes**), and leaving the
page asks for confirmation (**Stay** or **Leave Page**). The trash icon in the properties panel deletes a feature after confirmation.

Each feature downloads as GeoJSON from its tree row or panel, and the terrain and land cover as their files. The Model Geometry download saves all
the geometry as one GeoJSON file, with the mesh (.h5, .hdf) once it is computed.

Changes to the geometry, terrain, land cover or mesh do not clear later results. Inundation and consequence results that depend on them are marked
out of date instead, and are replaced by running them again.

## Inundation Analysis

The Inundation Analysis page, at **Consequences > Inundation Analysis**, defines and runs the flood simulations. An analysis is a named set of
loading levels, which are reservoir water-surface elevations. Each loading level is simulated in HEC-RAS with the analysis's boundary conditions and
plan settings, and the analysis's results are one flood map per loading level.

### Analyses

Each analysis is a tile in the Analysis Configuration panel; selecting a tile makes it the active analysis. The icons on a tile are **Edit
analysis**, **Run the simulation**, **Copy analysis** and **Delete analysis**. A status dot shows the last simulation: green "Simulated.", amber
"Inputs have changed since the last simulation.", or red when the last simulation did not complete or could not start.

**Add analysis** creates an analysis named "Set N" and opens its **Edit Analysis** dialog. **Copy analysis** copies the plan settings, loading levels
and boundary conditions into "[name] (Copy)". **Delete analysis** asks for confirmation; it deletes the analysis and all of its loading levels, and
cancels a simulation that is running.

The **Loading Level** table lists the analysis's loading levels, one row each, with the columns **Loading Level** (the pool elevation, a positive

### Boundary conditions

The panel below the tiles lists the features that can take a boundary condition in the active analysis: the dam centerline, the model area, the
rivers that enter the model area, and the coastlines. Selecting one opens its boundary conditions at the right, under the heading "[feature]
([analysis])". Settings apply to every loading level of the analysis, except the values entered in tables with one row per loading level. The
boundary conditions save as they are entered; there is no Save button.

#### Dam

**Boundary Condition Type** is **None** (the default), **Hydrograph** or **1D Reservoir**:

- **Hydrograph** applies a dam-breach hydrograph directly to the 2D mesh. Its location is set with **Latitude** and **Longitude**, or with the
  crosshair button and a click on the dam centerline, and **Width** is the breach width over which it enters the mesh. **Hydrograph Type** and its
  tables are described in [Hydrographs](#hydrographs).
- **1D Reservoir** routes an inflow through a reservoir and a breach, on three tabs: **Reservoir**, **Inflow** and **Breach**.

The **Reservoir** tab states how the reservoir releases water: through the Stage-Outflow curve on the Operational Data page, or, without one, only
over the dam crest. **Storage & outflow curves** shows the Operational Data curves the reservoir uses, read-only, with a link to edit them there. Its
table gives, for each loading level, the **Initial WSE** (the reservoir level at the start of the simulation, defaulting to the loading level) and,
when the outlet curve is in use, the **Release at start**. A red message explains any problem in the Stage-Outflow curve that stops a run.

The **Inflow** tab's **Inflow Hydrograph** check box routes an inflow hydrograph into the reservoir (see [Hydrographs](#hydrographs)). The **Breach**
tab's **Breach** check box turns the breach on.

#### Breach

The breach fields appear on the Breach tab once **Breach** is checked. <TableReference tableKey="dst-users-guide-table-consequences-breach" /> lists
them. **Breach Method** decides where the breach geometry comes from: **User Defined** takes the development time, bottom width and side slopes for
each loading level, and **FEMA Defaults** calculates them from the dam type, height and length (FEMA P-946, Table 9.3) for every loading level.
Values entered under User Defined are kept while FEMA Defaults is selected.

The **Breach Geometry** plot draws the terrain along the dam, the dam crest, the breach location and each loading level's breach trapezoid, and the
initial pipe for a piping failure. Under User Defined, the calculator icon opens the **Breach Calculator**, which estimates the breach with the
**MacDonald-Langridge-Monopolis** or the **Xu & Zhang / Von Thun** method from the failure mode, the top of dam, breach bottom and pool elevations,
the pool volume at failure, and the method's own dam inputs. **Calculate** shows the **Calculator Results**, and **Apply Results** writes them to
every loading level.

#### Model area, rivers and coastlines

- **Model area.** **Normal Depth Slope** (default 0.001, greater than 0 and at most 1) is the outflow boundary that lets water leave the model area
  around its perimeter. The table's **Initial water level** for each loading level is left blank; the simulation service cannot yet start a 2D area
  wet, and a run with a value refuses to start.
- **River.** **Upstream Boundary** is **None** or **Hydrograph**, an inflow series for the river. A river that crosses the dam takes its flow from
  the dam, and a river that starts inside the model area cannot take an inflow.
- **Coastline.** **Boundary** is **None** or **Hydrograph**, a stage series applied along the model area edge between the ends of the coastline.
  Stages may be negative.

#### Hydrographs

**Hydrograph Type** is **Trapezoidal** (the default) or **Tabular**. A trapezoidal hydrograph has, for each loading level, a **Base** value, a **Base
Time** (hrs), a **Peak** value and a **Peak Time** (hrs): it rises from base to peak, holds the peak and returns to base by the base time, so the Peak
Time must not exceed the Base Time. A tabular hydrograph has a tab for each loading level with **Time (hr)** and **Value** columns and at least two
rows. Values are flows, or stages for a coastline. A chart plots one series per loading level.

### Running an analysis

The flood simulations are one of the two places in DST where the user starts a calculation (see
[Automatic Calculation](./04-working-in-dst.mdx#automatic-calculation)); nothing runs on its own. Each analysis tile has a play button, **Run the
simulation** (**Run the simulation again** once it has run), which simulates every loading level of that analysis. The button is unavailable, with
the reason, when the analysis has no loading levels ("Add a loading level to run the analysis") or when simulations are not available. While the
run is in progress the button becomes a spinner; selecting it cancels the run ("[status]. Click to cancel."). There is no separate retry: a failed
or cancelled analysis is run again with the same button.

Before starting, DST checks the model and refuses to start with the reasons when, for example, the mesh is not computed, there is no terrain or land
cover, no inflow is defined, or a breach is incomplete. Warnings that do not stop the run, such as a breach the reservoir never reaches, are reported
as the run starts.

The **[analysis] Simulation** band under the boundary conditions shows the run's status: Waiting for a model server, Sending files, Queued, Building
model, Running (with a percentage), Complete, Failed, Cancelled, Timed out or Lost. Its rows show each loading level, as Computed, Did not compute,
Not run or Not verified, and its icons open the dam results, open **Simulation details**, and download the HEC-RAS project. When inputs change after
a run, the status shows a warning: "Inputs have changed since this simulation ran. The depth grids show the earlier inputs; run the analysis again to
update them."

### Non-failure hazard zone

### Set the advanced notice

The advanced notice is how long before the hazard occurs the threat is identified: **Delayed** (−2 to 0 h), **Very short** (0–2 h), **Short**
(2–4 h), **Moderate** (4–8 h), **Long** (8–24 h) or **Very long** (24–48 h). It has no default, and an analysis cannot run until it is chosen. It is
set for each analysis, and **Set by loading level** sets a level differently. When a zone exists, the zone has one notice for every analysis. **Add the
basis** records why the notice was chosen, up to 2000 characters.

### Run

The **Run** panel states how many loading levels are ready to run, and what keeps the others from running, with a link to the page that fixes it.
The consequence simulations, like the flood simulations, start only when the user starts them. The run controls are:

- **Run all** (**Run all [N]** when some levels are ready), at the top of the panel, runs every ready loading level that has no current result. It is
  unavailable when no level is ready, or when every ready level is already running or current.
- **Run**, **Run 1 level** or **Run [N] levels**, beside each analysis, runs that analysis's ready levels.
- The play button beside each loading level runs that level again, whatever its status.
- **Cancel** or **Cancel [N]** cancels the runs in progress, at the top or beside an analysis, and the stop button beside a level cancels that
  level.

None of these asks for confirmation. The panel lists each level's mean life loss, with warning and evacuation, or its status:
Not run, Queued, Simulating, Writing results, Failed, Cancelled, Could not start, or the reason it is blocked. A result marked **Out of date** was
simulated with inputs that have since changed.

Selecting a level's status opens its details: the run's status and timing, any errors and warnings, the result over its 1,000 iterations (mean,
median, 5th to 95th percentile and range of life loss, the mean population at risk and the mean damages), and the hazard and notice it ran with.

### Consequence functions

A card at the top represents each function. One function can be the non-failure function, chosen with its **Non-fail** button; every other function
is a failure function. **Add function** adds a function entered as tables, with a **Name** (up to 100 characters, unique) and a **Role** (Failure or
Non-failure); such a function can be renamed or deleted from its card. **Download all (.csv)** saves every function's table as the risk analysis
receives it.

### Graphs and tables

**Graphs** shows the function's **Life loss** and **Economic damages** against stage: life loss as a box per loading level with its mean, damages as
bars by damage category. Notes below say which levels are left out and why, such as a level that is out of date or not run, and how the function is
held beyond its lowest and highest stages.

**Tables** lists the rows the risk analysis receives. <TableReference tableKey="dst-users-guide-table-consequences-function-rows" /> lists the
columns. DST's rows, from the loading levels, are gray and read-only. User-defined rows can be added, pasted and edited like any DST table (see
[Tables](./04-working-in-dst.mdx#tables)); they sort by stage and save as they are entered, and a user row at a level's stage is sent in place of the
level's. Changes are not saved while any cell is in error.

### Loading level summary and plots

For a function from an analysis, the **Loading level** selector picks one level. **Summary** shows its mean life loss and 5–95% range, mean damages,
population at risk, life loss ÷ population at risk, first water and the share warned and leaving, the inputs it **Ran with**, and its **Damages by
category**. **Plots** shows life loss across the iterations, life loss by the time of day, when people left before the water arrived, and the warning
and evacuation steps from population at risk to lives lost.

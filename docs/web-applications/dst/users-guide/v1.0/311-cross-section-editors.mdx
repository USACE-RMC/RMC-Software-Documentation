# Cross-Section Editors

The cross-section editors define the geometry of each embankment and concrete cross section listed on the Dam Information page. The
embankment editor draws the zones of an earth or rockfill section and its seepage berm. The concrete editor draws the outline of a concrete
monolith, or the weir and pier of a gated overflow section, with its gate, drain and applied forces. The failure mode pages read each section's
geometry, materials and derived crest, toe and slope measures from what the user saves here.

The list of cross sections and each section's card are described in [Cross Sections](./310-dam-information.mdx#cross-sections). Named cross
sections have no editor.

## Opening an Editor

Selecting the drawing on an embankment or concrete cross section's card opens its editor. A section added with **Add Earth Section** or **Add
Concrete Section** opens its editor at once. The editor's title bar shows:

- **Name.** The section's name, or "Unnamed cross section". **Edit name** (the pencil), or a double-click on the name, makes it editable; Enter or
  leaving the box keeps the new name, and Escape restores the previous one. A name holds up to 200 characters and saves at once, without Save.
- **Issue chip.** A count such as **3 issues**: red when any message is an error, amber otherwise. Selecting it lists every message, errors first.
  DST checks the drawing again after each completed gesture.
- **Status.** **Unsaved changes** while the section holds edits not yet saved. The same place shows an edit DST refused and the editing conflict
  notice (see [Common Problems](#common-problems)).

The ×, Escape, or a selection outside the editor closes it. Users who cannot edit the screening, and every user while its inputs are locked (see
[Input Lock](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#input-lock)), open the editors read-only: they can view the drawing and read point coordinates, but the tools,
panels and Save are unavailable.

## Saving and Canceling

The cross-section editors are the one place in DST where edits wait for the user to save them. Every change to the drawing and the section's
settings, such as points, zones and their materials, the seepage berm, the foundation, the concrete section type, the gate, the drain and the
forces, stays in the editor until the user selects **Save**. The section's name and its reference image (see [Reference Images](#reference-images))
save at once instead.

**Save** and **Cancel** appear in the toolbar as soon as the section has unsaved changes. **Save** stores the section. **Cancel** ("Discard the
changes since the last save") returns the section to its last saved state.

The editors have no undo or redo, and Ctrl+Z does nothing on the canvas. Cancel discards every change since the last save at once. Within a drawing
tool, Backspace removes the last point of an outline still being drawn, and Escape or **Cancel** on a pending card (such as **New zone** or
**Split**) drops the result before it is applied.

:::tip

Saving after each change worth keeping limits what **Cancel** discards to the most recent edits.

:::

Saving a section clears the verification of the Dam Information page (see [Human Verification](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#human-verification)). When
the change makes a failure mode page's results out of date, DST clears those results and says so in a notice (see
[Automatic Calculation](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#automatic-calculation)).

### Unsaved changes

Closing an editor that holds unsaved changes opens the **Unsaved changes** dialog: "This cross section has changes that have not been saved."
**Save** saves the section and closes the editor, **Discard** closes it without saving, and **Keep editing** returns to the editor.

Leaving the Dam Information page with an unsaved section opens the **Unsaved Cross Section Changes** dialog: "You have unsaved cross-section edits.
If you leave this page, your changes will be lost." Its buttons are described in [Saving](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#saving).

### Placing and moving points

Points are the corners of every zone, outline and berm. One point can belong to several zones, so a boundary shared by two zones is drawn once.

- **Placing.** With no drawing tool active, a click on empty canvas places a free point. A click on an existing point selects it instead.
- **Inserting on an edge.** When the pointer rests on the edge of a zone or outline, a faint point shows where a new point will land. A click adds
  it to the edge, shared by every zone that uses that edge.
- **Moving.** Dragging a point moves it. Dropping it on another point merges the two into one. Escape during a drag returns the point to where it
  started.
- **Reading coordinates.** Resting the pointer on a point shows its coordinates, for example "X 120.00 · Y 1,050.00".
- **Typing exact coordinates.** A right-click on a point opens the point editor, where **X** and **Y** take exact values (see
  [Point and region menus](#point-and-region-menus)).

A click inside a zone places a free point; it does not select the zone. To select a zone, the user selects its row in the **Zones** list, or
right-clicks inside it and selects **Select zone**. A section holds at most 200 points.

<VideoReference videoKey="dst-users-guide-video-cross-section-edit-points" /> shows a generated embankment being edited.
- The bench point on the downstream face is dragged to a new position, which changes both slopes beside it.
- A point is added on the upstream face by clicking the edge.
- That point's elevation is typed in the point editor.
- The point is then removed with **Delete point**.

### Selecting points

A click on a point selects it, and Ctrl+click adds it to the selection or removes it. Shift+drag draws a selection rectangle and selects the points
inside it. With two or more points selected, a chip reads, for example, "4 selected · 18 unselected", with a **Delete** button that deletes them.
Escape, or a click outside the canvas, clears the selection.

### Point and region menus

A right-click on a point opens the point editor (<FigReference figKey="dst-users-guide-fig-dam-information-point-menu" />); on a touch screen, a
press and hold on the point opens it. The editor is headed **Point**, sometimes followed by a role the point holds, such as **Heel**. Its **X** and
**Y** boxes take exact coordinates to 0.01; a value applies when the user leaves the box or presses Enter. Its actions are:

- **Use as upstream crest**, **Use as downstream crest**, **Use as upstream toe** and **Use as downstream toe**, in the embankment editor, for the
  roles the point is eligible for (see [Crest and toe points](#crest-and-toe-points)).
- **Add to Monolith outline** (or the Weir or Pier outline), in the concrete editor, for each outline the point is not yet part of.
- **Delete point**.

A right-click inside a zone opens a menu with **Select zone**, **Merge with adjacent zone** and **Delete zone**. Inside the seepage berm it offers
**Select berm** and **Delete berm**, and inside a concrete outline **Delete region**. Users who cannot edit see the point's coordinates and no
actions.

## Reference Images

A reference image is a drawing or screenshot of the cross section, loaded into the editor for tracing. The image saves with the screening as soon as it is added or changed,
never needs Save, and does not clear the page's verification. Each section holds one image. The image controls are on the **Image** tab
(<FigReference figKey="dst-users-guide-fig-dam-information-image-calibration" />).

### Adding an image

With no image, the **Image** tab offers **Upload image** and **Paste screenshot**. The image must be a PNG or JPEG file of 10 MB or less, no more
than 16,384 pixels on a side and 40,000,000 pixels in all. Once an image is in place, the tab offers:

- **Show**, which hides or shows the image.
- **Flip**, which mirrors the image left to right, for a drawing with upstream on the right. It is unavailable while the image is hidden or
  calibrated from three known points, which sets the orientation itself.
- **Replace image**, **Replace with a pasted screenshot** and **Remove image and calibration**.

**Show**, **Flip** and **Opacity** save at once with the image, so it reopens as the user left it.

A shown image must be calibrated before drawing: until then the drawing tools are unavailable and clicks on the canvas place calibration marks.
**Calibrate by** chooses **Scale + datum** or **Known points**; DST keeps the entries of both. A guidance line under the choice names the next step,
for example "Start with Level — mark two points along something horizontal (base, water line, crest).", and reads "Calibrated — …" when
calibration is complete. Each step has a **Place** button, which becomes **Move** once the mark is placed and **Cancel** while it waits for a click.
Escape pauses calibration.

### Calibrating by known points

**Known points** uses up to three points whose coordinates can be read off the drawing, labeled **A**, **B** and **C**. For each, the user places
the mark on the image and enters its **X** and **Y**. A and B calibrate the image; the guidance then reads "Calibrated from A and B. Add C to
correct skew or stretch, or start tracing." C is optional and also corrects skew and stretch; the user selects its **Place** button to add it.
With all three, the guidance reads "Calibrated — full affine from A, B, C. Trace the section by clicking."

Points far apart give the best result: A and B at the two toes or the ends of the base, and C at a crest corner, well off the line through A and B.

- **C** at the upstream end of the crest (255, 812).

### Tracing the image

Once the image is calibrated, the drawing tools return and the user traces the section by clicking on the features of the image.
- Snap is off by default while an image is shown, so a click lands where the drawing shows the corner, not at the nearest grid point.
- The pointer coordinates in the status bar confirm the position before each click.
- Tracing the outer outline first as one zone, then drawing each internal zone over it, cuts the internal zones out of the outline (see
  [Drawing, splitting and merging zones](#drawing-splitting-and-merging-zones)).
- Lowering **Opacity**, or turning **Show** off, makes the traced zones easier to check against the drawing.

<VideoReference videoKey="dst-users-guide-video-cross-section-trace" /> traces the calibrated drawing:
- The outer outline is traced through the two toes and the crest corners and named **Shell**, with an upstream shell material.
- The core is drawn over it from the crest to the base and given the clay core material. DST cuts the core out of the shell, which leaves two
  pieces of Shell, one on each side.
- **Save** stores the drawing.

### Drawing, splitting and merging zones

The Body tab has three tools. Selecting an active tool again, or pressing Escape, cancels it. While a tool is active, a note over the canvas
describes the next click, for example "Click points in order; click the first point to close the zone".

- **Draw zone** ("Draw a zone outline — click points in order, click the first point to close"). The user clicks the zone's corners in order; a
  click on an existing point uses that point. A click on the first point, or Enter after three or more points, closes the zone, and Backspace
  removes the last point. The **New zone** card then says how the new zone affects existing zones, for example "The new zone is cut out of 2
  existing zones.", with **Zone Name**, **Material**, **Cancel**, **Skip assignment** (adds the zone without a name or material) and **Apply
  zone**.
- **Split** ("Cut zones in two along a line drawn between boundary points"). The user starts the cut on a zone boundary, adds any corner points,
  and ends on a boundary; Enter ends the cut after two or more points. The **Split** card reads, for example, "1 zone will be split into 2 zones.
  Names and material assignments are inherited.", with **Cancel** and **Apply split**.
- **Merge** ("Combine two zones that share an edge"). The user selects a zone, then **Merge**, then the adjacent zone. The two must share a full
  edge. In the **Merge** card the user chooses the zone that keeps its name and material, then selects **Apply merge** or **Cancel**.

A drawing holds at most 20 zones and berms.

Drawing a zone over existing zones is the most direct way to add an internal zone such as a core, a filter or a drain. The user outlines the internal
zone with its edges on the outer boundary, and DST cuts it out of the zones it covers. When the cut divides a zone into separate pieces, each piece
becomes its own zone with the original name and material, and the **New zone** card says so, for example "The new zone is cut out of 1 existing
zone. 2 remainder pieces keep the original name and material."

- The zone is named **Core** and given the clay core material.
- The single Embankment zone becomes two Embankment zones, one on each side of the core.

### Assigning materials

Every zone needs a material; until it has one, the zone list shows **Unassigned** and the message "Assign a material to this zone." Materials come
from the Materials section of the Dam Information page, so they must be defined there first (see [Materials](./310-dam-information.mdx#materials)).
A zone receives its material in one of these ways:
- **When it is drawn.** The **New zone** card has **Zone Name** and **Material**, and **Apply zone** applies both.
- **Afterward.** Selecting the zone, in the **Zones** list or with **Select zone** on its right-click menu, shows **Selected Zone Name** and
  **Selected Zone Material**. The change is stored with the rest of the section at **Save**.
- **By inheritance.** The parts of a split zone, and the pieces left when a new zone cuts one in two, keep the original zone's material. A merged
  zone keeps the material of the zone chosen in the **Merge** card.
- **From the generator.** **Generate drawing** assigns the Embankment Material, and for a zoned section the Core Material.

### Berm tab

A seepage berm, a separate component downstream of the embankment toe, is added on the **Berm** tab. The tab offers **Draw berm** (or **Redraw berm**
once a berm exists) and **Delete berm**. The berm is drawn the same way as a zone, usually sharing points with the downstream face and the
downstream toe so that it sits against the embankment without overlapping it. When it closes, the **Seepage berm** card reads "The body zones are
unchanged. Assign one main embankment material to the berm.", with **Berm Material**, **Cancel**, **Skip assignment** and **Apply berm**.

With a berm, the tab shows **Berm Material** and, under **Derived berm measures**, the read-only **Berm Length** and **Berm Toe Elevation** that DST
measures from the drawing. Without one, it reads "No berm yet — use Draw berm in the canvas toolbar."

- A click on the first point closes it.
- The **Seepage berm** card assigns the berm fill material, and **Apply berm** adds the berm.
- **Derived berm measures** then shows its length and toe elevation.

### Crest and toe points

DST finds the crest and toe points of the embankment from its outline and labels them **US Crest**, **DS Crest**, **US Toe** and **DS Toe**. When

role offer it.

### Section type

**Section Type** sets the kind of concrete section: **Overflow (Gated)**, **Overflow (Ungated)** or **Non-Overflow**. An ungated overflow or
non-overflow section has one outline, the **Monolith**, and the tabs **Monolith**, **Drain**, **Forces** and **Image**. A gated overflow section has
two outlines, the **Weir** and the **Pier**, and the tabs **Weir**, **Pier**, **Gate**, **Drain**, **Forces** and **Image**. Changing the section type
keeps the section's points and other entries: leaving the gated type turns the weir back into a monolith and keeps the pier and gate entries in case
the user returns to it.

### Outline tabs

The **Monolith**, **Weir** and **Pier** tabs each define one outline, with its number of points or **Not defined**:

- **Define by clicking** (or **Redefine by clicking**) starts the outline. The user clicks its corners in order; a click on empty canvas places a
  new point and a click on a point uses it. A click on the first point, or **Finish region** after three or more points, closes the outline.
  Backspace undoes the last point, and Escape or **Cancel** stops without changing the outline.
- The trash icon ("Delete region (points are kept)") deletes the outline and leaves its points on the canvas.
- **Width, into page** is the width of the component along the dam axis.
- **Upstream end of crest** and **Downstream end of crest**, on the Monolith and Weir tabs, show the points that bound the crest. **Pick** (or
  **Change**) waits for a click on a point of the outline, and × clears the choice.

An outline is edited like a zone: dragging a point moves it, a right-click on a point opens the point editor for exact coordinates or deletion, and
a click on an edge adds a point. DST labels the **Heel**, **Toe** and crest points. The base must be flat: the heel and toe share one elevation, with no base point between
them above it.

### Gate tab

The **Gate** tab, on a gated overflow section, defines the **Tainter Gate**, whose face is drawn as a circular arc about the trunnion between the
sill-contact and top-of-gate elevations. **Place trunnion** (or **Move trunnion**) waits for a click on the canvas, which places a new point or uses
an existing one; **Cancel placement** stops. The gate's other inputs are listed below.

### Drain tab

The **Drain** tab defines an optional drain, one per cross section. **Top** and **Bottom** each have a **Place** (or **Move**) button that waits for
a click on the canvas. **Clear drain** removes the drain.

### Forces tab

The **Forces** tab lists optional applied forces, up to 100. **Add force** adds a row, numbered **F1**, **F2** and so on. In each row the
crosshair button waits for a click at the force's application point, on or off the structure; a click on a point uses it. **Remove force** (the
trash icon) removes the force, and **Cancel placing** (×) stops placing a point.

## Import Points

The **Import points** dialog adds points from coordinates copied from a spreadsheet, a CSV file or any two-column text
(<FigReference figKey="dst-users-guide-fig-dam-information-import-points" />). It opens from **Paste coordinates** on an empty canvas or from the
**Import points** button on the canvas.

The user pastes the block into **Pasted coordinates**: two values per line, X then Y, separated by a tab, a comma or spaces. A header row is skipped,
blank lines and lines beginning with # are ignored, and coordinates are rounded to 0.01. A pasted point at the coordinates of an existing point uses
that point instead of adding a second one.

- **In the embankment editor**, the dialog reads, for example, "12 points will be imported." The imported points are free points; the user then
  draws zones through them with **Draw zone**.
- **In the concrete editor**, **Connect as** is **None — just add points** or an outline, such as **Monolith outline**, which connects the points in
  paste order; a closing point repeated at the end is folded into the outline. Importing into an outline that already exists replaces its point
  order and keeps its points. **Replace all existing points** removes every existing point first, and everything that refers to those points. A
  **Summary** lists the **Rows**, the **New points**, the **Matched existing points (reused)** and, when connecting, the outline's vertices.

**Import** adds the points and closes the dialog; like any other edit, the import waits for **Save**. Closing the dialog without **Import** adds
nothing.

<VideoReference videoKey="dst-users-guide-video-cross-section-paste-coordinates" /> starts an embankment section from pasted coordinates:
- **Paste coordinates** opens the dialog, and a block copied from a spreadsheet is pasted with Ctrl+V. The block has a header row and five X, Y
  pairs: the toes, the two ends of the crest and a bench point.
- The dialog reads "5 points will be imported", and **Import** places the five points and fits the view to them.
- **Draw zone** then joins them in order into a zone named **Embankment**, with a material.

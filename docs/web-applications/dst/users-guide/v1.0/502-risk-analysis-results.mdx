# Results

The Results page, at **Risk Analysis > Results**, shows the risk of the screening. It has no inputs; its toggles only change the view. While the risk
computes, the page reads "Computing risk results…", and values not yet computed show "—".

## Excess Risk Summary

Four tiles summarize the excess risk: **Total APF** (the adjusted annual probability of failure), **Total EALL** (the expected annual life loss),
**Total EAD** (the expected annual damages) and **Individual Risk**, which is the total APF times a fatality rate. The fatality rate is the highest
fatality rate of the failure functions in use, from the loading level or table row it comes from, or 71%, the LifeSim average fatality rate for a high
hazard individual, when none of the functions has one.

The info button on the **Individual Risk** tile opens a popover that shows the calculation with the screening's own values:

- **Individual risk** = total APF × fatality rate, followed by the numbers, in the form "[total APF] × [fatality rate] = [individual risk]".
- **Fatality rate**, with its value and its source: the consequence function and loading level it comes from, the function's table and level when
  the rate was entered on the Results Data page, or "the LifeSim average fatality rate for a high hazard individual" when none of the consequence
  functions in use has a fatality rate.

The popover also explains the rule. The fatality rate is the highest among the failure consequence functions assigned on the Setup page: for each
loading level, the highest mean fatality rate of any structure in its LifeSim results, and any rate entered on Results Data. Non-failure
consequence functions are not used, and neither are failure functions that no failure mode uses. When two levels share the highest rate, the higher
level is used.

Until the total risk is computed, the tile shows "—" and the popover reads "Individual risk is calculated once the total risk is computed." While
DST is still reading the fatality rates from the consequence results, the popover reads "Fatality rates are still being read from the consequence
results. Reload the page shortly."

## Societal Excess Life Safety Risk

This section shows the life safety risk of failure, above the background risk:

- **Mean Risk Plot for Excess Societal Risk** plots each failure mode and the **Total (adjusted)** as APF against expected life loss given failure.
  **TRG line** (on by default) shows the EALL = 1E-03 line, and **Adjusted PFM values** (off by default) plots the failure modes' adjusted values in
  place of their marginal ones.
- **Loss Exceedance Curve for Expected Annual Life Loss** plots the annual exceedance probability of each excess life loss.
- **Risk Contribution** lists the share of the adjusted total APF and EALL from each failure mode, the top eight and then "All others". Resting the
  pointer on a row highlights its series on the plots.

The two toggles above the plots change the view only:

- **TRG line** draws the tolerable risk guideline as a dashed diagonal labeled "EALL = 1E-03" on both life safety plots: on the Mean Risk Plot, the
  points where APF times expected life loss given failure equals 1E-03, and on the Loss Exceedance Curve, the points where the annual exceedance
  probability times the life loss equals 1E-03. Turning the toggle off removes the line from both plots. The economic plots have no guideline line.
- **Adjusted PFM values** switches the failure modes between their marginal values and their combination-adjusted values. It applies to every failure
  mode point and curve on the page: both Mean Risk Plots and both Loss Exceedance Curves, for life loss and for damages. The **Total (adjusted)** is
  always the adjusted total, and the **Risk Contribution** panels always show adjusted values as shares of the adjusted total, whichever way the
  toggle is set.

A failure mode with locations is plotted as one composite point, named with its number followed by "(composite)"; its locations are
not plotted. Each Mean Risk Plot has faint diagonal lines of constant risk, one per decade, and draws a dashed line of constant risk
through the point of the highlighted failure mode.

Highlighting is shared across the life safety and economic sections. Resting the pointer on a failure mode's point, curve or **Risk Contribution**
row dims every other failure mode on the plots and in the **Risk Contribution** panels, and moving the pointer away restores them. The "All others"
row has no series to highlight.

Below the **Risk Contribution** rows, notes list the failure modes that are not plotted: those with an APF below 1E-07, and those not computed, with
the reason, such as "no active system response curve". When the contributions cannot be shown, the panel reads "Adjusted contributions unavailable
or total is zero."; when the adjusted failure mode values do not add up to the adjusted total, it reads "Adjusted PFM values do not sum to the
adjusted Total."

## Societal Excess Economic Risk

This section shows the same views for damages: the **Mean Risk Plot for Excess Economic Risk**, with expected damages given failure, the **Loss
Exceedance Curve for Expected Annual Damages**, and the failure modes' **Risk Contribution** to EAD.

## Total Risk

This section adds the background risk from the non-failure function to the excess risk. The **Loss Exceedance Curves for Annual Life Loss** and
**Loss Exceedance Curves for Annual Damages** plot the **Excess**, **Background** and **Total** curves.

<TableReference tableKey="dst-users-guide-table-risk-components" /> lists the rows of the risk components table, which gives each as life (EALL) and
economic (EAD) risk. **Risk Contribution** bars show the excess and background risk as shares of the total.

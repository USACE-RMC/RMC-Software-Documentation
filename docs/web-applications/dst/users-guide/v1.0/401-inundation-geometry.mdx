# Inundation Geometry

The flood simulations of the Inundation Analysis page run on the hydraulic model defined on the Inundation Geometry page (**Consequences >
Inundation Geometry**). The model consists of the area it covers, the dam, the rivers and other lines that shape the mesh, the terrain and land
cover, and the computational mesh built from them.

The **Model Configuration** panel holds the add buttons, a **Search features…** box and a tree of the model's features. Resting the pointer on an
add button gives the feature's full name. A screening has one model area, one dam centerline, one terrain and one land cover; once one exists, its
button replaces it ("Replace the …"). A warning icon in the tree marks a feature whose settings are incomplete or invalid; resting the pointer
on it gives the reason. Selecting a feature opens its **[Feature] Properties** panel.

## The map

Every page of the Consequences module except Consequence Analysis and Results Data is a map. The map toolbar changes the map style, identifies the
features under the pointer (**Identify features**), measures distances, zooms in, out and to all layers, resets the bearing to north and exaggerates
the terrain. When an editable feature is selected, the toolbar adds **Edit selected feature**, and while editing, **Save edits**, **Cancel edits
(Escape)** and **Add points**. The status strip gives the pointer's longitude, latitude and zoom, and a hint for the current tool, such as "Click a
feature to select".

The layers button opens the legend, which turns the layers on and off in the groups **Inundation Geometry**, **Consequence Data**, **Inundation
Results** and **Reference Layers** (USGS rivers and lakes, and the levees of the National Levee Database). DST remembers which layers are on for
each page during the session. Below zoom 13 the map shows only a sample of the structures; zooming in shows every one.

### Identifying features

With **Identify features** on, selecting a point on the map lists every feature under it, grouped by type, such as the model area, the rivers and
the **Structures**. Selecting a structure's row opens its attributes; selecting any other row selects that feature. Resting the pointer on a row
highlights its feature on the map, which tells overlapping features apart. Large groups list 50 rows at a time, with **Show [N] more**.

Below zoom 13 the list holds only the sampled structures, and notes "Below zoom 13 the map shows a sample of the structures. Zoom in to list every
one here."

### Structure symbology

The structure layer's style editor in the legend sets how the structures are drawn on every page of the module. **Color by** chooses what colors
them: **Damage category** (the default), **Occupancy class**, **Building type**, **Foundation type**, **Foundation height**, **Stories**,
**Structure value**, **Total value**, **Day population**, **Night population**, **LifeSim data checks** or **Single color**. The legend lists the
classes with the number of structures in each.

- From zoom 13, **Damage category** draws each category as its own shape: a house for Residential, a square for Commercial, a triangle for
  Industrial and a diamond for Public.
- Total value is the structure, content and vehicle values together; the populations are the under-65 and 65-and-over counts together.
- **LifeSim data checks** colors each structure by its most serious problem: **Outside the Emergency Response area**, **Missing population**,
  **Missing building data**, **Missing values** or **Unusual population**, or **No problems found** (see
  [Structure inventory](./403-consequence-data.mdx#structure-inventory)).

The style editor also sets the symbol size, outline, opacity and marker, and each class can be edited on its own. DST keeps these choices across the
pages of the module until the page is reloaded.

## Terrain

The terrain supplies the ground elevations of the mesh. **Terrain** opens the **Add Terrain** dialog, with two tabs:

- **OpenTopography** downloads a terrain over the model geometry with a 660 ft (200 m) buffer. The **Dataset** is Copernicus 30m (COP30, the
  default) or 90m (COP90), SRTM 30m (SRTMGL1) or 90m (SRTMGL3), or USGS 1m, 10m or 30m (3DEP, United States only). A **Download Area** preview shows
  the area to be requested. One download covers at most 4° of latitude or longitude, 1.5 billion cells and 40 requests. **Download** starts it, and
  it continues after the dialog closes.
- **Upload File** reads a GeoTIFF (.tif, .tiff) digital elevation model up to 11 GB, chosen with **Select GeoTIFF File**. **Elevation Units**
  (Meters or Feet) is required; meters are converted to feet.

**Name** is optional; a blank name is generated. While the terrain processes, its row shows a spinner, and DST reports "Terrain "[name]" is
ready!" when it finishes. Its properties panel shows its source, resolution, units, dataset and bounding box, and warns when the model geometry
extends past the terrain.

:::caution

Replacing a downloaded terrain deletes the old one when the download starts. If the download fails, the model has no terrain until another is
added.

:::

## Land cover

The land cover sets the roughness (Manning's n) of the floodplain. **Land Cover** opens the **Add Land Cover** dialog, with two tabs:

- **NLCD** downloads the National Land Cover Database over the model geometry with a 660 ft buffer. **NLCD Year** is **Latest available** (the
  default) or a release from 2021 back to 2001.
- **Upload File** reads a GeoTIFF of whole-number land cover classes. NLCD codes keep their NLCD names and Manning's n; any other code becomes
  "Class N" with n = 0.06 until it is set.

The properties panel shows the land cover's source, year, resolution and bounding box. **Edit Manning's n…** opens the **Manning's n by Land Cover
Class** dialog, which lists each class's **Code**, **Land Cover Class**, **Default n** and **Manning's n**. Each Manning's n is greater than 0 and
at most 1. **Reset to defaults** restores the defaults and **Save** keeps the changes. Changing a value marks existing simulation results as out of
date. Where the model extends past the land cover, the mesh uses a Manning's n of 0.06.

## Computational mesh

**Mesh** computes the computational mesh from the model geometry; selecting it again while the mesh computes cancels it. The status under **Model
Geometry** reads "Queued…", a percentage, "[N] cells", "[N] cells · [K] warnings", "Failed", "Cancelled" or "Not computed". A mesh needs a model
area with a drawn boundary.

When the mesh fails or has warnings, the **Mesh failed** or **Mesh warnings** card opens over the map. It lists each message from the mesher with
**Show on Map**, which outlines and zooms to the problem.

:::note

DST does not recompute the mesh when the geometry changes. The user selects **Mesh** again after changing the geometry or the dam's mesh settings.

:::

## Editing, saving and downloading geometry

The fields in a properties panel save when the user leaves the field (see [Saving](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#saving)). Outlines are edited
explicitly:

- **Edit Geometry** in the properties panel, or **Edit selected feature** in the toolbar, starts an edit, and **Save** or **Cancel** ends it; Escape
  cancels.
- Switching features with unsaved edits asks "Discard Unsaved Changes?" (**Keep Editing** or **Discard Changes**), and leaving the page asks for
  confirmation (**Stay** or **Leave Page**).
- The trash icon in the properties panel deletes a feature after confirmation.

Each feature downloads as GeoJSON from its tree row or panel, and the terrain and land cover as their files. The **Model Geometry** download saves
all the geometry as one GeoJSON file, with the mesh (.h5, .hdf) once it is computed.

Changes to the geometry, terrain, land cover or mesh do not clear later results. DST marks the inundation and consequence results that depend on
them as out of date until they run again.

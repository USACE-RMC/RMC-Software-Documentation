# Dam Information

The failure mode pages evaluate the dam as the Dam Information page (**Response > Dam Information**) describes it: the features of the
embankment, the materials of construction, the cross sections and the slopes of the abutments. The Failure Mode Screening page reads the
embankment features, and each failure mode page reads the materials, cross sections and abutment slopes that apply to it. The cross sections
themselves are drawn in the [Cross-Section Editors](./311-cross-section-editors.mdx).

<FigReference figKey="dst-users-guide-fig-dam-information-page" /> shows the top of the page for a dam with embankment and concrete sections, and
<TableReference tableKey="dst-users-guide-table-dam-information-page-regions" /> describes each numbered region.

## Materials

**Materials** defines the materials of which the cross sections are built: the embankment materials table for an embankment dam, the concrete
unit weight for a concrete dam, or both.

### Embankment materials

The table has two groups of rows. **Embankment** holds the main materials, **Material 01**, **Material 02** and so on. **Drains & filters** holds
the drain and filter materials, **Drain/Filter 01** and so on, and the **Conduit Filter** row that DST adds when the conduit has a diaphragm filter.
Each material's color swatch matches the color of the zones drawn in it in the cross sections.

**Add material** and **Add drain or filter** add a row, up to 20 materials in all. **Remove material** (the trash icon) deletes a row after the
**Delete Material** confirmation ("This action cannot be undone."). The table always keeps at least one embankment material, and the Conduit Filter
row cannot be deleted. A badge beside a row's label counts its errors.

:::caution
Deleting a material leaves every zone and seepage berm drawn in it without a material. Those zones read **Unassigned** in the editor until the user
assigns another material and saves (see [Common Problems](#common-problems)).
:::

The table's page messages include the required inputs "Add at least one embankment material.", "Select the material type for [material]." and
"Indicate whether [material] contains dispersive soils.", and the warning "[material]: gradation is marked available but no gradation rows have
been entered."

### Gradation

To enter a gradation, the user sets **Gradation** to **Yes** and selects **Enter gradation**. The dialog, headed for example "Gradation — Material
01 — Zone I - Impervious", holds two tables, **Coarse Band** and **Fine Band**, and a chart of both curves
(<FigReference figKey="dst-users-guide-fig-dam-information-gradation" />). On a phone, **Tables** and **Plot** switch between the tables and the
chart. Each table has the columns:

- **Sieve Size**: a standard sieve chosen from the list, or **Custom** or **Hydrometer** for a size entered by hand.
- **D (mm)**: the particle size. DST fills it for a standard sieve; the user enters it for Custom and Hydrometer.
- **Coarse (%)** or **Fine (%)**: the percent passing, between 0 and 100.

The tables work like the other tables in DST, including pasting rows from a spreadsheet (see [Tables](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#tables)), and save as
the user edits. Each band holds up to 500 rows. The issue chip in the dialog's title lists the gradation's messages: the errors "[material] ([coarse
or fine] gradation): particle size at row [n] must be greater than zero." and "[material] ([coarse or fine] gradation): percent passing at row [n]
must be between 0 and 100.", and the warnings "[material] ([coarse or fine] gradation): particle sizes are not in decreasing order — check the
curve." and "[material] ([coarse or fine] gradation): percent passing increases as particle size decreases — check the curve."

### Adding a cross section

Each group ends with an add card: **Add Earth Section**, **Add Concrete Section** or **Add Named Section**. Selecting it adds a section. A new
embankment or concrete section opens its editor at once (see [Opening an Editor](./311-cross-section-editors.mdx#opening-an-editor)). When the
screening has more than one named-only dam type, **Add Named Section** first asks "Which dam type does this section represent?" with a button for
each dam type.

### Section cards

Each card shows:

- **Name.** The name or station range, or "Unnamed cross section". Selecting the name ("Rename") makes it editable, up to 200 characters; Escape
  restores the previous name. A section needs a name ("Provide a name or station range for the cross section.").
- **Issue badge.** A count of the section's errors (red) or warnings (amber). Selecting it lists them.
- **Crest justification chip.** Shown when the section's drawn crest differs by more than 0.5 (in the screening's elevation units) from the dam crest
  elevation on Dam Data, or, for a concrete overflow section, from the spillway crest. It explains the difference, for example "This crest (1250.80
  ft-NAVD88) is 0.80 ft above the Dam Data dam crest (1250.00).", and **Add justification** opens a box for the reason. The drawn crest still governs
  every calculation.
- **Type.** **Zoned**, **Homogeneous Earthfill** or **Earth Embankment** for an embankment section (the first two once every zone has a material),
  **Concrete Overflow (Gated)**, **Concrete Overflow (Ungated)** or **Concrete Non-Overflow**, or **Named**.
- **Drawing.** A thumbnail of the section. Selecting it opens the section's editor.

A named section has no drawing. Its card shows the dam type it represents and a box, "Describe this section (optional)", of up to 500 characters. A
named section needs its dam type ("Select the dam type this section represents.").

### Copying and deleting

The menu on a card offers:

- **Copy** adds a copy named after the section with " (Copy)" added, placed last in its group. It carries the section's drawing, description, crest
  justification and reference image with its calibration.
- **Copy at new base elevation**, for an embankment section with zones, copies the section cut off at a higher base (see
  [Copying at a new base elevation](#copying-at-a-new-base-elevation)).
- **Delete** opens the **Delete Cross Section** confirmation: "Are you sure you want to delete [name]? This action cannot be undone."

:::tip
Several similar sections are most efficiently produced by drawing one, copying it with **Copy** and editing each copy. A copy keeps the reference
image and its calibration, so tracing continues without calibrating again.
:::

### Copying at a new base elevation

**Copy at new base elevation** makes a shorter copy of a section already drawn, with its base raised, for a station where the foundation stands
higher. DST cuts every zone at the new base and keeps what lies above it; a drain or filter along the old base is raised with the base. The source
section does not change, and the copy is placed immediately after it. The copy does not carry the source's reference image.

### Reference images

An embankment or concrete section can hold a reference image: a drawing or screenshot of the section placed under the editor's canvas for tracing.
The image saves as soon as it is added, changed or removed, and these changes do not clear the page's verification. Images are added and calibrated
in the editors (see [Reference Images](./311-cross-section-editors.mdx#reference-images)).

### PFM-02 locations

The abutment slopes also decide where PFM-02 is evaluated. Each saved change to the section brings the PFM-02 locations into line with the slopes and
benches (see [Location and Cross Section](./352-pfm-02.mdx#location-and-cross-section)):

- **Abutments.** An abutment whose top slope is between 15° and 65° is a location, **Left Abutment** or **Right Abutment**.
- **Benches.** Every bench is a location, for example **Left Abutment Bench 1**, whatever the top slope.
- **Cutoff walls.** When the cutoff or seepage barrier wall is marked **At crest** and Failure Mode Screening question 7a says it ties into an
  abutment, DST hides that abutment's locations. They return, with their inputs, when the tie is removed.

DST adds new locations only while PFM-02 is selected for evaluation. When no abutment or bench yields a location, PFM-02 keeps a single location,
**Location 1**, that the team describes itself.

:::caution
Moving a top slope outside 15° to 65° deletes that abutment's PFM-02 location with its inputs and results. Deleting a bench deletes the location of
that side's last bench; the benches below move up a number and take over the locations, and the inputs, of their new numbers.
:::

# Dam Information

The Dam Information page (**Response > Dam Information**) holds the physical description of the dam that the failure mode pages evaluate: the
features of the embankment, the materials it is built from, its cross sections and the slopes of its abutments. The Failure Mode Screening page
reads the embankment features, and each failure mode page reads the materials, cross sections and abutment slopes that apply to it.

This page describes the page and the list of cross sections. The editors that draw each cross section are described in
[Cross-Section Editors](./311-cross-section-editors.mdx).

## Materials

The **Materials** section holds the materials the cross sections are built from. For an embankment dam it holds the embankment materials table; for
a concrete dam it holds the concrete unit weight; a dam of both kinds shows both side by side.

### Embankment materials

The table has two groups of rows. **Embankment** holds the main materials, labeled **Material 01**, **Material 02** and so on. **Drains & filters**
holds the drain and filter materials, **Drain/Filter 01** and so on, and the **Conduit Filter** row that DST adds when the conduit has a diaphragm
filter. Each material's color swatch matches the color of the zones drawn in it. <TableReference tableKey="dst-users-guide-table-dam-information-material-inputs" />
lists the columns.

**Add material** and **Add drain or filter** add a row. A screening holds at most 20 materials; when the limit is reached, the buttons are
unavailable ("Material limit reached. Delete a material to add another."). **Remove material** (the trash icon) opens the **Delete Material**
confirmation, which warns "This action cannot be undone."; the table always keeps at least one embankment material, and the Conduit Filter row
cannot be deleted. A badge beside a row's label counts its errors.

Deleting a material does not delete what was drawn in it. In every embankment cross section, the zones and the seepage berm that used the material
lose their material, and so does the core material the embankment generator remembers for the section. The zones then read **Unassigned** on the
editor's Body tab, the section asks again for "Assign a material to this zone." or "Assign a material to the seepage berm.", and its card shows the
type **Earth Embankment** until every zone has a material again. To restore the section, the user assigns another material in the editor and saves.

The page messages for the table include "Add at least one embankment material.", "Select the material type for [material].", "Indicate whether
[material] contains dispersive soils." (required inputs) and "[material]: gradation is marked available but no gradation rows have been entered." (a
warning).

### Gradation

**Enter gradation** opens the gradation dialog for the material, headed, for example, "Gradation — Material 01 — Zone I - Impervious"
(<FigReference figKey="dst-users-guide-fig-dam-information-gradation" />). It holds two tables, **Coarse Band** and **Fine Band**, and a chart of both
curves. On a narrow screen, **Tables** and **Plot** switch between the tables and the chart. Each table has the columns:

- **Sieve Size**: a standard sieve chosen from the list, or **Custom** or **Hydrometer** for a size entered by hand.
- **D (mm)**: the particle size. DST fills it for a standard sieve; the user enters it for Custom and Hydrometer.
- **Coarse (%)** or **Fine (%)**: the percent passing, between 0 and 100.

The tables work like the other tables in DST (see [Tables](./040-working-in-dst.mdx#tables)) and save as the user edits. The issue chip in the
dialog's title lists the gradation's messages: the errors "[material] ([coarse or fine] gradation): particle size at row [n] must be greater than
zero." and "[material] ([coarse or fine] gradation): percent passing at row [n] must be between 0 and 100.", and the warnings "[material] ([coarse
or fine] gradation): particle sizes are not in decreasing order — check the curve." and "[material] ([coarse or fine] gradation): percent passing
increases as particle size decreases — check the curve." The × closes the dialog.

Each band holds up to 500 rows. A gradation with a longer table is not saved, and DST reports "A gradation table can hold at most 500 rows."

### Adding a cross section

Each group ends with a dashed card: **Add Earth Section**, **Add Concrete Section** or **Add Named Section**. Selecting it adds a section. A new
embankment or concrete section opens its editor at once (see
[Opening an Editor](./311-cross-section-editors.mdx#opening-an-editor)). When the screening has more than one named-only dam type,
**Add Named Section** first asks "Which dam type does this section represent?" with a button for each dam type.

### Section cards

Each card's header holds the section's name, its badges and its type, and its body holds the drawing:

- **Name.** The name or station range, or "Unnamed cross section". Selecting the name ("Rename") makes it editable, up to 200 characters; Escape
  restores the previous name. A section needs a name ("Provide a name or station range for the cross section."), and two sections with the same name
  show the warning "More than one cross section is named '[name]' — rename one so failure modes can tell them apart."
- **Issue badge.** A count of the section's errors, red, or of its warnings, amber. Selecting it lists them.
- **Crest justification chip.** Shown when the section's drawn crest differs by more than 0.5 (in the screening's elevation units) from the dam crest elevation on Dam Data, or for a
  concrete overflow section from the spillway crest. It explains the difference, for example "This crest (1250.80 ft-NAVD88) is 0.80 ft above the Dam Data
  dam crest (1250.00).", and **Add justification** opens a box for the reason. The drawn crest still governs every calculation. The section header
  counts the sections still missing a justification, for example "2 crests need justification".
- **Type.** The section type: **Zoned**, **Homogeneous Earthfill** or **Earth Embankment** for an embankment section (the first two once every zone
  has a material), **Concrete Overflow (Gated)**, **Concrete Overflow (Ungated)** or **Concrete Non-Overflow**, or **Named**.
- **Drawing.** A thumbnail of the section. Selecting it opens the section's editor.

A named section has no drawing. Its card shows the dam type it represents and a single box, "Describe this section (optional)", of up to 500
characters, which saves when the user leaves it. A named section needs its dam type ("Select the dam type this section represents.").

### Copying and deleting

The menu at the right of a card header offers:

- **Copy** adds a copy of the section. The copy is named after the section with " (Copy)" added and is placed last in its group. It carries the
  section's drawing, description and crest justification, and its reference image together with the image's calibration, so tracing can continue on
  the copy without calibrating again.
- **Copy at new base elevation**, for an embankment section with zones, opens a dialog that copies the section cut off at a new, higher base. The user
  enters **New base elevation** (the hint gives the allowed range, "Above [x] and below [y] ft") and a **Name**, which defaults to the section name
  followed by "@ El." and the elevation. A preview shows the original and the copy, with the new **Height** and **Base width** beside their former
  values, and **Review before creating** lists anything DST changed, such as a seepage berm that was not carried over. **Create** adds the copy;
  **Create and edit** adds it and opens its editor; **Cancel** closes the dialog.
- **Delete** opens the **Delete Cross Section** confirmation: "Are you sure you want to delete [name]? This action cannot be undone."

A screening holds at most 100 cross sections; a copy beyond that is refused with "A screening can hold at most 100 cross sections."

### Copying at a new base elevation

**Copy at new base elevation** makes a shorter copy of a section already drawn, with its base raised, for a station where the foundation stands
higher. DST cuts every zone of the source at the new base and keeps what lies above it; a zone whose base is already above the new
base is kept as it is. A drain or filter layer that runs along the old base is raised with the base, so the copy keeps its drainage at its foundation.
The source section is not changed, and the copy is placed right after it.

DST builds the preview when the user leaves the **New base elevation** box. The preview draws the copy over the original, and it zooms and pans
like the embankment generator's preview (see [Generating a simple embankment](./311-cross-section-editors.mdx#generating-a-simple-embankment)).
The **Name** holds up to 200 characters; clearing it restores the default name. The copy does not carry the source's reference image.

DST refuses a copy it cannot make, and shows the reason under the **New base elevation** box:

- "Draw this cross section before copying it at a new base elevation." when the section has no zones.
- "Assign a material to every zone before copying this cross section at a new base elevation."
- "Fix this cross section so its zones form one closed shape before copying it at a new base elevation."
- "Enter a base elevation above [x] and below [y]." for an elevation outside the allowed range.
- "This cross section can't be copied at El. [x]: "[zone]" would surround another zone."
- "This cross section could not be cut cleanly at El. [x]. Copy it and edit the copy instead."
- "The copy would need more than 200 points or 20 zones. Copy the section and edit it instead."

A copy DST can make may still need a look. **Review before creating** lists each change DST made to the drawing:

- "The seepage berm was not carried over. Draw it again if it applies at this location."
- ""[zone]" is entirely below the new base and was removed."
- "Only a sliver of "[zone]" was left above the new base, so it was merged into the zone next to it."
- "Only a sliver of "[zone]" is left above the new base. Check whether to keep it." The sliver is kept.
- ""[zone]" was cut into [n] separate pieces." Each piece becomes its own zone, named "[zone] (1)", "[zone] (2)" and so on, with the zone's
  material.
- "A small piece of "[zone]" was cut off from the rest of the section by the new base and was removed."
- "Part of [drain] along the base was trimmed where it meets the outside of the new section (about [n]% of it). Check its ends."

The notices are advice, not errors: **Create** and **Create and edit** stay available once the preview matches the entered elevation and the copy has
a name, and the copy can be corrected in its editor afterward.

### Reference images

An embankment or concrete section can hold a reference image: a drawing or screenshot of the section placed under its editor's canvas for tracing.
The image saves with the screening as soon as it is added, changed or removed, and these changes do not clear the page's verification. Images are
added and calibrated in the editors (see [Reference Images](./311-cross-section-editors.mdx#reference-images)).

### PFM-02 locations

The abutment slopes also decide where PFM-02 is evaluated. Each saved change to the section brings the PFM-02 locations into line with the slopes and
benches (see [Location and Cross Section](./352-pfm-02.mdx#location-and-cross-section)):

- **Abutments.** An abutment whose top slope is between 15° and 65° is a location, **Left Abutment** or **Right Abutment**. When its top slope
  moves outside that range, DST deletes the location with its inputs and results.
- **Benches.** Every bench is a location, for example **Left Abutment Bench 1**, whatever the top slope. DST matches bench locations to benches by
  number, so deleting a bench deletes the location of that side's last bench, with its inputs and results. The benches below a deleted bench move up
  a number and take over the locations of their new numbers, with the inputs entered there; after deleting any bench but the last, the user reviews
  those locations.
- **Cutoff walls.** When the cutoff or seepage barrier wall is marked **At crest** and Failure Mode Screening question 7a says it ties into an

# Erosion Model

For each Monte Carlo realization at each overtopping depth, the model steps through the hydrograph ([Chapter 4](./04-hydrograph-generation.mdx)) in two sequential phases. The first
phase models the removal of the protective grass cover under sustained overtopping flow. The second phase models the progressive erosion of the
exposed embankment soil until breach occurs or the hydrograph ends.

## Phase 1: Grass Cover Removal

When the downstream face has grass cover (any grass cover condition other than No Grass Cover), the model tracks the cumulative overflow volume in excess
of a critical discharge and removes the cover when that volume exceeds an erosional limit. The method follows the CIRIA-based approach for tolerable
time-varying overflow on grass-covered slopes <Citation citationKey="Hughes2015" /> <Citation citationKey="Thornton2014" />.

### Critical Discharge

The critical discharge $q_c$ is the unit discharge at which grass damage begins. It is computed from the critical velocity $u_c$ of the grass cover
condition, the friction factor $f$ and the slope angle:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-critical-discharge" equation="q_c = \frac{f}{2 g \sin\theta} \cdot u_c^3" />

where:

> $g$ = gravitational acceleration, 9.81 m/s²  
> $\theta = \arctan(S)$ = angle of the downstream face from horizontal, with $S = 1/Z$ the slope gradient  
> $u_c$ = critical velocity of the grass cover condition (m/s)  
> $f$ = friction factor of flow over the grass cover, named in the model as the Darcy-Weisbach friction factor ([Chapter 3](./03-input-parameters.mdx))

Because $u_c$ and $g$ are in SI units, the equation gives $q_c$ in m²/s; for a US Customary analysis, $q_c$ is converted to ft²/s by dividing by
$(0.3048)^2$. The grass cover phase uses the sine of the slope angle, $\sin\theta$, whereas the erosion phase uses the slope gradient
$S = \tan\theta$ (Assumption 13 in [Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-18 — Definition]
The model names $f$ the Darcy-Weisbach friction factor, but the form $q_c = f u_c^3 / (2 g \sin\theta)$, and likewise $VET_c = G_f f / (2 g \sin\theta)$,
corresponds to the Fanning friction factor; with the Darcy-Weisbach factor the denominator would be $8 g \sin\theta$. The equations here are written
exactly as the model evaluates them. Confirm which friction factor is meant, checking against the formulation in Hughes and Thornton (2015), so that
the parameter name, the default value of 0.015 (IN-07) and the equations agree.
:::

### Erosional Limit Volume

The erosional limit volume $VET_c$ is the cumulative excess overflow volume per unit crest length at which the grass cover is removed:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-vet" equation="VET_c = \frac{G_f \cdot f}{2 g \sin\theta}" />

where $G_f$ is the erosional limit of the grass cover condition, tabulated in [Chapter 3](./03-input-parameters.mdx) with the units lb·s/ft³. The model evaluates the equation
with $g$ = 9.81 m/s² in both unit systems. For a US Customary analysis it uses the tabulated $G_f$ and divides the result by $(0.3048)^2$; for an SI
analysis it multiplies the tabulated $G_f$ by 0.3048 and applies no further conversion.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-19 — Definition]
The physical quantity that the erosional limit $G_f$ represents, and its consistent units, are not established. The tabulated units (lb·s/ft³) do not
combine with $f$ and $g$ (m/s²) to give a volume per unit crest length for $VET_c$, so the unit conversions above cannot be checked. As evaluated, the
US Customary and SI results for the same grass cover condition differ by a factor of $1/(0.3048)^3$, whereas converting a volume per unit crest length
from m² to ft² requires $1/(0.3048)^2$; the model owner should check whether the two unit systems give the same grass cover threshold. Provide the
definition of $G_f$ (with reference to Hughes and Thornton, 2015) and its consistent units, and confirm the conversions.
:::

### Cumulative Excess Volume

At each time step, the model computes the discharge in excess of the critical discharge and accumulates the excess volume with the trapezoidal rule:

<Equation
  equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-excess-volume"
  equation="V_{\text{cum}}(t_i) = V_{\text{cum}}(t_{i-1}) + \frac{\Delta t_s}{2} \left[ \max(q_i - q_c, 0) + \max(q_{i-1} - q_c, 0) \right]"
/>

where:

> $V_{\text{cum}}(t_i)$ = cumulative excess volume per unit crest length at time step $i$  
> $\Delta t_s$ = hydrograph time step expressed in seconds, $\Delta t_s = 3600 \cdot \Delta t$ with $\Delta t$ in hours  
> $q_i$ = unit discharge at time step $i$  
> $q_c$ = critical discharge from <EquationReference equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-critical-discharge" />

The grass cover is removed when $V_{\text{cum}} > VET_c$. Erosion of the exposed soil begins in the same time step. With No Grass Cover, the soil
is exposed from the onset of overtopping and the model starts in Phase 2.

## Phase 2: Embankment Erosion

Once the grass cover is removed, the model computes the erosion rate of the exposed soil at each time step with positive discharge, using the excess
shear stress formulation applied in WinDAM C (USDA NRCS) <Citation citationKey="Hanson2011" />.

### Flow Depth on the Slope

The flow depth on the downstream face is the normal depth from Manning's equation for steady uniform flow (Assumption 12 in [Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)). Both the
flow depth and the applied shear stress use the slope gradient $S = 1/Z = \tan\theta$ in place of the sine of the slope angle (Assumption 13 in
[Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)):

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-flow-depth" equation="h_s = \left(\frac{q \cdot n}{k_n \sqrt{S}}\right)^{0.6}" />

where:

> $h_s$ = flow depth on the slope (ft or m)  
> $q$ = unit discharge at the current time step  
> $n$ = Manning's roughness coefficient (sampled; [Chapter 6](./06-monte-carlo-simulation.mdx))  
> $k_n$ = Manning's equation coefficient, 1.486 (US Customary) or 1.0 (SI)  
> $S = 1/Z$ = slope gradient of the downstream face

### Applied Shear Stress

The shear stress the overtopping flow applies to the embankment surface is:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-shear-stress" equation="\tau = \gamma_w \cdot h_s \cdot S" />

where:

> $\tau$ = applied shear stress (psf or Pa)  
> $\gamma_w$ = unit weight of water, 62.4 lb/ft³ (US Customary) or 9,810 N/m³ (SI)  
> $h_s$ = flow depth from <EquationReference equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-flow-depth" />

### Erosion Rate

The erosion rate follows the excess shear stress equation:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-erosion-rate" equation="\varepsilon = k_d \cdot \max(\tau - \tau_c, \, 0)^\alpha" />

where:

> $\varepsilon$ = erosion rate (ft/hr or m/hr)  
> $k_d$ = erodibility coefficient (sampled; [Chapter 6](./06-monte-carlo-simulation.mdx))  
> $\tau$ = applied shear stress from <EquationReference equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-shear-stress" />  
> $\tau_c$ = critical shear stress (sampled; [Chapter 6](./06-monte-carlo-simulation.mdx))  
> $\alpha$ = erosion exponent ([Chapter 3](./03-input-parameters.mdx); default 1.0)

When the user excludes the critical shear stress from the erosion equation ([Chapter 3](./03-input-parameters.mdx)), $\tau_c$ is taken as zero and the erosion rate becomes
$\varepsilon = k_d \cdot \tau^\alpha$.

### Cumulative Erosion and Breach

The erosion depth accumulates over the time steps:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-cumulative-erosion" equation="D(t_i) = D(t_{i-1}) + \varepsilon(t_i) \cdot \Delta t" />

where $D(t_i)$ is the cumulative erosion depth at time step $i$ (ft or m) and $\Delta t$ is the hydrograph time step in hours (the same step as
$\Delta t_s$ in the grass cover phase), matching the units of $\varepsilon$. Breach occurs when the cumulative erosion depth reaches the critical erosion depth:

<EquationNoRef equation="D(t_i) \geq D_c" />

Once breach occurs, the realization is recorded as a breach at that overtopping depth and no further time steps are evaluated. If the hydrograph ends
first, the realization is recorded as no breach.

## Critical Erosion Depth

The critical erosion depth $D_c$ is the depth, measured perpendicular to the downstream face, that the face must erode for its retreating surface to
reach the upstream edge of the crest. The model treats erosion of the downstream face as uniform over the whole slope (Assumption 2 in [Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)): the
face retreats parallel to itself until it reaches the upstream crest edge (Assumption 3 in [Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)).

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-dc" equation="D_c = \max\!\left(\frac{W_c}{\sqrt{1 + Z^2}}, \; D_{\min}\right)" />

where:

> $W_c$ = crest width (ft or m)  
> $Z$ = downstream slope ratio (H:V)  
> $D_{\min}$ = minimum critical erosion depth, 2.0 ft (US Customary) or 0.6 m (SI)

The minimum prevents a small breach threshold for a narrow crest; for a 3H:1V slope it governs when the crest is narrower than about 6.3 ft. The
critical erosion depth depends only on the geometry, so it is the same for every realization and overtopping depth.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-13 — Engineering Basis]
The breach criterion — breach when the uniformly retreating downstream face reaches the upstream edge of the crest — has no stated engineering basis.
Provide the literature source (with full bibliographic details) or the engineering-judgment attribution for this criterion.
:::

The minimum critical erosion depth $D_{\min}$ = 2.0 ft (0.6 m) has no stated engineering basis. Provide the literature source or the
engineering-judgment attribution for this value.

## Calculation Sequence

For one realization at one overtopping depth, the model evaluates the following at each time step of the hydrograph, from the onset of overtopping
until breach occurs or the scaled duration ends:

<ProcessList
  items={[
    'While the grass cover is intact, add the excess overflow volume for the time step to the cumulative excess volume, and remove the cover if the cumulative excess volume exceeds the erosional limit volume.',
    "Once the grass cover is removed (or from the onset, with No Grass Cover), and while the discharge is positive, compute the flow depth on the slope from Manning's equation, the applied shear stress, and the erosion rate, and add the erosion for the time step to the cumulative erosion depth.",
    'If the cumulative erosion depth reaches the critical erosion depth, record a breach and stop.',
  ]}
/>

The result of each realization at each depth is whether the grass cover was removed and whether breach occurred; [Chapter 6](./06-monte-carlo-simulation.mdx) describes how these
outcomes become probabilities.

# Monte Carlo Simulation

The model uses Monte Carlo simulation to propagate the uncertainty in the embankment material properties through the erosion model ([Chapter 5](./05-erosion-model.mdx)) and
compute the probability of breach at each overtopping depth. The sampled quantities are the erodibility coefficient $k_d$, the critical shear stress
$\tau_c$ and Manning's roughness coefficient $n$. The geometry, the grass cover parameters, the hydraulic coefficients and the hydrographs are not
sampled.

## Correlated Material Property Sampling

The three material properties are sampled jointly rather than independently, so that each realization is a physically consistent combination of
properties ([Chapter 3](./03-input-parameters.mdx) gives the correlation inputs).

### Gaussian Copula

The model uses a Gaussian copula, which imposes a correlation structure while preserving each property's own (marginal) distribution (the literature
basis for this method and for Latin Hypercube sampling is pending, IN-20 below):

### Correlation Matrix

The correlation matrix is:

<Equation
  equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-correlation-matrix"
  equation="\mathbf{R} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \rho_{k_d,\tau_c} & \rho_{k_d,\tau_c} \cdot \rho_{\tau_c,n} \\ \rho_{k_d,\tau_c} & 1 & \rho_{\tau_c,n} \\ \rho_{k_d,\tau_c} \cdot \rho_{\tau_c,n} & \rho_{\tau_c,n} & 1 \end{bmatrix}"
/>

With the default correlations ($\rho_{k_d,\tau_c} = -0.6$, $\rho_{\tau_c,n} = -0.2$), the matrix is:

<EquationNoRef equation="\mathbf{R} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.0 & -0.6 & 0.12 \\ -0.6 & 1.0 & -0.2 \\ 0.12 & -0.2 & 1.0 \end{bmatrix}" />

Setting the correlation between $k_d$ and $n$ to the product of the other two is equivalent to assuming that, in the copula's normal space, $k_d$ and
$n$ are independent once $\tau_c$ is known: their only association comes through the critical shear stress (Assumption 11 in [Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)). A matrix with
this structure is a valid (positive-definite) correlation matrix for any pair of specified correlations strictly between −1 and 1.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-15 — Engineering Basis]
The implied correlation structure — $k_d$ and $n$ associated only through $\tau_c$, so that $\rho_{k_d,n} = \rho_{k_d,\tau_c} \cdot \rho_{\tau_c,n}$
— has no stated engineering basis. The previous edition stated that the relationship arises because the other two correlations "are established
through data analysis," without a source. Provide the literature source or the engineering-judgment attribution for this structure.
:::

### Latin Hypercube Sampling

Latin Hypercube sampling divides the probability range of each variable into equal-probability intervals and draws one value from each interval. It
covers the full range of each property more evenly than simple random sampling, so stable breach probabilities are reached with fewer realizations.

## Simulation of Each Realization

Each realization draws one set of material properties and evaluates the erosion model ([Chapter 5](./05-erosion-model.mdx)) at every overtopping depth on the system response
curve with that same set. Because the same properties are used at every depth, the differences between depths in the result come from the
hydrographs alone, not from resampling the material.

## Breach Probability

After all realizations are complete, the breach probability at each overtopping depth is the proportion of realizations that reached breach:

<Equation
  equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-breach-prob"
  equation="P(\text{breach} \mid H) = \frac{\text{number of realizations with breach at depth } H}{N}"
/>

where $N$ is the number of Monte Carlo realizations. The model also counts, at each depth, the realizations in which the grass cover was removed;
[Chapter 8](./08-output-and-interpretation.mdx) describes the grass removal and conditional breach curves built from those counts.

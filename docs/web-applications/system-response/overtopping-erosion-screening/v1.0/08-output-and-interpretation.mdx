# Output and Interpretation

## Result Curves

The model produces three curves, each giving a probability at every overtopping depth evaluated ([Overtopping Depth Range](./03-input-parameters.mdx#overtopping-depth-range) in Chapter 3):

- **System response curve** — the probability of breach at each overtopping depth: the proportion of Monte Carlo realizations that reached breach
  ([Chapter 6](./06-monte-carlo-simulation.mdx)). This is the model's primary result and the system response curve used in the risk analysis.
- **Grass removal curve** — the probability that the overtopping flow removes the grass cover at each depth: the proportion of realizations in which
  the cumulative excess overflow volume exceeded the erosional limit volume ([Chapter 5](./05-erosion-model.mdx)).
- **Conditional breach curve** — the probability of breach given that the grass cover was removed: the number of realizations with breach divided by
  the number with grass removal. Where no realization removes the grass cover, the conditional probability is undefined; the model reports it as zero by convention.

## Reading the System Response Curve

The system response curve maps each overtopping depth to a breach probability between 0 and 1. At zero overtopping depth there is no flow and the
probability is zero. The curve normally rises with depth, because a larger depth produces both a larger peak discharge and a longer overtopping duration
while each realization keeps the same material properties at every depth ([Chapter 6](./06-monte-carlo-simulation.mdx)).

Where the curve rises depends on the balance between the protection, the erosion resistance and the exposure:

- **Grass cover** — breach cannot occur until the grass cover is removed, so a better grass cover condition, with a higher critical velocity and
  erosional limit, moves the rise of the curve to larger depths. With No Grass Cover, erosion starts at the onset of overtopping.
- **Material erodibility** — once the soil is exposed, a higher erodibility coefficient and a lower critical shear stress produce faster erosion, so
  more erodible classifications tend to reach breach at smaller depths.
- **Roughness** — a higher Manning's roughness coefficient raises the flow depth on the slope and with it the applied shear stress. The
  classifications differ in their default roughness ranges as well as in their erodibility ([Chapter 3](./03-input-parameters.mdx)).
- **Overtopping duration** — a longer base duration lengthens the exposure at every depth, which raises, or leaves unchanged, the breach probability at each depth.
- **Crest width** — a wider crest raises the critical erosion depth, so more erosion is needed for breach; where the minimum critical erosion depth
  governs, the crest width has no effect.
- **Downstream slope** — a steeper slope raises the applied shear stress for a given discharge and lowers the grass cover thresholds, which increases
  erosion; it also raises the critical erosion depth, which resists breach. Which effect controls depends on the other inputs.
- **Correlation of the erodibility coefficient and the critical shear stress** — a stronger negative correlation makes combinations of high
  erodibility coefficient with low critical shear stress, and the reverse, more frequent, which widens the spread of erosion behavior among
  realizations.

## Decomposing the Result

Because breach can occur only after the grass cover has been removed, the system response curve is the product of the other two curves at every
depth where the grass cover is removed in at least one realization:

<EquationNoRef equation="P(\text{breach} \mid H) = P(\text{grass removed} \mid H) \cdot P(\text{breach} \mid \text{grass removed}, H)" />

The two factors separate the protection the grass cover provides from the erosion resistance of the embankment soil:

- A high grass removal probability with a low conditional breach probability means the exposed soil resists erosion to breach even after the grass
  cover fails.
- A low grass removal probability with a high conditional breach probability means the grass cover is the main protection against breach at that
  depth.
- With No Grass Cover, the grass removal probability is 1 at every depth and the conditional breach curve equals the system response curve.

## Checking the Result

Before using a screening curve, the engineer should check:

- **The scaled durations** — that the durations produced from the base duration ([Chapter 4](./04-hydrograph-generation.mdx)) are plausible for the reservoir across the depth range
  (Assumption 4).
- **The material representation** — that the selected erodibility classification, or the supplied distributions, represent the downstream face
  material, and that the embankment is close enough to homogeneous for Assumption 1.
- **The protection and internal features** — that the face has no riprap or other armoring and the embankment no non-erodible elements that the model
  would ignore (Assumptions 9 and 10).
- **The shape of the curve** — that the curve rises from zero and that the grass removal and conditional breach curves explain where it rises.

The curve values are point estimates computed from a finite number of realizations; the model does not report confidence bounds on them.

## Screening Versus Detailed Analysis

The screening system response curve is an estimate intended to identify structures that warrant further analysis. The screening results have been
compared with Quantitative Risk Assessment (QRA) response functions from USACE dam safety studies of Benbrook, Cherry Creek, Delaware, Grapevine,
J. Percy Priest, Keystone, Kinzua, Lookout Point, Mojave, Painted Rock, Stillhouse Hollow, W. Kerr Scott and Wister Dams (source pending, IN-17 in
[Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)).

A high screening breach probability does not by itself indicate a high risk of failure; it indicates that the structure should be evaluated with
detailed overtopping erosion analysis software, in which:

- Site-specific hydrographs from hydrologic routing replace the gamma-shaped approximation.
- Zoned embankment geometry is represented with distinct material zones and internal features.
- Zone-specific erosion parameters calibrated from testing are applied to each material zone.
- Headcut erosion is modeled explicitly.
- Riprap and other armoring are accounted for.

A low screening breach probability across the credible range of overtopping depths indicates low vulnerability to overtopping erosion, within the
assumptions of [Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx). It is not by itself a finding of low risk: the system response curve is combined with the frequency of overtopping loading
in the risk analysis.

# Hydrograph Generation

The model generates a synthetic overtopping hydrograph for each overtopping depth $H$ on the system response curve. The hydrograph is the time history
of unit discharge (discharge per unit crest length) flowing over the crest and down the downstream face. Its peak discharge and its duration both
depend on the overtopping depth; its shape is the same at every depth. Because the hydrograph depends only on the overtopping depth and the hydrograph
inputs, the same hydrograph is used for every Monte Carlo realization at that depth.

## Peak Discharge

The peak unit discharge is computed from the broad-crested weir equation<Citation citationKey="Fell2015" />:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-peak-discharge" equation="q_p = C_d \cdot H^{3/2}" />

where:

> $q_p$ = peak unit discharge (ft²/s or m²/s)  
> $C_d$ = broad-crested weir coefficient ([Chapter 3](./03-input-parameters.mdx))  
> $H$ = overtopping depth (ft or m)

## Duration Scaling

The user specifies the base duration $t_0$, the duration of overtopping at a reference overtopping depth. The duration at any other depth is scaled
from it with a power law:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-duration" equation="t_H = t_0 \cdot \left(\frac{H}{H_{\text{ref}}}\right)^{1/1.8}" />

where:

> $t_H$ = overtopping duration at depth $H$ (hours)  
> $t_0$ = base duration at the reference depth (hours)  
> $H_{\text{ref}}$ = reference depth, 0.5 ft (US Customary) or 0.15 m (SI)  
> $1/1.8 \approx 0.556$ = scaling exponent

The relationship makes larger overtopping depths last longer, with sub-linear growth: doubling the overtopping depth multiplies the duration by
$2^{1/1.8} \approx 1.47$. <TableReference tableKey="overtopping-screening-table-duration-example" /> shows the scaled durations for a base duration
of 4.0 hours.

:::caution
The reference depth and the scaling exponent are fixed. Reservoirs differ widely in how overtopping duration grows with depth, so the scaled
durations may be too short or too long for a specific reservoir. The user should check that the durations the scaling produces across the depth range
are reasonable for the reservoir, and adjust the base duration if they are not (Assumption 4 in [Chapter 7](./07-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)).
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-10 — Engineering Basis]
The duration scaling law (<EquationReference equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-duration" />) uses a fixed reference depth of 0.5 ft (0.15 m) and a fixed exponent of 1/1.8. The previous edition described
the exponent only as "a reasonable approximation for typical reservoirs," with no source. Provide the literature source (with full bibliographic details)

relative to inflow tend to have long overtopping events that lengthen slowly with depth, and reservoirs with small storage short, sharply peaked
events; provide a source or attribution if that guidance is to be printed.
:::

## Gamma Hydrograph Shape

The unit discharge at time $t$ after the onset of overtopping follows a gamma-shaped function:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-hydrograph" equation="q(t) = \frac{q_p}{C_a} \cdot \left(\frac{t}{k}\right)^{a-1} \cdot e^{-t/k}" />

where:

> $q(t)$ = unit discharge at time $t$ (ft²/s or m²/s)  
> $q_p$ = peak unit discharge from <EquationReference equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-peak-discharge" />  
> $a$ = hydrograph shape parameter ([Chapter 3](./03-input-parameters.mdx); default 3.5), dimensionless; not to be confused with the erosion exponent $\alpha$ of [Chapter 5](./05-erosion-model.mdx)  
> $C_a$ = normalizing constant  
> $k$ = hydrograph time-scale parameter (hours); not to be confused with the erodibility coefficient $k_d$ or the Manning's equation coefficient $k_n$ of [Chapter 5](./05-erosion-model.mdx)

The normalizing constant makes the peak of the hydrograph equal $q_p$:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-const-a" equation="C_a = e^{-(a-1)} \cdot (a-1)^{a-1}" />

The time-scale parameter ties the hydrograph to the scaled duration:

<Equation equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-k-shape" equation="k = \frac{C_a}{2 \cdot \Gamma(a)} \cdot t_H" />

where $\Gamma(a)$ is the gamma function evaluated at $a$ and $t_H$ is the scaled duration from
<EquationReference equationKey="overtopping-screening-eq-duration" />. With this time scale, the volume under the complete gamma curve equals
$q_p t_H / 2$, the volume of a triangular hydrograph with peak $q_p$ and base $t_H$.

The resulting hydrograph has the following properties:

- **Zero initial discharge** — discharge is zero at the onset of overtopping ($t = 0$) and rises to the peak.
- **Single peak** — the peak occurs at $t_{peak} = (a - 1) \cdot k$, where the discharge equals $q_p$. With the default shape parameter, the peak
  occurs at about 31 percent of the scaled duration.
- **Asymmetric shape** — the rising limb is steeper than the recession limb.
- **Same shape at every depth** — the peak discharge and the duration change with overtopping depth, but the shape, set by $a$, does not.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-11 — Engineering Basis]
The gamma hydrograph form, the default shape parameter $a$ = 3.5, and the time scale that gives the curve the volume of a triangular hydrograph of base
$t_H$ (the factor of 2 in the time-scale equation) have no stated engineering basis. The previous edition attributed the asymmetric shape to the behavior
of flood hydrographs in dam reservoirs, without a source. Provide the literature source (with full bibliographic details) or the engineering-judgment
attribution for the hydrograph form, the default shape parameter and the time scale.
:::

## Hydrograph Duration in the Simulation

The model evaluates the hydrograph at a fixed time step from the onset of overtopping through the scaled duration $t_H$; overtopping flow after $t_H$
is not simulated. The gamma curve has not returned to zero at $t_H$: with the default shape parameter, the discharge at $t_H$ is about 7 percent of
the peak, and the volume under the curve after $t_H$, about 2 percent of the total, is not applied to the embankment (Assumption 6 in Chapter 7).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-12 — Engineering Basis]
The simulation ends each hydrograph at the scaled duration $t_H$, when the discharge is still about 7 percent of the peak with the default shape
parameter. The previous edition described the recession as continuing asymptotically and stated that the tail has negligible erosive potential; that
description does not match the model, which omits the tail. Provide the engineering basis or attribution for ending the hydrograph at $t_H$, and
confirm the statement of its effect on results.
:::

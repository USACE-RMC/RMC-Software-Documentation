# Flaw Assessment

Node 1 of the event tree is the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous transverse frost crack exists across the crest. The flaw assessment also
produces the one flaw quantity the downstream nodes use — the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$, below which water cannot enter the crack — and a
crack width reported for reference. The assessment follows the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />,
in the form shared by the CLE screening family and documented in
[Flaw Conventions](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring).
It proceeds in five steps:

1. Score the three site factors — climate, crest soil, and surface cover — each with a likelihood factor.
2. Form the weighted sum of the likelihood factors.
3. Convert the weighted sum to a raw flaw probability, applying the lower threshold.
4. Apply the observation override to obtain $P_{flaw}$.
5. Take the crack depth from the climate and the bottom of the crack from the crest elevation less that depth; take the crack width from the
   weighted sum.

A preliminary check precedes the scoring: when every listed crest soil is one that cannot frost-crack, the mechanism does not apply and the
assessment ends there (see [Crest Soils That Cannot Frost-Crack](#crest-soils-that-cannot-frost-crack)).

## Factor Scores

Each factor $j$ carries a risk factor $RF_j$, its weight, and a likelihood factor $LF_j$, its score for the site on a scale on which 1 is the least
likely condition for a frost crack and 4 the most likely (<TableReference tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-4" />). The likelihood-factor
tables follow Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />.

None of the three factors is an incompatible-condition factor in the sense of the common scoring scheme: a likelihood factor of 1 does not by
itself force the flaw probability to zero. Only the climate can score 1, and an above-freezing climate is instead limited by its shallow crack (see
[Crack Depth and Bottom of the Crack](#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-the-crack)).

When several classes are listed, the governing likelihood factor is the highest among the listed classes that can frost-crack:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-1"
  equation="LF_{core} = \max_{s \in \mathcal{S}^{*}} LF_{core}(s)"
/>

where $\mathcal{S}^{*}$ is the set of listed crest soil classes, excluding the clean and nearly clean sands and gravels described below. The class
that attains the maximum is reported as the governing crest soil; when two classes tie, the first listed is reported. Taking the most
crack-susceptible soil is a screening design decision (engineering judgment): the maximum is the conservative choice, consistent with the
continuation method's use of the largest probability across the base soils a filter protects. When no class is listed, the crest soil is Unknown and $LF_{core} = 3$.

the core-material table of Fell et al. (2024) or a screening choice, and if the latter, provide the engineering-judgment attribution. Note that
the value is below the 4 of GC and MH and the 3.5 of CL-ML, so an Unknown crest scores lower than a crest known to be of any of those classes.

Proposed (unconfirmed): an intermediate value representing the most common fine-grained crest soils (SM, GM, ML, SC, CL all score 3). Confirm or
replace.
:::

## Weighted Sum

The three scores combine into the weighted sum of
[Flaw Conventions](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#weighted-sum):

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-2"
  equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_{clim} + 2\,LF_{core} + 1\,LF_{surf}"
/>

The weighted sum is always a whole number, because the one fractional score (3.5, for CL-ML) carries a risk factor of 2. It ranges from 9 — an
above-freezing climate, a fat clay (CH) crest, and a paved surface — to 24 — the longest freeze duration, a clayey gravel (GC) or high-plasticity
silt (MH) crest, and no road or pathway. Because the climate carries the largest weight, one step in the climate category moves the weighted sum by
3, one step in the crest soil by 2 (or 1 for the half step to or from CL-ML), and one step in the surface cover by 1.

## Raw Flaw Probability

The weighted sum is converted to the raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$ by the curve tabulated in
<TableReference tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-8" />. Between tabulated points, the logarithm of the probability is interpolated linearly in
the weighted sum, as described in
[Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
of the common methods manual. A weighted sum above 24 cannot occur, so the curve's upper end is the largest attainable value.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-8"
  alt="Raw flaw probability by weighted sum"
  caption="Raw flaw probability of a frost crack by weighted sum."
  colAlign={['center', 'center']}
  headers={[[{ value: 'Weighted Sum Σ' }, { value: 'Raw Flaw Probability P<sub>raw</sub>' }]]}
  columns={[
    ['Below 13', '13', '16', '21', '24'],
    ['0', '0.001', '0.01', '0.1', '0.9'],
  ]}
/>

The model applies a lower threshold of 13:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-3"
  equation="P_{raw}(\Sigma) = \begin{cases} 0 & \Sigma < 13 \\[4pt] \text{log-linear interpolation of the tabulated curve} & 13 \le \Sigma \le 24 \end{cases}"
/>

A weighted sum of exactly 13 takes the first tabulated value, 0.001; only a sum strictly below 13 gives zero. With a weighted sum of 14, for
example, the raw probability is one third of the way from 0.001 to 0.01 on a logarithmic scale, $10^{-3 + 1/3} \approx 2.15 \times 10^{-3}$.

The threshold can be reached only in the two mildest climates:

- **Above freezing** ($LF_{clim} = 1$) — the weighted sum reaches 13 only when $2\,LF_{core} + LF_{surf} \ge 10$: a GC or MH crest with any
  surface cover, a CL-ML crest with a gravel road or no road, or a crest scoring 3 (including Unknown) with no road or pathway. A CH crest is always
  below the threshold.
- **Temperate** ($LF_{clim} = 2$) — only a CH crest with a paved surface (a weighted sum of 12) is below the threshold.

In the two sub-arctic climates the weighted sum is at least 15, so the threshold never applies.

threshold of 13, below which the raw flaw probability is zero, require an engineering basis. Provide the table or figure of Fell et al. (2024) that
gives this relationship for frost cracking, or the engineering-judgment attribution, and state why a weighted sum below 13 is treated as
incompatible with a continuous frost crack rather than as a small probability.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the frost-cracking relationship between the weighted sum and the probability of cracking of Fell et al. (2024), with the

## Observation Override

When continuous frost cracking has been observed (see
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#continuous-frost-cracking-observed)), the flaw probability is 1.0. Otherwise it is the raw
probability, never less than a negligibly small value:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-4"
  equation="P_{flaw} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{continuous frost cracking observed} \\[4pt] \max\bigl(P_{raw}, \varepsilon\bigr) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}"
/>

where $\varepsilon$ is the negligibly small probability the CLE family uses in place of zero (see
[SRP Computation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation)). The override takes
precedence over the lower threshold: an observed continuous frost crack has a flaw probability of 1.0 even where the weighted sum is below 13. It
sets Node 1 only — the crack depth and every downstream node are evaluated as usual — and it applies only when the mechanism applies, as described
in [Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides).

## Crest Soils That Cannot Frost-Crack

The six clean and nearly clean sand and gravel classes — SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP, the classes with less than 12 percent fines — do not
frost-crack and are not scored. A listed class of this group is set aside, and the remaining listed classes govern by
<EquationReference equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-1" />. When every listed class belongs to this group, the mechanism does not apply, as
described in [Inapplicable Mechanism](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#inapplicable-mechanism):

- the flaw probability is the negligible value $\varepsilon$, and no weighted sum is formed;
- the observation override is not consulted, since observed cracking in such a crest cannot be frost cracking;
- no crack forms, so the crack depth and crack width are zero and the bottom of the crack is the crest elevation;
- initiation is zero at every pool level, and the SRP is negligible throughout.

The silty classes SM, GM, and ML are scored, although they can be non-plastic.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Engineering Basis]
The treatment of SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP as crest soils that cannot frost-crack — set aside when other classes are listed, and making the
mechanism inapplicable when they are the only classes listed — requires an engineering basis, including why the silty classes SM, GM, and ML, which
can also be non-plastic, are scored. Provide the literature source or the engineering-judgment attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): a cohesionless soil cannot sustain an open frost crack, so these classes are absent from the core-material table of
Fell et al. (2024). Confirm or replace.
:::

## Crack Depth and Bottom of the Crack

The crack depth $d_c$ depends on the climate category alone (<TableReference tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-5" />); it does not depend
on the crest soil, the surface cover, or the weighted sum. The bottom of the crack follows from
[Crest-crack models: bottom of the crack](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#crest-crack-models-bottom-of-the-crack):

<Equation equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-5" equation="z_{bc} = z_c - d_c" />

where $z_c$ is the crest elevation. Initiation is possible only at pool levels above $z_{bc}$ (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation)).
When the mechanism applies, the bottom of the crack is computed this way whatever the flaw probability, including when the override applies or the
weighted sum is below the threshold. When it does not apply, the crack depth is zero and the bottom of the crack is the crest elevation (see
[Crest Soils That Cannot Frost-Crack](#crest-soils-that-cannot-frost-crack)).

The shallow 1 ft crack of an above-freezing climate is what limits that climate's contribution: the flaw probability of an above-freezing site can
be non-negligible (up to $4.6 \times 10^{-3}$ from the scored factors, at a weighted sum of 15), but the SRP can rise from negligible only for
pools higher than 1 ft below the crest.

The tabulated depths are in feet. They are subtracted from the crest elevation as given, with no unit conversion.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-06 — Applicability]
The crack depths of <TableReference tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-5" /> are in feet and are subtracted from the crest elevation with no
conversion, so an analysis in which elevations are entered in meters would place the bottom of the crack 6, 4, 2, or 1 m below the crest — about
3.3 times too deep. State whether the model is to be used only with elevations in feet, or how a user working in SI units should proceed, so that
the limitation can be stated here. (The family-level question is marker IN-01 of the common methods manual.)
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-07 — Engineering Basis]
The crack depths by climate category (<TableReference tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-5" />: 6, 4, 2, and 1 ft), and their independence from
the crest soil and the surface cover, require an engineering basis. Provide the literature source (for example, a frost-penetration or
frost-crack-depth relationship) or the engineering-judgment attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the depths represent the depth of frost penetration into the crest for each freeze duration, over which frost cracks can
extend. Confirm or replace.
:::

### Crack width

The model also reports a crack width $w_c$, interpolated linearly (not logarithmically) in the weighted sum between the points of
<TableReference tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-9" />, following the crack-width relationship of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />.
A weighted sum at or below 12 gives zero width, and a sum of 24 gives the largest width, 20 mm. With a
weighted sum of 14, for example, the crack width is 3 mm.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-9"
  alt="Crack width by weighted sum"

  colAlign={['center', 'center']}

The crack width is reported for reference only. It does not enter the bottom of the crack, the initiation node, or the SRP, since initiation in an
open crest crack is taken as certain without a shear stress analysis. It can inform the crack opening assumed in a detailed crack-geometry
initiation analysis.

# Output and Interpretation

The model reports the SRP at each evaluated pool level together with the probability of each of the eight event-tree nodes, in the form shared by
the CLE crest-crack models and documented in
[Output and Interpretation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) of the common
methods manual, and the results of the frost-cracking flaw assessment.

## Results

- **Pool-independent results** — the flaw probability (Node 1), the probability of no crack filling (Node 5), the probability of no flow limitation
  with every listed element applying, and the roof-support minimum for each pathway that exists: the full-pathway value (absent when the filter
  caps the pathway) and the below-filter value (absent when no filter is present). See
  [Pool-Independent Results](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#pool-independent-results).
- **Pool-dependent series** — the pool levels, the tailwater at each (blank where there is none), and at each pool level the initiation,
  continuation, roof-support, flow-limitation, unsuccessful-intervention, and breach probabilities and the SRP. See
  [Pool-Dependent Series](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#pool-dependent-series).
- **Continuation result** — when a filter intersects the pathway, the result of the shared continuation method, including the probability of
  continuing erosion $P_{ce}$, the governing base soil, and the method that produced it (see
  [Output and Interpretation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) of the continuation
  manual). No continuation result is reported when no filter is present.

### Flaw-assessment results

- **Likelihood factors** — the climate, crest-soil, and surface-cover scores $LF_{clim}$, $LF_{core}$, and $LF_{surf}$.
- **Governing crest soil** — the listed class whose score governs $LF_{core}$; blank when the crest soil is Unknown or the mechanism does not apply.
- **Mechanism applies** — whether at least one listed crest soil can frost-crack, or no soil is listed. When the mechanism does not apply, the
  crest-soil score, the weighted sum, and the raw flaw probability are reported as zero.
- **Weighted sum** $\Sigma$.
- **Raw flaw probability** $P_{raw}$ — before the observation override and before the negligible floor; zero when the weighted sum is below 13.
- **Observation override applied** — whether continuous frost cracking was reported and set the flaw probability to 1.0.
- **Flaw probability** $P_{flaw}$ — the value used in the event tree.
- **Crack depth** $d_c$ in feet, **bottom of the crack** $z_{bc}$, and **crack width** $w_c$ in millimeters (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-the-crack)).

## Interpreting the System Response Curve

The curve has the shape of a crest-crack curve, described in
[Interpreting the System Response Curve](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#interpreting-the-system-response-curve):
negligible at and below the bottom of the crack, and piecewise constant above it, stepping only where the pool crosses the top of a filter or the
top of a wall that lies above the bottom of the crack. For frost cracking:

- **The climate sets where the curve rises.** When the mechanism applies, the bottom of the crack is 6, 4, 2, or 1 ft below the crest by climate
  category, so pools further below the crest contribute nothing whatever the other inputs. In an above-freezing climate, only pools higher than
  1 ft below the crest can give a non-negligible SRP.
- **The flaw probability scales the whole curve above the bottom of the crack.** With initiation certain above the bottom of the crack, the SRP at
  each pool level there is the flaw probability times the continuation and progression nodes that govern at that pool level and the intervention
  and breach probabilities. The weighted sum, and so the climate, crest soil, and surface cover, set the flaw probability unless an observation
  override sets it to 1.0.
- **A negligible curve at every pool level** results, for example, when the mechanism does not apply (every listed crest soil a clean sand or
  gravel), when the weighted sum is below 13 and no frost cracking has been observed, when a user-supplied intervention or breach probability is
  zero, or when no evaluated pool level rises above the bottom of the crack. The flaw-assessment results identify the first two cases; the inputs
  and the evaluated pool levels identify the others.
- **The crack width does not affect the curve.** The SRP depends on the weighted sum only through the flaw probability; the crack width derived
  from the weighted sum never enters the curve.

## Checking the Result

The model is deterministic apart from any Monte Carlo filter evaluation in the continuation method, so the curve can be reproduced from the reported
values: the flaw probability from the factor scores and the curve of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#raw-flaw-probability), the bottom of
the crack from the crest elevation and the climate's crack depth, and the SRP at each pool level from the product of the reported nodes. The
checks of [Checking the Result](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#checking-the-result)
apply. Confirm that the step in the curve sits at the crest elevation less the tabulated crack depth, on the same datum and in feet as the headwater. If
it does not, the crest and headwater elevations are on different datums or in different units (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#units)).
A metric analysis passes this check while still placing the crack too deep (see marker IN-06 in
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-the-crack)).

## Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis

The model is a screening tool. Its result indicates whether concentrated leak erosion through a frost crack at the crest warrants detailed analysis;
it is not a substitute for a detailed assessment. The screening simplifications — the three-factor scoring, the tabulated crack depths, the certain
initiation of an open crack, and the tabulated progression probabilities — are chosen to avoid false negatives. Where the result is significant to a
decision, the flaw probability, the crack depth, and the governing progression nodes should be re-examined with site-specific information, using the
methods of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, a crack-geometry initiation analysis, and the judgment of the risk team.

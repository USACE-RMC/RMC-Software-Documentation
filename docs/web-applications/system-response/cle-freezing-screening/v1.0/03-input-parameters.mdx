# Input Parameters

The model takes two groups of inputs. The **frost-cracking inputs** describe the flaw: the freeze-duration climate, the soils the crack would cross
at the crest, the surface cover over the core, and whether continuous frost cracking has been observed. The **shared inputs** — hydraulic loading,
the crest elevation and embankment type, the failure-pathway inputs, the upstream seepage-limiting elements, and the intervention and breach
probabilities — are common to the CLE screening family and are documented in
[Common Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) of the common methods
manual; this chapter states only how this model uses them. The model takes no critical shear stress, initial opening, seepage path length, or
embankment cross-section input, because its initiation node involves no shear stress analysis.

### Freeze-duration climate

The climate category describes the duration of below-freezing conditions at the site. It sets the climate likelihood factor, the most heavily
weighted of the three flaw factors, and the depth of the crack (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#climate)). The choices are:

- **Sub-arctic or alpine, below freezing three months or more** — the longest freeze duration; the most likely condition for a frost crack and the
  deepest crack.
- **Sub-arctic or alpine, below freezing one to three months**
- **Temperate, below freezing up to a month**
- **Above freezing, except possibly overnight for a couple of days** — the least likely condition and the shallowest crack.

The climate has no Unknown category; one of the four must be selected.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Definition]
The quantity against which "below freezing" and its duration are judged is not stated — for example the mean daily air temperature, the daily
minimum, or the ground temperature at the crest — nor how the duration is counted (continuous period or cumulative days in a typical or a severe
winter). State the criterion the user should apply to place a site in one of the four climate categories, citing the source of the categories in
Fell et al. (2024) if they are defined there.
:::

### Crest soil classifications

The crest soil classifications are the soil classes, in the Unified Soil Classification System (USCS), of every material other than drain or filter
material that the crack would cross at the crest. Each is one of the fifteen USCS classes listed in
[Failure Pathway and Progression](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-4-progression--roof-support)
of the common methods manual; other classes, such as organic soils, peat, or dual classes other than SP-SM, GP-GM, and CL-ML, are not accepted. They
set the core-material likelihood factor (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crest-soil)). This list is separate from the roof-support material lists of the failure pathway: it
describes where the crack forms, not the soils that must hold a roof over the enlarging pipe.

- **Several classes listed** — the class with the highest likelihood factor governs.
- **No classes listed** — the crest soils are taken as Unknown, which carries an intermediate likelihood factor.
- **Clean or nearly clean sands and gravels** — the classes SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP, with less than 12 percent fines, may be listed but
  are not scored. When every listed class is one of these, the mechanism does not apply: no crack forms and the SRP is negligible at every pool
  level (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crest-soils-that-cannot-frost-crack)).

### Surface cover over the core

The surface cover over the core at the crest sets the surface-cover likelihood factor, the least heavily weighted of the three flaw factors (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#surface-cover)). The choices are:

- **Paved** — a concrete or sealed roadway or pathway over the core; the least likely condition for a frost crack.
- **Gravel road**
- **No road or pathway** — the most likely condition for a frost crack.

### Continuous frost cracking observed

The observation is answered yes only when all three of the following hold: transverse cracking has been observed at the crest, the cracking is
judged to have been caused by freezing, and it is judged to be continuous across the crest. A yes sets the flaw probability to 1.0 whatever the
factors score (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#observation-override)). It does not change the crack depth, and it has no effect when
the mechanism does not apply. The default, no, leaves the flaw probability to the scored factors.

- **Hydraulic loading** — the headwater series and optional tailwater series of
  [Hydraulic Loading](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#hydraulic-loading). The headwater
  series needs at least two distinct values. Tailwater does not enter any node of this model; it is carried through to the results only.
- **Crest elevation** — the top of the embankment, from
  [Embankment Description](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description). The
  bottom of the crack is the crest elevation less the crack depth, and it must be on the same datum as the headwater series.
- **Embankment type** — homogeneous earthfill, zoned earthfill, or central-core earth and rockfill, from the same section. It sets Node 5, the
  probability of no crack filling.
- **Failure-pathway inputs** — the crest-crack form of
  [Failure-Pathway Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#failure-pathway-inputs): the
  top-of-filter elevation (blank when no filter intersects the pathway), the continuation inputs and the below-filter roof materials (both required
  when a filter is present and omitted otherwise), and the full-pathway roof materials (required unless the filter's top reaches the crest).
- **Upstream seepage-limiting elements** — any of the six element types of
  [Upstream Seepage-Limiting Elements](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#upstream-seepage-limiting-elements),
  with the top-of-wall elevation when a wall stops below the crest.
- **Intervention and breach** — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach of
  [Intervention and Breach](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#intervention-and-breach).

## Units

The crest, headwater, tailwater, filter, and wall elevations are entered on one common datum and in one length unit, as described in
[Units and Datum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#units-and-datum). The crack depths of
this model are tabulated in feet and are subtracted from the crest elevation without conversion (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-the-crack)). The crack width the model reports is always in millimeters.

# Model Overview

## Purpose

The CLE freezing screening model computes a system response curve for concentrated leak erosion through a frost crack at the crest of an
embankment, as a function of headwater elevation. The system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level is the conditional probability of
breach of the embankment by concentrated leak erosion through such a crack, given that pool level.

The flaw is a transverse crack — one running across the crest, from the upstream to the downstream side — opened in the crest soils by freezing. It
is open at the crest and extends down to a bottom-of-crack elevation set by the depth of cracking. When the pool rises above the bottom of the crack,
water can flow through it, erode its walls, and enlarge it into a concentrated leak.

The physical mechanism by which freezing opens a transverse crack at the crest is not described, and neither is the reason each of the model's
three site factors bears on it: why a longer freeze duration, a crest soil of a given classification, and the surface cover over the core
(paved, gravel road, or no road or pathway) make a continuous frost crack more or less likely. State the mechanism and the direction of each factor
so that it can be described here, with a literature source where one applies.

Proposed (unconfirmed): freezing of the crest soils causes contraction and ice segregation that open transverse cracks, which deepen and widen
with the duration of freezing; fine-grained soils are the most susceptible, and a surface cover that is kept clear of snow, such as a paved road,
changes the insulation of the crest and so the cracking. Confirm, correct, or replace — in particular the direction of the surface-cover factor,
under which a paved crest scores as the least likely condition.
:::

## Intended Use

The model is a screening-level assessment of the risk of concentrated leak erosion through a frost crack. It makes simplifying and generally
conservative assumptions with two goals:

- **Preventing false negatives** — The model aims to avoid underestimating risk at the screening level, so that embankments with a
  potential frost-cracking concern are flagged for further analysis. This is a goal rather than a guarantee: the score of an Unknown crest soil is
  not the most conservative choice, and a weighted sum below 13 gives a zero raw flaw probability (Assumptions 3 and 4 in
  [Assumptions and Limitations](./06-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#flaw-assessment)). Initiation is taken as certain once water can enter the crack, and the most
  crack-susceptible of the crest soils governs the flaw assessment.
- **Reducing input burden** — Three qualitative selections — a freeze-duration climate category, the soil classifications at the crest, and the
  surface cover over the core — substitute for site-specific measurements of frost penetration and crack geometry. The crack depth follows from
  the climate category alone.

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments. For higher-level analyses, the internal-erosion probability methods of
Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> and the crack-geometry initiation analyses of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation
toolbox provide the complete methodologies (see [Comparison with Detailed Models](#comparison-with-detailed-models)).

## How the Model Works

The model executes the three stages common to the CLE screening family (see the
[Model Overview](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#how-the-model-works) of the common
methods manual):

1. **Flaw assessment** — The model scores the freeze-duration climate, the most crack-susceptible soil the crack crosses at the crest, and the
   surface cover over the core with risk-factor and likelihood-factor weighting, and converts the weighted sum to the probability of a continuous
   frost crack. An observation of continuous frost cracking overrides the score to certainty. The climate category also sets the crack depth, and
   the bottom of the crack is the crest elevation less that depth. When every listed crest soil is one that cannot frost-crack, the mechanism does
   not apply and no crack forms (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).

2. **Pathway resolution** — The model takes the failure pathway as supplied: the top of the first filter the crack meets, if any, the filter and
   base-soil description, the soils that must hold a roof over the enlarging pipe below and above the filter's top, the embankment type, and the
   upstream seepage-limiting elements with the top of any wall (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#inputs-shared-with-the-cle-family)).

3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the model composes the eight CLE event-tree node probabilities and multiplies them to produce
   the SRP. Initiation is zero at or below the bottom of the crack and certain above it; continuation, roof support, and flow limitation are each
   evaluated for the pathway that governs at that pool level (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

Every node is deterministic except continuation, which the shared continuation method evaluates by Monte Carlo simulation when it assesses a
filter from the soil classifications of the base and filter soils.

## Physics Basis

The model combines the following engineering methods:

- **Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring** of the probability of a continuous flaw, in which each site factor receives a likelihood factor
  weighted by a risk factor and the weighted sum is mapped to a probability <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, as documented for the CLE family
  in [Flaw Conventions](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
- **A climate-dependent crack depth** that places the bottom of the frost crack below the crest (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-the-crack))
- **A headwater gate at the bottom of the open crack** for initiation, as documented for the crest-crack models in
  [Initiation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
- **Filter evaluation, roof-support, crack-filling, and flow-limitation probabilities** for continuation and progression, applied through the
  crest-crack pathway rules documented in
  [Failure Pathway and Progression](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression)

## Comparison with Detailed Models

In a detailed risk analysis, the probability of a frost crack, its depth and width, and the likelihood that erosion initiates in it are assessed by
the risk team from the site evidence, informed by the internal-erosion probability methods of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />.
The initiation of erosion in a crack of known geometry can be evaluated with the
[Vertical Rectangular Crack](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/vertical-rectangular-crack)
and [Vertical Triangular Crack](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/vertical-triangular-crack)
analyses of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox, which compare the hydraulic shear stress in the crack with the critical shear stress
of its walls. <TableReference tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-1" /> summarizes the differences.

## Relationship to the Shared Methods

The model is one of the four crest-crack models of the CLE screening family (see
[The Two Model Groups](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#the-two-model-groups) in the
common methods manual). Everything after the flaw assessment is common to those models: the event-tree structure, the headwater gate for
initiation, the crest-crack pathway rules for continuation and roof support, the crack-filling and flow-limitation tables, and the form of the
results. This manual documents the frost-cracking flaw assessment in full and, for every other node, states what this model supplies and links the
chapter of the common methods manual that documents the method. The continuation node hands the filter evaluation to the shared continuation method,
which combines its results across the base soils the filter protects as described in
[Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil).
The model uses no toolbox method.

The closest sibling is the desiccation screening model, which shares the structure of the flaw assessment — three factors, a weighted-sum
threshold of 13, an observation override, and a set of crest soils in which the mechanism cannot occur. It also scores the climate and the crest
soil, but on its own tables and with its factors weighted differently: its surface layer carries a risk factor of 3, its climate 2, and its crest
soil 1.

# Assumptions and Limitations

The CLE freezing screening model applies deliberate simplifications relative to a detailed internal-erosion risk analysis. They reduce input
requirements and are designed to avoid false negatives at the screening level. This chapter lists the assumptions specific to frost cracking. The
assumptions the model shares with the rest of the CLE family — the product form of the event tree, the single values for intervention and breach,
the headwater grid, the flaw probability being the same at every pool level, certain initiation above the bottom of a crest crack, the crest-crack
filter gate, and the roof-support, crack-filling, and flow-limitation conventions — are listed with their bases in
[Assumptions and Limitations](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) of the common
methods manual. Each assumption below states its engineering basis, its effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold. Where an
engineering basis is still awaiting confirmation, the assumption cross-references the input marker at its primary location in this document.

## Flaw Assessment

1. **The likelihood of a continuous frost crack is scored from three factors — climate, crest soil, and surface cover — weighted 3, 2, and 1.** No
   other site condition enters the flaw probability (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#factor-scores)).
   - *Engineering basis:* Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring and likelihood-factor tables of
     Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the physical basis of each factor is requested at marker IN-01 in
     [Model Overview](./02-model-overview.mdx#purpose).
   - *Effect on results:* The climate carries the most weight: one step in the climate category moves the weighted sum by 3, which moves the flaw
     probability by up to a factor of 10 within the tabulated range, or from zero to a non-negligible value where it crosses the threshold of 13.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where a site condition outside the three factors is known to control cracking at the crest; the risk team's estimate
     should then replace the screening value.
2. **The most crack-susceptible of the listed crest soils governs the crest-soil score.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): taking the highest likelihood factor is the conservative choice, consistent
     with the continuation method's use of the largest probability across base soils.
   - *Effect on results:* A crest that is mostly of a low-scoring soil, such as fat clay (CH), scores as its most susceptible listed soil. Once at
     least one scored soil is listed, adding a further soil can raise the flaw probability but never lower it.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the susceptible soil occupies a small, isolated part of the crest that a transverse crack would not cross.
3. **When no crest soil class is listed (Unknown), the crest-soil likelihood factor is 3.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-03 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crest-soil).
   - *Effect on results:* An unknown crest scores as SM, GM, ML, SC, or CL would, and below GC, MH, and CL-ML; it is not the most conservative
     choice.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the crest may be of clayey gravel (GC), high-plasticity silt (MH), or silty clay (CL-ML); listing the candidate
     classes then gives the conservative score.
4. **A weighted sum below 13 gives a raw flaw probability of zero, and the curve rises log-linearly from 0.001 at 13 to 0.9 at 24.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-04 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#raw-flaw-probability).
   - *Effect on results:* Above-freezing sites where $2\,LF_{core} + LF_{surf} \lt 10$, and temperate sites with a fat clay crest under a paved
     surface, have a negligible flaw probability and a negligible SRP unless frost cracking has been observed (the combinations are listed in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#raw-flaw-probability)).
   - *When it may not hold:* Where frost cracking has occurred at comparable sites in such conditions; the observation override, or a risk-team
     estimate, then applies.
5. **The clean and nearly clean sands and gravels — SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP — do not frost-crack.** They are set aside when other soils
   are listed, and the mechanism does not apply when only they are listed.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-05 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crest-soils-that-cannot-frost-crack).
   - *Effect on results:* A crest listed entirely in these classes gives a negligible SRP at every pool level, and an observation of continuous
     frost cracking is not consulted.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where part of a crest classified as a clean sand or gravel contains finer material; the class of that material should
     then be listed.
6. **An observed continuous frost crack has a flaw probability of 1.0, even where the weighted sum is below the threshold.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Engineering judgment shared by the CLE family: an observed continuous crack demonstrates the flaw (see
     [Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides)).
   - *Effect on results:* The SRP above the bottom of the crack is governed entirely by the continuation and progression nodes and the
     intervention and breach probabilities.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the observed cracking is not continuous across the crest or has another cause, such as desiccation or
     settlement; the override should be reserved for cracking judged to be frost cracking that is continuous.

## Crack Geometry

7. **The crack depth depends on the freeze-duration climate alone: 6, 4, 2, or 1 ft.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-07 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-the-crack).
   - *Effect on results:* The climate fixes the pool level at which the SRP rises from negligible. In an above-freezing climate only pools higher
     than 1 ft below the crest can initiate erosion, which limits that climate's contribution even where its flaw probability is non-negligible.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where site measurements or observations of frost penetration or crack depth are available; a detailed analysis then
     uses the site depth.
8. **The tabulated crack depths are in feet and are applied without unit conversion.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-06 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-the-crack).
   - *Effect on results:* With elevations in feet the bottom of the crack is placed as tabulated. With elevations in meters it would be placed
     about 3.3 times too deep, lowering the pool level at which the SRP rises (conservative, but not as intended).
   - *When it may not hold:* Whenever elevations are not entered in feet.
9. **The crack width does not enter the event tree.** The width is reported for reference only.
   - *Engineering basis:* Consequence of the certain initiation of an open crest crack, Assumption 9 in
     [Assumptions and Limitations: Initiation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations#initiation)
     of the common methods manual (see also
     [Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)).
   - *Effect on results:* The model gives no initiation credit for a narrow computed crack, which is conservative where the width would be small.
   - *When it may not hold:* For narrow cracks in erosion-resistant crest soils, where a crack-geometry initiation analysis could show that erosion
     does not initiate.
10. **Tailwater plays no part in the model.** It is carried through to the results only.
    - *Engineering basis:* The headwater gate of the crest-crack models depends only on whether the pool stands above the bottom of the crack (see
      [Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)).
    - *Effect on results:* A high tailwater does not reduce the SRP.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where tailwater stands high enough on the downstream face to reduce the flow through the crack materially; a
      detailed crack-geometry analysis accounts for it.

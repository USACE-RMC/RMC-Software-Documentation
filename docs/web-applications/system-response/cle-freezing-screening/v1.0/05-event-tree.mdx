# Event Tree

The model computes the SRP at each headwater level from the eight-node event tree shared by the CLE screening family, documented in
[Event Tree](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree) of the common methods manual. As a crest-crack
model, it evaluates initiation, continuation, roof support, and flow limitation at each pool level, and takes the flaw, crack-filling,
intervention, and breach probabilities as the same at every pool level (see
[Pool Dependence](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#pool-dependence)). The tree is evaluated at
every supplied headwater value and at intermediate levels between them, as described in
[Headwater Grid](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#headwater-grid).
<TableReference tableKey="cle-freezing-screening-table-10" /> summarizes how this model populates each node; the sections that follow give the
detail.

## Node 1: Flaw

*Question:* Does a continuous transverse frost crack exist across the crest?

$P_{flaw}$ is the result of the frost-cracking flaw assessment documented in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx): the scored probability
from the climate, the crest soil, and the surface cover, or 1.0 when continuous frost cracking has been observed. It is the same at every pool level.

## Node 2: Initiation

*Question:* Once water can enter the crack, does erosion of its walls begin?

Initiation is the headwater gate of the crest-crack models, documented in
[Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate),
applied at the bottom of the frost crack $z_{bc}$ from [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-the-crack):

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-6"
  equation="P_{init}(h) = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{the mechanism applies and } h > z_{bc} \\[4pt] 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}"
/>

where $h$ is the headwater elevation. Initiation is zero at a pool exactly at the bottom of the crack, and zero at every pool level when the
mechanism does not apply. No shear stress is computed and tailwater plays no part. Because the crack depth is set by the climate alone, the climate
category fixes the pool level at which the SRP can first rise: 6 ft below the crest for the longest freeze duration, and 1 ft below the crest for an
above-freezing climate.

## Node 3: Continuation

*Question:* Does an unfiltered seepage exit allow erosion to continue?

Continuation follows the crest-crack pathway rule of
[Node 3: Continuation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-3-continuation)
in the common methods manual:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-7"
  equation="P_{cont}(h) = \begin{cases} P_{ce} & \text{a filter is present and } \bigl(h \le z_{tf} \text{ or the filter caps the pathway}\bigr) \\[4pt] 1.0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}"
/>

where $z_{tf}$ is the top-of-filter elevation and $P_{ce}$ is the probability of continuing erosion past the filter. $P_{ce}$ is evaluated once,
from the filter and base-soil description, by the shared continuation method; with several base soils it is the largest of their pair
probabilities, as described in
[Governing Base Soil](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil#governing-base-soil)
of the continuation manual. The filter caps the pathway when its top reaches the crest and no full-pathway roof materials are given. With no filter
on the pathway, the continuation method is not evaluated and $P_{cont} = 1.0$ at every pool level.

## Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

*Question:* Does a continuous stable roof form over the enlarging pipe, so that it stays open?

Roof support is the minimum roof-support probability across the material set that governs at the pool level, per
[Node 4: Progression — Roof Support](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-4-progression--roof-support):

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-8"
  equation="P_{roof}(h) = \begin{cases} \min_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{bf}} P_{roof}(m) & \text{a filter is present and } \bigl(h \le z_{tf} \text{ or the filter caps the pathway}\bigr) \\[4pt] \min_{m \in \mathcal{M}_{fp}} P_{roof}(m) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}"
/>

where $\mathcal{M}_{bf}$ is the set of below-filter roof materials and $\mathcal{M}_{fp}$ the set of full-pathway roof materials. Roof support shares
its gate with continuation, so both nodes switch together where the pool crosses the top of the filter. The roof-material lists are separate from the
crest soil classifications of the flaw assessment.

## Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

*Question:* Does the enlarging pipe remain unfilled by material carried in from upstream?

$P_{nf}$ is taken from the embankment type per
[Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-5-progression--no-crack-filling).
It is the same at every pool level.

## Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

*Question:* Does the flow into the enlarging pipe remain unrestricted by upstream seepage-limiting elements?

$P_{nl}$ is the minimum probability of no flow limitation across the upstream elements that reach the pool level, or 1.0 when none is listed, per
[Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation).
When a top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ is supplied, every listed element applies at pool levels at or below $z_{tw}$; above it the walls drop out and
only an upstream facing element, if one is listed, still applies (otherwise $P_{nl} = 1.0$), as described in
[Walls That Stop Below the Crest](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#walls-that-stop-below-the-crest).

## Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

*Questions:* Do detection and intervention fail? Does an uncontrolled release of the impounded water occur?

$P_{int}$ and $P_{br}$ are the user-specified probabilities, each one value applied at every pool level and each defaulting to 1.0.

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each pool level is the product of the eight node probabilities:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-freezing-screening-eq-9"
  equation="\mathrm{SRP}(h) = P_{flaw} \times P_{init}(h) \times P_{cont}(h) \times P_{roof}(h) \times P_{nf} \times P_{nl}(h) \times P_{int} \times P_{br}"
/>

Where the product is zero — at and below the bottom of the crack, or where a user-supplied intervention or breach probability is zero — the SRP is
reported as the negligibly small value the CLE family uses in place of zero (see
[SRP Computation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation)). Above the bottom of the
crack, initiation is 1.0 and the SRP is the product of the remaining nodes at that pool level.

no filter intersects the pathway, the full-pathway roof material is the same silty sand, and a modern cutoff wall reaching the crest is the only
upstream element. Intervention and breach take their defaults.

The SRP is negligible at pool levels up to and including 263 ft and $1.831 \times 10^{-6}$ at every pool level above it, a single step at the
bottom of the crack. The cutoff wall governs the result there, reducing it by three orders of magnitude; were the wall's top below the crest but above
the bottom of the crack, the curve would step up by a factor of 1,000 where the pool rises above the top of the wall.

# Event Tree

The model computes the system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level from the eight-node event tree shared by the concentrated leak
erosion (CLE) screening models, documented in the
[Event Tree](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree)
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual. As a crest-crack model, it evaluates initiation, continuation, roof support, and flow limitation at each
pool level, and takes the flaw, crack-filling, intervention, and breach probabilities as the same at every pool level (see
[Pool Dependence](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#pool-dependence)).
The tree is evaluated at every supplied headwater value and at intermediate levels between them, as described in
[Headwater Grid](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#headwater-grid).

## Node 1: Flaw

*Question:* Does a continuous transverse frost crack exist across the crest?

$P_{flaw}$ is the result of the frost-cracking flaw assessment documented in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx): the scored probability
from the climate, the crest soil, and the surface cover, or 1.0 when continuous frost cracking has been observed. It is the same at every pool
level.

## Node 2: Initiation

*Question:* Is the hydraulic shear stress on the walls of the crack large enough to initiate concentrated leak erosion?

The model applies the headwater gate of the crest-crack models (Equation 8 in the
[Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
section of the CLE Common Methods manual) at the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$ of
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#bottom-of-crack-elevation): initiation is 1.0 at pool levels above $z_{bc}$ and zero at or below it.
When the mechanism does not apply, initiation is zero at every pool level, as that section states. No shear stress is computed and tailwater
plays no part. Because the crack depth is set by the climate alone, the climate category fixes the pool level at which the SRP can first rise: 6
ft below the crest for the longest freeze duration, and 1 ft below the crest for an above-freezing climate.

## Node 3: Continuation

*Question:* Does an unfiltered seepage exit exist that allows erosion to continue?

Continuation follows the crest-crack pathway convention (Equation 14 in the
[Node 3: Continuation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-3-continuation)
section of the CLE Common Methods manual). Where a filter is present, the node takes the probability of continuing erosion $P_{ce}$, the node
result of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening model (written $P_{cont}$ in that manual; see its
[Node Result](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#node-result)
section), at pool levels at or below the top-of-filter elevation $z_{tf}$, and at every pool level when the filter caps the pathway (its top
reaches the crest and no full-pathway roof materials are given); above $z_{tf}$ it is 1.0. $P_{ce}$ is evaluated once, from the filter and
base-soil description; with several base soils it is the largest of their pair probabilities, as described in
[Governing Base Soil](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil#governing-base-soil)
in the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual. With no filter on the pathway, the continuation method is not evaluated and
$P_{cont} = 1.0$ at every pool level.

## Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

*Question:* Does a continuous stable roof form over the enlarging pipe, so that it stays open?

Roof support is the minimum roof-support probability across the material set that governs at the pool level (Equation 15 in the
[Node 4: Progression — Roof Support](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-4-progression--roof-support)
section of the CLE Common Methods manual): the below-filter roof materials at pool levels at or below $z_{tf}$, and at every pool level when the
filter caps the pathway; the full-pathway roof materials above $z_{tf}$, and at every pool level when no filter is present. Roof support shares
its gate with continuation, so both nodes switch together where the pool crosses the top of the filter. The roof-material lists are separate from
the crest soil classifications of the flaw assessment.

## Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

*Question:* Does the enlarging pipe remain unfilled, rather than being filled and healed by material carried in from upstream?

$P_{nf}$ is taken from the embankment type per
[Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-5-progression--no-crack-filling)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. It is the same at every pool level.

## Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

*Question:* Does the flow into the enlarging pipe remain unrestricted by upstream seepage-limiting elements?

$P_{nl}$ is the probability of no flow limitation of the upstream facing element times that of the governing wall (the most effective of the walls
listed), counting only the elements that reach the pool level; an element that is absent or does not reach it contributes 1.0, so $P_{nl}$ is 1.0
when none is listed, per
[Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. When a top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ is supplied, the walls apply at pool levels at or below $z_{tw}$; above
it the walls drop out and only an upstream facing element, if one is listed, still applies (otherwise $P_{nl} = 1.0$), as described in
[Walls That Stop Below the Crest](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#walls-that-stop-below-the-crest).

## Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

*Questions:* Do detection and intervention fail (Node 7)? Does an uncontrolled release of impounded water occur (Node 8)?

Both nodes are the shared intervention and breach inputs, documented with their defaults in the
[Nodes 7 and 8](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#nodes-7-and-8-unsuccessful-intervention-and-breach)
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. The model applies them without change.

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each headwater (pool) elevation $h$ is the product of the eight node probabilities, by Equation 1 in the
[SRP Computation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation)
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. In this model $P_{init}$, $P_{cont}$, $P_{roof}$, and $P_{nl}$ vary with $h$, and the other nodes are
the same at every pool level.

Where the product is zero — at and below the bottom of the crack, or where an intervention or breach probability is zero — the SRP is reported
as the negligibly small value the CLE family uses in place of zero. Above the bottom of the crack, initiation is 1.0 and the SRP is the product of
the remaining nodes at that pool level.

The SRP is negligible at pool levels up to and including 263 ft and $1.831 \times 10^{-6}$ at every pool level above it, a single step at the
bottom of the crack. The cutoff wall governs the result there, reducing it by three orders of magnitude; were the wall's top below the crest but
above the bottom of the crack, the curve would step up by a factor of 1,000 where the pool rises above the top of the wall. Had an upstream facing
element, with a probability of no flow limitation of 0.5, also been present, it would multiply the wall's value:
$P_{nl} = 0.5 \times 0.001 = 0.0005$ and an SRP of $9.16 \times 10^{-7}$ above the bottom of the crack, and 0.5 alone above the top of a wall that stops below the crest.

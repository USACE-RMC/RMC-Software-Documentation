# Flaw Assessment

Node 1 of the event tree is the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous transverse crack exists through the embankment. The flaw assessment also
supplies the one flaw quantity the downstream nodes use, the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$
(see [Crest-Crack Models: Bottom of the Crack](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#crest-crack-models-bottom-of-the-crack)
in the common methods manual). Unlike the other CLE screening models, the assessment does not score qualitative site factors. It is a chain of
relationships driven by the settlement and the abutment geometry:

1. The crest settlement is normalized by the dam height.
2. The normalized settlement and the abutment slope, with any abutment step, give the localized tensile strain at the crest.
3. The strain gives the flaw probability and the crack width.
4. The crack width gives the crack depth, and the crack depth gives the bottom-of-crack elevation.

Every step is deterministic, and every quantity is the same at every pool level.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-04 — Engineering Basis]
The flaw assessment treats the settlement (or the year-of-construction default), the dam height, the abutment slope, and the step height as single
values, so the flaw probability, crack width, and crack depth carry no uncertainty and the bottom of the crack is one elevation. State the
engineering basis for this deterministic treatment, or provide the engineering-judgment attribution to state here.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the inputs are geometric and survey quantities entered as best estimates at the screening stage, and the analyst brackets
their uncertainty by repeating the analysis. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Normalized Settlement

When the maximum post-construction settlement $s$ is supplied, the normalized settlement, in percent of the dam height, is

<Equation equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-1" equation="\bar{s} = 100\,\frac{s}{H}" />

where $H$ is the dam height, in the same length unit as $s$. When no settlement is supplied, the normalized settlement is assigned from the year
of construction $Y$:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-2"
  equation="\bar{s} = \begin{cases} 0.5 & Y < 1950 \\[4pt] 0.25 & Y \ge 1950 \end{cases}"
/>

A dam completed in 1950 takes the smaller default. A supplied settlement always takes precedence over the year of construction.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Engineering Basis]
The default normalized settlement assigned from the year of construction
(<EquationReference equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-2" />: 0.5 percent of the dam height for a dam completed before 1950,
0.25 percent for a dam completed in 1950 or later) requires an engineering basis. Provide the literature source for the two values and for the
1950 boundary, or the engineering-judgment attribution to state here.

Proposed (unconfirmed): dams built before about 1950 were generally compacted less thoroughly than later dams and settled more after
construction, so the older dams take the larger default. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Localized Tensile Strain

The localized tensile strain $e_L$, in percent, is the measure of crest stretching from which the flaw probability and the crack width are read.
It is interpolated from tables of strain against the normalized settlement $\bar{s}$ and the abutment slope $\theta$. Which tables apply depends on
whether the abutment has a step.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-06 — Definition]
State the physical meaning of the localized tensile strain $e_L$: where along the crest and at what depth it applies (for example, the
horizontal tensile strain along the dam axis in the crest region over the abutment), and over what gauge length it is "localized." The tables
below are stated in percent, and the flaw probability and crack width relationships are stated in the same strain.
:::

### Abutment without a step

When no step height is entered, the strain is interpolated bilinearly from <TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-5" /> and
<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-6" />, which tabulate it at eight normalized settlements and seven abutment slopes.
With $\bar{s}_i \le \bar{s} \le \bar{s}_{i+1}$ and $\theta_j \le \theta \le \theta_{j+1}$ on the tabulated axes,

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-3"
  equation="e_L = (1-t)(1-u)\,e_{i,j} + t\,(1-u)\,e_{i+1,j} + t\,u\,e_{i+1,j+1} + (1-t)\,u\,e_{i,j+1}, \qquad t = \frac{\bar{s} - \bar{s}_i}{\bar{s}_{i+1} - \bar{s}_i}, \quad u = \frac{\theta - \theta_j}{\theta_{j+1} - \theta_j}"
/>

35 degrees only to keep them readable; they form one relationship. Like the step tables that follow, they list the normalized settlement by row

### Abutment with a step

When a step height $h_1$ is entered, the strain depends also on the relative step height $r = h_1/H$. Three tables give the strain at relative
step heights of 0.6, 0.8, and 0.9 (<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-7" /> to
<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-9" />), each at six normalized settlements and four abutment slopes. The calculation has
two stages:

1. Each of the three tables is interpolated bilinearly at $(\bar{s}, \theta)$ as in
   <EquationReference equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-3" />, giving the strains $e_{0.6}$, $e_{0.8}$, and $e_{0.9}$.
2. The strain at the dam's relative step height is interpolated linearly through those three values and a fourth point fixed at zero strain for a
   relative step height of 0.4:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-4"
  equation="e_L = e_k + \frac{r - r_k}{r_{k+1} - r_k}\,\bigl(e_{k+1} - e_k\bigr), \qquad (r_k, e_k) \in \{(0.4,\ 0),\ (0.6,\ e_{0.6}),\ (0.8,\ e_{0.8}),\ (0.9,\ e_{0.9})\}"
/>

A relative step height of 0.4 or less takes zero strain, and one of 0.9 or more takes $e_{0.9}$.

"no added strain" rather than "no strain." Confirm or replace.

## Flaw Probability

The strain, converted from percent to a decimal strain $\varepsilon_L = e_L / 100$, gives the probability of a continuous transverse crack from
the relationship of <TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-10" />. That probability, $P_{raw}$, is
the flaw probability from the strain before any observation override (see [Observation Override](#observation-override)). Between two
tabulated points $(\varepsilon_k, P_k)$ the logarithm of the probability is interpolated linearly in the strain:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-5"
  equation="\log_{10} P_{raw} = \log_{10} P_k + \frac{\varepsilon_L - \varepsilon_k}{\varepsilon_{k+1} - \varepsilon_k}\,\bigl(\log_{10} P_{k+1} - \log_{10} P_k\bigr), \qquad \varepsilon_k \le \varepsilon_L \le \varepsilon_{k+1}"
/>

A strain below the first tabulated point takes $5 \times 10^{-5}$, and one at or above the last takes 1.0.

The source relationship gives one set of probabilities for a pool at or below the period of record (the highest pool of record) and another for
a pool above it. The model adopts the set for a pool above the period of record as the conservative choice and applies it at every pool level
(see marker IN-11 below).

The last point, at a strain of 0.0443, extends the relationship to a probability of 1.0 beyond its last value of 0.1 at a strain of 0.03. The
strain tables reach at most 3.29 percent without a step and 3.5 percent with one, so the flaw probability from the relationship is at most about
0.16 without a step and about 0.22 with one; a probability of 1.0 arises only from the observation override.

(1) the tabulated probabilities from a strain of 0.0005 to 0.03, (2) the added point at a strain of 0.0443 with a probability of 1.0, which
extends the relationship beyond its last value of 0.1, (3) the interpolation of the logarithm of probability linearly in strain
(<EquationReference equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-5" />), and (4) the floor of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ held for every smaller strain,
including zero strain. Provide the source (author, year, title, publisher or report number, and the figure or table) or the engineering-judgment
attribution for each.
:::

Proposed (unconfirmed): a conservative screening choice, since a crack that existed would likely have revealed itself at pools already

### Observation override

When continuous transverse cracking has been observed at the crest, with a cause attributable to settlement or unknown, the flaw probability is set
to 1.0:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-6"
  equation="P_{flaw} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{continuous transverse cracking observed} \\[4pt] P_{raw} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}"
/>

The override changes the flaw probability only. The crack width, crack depth, and bottom-of-crack elevation are still computed from the strain, so
the pool level at which the observed crack begins to contribute is set by the settlement and abutment inputs, not by the observed crack. This
follows the common convention for observation overrides (see
[Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) in the
common methods manual).

## Crack Width

The decimal strain also gives the crack width $W$, in millimeters, interpolated linearly from
<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-11" />:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-7"
  equation="W = W_k + \frac{\varepsilon_L - \varepsilon_k}{\varepsilon_{k+1} - \varepsilon_k}\,\bigl(W_{k+1} - W_k\bigr), \qquad \varepsilon_k \le \varepsilon_L \le \varepsilon_{k+1}"
/>

A strain at or below 0.0005 gives a negligible width, and a strain at or above 0.025 gives 75 mm. The crack width is in millimeters whatever the
length unit of the analysis.

## Crack Depth and Bottom-of-Crack Elevation

The crack depth $d_c$ follows from the crack width by an empirical relationship attributed to Pierson (2023), fitted with the width in millimeters
and the depth in meters:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-8"
  equation="d_{c,m} = \frac{W}{1000}\,\bigl(250 - 1.33\,W\bigr), \qquad d_c = \begin{cases} d_{c,m} & \text{analysis in meters} \\[4pt] d_{c,m} / 0.3048 & \text{analysis in feet} \end{cases}"
/>

where $W$ is the crack width in millimeters and $d_{c,m}$ the crack depth in meters. Over the range of widths the model produces, 0 to 75 mm, the
depth increases with the width, from negligible to about 11.3 m (37.0 ft) at 75 mm. The bottom of the crack is then

<Equation equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-9" equation="z_{bc} = z_c - d_c" />

as in the common convention for the crest-crack models (see
[Crest-Crack Models: Bottom of the Crack](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#crest-crack-models-bottom-of-the-crack)).
A negligible crack width gives a bottom of the crack at the crest, so the pool must rise above the crest before initiation can occur.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-13 — Bibliography]
The crack-depth relationship (<EquationReference equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-8" />) is attributed to Pierson (2023), whose
bibliographic details are not recorded. Provide the full reference — authors, year, title, journal or publisher, and report number — so that it
can be added to the bibliography and cited here, and confirm that the published form gives the depth in feet as
$d_c = (W/1000)(250 - 1.33\,W)/0.3048$ with the width in millimeters.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-14 — Applicability]
The crack depth is not limited by the height of the embankment. Up to about 37 ft (11.3 m) of crack depth is possible, so for a low dam the
bottom-of-crack elevation can fall below the foundation contact, and initiation then begins at the lowest pool evaluated. State whether the crack
depth should be limited to the dam height (or to another bound) or whether the model is not intended for dams lower than the largest crack depth,
and whether the relationship applies to the full range of widths the model produces.
:::

## Range of the Relationships

Each relationship is tabulated over a limited range, and an input beyond the range takes the value at the nearer end:

- **Abutment slope** — tabulated from 15 to 60 degrees. A flatter slope takes the strain at 15 degrees; a steeper one takes the strain at 60
  degrees.
- **Normalized settlement** — tabulated from 0 to 2.5 percent without a step and from 0 to 2 percent with one. A larger settlement takes the strain
  at the end of the table.
- **Relative step height** — interpolated from 0.4 to 0.9; see [Abutment with a Step](#abutment-with-a-step).
- **Strain** — the flaw probability is held at $5 \times 10^{-5}$ below a strain of 0.0005 and at 1.0 above 0.0443; the crack width is held
  negligible below 0.0005 and at 75 mm above 0.025.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-15 — Applicability]
State the range of abutment slopes, normalized settlements, and relative step heights over which the model is applicable. Beyond the tabulated
range the model holds the end value rather than extrapolating, so a slope steeper than 60 degrees or a normalized settlement above 2.5 percent
(2 percent with a step) is assessed as if it were at the end of the table, which may understate the strain. State whether such inputs are outside
the intended use of the model, or whether holding the end value is an accepted screening treatment.
:::

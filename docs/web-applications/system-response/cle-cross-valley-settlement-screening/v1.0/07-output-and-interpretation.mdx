# Output and Interpretation

The model reports the SRP at each evaluated pool level together with the probability of each of the eight event-tree nodes, in the form shared by
the CLE crest-crack models, and the results of its flaw assessment. The node results and the system response curve are described in the
[Output and Interpretation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) chapter of the
common methods manual; this chapter describes the flaw-assessment results and what is specific to reading a cross-valley settlement result.

## Node Results

The model reports the results of the crest-crack models (see
[Pool-Independent Results](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#pool-independent-results)
and
[Pool-Dependent Series](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#pool-dependent-series)):

- **Pool-independent values** — the flaw probability; the roof-support minimums of the full pathway and of the below-filter pathway, each where
  that pathway exists; the probability of no crack filling; and the probability of no flow limitation with every listed element applying.
- **Series at each pool level** — the pool levels and tailwater, and the initiation, continuation, roof-support, flow-limitation,
  unsuccessful-intervention, and breach probabilities, and the SRP.
- **Continuation results** — when a filter intersects the pathway, the probability of continuing erosion from the shared continuation method and
  the filter-evaluation method that set it (see the
  [Output and Interpretation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) chapter of the
  continuation manual).

## Flaw-Assessment Results

The flaw assessment reports each quantity in its chain, so that every stage can be traced (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)):

- **Normalized settlement** $\bar{s}$ — in percent of the dam height, from the supplied settlement or the year-of-construction default.
- **Localized tensile strain** $e_L$ — in percent.
- **Crack width** $W$ — in millimeters, whatever the length unit of the analysis.
- **Crack depth** $d_c$ — in the length unit of the analysis.
- **Bottom-of-crack elevation** $z_{bc}$ — in the length unit and datum of the analysis.
- **Observed cracking** — whether the probability of a continuous transverse crack was set to 1.0 by the observation of continuous transverse
  cracking.
- **Flaw probability** $P_{flaw}$ — the value used in Node 1.

When the override applies, the reported strain, width, depth, and bottom of the crack are still those computed from the settlement and abutment
inputs.

## Reading the System Response Curve

The curve has the shape of every crest-crack curve (see
[Interpreting the System Response Curve](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#interpreting-the-system-response-curve)):
negligible at and below the bottom of the crack, then piecewise constant, with further jumps possible at the top of a filter and the top of a wall
that lie above the bottom of the crack. Two features are specific to this model:

- **The flaw-assessment inputs set both the level and the start of the curve.** The settlement and abutment inputs set the strain, and the strain
  sets both the flaw probability, which scales the curve, and the crack depth, which sets the pool level at which it rises. Within the tabulated
  range, a larger settlement or a steeper abutment gives a larger strain, except with a step whose relative height $h_1/H$ is 0.4 or less, which
  holds the strain at zero. A larger strain raises the curve, unless continuous transverse cracking has been observed and the flaw probability is
  held at 1.0. It also lowers the pool level at which the curve rises, until the crack width reaches 75 mm at a strain of 2.5 percent; the strain
  tables reach 3.29 percent (3.5 percent with a step), so above 2.5 percent a larger strain can still raise the curve but no longer moves its rise.
  At or below a strain of 0.05 percent the flaw probability stays at its floor, the crack width is negligible, and the bottom of the crack is at
  the crest, so the curve stays negligible at every pool at or below the crest, and a small increase in settlement or slope within that range
  changes neither the level nor the rise of the curve. Somewhat larger strains give a shallow but non-zero crack.
- **The observation override raises the curve but does not move its rise.** With continuous transverse cracking observed, the curve above the
  bottom of the crack equals the product of the downstream nodes, but it still rises at the computed bottom of the crack.

## Checking the Result

Every value of the model can be reproduced by hand, except the continuation probability when the continuation method samples it:

- **Flaw chain** — the normalized settlement, strain, flaw probability, crack width, and crack depth can be recomputed from the reported inputs
  and the tables and relationships of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx), as in its worked example.
- **Bottom of the crack** — the reported bottom-of-crack elevation equals the crest elevation less the reported crack depth, and the first pool
  level with non-zero initiation is the first one above it.
- **Level of the curve** — above the bottom of the crack, the SRP at each pool level equals the product of the node values reported for that pool
  level.
- **Plausibility of the crack** — a crack depth comparable to or greater than the dam height, or a bottom of the crack below the foundation,
  should be examined, because the crack depth is not limited by the dam height (see marker IN-14 in
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-crack-elevation)). A settlement or slope at or beyond the end of the
  tables is assessed at the end value (see [Range of the Relationships](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-relationships)).

## Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis

The model is a screening tool. Its result indicates whether cross-valley settlement cracking warrants detailed analysis; it is not a substitute
for a detailed assessment of transverse cracking. Where the result is significant to a decision, the flaw probability, the crack depth, and the
governing downstream nodes should be re-examined with the settlement record, the abutment profile, and any observed cracking, and the detailed
methods and the judgment of the risk team should replace the screening values (see
[Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#screening-results-relative-to-detailed-analysis)
in the common methods manual).

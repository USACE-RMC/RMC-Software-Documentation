# Input Parameters

This chapter documents the inputs specific to the cross-valley settlement model: the length unit, the dam height, the abutment geometry, the
settlement or the year of construction, and the observation that overrides the flaw probability. The model also takes the inputs shared by the
CLE crest-crack models — hydraulic loading, the crest elevation and embankment type, the failure-pathway inputs, the upstream seepage-limiting
elements, and the intervention and breach probabilities — which are documented in the
[Common Input Parameters](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) chapter of the common
methods manual and summarized under [Shared Inputs](#shared-inputs) below. The model takes none of the initiation material inputs of the
compaction models (see
[The Two Model Groups](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#the-two-model-groups)).

The dam geometry, abutment geometry, and settlement groups give the short facts for their quantities in a table, followed by a description of each
input; the length unit and the observation of cracking are single selections described in prose, with the choices and what each implies.

## Length Unit

The user declares the length unit of the analysis, feet or meters (default feet). Every length and elevation the model takes — headwater and
tailwater, the crest, filter, and wall elevations, the dam height, the step height, and the settlement — is entered in that unit, on the common
datum described in [Units and Datum](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#units-and-datum).
The declared unit affects one step of the calculation: the crack-depth relationship, which is evaluated in metric units and converted to feet
when the analysis is in feet (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-crack-elevation)). The normalized
settlement, the tensile strain, the flaw probability, and the crack width are unaffected by the unit, so an analysis in meters gives the same
system response curve as the equivalent analysis in feet.

- **Dam height** $H$ — the height of the embankment, used to normalize the crest settlement (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#normalized-settlement)) and, when an abutment step is described, the step height.
- **Crest elevation** $z_c$ — the top of the embankment, from which the crack depth is measured down to the bottom of the crack. It is the shared
  input described in
  [Embankment Description](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-01 — Definition]
The dam height $H$ and the maximum post-construction settlement $s$ (see [Settlement](#settlement)) are used only as the ratio $s/H$, and
neither is defined beyond its name. State how each is to be measured: for $H$, whether it is the maximum height of the embankment at the deepest
section of the valley, measured from the crest to the lowest foundation contact or to the original ground surface; and for $s$, whether it is the
largest crest settlement measured along the crest since the end of construction, and whether it includes settlement of the foundation.

Proposed (unconfirmed): $H$ is the maximum embankment height at the deepest section, and $s$ is the largest crest settlement observed since the
end of construction, both from survey records. Confirm or replace.
:::

    ['Required', 'No step'],

/>

- **Abutment slope** $\theta$ — the inclination of the abutment, in degrees, which with the normalized settlement sets the localized tensile
  strain at the crest. One slope is entered; the model analyzes one abutment. The strain tables cover slopes from 15 to 60 degrees; a slope outside
  that range takes the strain at the nearer end (see
  [Range of the Relationships](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-relationships)).
- **Abutment step height** $h_1$ — the height of an abrupt step in the abutment profile. When no step is described, the abutment has no step and
  the no-step strain tables are used. When a step is described — including one of zero height — the step tables are used, interpolated on the relative step height
  $h_1/H$ (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#abutment-with-a-step)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Definition]
The abutment slope $\theta$ is not defined beyond its name and unit. State how it is measured — for example, the angle of the abutment surface
from the horizontal in the cross-valley profile beneath the crest, taken over what height — and which abutment the user should analyze when the
two differ (for example, the steeper, or each in turn as separate analyses). Also state the engineering basis for describing the
crack with one abutment per analysis (Assumption 3 in [Assumptions and Limitations](./06-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#settlement-and-abutment-geometry)).

Proposed (unconfirmed): the angle from the horizontal of the abutment surface in the longitudinal profile along the dam axis, with the steeper
abutment analyzed, or each abutment analyzed separately. Confirm or replace.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Definition]
The abutment step height $h_1$ is not defined beyond its name. State how the step and its height are to be identified in the abutment profile,
including the reference from which $h_1$ is measured (for example, the height of the step above the valley floor, or the height of the near-vertical
face of the step), so that $h_1/H$ is consistent with the tabulated relative step heights of 0.6, 0.8, and 0.9.
:::

## Settlement

The settlement may be entered directly, or estimated from the year of construction when no settlement record is available
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-4" />). One of the two must be supplied.

- **Maximum post-construction settlement** $s$ — the settlement of the crest after construction, in the same length unit as the dam height. When
  it is supplied, it sets the normalized settlement directly and the year of construction is not used.
- **Year of construction** $Y$ — the year the dam was completed. It is used only when no settlement is supplied, to assign a default normalized
  settlement: 0.5 percent of the dam height for a dam completed before 1950, and 0.25 percent for a dam completed in 1950 or later (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#normalized-settlement)).

## Observed Continuous Transverse Cracking

The user states whether continuous transverse cracking has been observed at the crest, with a cause attributable to settlement or unknown (default:
not observed). When it has, the flaw probability is set to 1.0; the strain, crack width, crack depth, and bottom-of-crack elevation are still
computed from the settlement and abutment inputs, so the observation changes Node 1 only (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#observation-override) and
[Observation Overrides](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) in the
common methods manual).

## Shared Inputs

The remaining inputs are those of the CLE crest-crack models, documented in the common methods manual:

- **Hydraulic loading** — the headwater series and the optional tailwater series
  ([Hydraulic Loading](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#hydraulic-loading)). Tailwater
  is reported with the results but does not enter the calculation.
- **Embankment type** — homogeneous earthfill, zoned earthfill, or central-core earth and rockfill, which sets the crack-filling probability
  ([Embankment Description](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description)).
  The model takes no slope-segment description of the embankment section.
- **Failure-pathway inputs** — the top-of-filter elevation, the continuation inputs, and the full-pathway and below-filter roof materials of the
  crest-crack models
  ([Failure-Pathway Inputs](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#failure-pathway-inputs)).
  The below-filter materials are taken at the bottom-of-crack elevation that the flaw assessment computes, so the user resolves them after the
  crack depth is known.
- **Upstream seepage-limiting elements** — the elements present and the top of any wall
  ([Upstream Seepage-Limiting Elements](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#upstream-seepage-limiting-elements)).
- **Intervention and breach** — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach, each defaulting to 1.0
  ([Intervention and Breach](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#intervention-and-breach)).

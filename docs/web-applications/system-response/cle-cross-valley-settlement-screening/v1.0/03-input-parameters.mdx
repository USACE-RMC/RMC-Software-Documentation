# Input Parameters

This chapter documents the inputs specific to the cross-valley settlement model: the unit system, the dam height, the abutment geometry, the
settlement or the year of construction, the observation that overrides the flaw probability, and the measured depth of an observed crack. The
model also takes the inputs shared by the concentrated leak erosion (CLE) crest-crack models — hydraulic loading, the crest elevation and
embankment type, the failure-pathway inputs, the upstream seepage-limiting elements, and the intervention and breach probabilities — which are
documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters">Common Input Parameters</DocLink>
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual and summarized under [Shared Inputs](#shared-inputs) below. The model takes none of the initiation
material inputs of the compaction models (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="model-overview" anchor="the-two-model-groups">The Two Model Groups</DocLink>).

The model takes one of two sets of flaw inputs. Ordinarily, the abutment geometry and the settlement (or the year of construction) set the crack
through the chain of relationships in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx). When continuous transverse cracking has been observed and its
depth measured, the measured depth may be entered instead; the abutment geometry and the settlement inputs are then not used and need not be
supplied (see [Measured Crack Depth](#measured-crack-depth)).

## Unit System

The model takes a unit system input, US customary (the default) or SI. All elevations and lengths are entered in the length unit of that system
(feet or meters) and share one vertical datum: headwater and tailwater, the crest, filter, and wall elevations, the dam height, the step height,
the settlement, and the measured crack depth. The unit system changes one step of the calculation, the crack-depth relationship, which is
evaluated in meters and converted to feet when the unit system is US customary (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth)); a measured crack depth is used in the unit entered, without
conversion. The normalized settlement, the localized tensile strain, the flaw probability, and the crack width (in millimeters) do not depend on
the unit system, so an analysis in SI units gives the same system response curve as the equivalent analysis in US customary units. The datum
convention shared by the CLE models is described in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="unit-system">Unit System</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual.

- **Dam height** $H_d$ — the height of the embankment, used to normalize the crest settlement (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#normalized-settlement)) and, when an abutment step is described, the step height. It also bounds a
  measured crack depth, which may not exceed it. The dam height is required whichever set of flaw inputs is used.
- **Crest elevation** $z_c$ — the top of the embankment, from which the crack depth is measured down to the bottom of the crack. It is the shared
  input described in
  <DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="embankment-description">Embankment Description</DocLink>.

In this model the dam height also sets the relative step height and bounds a measured crack depth.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-01 — Definition]
The dam height $H_d$ and the maximum post-construction settlement $s$, which form the normalized settlement $s/H_d$, are not defined beyond their
names. State (1) how $H_d$ is measured — for example, whether it is the maximum height of the embankment at the deepest section of the valley or
the height of the embankment at the section being evaluated, and whether it is measured from the crest to the lowest foundation contact or to the
original ground surface; and (2) how $s$ is measured — whether it is the largest crest settlement along the crest or the settlement at the section
being evaluated, over what period it is taken (for example, from the end of construction to the time of the assessment, or the expected long-term
settlement), and whether it includes settlement of the foundation.

Proposed (unconfirmed): $H_d$ is the maximum embankment height at the deepest section, and $s$ is the largest crest settlement observed since the
end of construction, both from survey records. Confirm or replace.
:::

<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-settlement-screening-table-3" /> lists the abutment geometry inputs.

  ]}
/>

- **Abutment slope** $\beta_a$ — the inclination of the abutment, in degrees, which with the normalized settlement sets the localized tensile
  strain at the crest. One slope is entered; the model analyzes one abutment. The strain tables cover slopes from 15 to 60 degrees; a slope
  outside that range takes the strain at the nearer end (see
  [Range of the Relationships](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-relationships)).
- **Abutment step height** $h_1$ — the height of an abrupt step in the abutment profile. When no step is described, the abutment has no step and
  the no-step strain tables are used. When a step is described — including one of zero height — the step tables are used, interpolated on the
  relative step height $h_1/H_d$ (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#abutment-with-a-step)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Definition]
The abutment slope $\beta_a$ is not defined beyond its name and unit. State how it is measured — for example, the angle of the abutment surface
from the horizontal in the cross-valley profile beneath the crest, taken over what height — and which abutment is to be analyzed when the two
differ (for example, the steeper, or each in turn as separate analyses). Also state the engineering basis for describing the crack with one
abutment per analysis (Assumption 3 in [Assumptions and Limitations](./06-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#settlement-and-abutment-geometry)).

Proposed (unconfirmed): the angle from the horizontal of the abutment surface in the longitudinal profile along the dam axis, with the steeper
abutment analyzed, or each abutment analyzed separately. Confirm or replace.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Definition]
The abutment step height $h_1$ is not defined beyond its name. State how the step and its height are to be identified in the abutment profile,
including the reference from which $h_1$ is measured (for example, the height of the step above the valley floor, or the height of the
near-vertical face of the step), so that $h_1/H_d$ is consistent with the tabulated relative step heights of 0.6, 0.8, and 0.9.
:::

## Settlement

The normalized settlement is formed from a measured post-construction settlement when one is supplied, or from a default selected by the year of
construction when it is not (<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-settlement-screening-table-4" />). One of the two must be supplied unless a
measured crack depth is entered.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-cross-valley-settlement-screening-table-4"
  widthMode="auto"
  alt="Settlement inputs"
  caption="Settlement inputs."
  headers={[[{ value: 'Input' }, { value: 'Symbol' }, { value: 'Units' }, { value: 'Default' }, { value: 'Description' }]]}
  columns={[
    ['Maximum post-construction settlement', 'Year of construction'],
    ['<i>s</i>', '<i>Y</i>'],
    ['ft or m', 'year'],
    ['From the year of construction', 'Not supplied'],
    ['Settlement of the crest after construction; zero or greater', 'Year the dam was completed'],
  ]}
/>

- **Maximum post-construction settlement** $s$ — the settlement of the embankment after the end of construction, in the same length unit as the
  dam height. When it is supplied, the normalized settlement is computed from it and the year of construction is not used by the flaw assessment.
- **Year of construction** $Y$ — the year the dam was completed. It is used only when no settlement is supplied, to select the default
  normalized settlement: 0.5 percent of the dam height for a dam completed before 1950, and 0.25 percent for a dam completed in 1950 or later (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#normalized-settlement)). In this model it is also not used when a measured crack depth is entered.

## Observed Continuous Transverse Cracking

The observation input states whether continuous transverse cracking has been observed at the crest, with a cause attributable to settlement or
unknown (default: not observed). When it has, the flaw probability is set to 1.0. Unless a measured crack depth is entered, the strain, crack
width, crack depth, and bottom-of-crack elevation are still computed from the settlement and abutment inputs, so the observation changes Node 1
only (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#observation-override) and
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="flaw-conventions" anchor="observation-overrides">Observation Overrides</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

:::note[Requires]
An observation of continuous transverse cracking. The measured crack depth may be entered only when such cracking has been observed.
:::

- **Measured crack depth** $d_m$ — the depth of the observed continuous transverse crack below the crest, in the length unit of the analysis.
  When it is entered, the crack depth is the measured depth, the
  bottom of the crack is the crest elevation less the measured depth, and the chain of relationships from settlement to crack depth is not
  evaluated, so the abutment slope, the step height, the settlement, and the year of construction are not used (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#measured-crack-depth)). When it is not entered, the crack depth is computed from those inputs, whether
  or not cracking has been observed.

The measured crack depth is the depth of the crack as measured in the field. Measuring it is the responsibility of the engineer; the model uses
the depth as entered.

## Shared Inputs

The remaining inputs are those of the CLE crest-crack models, documented in the CLE Common Methods manual:

- **Hydraulic loading** — the headwater series and the optional tailwater series
  (<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="hydraulic-loading">Hydraulic Loading</DocLink>).
  Tailwater is reported with the results but does not enter the calculation.
- **Embankment type** — homogeneous earthfill, zoned earthfill, or central-core earth and rockfill, which sets the crack-filling probability
  (<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="embankment-description">Embankment Description</DocLink>).
  The model takes no slope-segment description of the embankment section.
- **Failure-pathway inputs** — the top-of-filter elevation, the continuation inputs, and the full-pathway and below-filter roof materials of the
  crest-crack models
  (<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="failure-pathway-inputs">Failure-Pathway Inputs</DocLink>).
  The below-filter materials are taken at the bottom-of-crack elevation that the flaw assessment computes, or at the crest elevation less a
  measured crack depth, so they are resolved after the crack depth is known.
- **Upstream seepage-limiting elements** — the elements present and the top of any wall
  (<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="upstream-seepage-limiting-elements">Upstream Seepage-Limiting Elements</DocLink>).
- **Intervention and breach** — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach
  (<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="intervention-and-breach">Intervention and Breach</DocLink>).

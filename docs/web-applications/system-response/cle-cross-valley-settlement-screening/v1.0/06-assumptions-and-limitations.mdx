# Assumptions and Limitations

The cross-valley settlement model applies deliberate simplifications relative to a detailed assessment of transverse cracking and concentrated
leak erosion. They reduce input requirements, and most are designed to avoid false negatives at the screening level; Assumptions 2, 4, 5, and 10
note where they may not. This chapter lists the assumptions
of the model's flaw assessment. The assumptions it shares with the other CLE screening models — the product form of the event tree, the
headwater grid, the pool-independent flaw probability, the certain initiation of an open crest crack, the pathway gate at the top of the filter,
and the progression tables — are listed in the
[Assumptions and Limitations](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) chapter of
the common methods manual, where they are numbered 1 to 4, 7, 9, 15, and 17 to 19. Each assumption below states its engineering basis, its
effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold. Where an engineering basis is still awaiting confirmation, the assumption
cross-references the input marker at its primary location in this document.

## Settlement and Abutment Geometry

1. **The settlement, the dam height, the abutment slope, and any abutment step are single values.** The flaw assessment carries no uncertainty in
   them, and the flaw probability, crack width, and crack depth are each one value.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-04 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* The SRP reflects the entered geometry exactly; uncertainty in a measured settlement or slope does not spread the result,
     and the bottom of the crack is one elevation rather than a range.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the settlement record or the abutment profile is uncertain and the result is sensitive to it; the analysis may
     then be repeated at candidate values.
2. **Without a settlement record, the normalized settlement is 0.5 percent of the dam height for a dam completed before 1950 and 0.25 percent for
   one completed in 1950 or later.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-05 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#normalized-settlement).
   - *Effect on results:* The default fixes the strain, flaw probability, and crack depth for a given abutment; the two eras differ by a factor of
     two in normalized settlement.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the embankment was built by methods atypical of its era, or where it sits on a compressible
     foundation and the default may understate the settlement (non-conservative).
3. **One abutment is analyzed.** One abutment slope, and at most one step, describe the abutment geometry.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-02 in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#abutment-geometry),
     which also asks the basis for analyzing one abutment.
   - *Effect on results:* The result describes a crack at the abutment entered; a crack at the other abutment is not assessed in the same
     analysis.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where both abutments are steep or stepped; each may then be analyzed in turn.

## Localized Tensile Strain

4. **The localized tensile strain at the crest is a tabulated function of the normalized settlement and the abutment slope, and, with a step, of
   the relative step height.** The tables are interpolated linearly, and an input beyond them takes the value at the nearer end.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see markers IN-07 and IN-08 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#localized-tensile-strain), and IN-15 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-relationships) for the range.
   - *Effect on results:* The strain rises with settlement and slope within the tables, and is held constant beyond them, so a slope steeper than
     60 degrees or a normalized settlement above the tabulated range does not raise the strain further and may understate it
     (non-conservative).
   - *When it may not hold:* For abutment profiles that are not well described by one slope and one step, for valleys whose shape concentrates
     settlement differently, and for inputs beyond the tabulated range.
5. **A step with a relative height of 0.4 or less contributes zero strain.** The strain is interpolated toward zero between relative step
   heights of 0.6 and 0.4.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-09 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#abutment-with-a-step).
   - *Effect on results:* Entering a step with a relative height of 0.4 or less, including a step height of zero, gives a strain, flaw
     probability, and crack depth no higher — and generally lower — than describing no step for the same abutment slope and settlement
     (non-conservative relative to the no-step result).
   - *When it may not hold:* Where a small step sits on a steep abutment whose slope alone would produce significant strain.

## Flaw Probability and Crack Geometry

6. **The flaw probability is read from the relationship for a pool above the period of record, at every pool level.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-11 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability).
   - *Effect on results:* For pools within the period of record, the flaw probability is no lower than the at-or-below-record set would give,
     so the SRP there may be overstated (conservative).
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the pool range of interest has been experienced without cracking or leakage; a detailed assessment may then
     credit that performance.
7. **The flaw probability is interpolated in the logarithm of probability between tabulated strains, with a floor of 5 × 10<sup>-5</sup>.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-10 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability).
   - *Effect on results:* Even a strain of zero — from zero settlement on an abutment slope of 15 degrees or
     flatter, or from a step with $h_1/H$ of 0.4 or less — gives a flaw probability of 5 × 10<sup>-5</sup>; within the tabulated strains the
     flaw probability does not exceed about 0.16 without a step and about 0.22 with one.
   - *When it may not hold:* For strains near the ends of the relationship, where the floor and the added end point control the value.
8. **The crack width is a tabulated function of the strain, and the crack depth follows from the width by an empirical relationship.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Pierson (2023) for the width-depth relationship, whose bibliographic details are requested at marker IN-13; the basis of
     the width relationship is requested at marker IN-12 (both in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-crack-elevation)).
   - *Effect on results:* The crack depth, and so the pool level at which the SRP rises, follows the strain: a crack can reach about 37 ft
     (11.3 m) below the crest at the largest width, 75 mm.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where cracks have been observed and their depth is known, or where the fill's tensile behavior differs from that
     underlying the relationships.
9. **The crack depth is not limited by the height of the embankment.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-14 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-crack-elevation).
   - *Effect on results:* For a dam lower than the computed crack depth, the bottom of the crack falls below the base of the embankment and
     initiation is certain at every pool level evaluated above it (conservative).
   - *When it may not hold:* For low dams, where a crack cannot physically extend below the foundation contact.
10. **An observation of continuous transverse cracking sets the flaw probability to 1.0 but not the crack geometry.** The crack width, depth, and
    bottom-of-crack elevation are still computed from the settlement and abutment inputs.
    - *Engineering basis:* The common CLE observation convention, under which the override sets Node 1 only (Assumption 7 of the common methods
      manual; see [Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides)).
    - *Effect on results:* The SRP above the bottom of the crack rises to the product of the downstream nodes, while the elevation at which it
      rises stays at the computed bottom of the crack.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where the observed cracking extends deeper than the computed crack; the computed bottom of the crack may then place
      the rise of the curve too high.

## Limitations

- **One crack, one mechanism.** The model assesses one transverse crack formed by cross-valley differential settlement at one abutment. Cracking
  from settlement adjacent to a wall or cliff, from desiccation, or from frost is assessed by the corresponding CLE screening models.
- **No crack hydraulics.** Initiation is a gate on the bottom of the crack (see
  [Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate));
  the crack width is used only to estimate the crack depth, not to compute the flow or shear stress in the crack.
- **Tabulated range.** The strain, flaw probability, and crack width relationships are tabulated over limited ranges, and inputs beyond them take
  the end values (see [Range of the Relationships](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-relationships)).

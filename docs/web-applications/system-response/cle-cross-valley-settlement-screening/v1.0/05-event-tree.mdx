# Event Tree

The model evaluates the eight-node event tree shared by the CLE screening models at each pool level and multiplies the node probabilities to give
the SRP. The tree, its node questions, and the reasons for its structure are documented in the
[Event Tree](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree) chapter of the common methods manual. This
chapter states how each node is populated for cross-valley settlement. Every node follows the common treatment of the crest-crack models; the
model adds no node and changes no node rule.

### Node 1: Flaw

$P_{flaw}$ is the result of the flaw assessment, $P_{raw}$ from the localized tensile strain or 1.0 when continuous transverse cracking has
been observed (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability)). It is the same at every pool level.

### Node 2: Initiation

Initiation is the headwater gate of the crest-crack models at the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$ from the flaw assessment: zero while the pool
is at or below $z_{bc}$, and 1.0 once it rises above it (see
[Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
in the common methods manual). Tailwater takes no part. Because $z_{bc}$ is the crest elevation less the crack depth, the settlement and abutment
inputs set the pool level at which the SRP rises from negligible. Between strains of 0.0005 and 0.025 (0.05 and 2.5 percent), a larger strain gives a
wider and deeper crack and so a lower pool level at which the SRP rises; at or below 0.0005 the crack width is negligible and the bottom of the
crack stays at the crest; above 0.025 the crack width holds at 75 mm, and the crack depth and that pool level
no longer change (see [Crack Width](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-width)).

### Node 3: Continuation

Where a filter intersects the pathway along the crack, the probability of continuing erosion $P_{ce}$ is evaluated by the shared continuation
method from the filter and base-soil description (see
[Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil)
in the continuation manual). It applies at pool levels at or below the top-of-filter elevation $z_{tf}$, and at every pool level when the filter
caps the pathway; above the filter's top, and at every pool level when no filter intersects the pathway, continuation is 1.0 (see
[Node 3: Continuation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-3-continuation)
in the common methods manual).

### Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

Roof support is the minimum roof-support probability over the below-filter materials at pool levels at or below $z_{tf}$, and over the
full-pathway materials above it or with no filter (see
[Node 4: Progression — Roof Support](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-4-progression--roof-support)).
The below-filter materials are those on the pathway from the upstream face to the filter at the bottom-of-crack elevation computed in
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-crack-elevation).

### Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

The probability of no crack filling is set by the embankment type and is the same at every pool level (see
[Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-5-progression--no-crack-filling)).

### Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

The probability of no flow limitation is the minimum over the upstream elements present. When a top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ is given, the
walls apply only at pool levels at or below it, and only the upstream facing element applies above it (see
[Walls That Stop Below the Crest](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#walls-that-stop-below-the-crest)).

### Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

The user-specified probabilities $P_{int}$ and $P_{br}$, each one value at every pool level, defaulting to 1.0 (see
[Nodes 7 and 8](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#nodes-7-and-8-unsuccessful-intervention-and-breach)).

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each headwater (pool) elevation $h$ — not to be confused with the abutment step height $h_1$ — is the product of the eight node probabilities, as in the common methods manual's
[SRP Computation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation):

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-eq-10"
  equation="\mathrm{SRP}(h) = P_{flaw} \times P_{init}(h) \times P_{cont}(h) \times P_{roof}(h) \times P_{nf} \times P_{nl}(h) \times P_{int} \times P_{br}"
/>

where:

- $P_{flaw}$ is the flaw probability of Node 1;
- $P_{init}(h)$ is the initiation probability of Node 2;
- $P_{cont}(h)$ is the continuation probability of Node 3: the probability of continuing erosion $P_{ce}$ from the shared continuation method at
  pool levels at or below $z_{tf}$ (at every pool level when the filter caps the pathway), and 1.0 above $z_{tf}$ or when no filter intersects the
  pathway;
- $P_{roof}(h)$ is the roof-support probability of Node 4;
- $P_{nf}$ is the probability of no crack filling of Node 5;
- $P_{nl}(h)$ is the probability of no flow limitation of Node 6;
- $P_{int}$ and $P_{br}$ are the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach of Nodes 7 and 8.

$P_{init}$, $P_{cont}$, $P_{roof}$, and $P_{nl}$ are evaluated at $h$, and the other nodes are the same at every pool level. Where the product is
zero — for example at and below the bottom of the crack, or wherever a user-specified intervention or breach probability or the continuation
probability is zero — the SRP is reported as a negligibly small value. The tree is evaluated on the headwater
grid of the common methods
([Headwater Grid](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#headwater-grid)): every supplied headwater
value and additional levels between them. The bottom of the crack is not inserted into the grid, so the rise of the curve is located to within
one grid interval; supplying headwater values at $z_{bc}$ and just above it places the rise exactly.

## Elevations Where the Curve Changes

Three elevations decide where the curve can change: the bottom of the crack $z_{bc}$, the top of the filter $z_{tf}$, and the top of a wall
$z_{tw}$. Because initiation is zero at and below $z_{bc}$, a filter or wall top matters only where it lies above the bottom of the crack:

- **A filter top at or below $z_{bc}$** — unless the filter caps the pathway, every pool level at which initiation is non-zero is above the
  filter's top, so continuation is 1.0 and the full-pathway roof materials govern there. The filter then has no effect on the SRP.
- **A filter top above $z_{bc}$** — between $z_{bc}$ and $z_{tf}$ the below-filter pathway governs, with continuation $P_{ce}$; above $z_{tf}$ the
  full pathway governs. The curve can jump up or down at $z_{tf}$, depending on $P_{ce}$ and the two roof-support minimums (see
  [Node 3: Continuation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-3-continuation)).
- **A wall top at or below $z_{bc}$** — the walls never apply at a pool level where initiation is non-zero, so only the upstream facing element,
  if present, limits flow.
- **A wall top above $z_{bc}$** — between $z_{bc}$ and $z_{tw}$ the walls apply with the other elements present; above $z_{tw}$ the wall credit
  drops out and only the upstream facing element, if present, limits flow. The curve rises at $z_{tw}$ unless the facing element alone gives the
  same minimum.

Because the crack depth comes from the flaw assessment, a change in the settlement or abutment inputs can move $z_{bc}$ across a filter or wall top
and so change which of these cases applies.

## Worked Example

The dam of the flaw-assessment [worked example](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#worked-example) has a flaw probability of 0.00614 and a bottom of the
crack at elevation 240.0 ft. Take it as a homogeneous earthfill embankment of silty sand (SM) with no filter on the pathway, no upstream
seepage-limiting element, and the default intervention and breach probabilities
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-14" />).

The system response curve is negligible up to elevation 240.0 ft and constant at 0.00522 above it. Adding a modern cutoff wall that reaches the
crest would multiply the SRP above the bottom of the crack by its probability of no flow limitation, 0.001, giving $5.2 \times 10^{-6}$; an
observation of continuous transverse cracking would raise the flaw probability to 1.0 and the SRP above the bottom of the crack to 0.85.

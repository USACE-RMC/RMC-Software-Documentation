# Event Tree

The model evaluates the eight-node event tree shared by the concentrated leak erosion (CLE) screening models at each pool level and multiplies
the node probabilities to give the system response probability (SRP). The tree, its node questions, and the reasons for its structure are
documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree">Event Tree</DocLink>
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual. This chapter states how each node is populated for cross-valley settlement. Every node follows the
common treatment of the crest-crack models; the model adds no node and changes no node rule. A measured crack depth, when entered, changes only
how the flaw assessment sets the bottom of the crack (see [Measured Crack Depth](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#measured-crack-depth)).

## Node 1: Flaw

*Question:* Does a continuous transverse crack caused by cross-valley differential settlement exist through the embankment?

$P_{flaw}$ is the result of the flaw assessment, $P_{raw}$ from the localized tensile strain or 1.0 when continuous transverse cracking has
been observed, whether or not its depth was measured (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability)). It is the same at every
pool level.

## Node 2: Initiation

*Question:* Is the hydraulic shear stress on the walls of the crack large enough to initiate concentrated leak erosion?

The model applies the headwater gate of the crest-crack models (<EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-8" fallback="Equation 8" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="crest-crack-models-headwater-gate">Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual) at the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$ from the flaw assessment. Tailwater takes no part. Because
$z_{bc}$ is the crest elevation less the crack depth, the crack depth sets the pool level at which the SRP rises from negligible. When a measured
crack depth is entered, $z_{bc}$ is the crest elevation less the measured depth (see
[Measured Crack Depth](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#measured-crack-depth)), and the gate is otherwise unchanged. Otherwise the settlement and
abutment inputs set the crack depth: between strains of 0.0005 and 0.025 (0.05 and 2.5 percent), a larger strain gives a wider and deeper crack
and so a lower pool level at which the SRP rises; at or below 0.0005 the crack width is negligible and the bottom of the crack stays at the crest;
above 0.025 the crack width holds at 75 mm, and the crack depth and that pool level no longer change (see
[Crack Width](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-width)).

## Node 3: Continuation

*Question:* Does an unfiltered seepage exit exist that allows erosion to continue?

Continuation follows the crest-crack pathway convention (<EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-14" fallback="Equation 14" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-3-continuation">Node 3: Continuation</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual). Where a filter intersects the pathway along the crack, the node takes the probability of continuing erosion
$P_{ce}$, the node result of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening model (written $P_{cont}$ in that manual; see its
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="output-and-interpretation" anchor="node-result">Node Result</DocLink>
section), evaluated from the filter and base-soil description (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="method-selection-and-governing-base-soil">Method Selection and Governing Base Soil</DocLink>
in the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual). It applies at pool levels at or below the top-of-filter elevation $z_{tf}$, and at
every pool level when the filter caps the pathway; above the filter's top, and at every pool level when no filter intersects the pathway,
continuation is 1.0.

## Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

*Question:* Does a continuous stable roof form over the enlarging pipe, so that it stays open?

Roof support is the minimum roof-support probability over the below-filter materials at pool levels at or below $z_{tf}$, and over the
full-pathway materials above it or with no filter (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-4-progression--roof-support">Node 4: Progression — Roof Support</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual). The below-filter materials are those on the pathway from the upstream face to the filter at the
bottom-of-crack elevation computed in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#bottom-of-crack-elevation), or set by a
measured crack depth (see [Measured Crack Depth](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#measured-crack-depth)).

## Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

*Question:* Does the enlarging pipe remain unfilled, rather than being filled and healed by material carried in from upstream?

The probability of no crack filling is set by the embankment type and is the same at every pool level (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-5-progression--no-crack-filling">Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

## Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

*Question:* Does the flow into the enlarging pipe remain unrestricted by upstream seepage-limiting elements?

The probability of no flow limitation is the probability of the upstream facing element times that of the governing wall, the most effective of
the walls listed; an element that is absent contributes 1.0. When a top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ is given, the walls apply only at pool levels
at or below it, and only the upstream facing element applies above it (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="walls-that-stop-below-the-crest">Walls That Stop Below the Crest</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

## Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

*Questions:* Do detection and intervention fail (Node 7)? Does an uncontrolled release of impounded water occur (Node 8)?

Both nodes are the shared intervention and breach inputs, documented with their defaults in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="nodes-7-and-8-unsuccessful-intervention-and-breach">Nodes 7 and 8</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. The model applies them without change.

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each headwater (pool) elevation $h$ is the product of the eight node probabilities, by <EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-1" fallback="Equation 1" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="srp-computation">SRP Computation</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. The headwater elevation $h$ is not to be confused with the abutment step height $h_1$. In this model
$P_{init}$, $P_{cont}$, $P_{roof}$, and $P_{nl}$ vary with $h$, and the other nodes are the same at every pool level.

Where the product is zero — for example at and below the bottom of the crack, or wherever an intervention or breach probability or the
continuation probability is zero — the SRP is reported as a negligibly small value. The tree is evaluated on the headwater grid of the CLE Common Methods manual
(<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="headwater-grid">Headwater Grid</DocLink>):
every supplied headwater value and additional levels between them. The bottom of the crack is not inserted into the grid, so the rise of the
curve is located to within one grid interval; supplying headwater values at $z_{bc}$ and just above it places the rise exactly.

## Elevations Where the Curve Changes

Three elevations decide where the curve can change: the bottom of the crack $z_{bc}$, the top of the filter $z_{tf}$, and the top of a wall
$z_{tw}$. Because initiation is zero at and below $z_{bc}$, a filter or wall top matters only where it lies above the bottom of the crack:

- **A filter top at or below $z_{bc}$** — unless the filter caps the pathway, every pool level at which initiation is non-zero is above the
  filter's top, so continuation is 1.0 and the full-pathway roof materials govern there. The filter then has no effect on the SRP.
- **A filter top above $z_{bc}$** — between $z_{bc}$ and $z_{tf}$ the below-filter pathway governs, with continuation $P_{ce}$; above $z_{tf}$ the
  full pathway governs. The curve can jump up or down at $z_{tf}$, depending on $P_{ce}$ and the two roof-support minimums (see
  <DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-3-continuation">Node 3: Continuation</DocLink>
  in the CLE Common Methods manual).
- **A wall top at or below $z_{bc}$** — the walls never apply at a pool level where initiation is non-zero, so only the upstream facing element,
  if present, limits flow.
- **A wall top above $z_{bc}$** — between $z_{bc}$ and $z_{tw}$ the governing wall multiplies the upstream facing element's probability, if a
  facing element is present; above $z_{tw}$ the wall credit drops out and only the upstream facing element, if present, limits flow. The curve
  rises at $z_{tw}$ by the inverse of the governing wall's probability.

Because the crack depth comes from the flaw assessment, a change in the settlement or abutment inputs, or in a measured crack depth, can move
$z_{bc}$ across a filter or wall top and so change which of these cases applies.

## Worked Example

The dam of the flaw-assessment [worked example](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#worked-example) has a flaw probability of 0.00614 and a bottom of the
crack at elevation 240.0 ft. Take it as a homogeneous earthfill embankment of silty sand (SM) with no filter on the pathway, no upstream
seepage-limiting element, and the default intervention and breach probabilities
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-settlement-screening-table-15" />).

The system response curve is negligible up to elevation 240.0 ft and constant at 0.00522 above it. Adding a modern cutoff wall that reaches the
crest would multiply the SRP above the bottom of the crack by its probability of no flow limitation, 0.001, giving $5.2 \times 10^{-6}$. An
upstream facing element and a sheet pile wall whose top is at elevation 240 ft, each with a probability of no flow limitation of 0.5, would give a
flow-limitation probability of $0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25$ at pool levels at or below 240 ft and 0.5 above it; because the wall's top is at the
bottom of the crack, only the facing element applies where initiation is possible, and the SRP above 240.0 ft would be 0.00261. An
observation of continuous transverse cracking would raise the flaw probability to 1.0 and the SRP above the bottom of the crack to 0.85. If the
observed crack were also measured at 12.5 ft deep, the bottom of the crack would move up to elevation 252.5 ft (see the
[flaw-assessment worked example](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#worked-example)): the curve would be negligible up to 252.5 ft and 0.85 above it.

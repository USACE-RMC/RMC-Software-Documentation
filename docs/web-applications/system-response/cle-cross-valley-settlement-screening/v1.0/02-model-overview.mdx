# Model Overview

## Purpose

The cross-valley settlement screening model computes a system response curve for concentrated leak erosion through a transverse crack caused by
differential settlement of the embankment across the valley. The system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level is the conditional
probability of breach of the embankment by concentrated leak erosion through such a crack, given that pool level.

An embankment settles more where the fill beneath the crest is thicker, so the crest settles most over the deepest part of the valley and least
near the abutments. Where the abutment is steep, or where it carries an abrupt step, the settlement changes rapidly along the
crest and stretches the fill near the top of the embankment. If that stretching exceeds what the fill can sustain, a transverse crack opens at the
crest and extends some depth into the embankment. Once the pool rises above the bottom of the crack, water can flow through it, and the crack
becomes the flaw from which concentrated leak erosion can develop.

## Intended Use

The model is a screening-level assessment of concentrated leak erosion through a cross-valley settlement crack. Like the other CLE screening
models, it makes simplifying and generally conservative assumptions with two goals (see the
[Intended Use](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#intended-use) section of the common
methods manual):

- **Preventing false negatives** — The flaw probability is read from the more conservative of two available relationships, the one for a pool
  above the period of record, and initiation is taken as certain once the pool rises above the bottom of the crack.
- **Reducing input burden** — The flaw assessment needs only the dam height, the abutment slope, an optional abutment step, and either the
  measured settlement or the year of construction, from which a default settlement is assigned.

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments of transverse cracking. Its flaw assessment uses tabulated
relationships over a limited range of settlement and abutment slope (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)), and its initiation
treatment does not analyze the hydraulics of the crack.

## How the Model Works

The model executes the three stages common to the CLE screening models (see
[How the Model Works](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#how-the-model-works) in the common
methods manual), with a flaw assessment specific to cross-valley settlement:

1. **Flaw assessment** — The maximum post-construction crest settlement, or a default from the year of construction, is normalized by the dam
   height. The normalized settlement and the abutment slope give the localized tensile strain at the crest, interpolated from tables for an abutment
   without a step or, when a step is described, from tables for three relative step heights. The strain then gives the probability of a continuous
   transverse crack, the crack width, and, through a relationship between crack width and crack depth, the depth of the crack below the crest and
   so the bottom-of-crack elevation. An observation of continuous transverse cracking sets the flaw probability to 1.0 (see
   [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).
2. **Pathway description** — The model applies the user's description of the failure pathway along the crack, taken at the bottom-of-crack
   elevation: the top of the first filter the pathway meets, the materials of the full and the below-filter pathways, and the upstream
   seepage-limiting elements and the top of any wall (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#shared-inputs)).
3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the model evaluates the eight nodes of the CLE event tree and multiplies them. Initiation
   steps from zero to 1.0 where the pool rises above the bottom of the crack; continuation, roof support, and flow limitation change where the pool
   crosses the top of a filter or of a wall (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

Every step is deterministic. The only part of the calculation that can involve sampling is the continuation probability, when the shared
continuation method evaluates the filter by its route through the soil classifications of the base and filter soils (see
[Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil)
in the continuation manual).

## Physics Basis

The model combines the following engineering relationships:

- **Localized tensile strain from settlement and abutment geometry** — tabulated relationships between the normalized crest settlement, the
  abutment slope, the relative height of an abutment step, and the localized tensile strain at the crest (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#localized-tensile-strain))
- **Flaw probability and crack width from the localized tensile strain** — tabulated relationships between the strain, the probability of a
  continuous transverse crack, and the crack width (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability))
- **Crack depth from crack width** — an empirical relationship attributed to Pierson (2023) (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth-and-bottom-of-crack-elevation))
- **Initiation in an open crest crack** — the headwater gate shared by the crest-crack models, documented in the
  [Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
  section of the common methods manual
- **Continuation, roof support, crack filling, and flow limitation** — the failure-pathway conventions and the tabulated progression
  probabilities of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, documented in the
  [Failure Pathway and Progression](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression)
  chapter of the common methods manual, with filter evaluation by the shared continuation method

## Comparison with Detailed Models

In a detailed risk analysis, the likelihood, width, and depth of a transverse crack are estimated by the risk team from the totality of the
evidence, and initiation in the crack is assessed with a shear stress analysis of the crack geometry, for example with the
[Vertical Rectangular Crack](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/vertical-rectangular-crack)
or [Vertical Triangular Crack](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/vertical-triangular-crack)
geometry of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox. <TableReference tableKey="cle-cross-valley-screening-table-1" /> summarizes the differences.

## Relationship to the Common CLE Methods

The model is one of the four crest-crack models of the CLE family, with settlement adjacent to a wall or cliff, desiccation cracking, and frost
cracking (see [The Two Model Groups](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#the-two-model-groups)
in the common methods manual). It differs from the other three in its flaw assessment: it is the one CLE model that does not use risk-factor and
likelihood-factor scoring, estimating the flaw probability instead from the settlement-based relationships documented in
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx). It is also the one crest-crack model that derives the crack depth from a crack width rather than
from tabulated depths.

Everything downstream of the flaw assessment follows the common methods: the eight-node event tree, the headwater grid, the initiation gate, the
pathway gates on the top of the filter and the top of a wall, and the progression tables. This manual does not repeat them; [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)
states how each node is populated for this model and links the section of the common methods manual that documents it.

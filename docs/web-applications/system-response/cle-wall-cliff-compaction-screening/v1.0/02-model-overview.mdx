# Model Overview

## Purpose

The wall or cliff compaction screening model computes a system response curve for concentrated leak erosion through poorly compacted fill placed
against a wall or a cliff. The system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level is the conditional probability of breach of the embankment
by concentrated leak erosion through such a zone, given that pool level. The curve of SRP values across the range of headwater levels characterizes
the embankment's vulnerability to this mechanism.

The flaw is a zone of poorly compacted fill along the face of the wall or cliff, continuous through the embankment from its upstream side to its
downstream side. The model judges the likelihood of such a zone from three conditions: how the fill against the face was compacted, how steep the
face is, and whether a concrete buttress is present.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-01 — Applicability]
State which structures and faces the model is intended to cover — for example concrete walls of spillways, outlet works, or powerhouses that the
embankment abuts, and natural rock faces at a steep abutment — and whether a wall and a cliff are intended to be treated identically by every input,
including the concrete-buttress factor, which has no direct counterpart on a natural cliff. Provide also the physical description of the mechanism
to state here (why fill against a face is prone to poor compaction, and how face slope and a buttress affect it), with its literature source.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the model applies to any steep, effectively rigid face against which embankment fill was placed, natural or constructed,
and a cliff is scored with the concrete buttress absent; fill against a face is prone to poor compaction because compaction equipment cannot work
tight against it, and a steeper face or a projecting buttress enlarges the zone that receives less compactive effort. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Intended Use

The model is a screening-level assessment of concentrated leak erosion along a wall or cliff. Like the other CLE screening models, it makes
simplifying and generally conservative assumptions to avoid false negatives and to reduce input burden: three qualitative selections describe the
likelihood of a flaw, the base of the flaw follows from the wall or cliff height, and default distributions cover the erodibility and initial-opening
properties that are typically unavailable at the screening stage (see
[Intended Use](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#intended-use) in the CLE Common Methods
manual).

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments. For higher-level analyses, the concentrated leak erosion toolbox
methods and the internal-erosion probability methods they draw on provide the complete methodologies (see
[Comparison with Detailed Models](#comparison-with-detailed-models)).

## How the Model Works

The model executes the three stages common to the CLE compaction models (see
[How the Model Works](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#how-the-model-works) in the CLE
Common Methods manual):

1. **Flaw assessment** — The compaction of the fill against the wall, the slope of the wall, and the presence of a concrete buttress are each scored
   with a likelihood factor. Their weighted sum is converted to a raw flaw probability, which is multiplied by a flaw-continuity probability keyed to
   the compaction score. An observation of leakage adjacent to the wall sets the flaw probability to 1.0. The base of the flaw is placed at the crest
   elevation less the wall or cliff height (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).
2. **Pathway resolution** — The failure pathway runs through the embankment section at the base of the flaw. The user states whether a filter
   intersects it, lists the materials it passes through, and lists the upstream seepage-limiting elements; a cutoff wall whose top is below the base
   of the flaw does not limit flow through it.
3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the flaw probability is multiplied by the initiation probability from a Monte Carlo
   cylindrical-pipe analysis at the base of the flaw and by the continuation, progression, intervention, and breach probabilities, which are the
   same at every pool level (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

## Physics Basis

The model combines the following engineering methods, each documented in a shared manual:

- **Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring** for the probability of a continuous flaw <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, as documented in
  [Flaw Conventions](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
  in the CLE Common Methods manual. The three factors and their scores are documented in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx).
- **Hydraulic shear stress on the walls of a cylindrical pipe** compared with the critical shear stress of the soil, as documented in
  [Initiation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#delegated-method-cylindrical-pipe-initiation)
  in the CLE Common Methods manual and in the
  [Cylindrical Pipe](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/cylindrical-pipe) chapter
  of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation manual.
- **Filter evaluation** for the probability that erosion continues past a filter intersecting the flaw, through the shared continuation method
  documented in the
  [Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil)
  chapter of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening Technical Reference Manual.
- **Tabulated progression probabilities** for roof support, crack filling, and flow limitation <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, as documented in
  [Failure Pathway and Progression](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression)
  in the CLE Common Methods manual.

## Relationship to the Shared Manuals

This model belongs to the compaction group of the CLE screening models, with the core, conduit, and foundation or abutment compaction models (see
[The Two Model Groups](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#the-two-model-groups) in the CLE
Common Methods manual). It shares with that group the cylindrical-pipe initiation analysis, the single flaw elevation at which every downstream node
is evaluated, and the tabulated progression nodes. What is specific to it is:

- **The flaw assessment** — three factors weighted 3, 2, and 1, a weighted-sum curve, and the flaw-continuity stage keyed to the compaction of the
  fill against the wall (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).
- **The base of the flaw** — derived from the wall or cliff height rather than from a normal-pool heuristic or a direct input, and unchanged by the
  leakage observation (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#base-of-the-flaw)).
- **The default initial pipe diameter** — a triangular distribution from 5 to 10 mm whose most likely value is its low value (see
  [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation)).
- **The filter statement** — whether a filter intersects the flaw is stated directly; the continuation inputs are used only when it does (see
  [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#filter-at-the-flaw)).

The model uses no toolbox method other than the cylindrical-pipe initiation analysis, and it delegates the continuation node to the shared
continuation method when a filter is stated.

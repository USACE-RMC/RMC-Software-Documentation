# Input Parameters

This chapter documents the inputs specific to the concentrated leak erosion (CLE) wall or cliff compaction model: the unit system, the wall or
cliff height, the three flaw-factor selections, the observation of leakage adjacent to the wall, and the statement of whether a filter intersects
the flaw. The model also takes the inputs shared by the CLE compaction models, documented in the
[Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters)
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual; the last section below lists them and notes how each applies to this model.

## Unit System

The model takes a unit system input, US customary (the default) or SI. All elevations and lengths are entered in the length unit of that system
(feet or meters) and share one vertical datum. The input changes no formula in this model, and quantities with fixed units (critical shear stress
in lb/ft², initial pipe diameter in millimeters) are stated in those units regardless of the unit system. The datum convention shared by the CLE
family, including the requirement that the base of the flaw and the top of the section used for initiation lie at positive elevations, is
documented in
[Unit System](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#unit-system)
in the CLE Common Methods manual.

The wall or cliff height $h_w$ is the height of the wall or cliff against which the fill was placed. It is distinct from the pool level $h$. The
base of the flaw is placed at the crest elevation less this height, $z_f = z_c - h_w$ (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#base-of-flaw-elevation)). The height must be positive, and the wall or cliff may not be taller than the
embankment: the resulting base of the flaw may not lie below the lowest segment bottom of the shell section. It must also lie within the section
used for initiation — not above its top (the core top, when a core section is supplied) and not below the lowest segment bottom of either of its
faces — as documented in
[Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
in the CLE Common Methods manual.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Definition]
State how the wall or cliff height $h_w$ is to be measured. The model places the base of the flaw at $z_c - h_w$, which reads the height as the
depth below the embankment crest of the lowest point of the face in contact with the fill. State whether that is the intended definition, and how the
user should enter the height when the wall or cliff does not reach the crest, when its foot varies in elevation along the embankment, or when the
fill against it rests on a foundation above the wall's foot.

Proposed (unconfirmed): $h_w$ is the vertical distance from the embankment crest down to the lowest elevation at which fill is in contact with the
wall or cliff face on the section being screened. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Flaw-Factor Selections

The flaw assessment scores three factors, each a choice from a list of categories
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-compaction-screening-table-3" />). Each selection is required unless leakage adjacent to the wall is
reported (see [Leakage Observation](#leakage-observation)).

  columns={[

    [
      'One of five categories; how the fill against the wall or cliff was placed and compacted.',

      'One of three categories; whether a concrete buttress is present at the wall or cliff.',
    ],

/>

The wall-fill compaction is mechanical, slightly wet of optimum; hand, slightly dry of optimum; hand, thick layers, dry of optimum; no formal
compaction; or Unknown. The wall slope is vertical; H/V from 0.1 to 0.25; H/V above 0.25; or Unknown. The concrete buttress is absent, present, or
Unknown. <TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-compaction-screening-table-6" /> gives the likelihood factor of each category. Mechanical and
hand compaction refer to mechanical and hand-operated equipment, and the water content is stated relative to optimum. No formal compaction covers
fill placed against the face without formal compaction or poorly compacted in very thick lifts without moisture control. A battered face of H/V from
0.1 to 0.25 has a horizontal run of 0.1 to 0.25 per unit of vertical rise, and H/V above 0.25 is battered more flatly. Unknown applies wherever the
compaction of the fill against the face, the slope of the face, or the presence of a buttress is not known.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Definition]
The wall-fill compaction categories do not state their boundaries, and they do not cover every combination of compaction method and water content.
State (1) what layer thickness distinguishes "thick layers" from the layers of the other categories; (2) what range of water content is meant by
"slightly wet" and "slightly dry" of optimum; and (3) which category the user should select for fill that does not match any category exactly — for
example mechanically compacted fill dry of optimum, or hand-compacted fill wet of optimum.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-04 — Definition]
The wall-slope categories leave two conditions undefined. State which category the user should select (1) for a face battered at less than 0.1
horizontal per unit of vertical rise, which is neither vertical nor within the 0.1 to 0.25 band, and (2) for an overhanging face. State also
whether the slope is that of the face over its full height or of its steepest part, and whether a cliff's slope is to be judged the same way as a
wall's.

Proposed (unconfirmed): "Vertical" covers every face steeper than 0.1 horizontal per unit of vertical rise, including an overhanging face, and the
slope is judged over the height in contact with the fill. Confirm or replace.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Definition]
State what qualifies as a concrete buttress for this factor — for example a buttress or counterfort projecting from the wall face into the fill,
a buttress supporting the wall from the embankment side, or a concrete block placed against a cliff — and what physical effect of the buttress
makes a flaw more likely (the Present category scores higher than Absent).

Proposed (unconfirmed): a concrete buttress is any concrete element projecting from the face into the fill, which creates confined corners and
re-entrant zones against which fill is difficult to compact. Confirm or replace.
:::

When leakage adjacent to the wall or cliff is reported, the flaw probability is set to 1.0 and the three flaw-factor selections become optional;
when all three are still supplied they are scored and reported, but they do not change the flaw probability. The base of the flaw is unchanged (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#observation-override)).

When no filter is stated, continuation is certain and the continuation inputs are not used. When a filter is stated, the continuation inputs — the
filter description and the base soils upstream of it — are required and are evaluated by the shared continuation method, as documented in the
[Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/input-parameters)
chapter of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual.

The model takes no filter elevation and does not check the filter statement against the base of the flaw, so a filter is stated as present only
after confirming that it intersects the pathway at that elevation.

## Shared Inputs

- **Hydraulic loading** — the headwater series and the optional tailwater series, as documented in
  [Hydraulic Loading](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#hydraulic-loading).
  Tailwater reduces the net head across the flaw in the initiation analysis where it stands above the base of the flaw.
- **Embankment description** — the crest elevation $z_c$, the embankment type, the shell section, and the optional core section, as documented in
  [Embankment Description](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description).
  In this model the crest elevation is also the elevation from which the wall or cliff height is measured (see
  [Wall or Cliff Geometry](#wall-or-cliff-geometry)). The embankment type sets the crack-filling node only; the core section, when supplied,
  replaces the shell for the seepage path length whatever the embankment type.
- **Embankment materials** — the soil classifications of the materials the flaw passes through, which govern roof support, as documented in
  [Failure-Pathway Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#failure-pathway-inputs).
  At least one is required.
- **Upstream seepage-limiting elements** — the upstream facing and cutoff walls present and, when one is a wall, its top elevation $z_{tw}$, as
  documented in
  [Upstream Seepage-Limiting Elements](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#upstream-seepage-limiting-elements).
  The wall in this input is an upstream cutoff or seepage-barrier wall that limits flow into the flaw; it is a different structure from the wall
  or cliff against which the fill was placed. Its top elevation is compared with the base of the flaw, not with the pool.
- **Initiation material inputs** — the core soil classification and the optional critical shear stress, initial pipe diameter, and seepage path
  length, as documented in
  [Initiation Material Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#initiation-material-inputs).
  The core soil classification is the soil of the fill against the wall or cliff, in which the flaw would erode. The default initial pipe
  diameter of this model is documented in [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation).
- **Intervention and breach** — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach, as documented in
  [Intervention and Breach](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#intervention-and-breach).

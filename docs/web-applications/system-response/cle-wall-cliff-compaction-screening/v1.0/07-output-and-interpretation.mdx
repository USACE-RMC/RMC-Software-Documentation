# Output and Interpretation

The model reports the SRP at each evaluated pool level together with the probability of each of the eight nodes, in the form shared by the CLE
compaction models (see [Output and Interpretation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation)
in the CLE Common Methods manual). This chapter describes the results specific to this model and how to read the system response curve of the wall
or cliff compaction mechanism.

## Results

**Pool-independent results:**

- **Flaw probability** — Node 1 (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)); 1.0 when leakage adjacent to the wall is reported.
- **Continuation probability** — Node 3: the continuation method's probability of continuing erosion when a filter is stated, otherwise 1.0.
- **Roof-support probability** — Node 4, the minimum across the embankment materials.
- **Crack-filling probability** — Node 5, the probability of no crack filling for the embankment type.
- **Flow-limitation probability** — Node 6, after any cutoff wall whose top is below the base of the flaw has dropped out; 1.0 when no upstream
  element applies.

**Pool-dependent series**, over every supplied headwater value and the additional levels between them: the pool levels, the tailwater (reported
as the base-of-flaw elevation where there is no tailwater), the initiation probability, the unsuccessful-intervention and breach
probabilities, the flow-limitation probability (the same value at every pool level as the pool-independent result above), and the SRP.

**Supporting results:**

- **Flaw assessment** — the likelihood factors of the wall-fill compaction, the wall slope, and the concrete buttress; the weighted sum; the raw
  flaw probability; the flaw-continuity probability; the flaw probability; whether the leakage observation override applied; and the base-of-flaw
  elevation $z_f$. When leakage is reported and a flaw-factor selection is omitted, the scoring results are not reported; the flaw probability
  (1.0), the override, and the base of the flaw still are.
- **Continuation** — when a filter is stated, the results of the continuation method, read as documented in
  [Output and Interpretation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) in the Continuation
  (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual. When no filter is stated, no continuation results are reported.
- **Initiation analysis** — at each pool level, the upstream and downstream heads above the base of the flaw, the hydraulic gradient, and the
  mean and 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles of the hydraulic shear stress; and the seepage path length used, either the supplied length or the
  length derived from the section (see
  [Supporting Results](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#supporting-results)).

## Interpreting the System Response Curve

The curve is negligible at and below the base of the flaw, $z_f = z_c - h_w$. Above it, where the node product exceeds the negligibly small floor
at which the SRP is reported (see [SRP Computation](./05-event-tree.mdx#srp-computation)), the curve rises with pool level as the hydraulic gradient
through the pipe grows, following the initiation probability. A rising tailwater that reduces the net head across the pipe can flatten or lower the curve
over that range. Every other node is the same at every pool level, so the curve approaches their product as
the initiation probability approaches 1.0:

<EquationNoRef equation="\mathrm{SRP}(h) \le P_{flaw} \times P_{cont} \times P_{roof} \times P_{nf} \times P_{nl} \times P_{int} \times P_{br}" />

The rise and the bound both hold only where the node product exceeds the reporting floor. Where it falls below the floor — always for mechanically
compacted fill slightly wet of optimum — the curve stays flat at the floor at every pool level and can sit above the stated bound.

The wall or cliff height sets where the curve begins: the taller the wall or cliff, the lower the pool at which initiation can begin. How quickly the
curve rises above that level is set by the critical shear stress and initial pipe diameter distributions and the seepage path length (see
[Interpreting the System Response Curve](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#interpreting-the-system-response-curve)
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

Without a leakage observation, the flaw probability is at most about 5.6 × 10<sup>-3</sup> (see
[Flaw-Continuity Stage](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-continuity-stage)), which bounds the curve at every pool level. With a leakage observation, the
flaw probability is 1.0 and the curve is set entirely by the downstream nodes.

## Reading the Node Breakdown

The readings of
[Reading the Node Breakdown](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#reading-the-node-breakdown)
in the CLE Common Methods manual apply. For this model:

- **The flaw probability** reflects the three selections, and the flaw assessment results show which drives it. The wall-fill compaction selection
  acts twice — through its weight of 3 in the weighted sum and through the flaw-continuity probability — so the flaw probability is especially
  sensitive to it.
- **A stated filter** scales the whole curve by the continuation probability (no change when it is 1.0).
- **An upstream cutoff wall** lowers the curve by its flow-limitation probability only if its top is at or above the base of the flaw; otherwise
  only an upstream facing element, if listed, does.

## Checking the Result

In addition to the checks of
[Checking the Result](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#checking-the-result) in
the CLE Common Methods manual:

- **Flaw probability** — without a leakage observation, it can be reproduced by hand from the reported likelihood factors with
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx): the weighted sum, the raw flaw probability read from the weighted-sum curve, and the flaw-continuity
  probability for the compaction category.
- **Base of the flaw** — the reported base-of-flaw elevation equals the crest elevation less the wall or cliff height, and the curve is negligible
  at and below it. A curve that rises from an unexpected pool level can point to a wall or cliff height, or a crest elevation, on a different datum
  from the headwater series.
- **Flow limitation** — a cutoff wall listed among the upstream elements but given no credit can indicate a top-of-wall elevation below the base
  of the flaw. It need not: where a listed facing element governs the minimum, a cutoff wall that reaches the flaw also has no visible effect.
  Compare the top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ with the reported base of the flaw $z_f$ directly.

## Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis

The model is a screening tool. Its result indicates whether concentrated leak erosion along a wall or cliff warrants detailed analysis; it is not a
substitute for one. Where the result is significant to a decision, the governing nodes should be re-examined with site-specific information — the
construction records of the fill against the face, the face geometry, the location of any observed leakage, and erosion testing of the fill — and the
detailed toolbox methods, the methods of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, and the judgment of the risk team should replace
the screening values (see
[Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#screening-results-relative-to-detailed-analysis)
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

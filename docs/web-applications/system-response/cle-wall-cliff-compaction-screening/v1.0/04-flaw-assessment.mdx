# Flaw Assessment

Node 1 of the event tree is the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous zone of poorly compacted fill exists along the wall or cliff, through the
embankment. The model estimates it in two stages, following the conventions shared by the CLE scoring models (see
[Flaw Conventions](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions) in the CLE Common Methods
manual): three site factors are scored by the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scheme of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />
and their weighted sum is converted to a raw flaw probability, which is then multiplied by a flaw-continuity
probability keyed to the compaction of the fill. An observation of leakage adjacent to the wall replaces the estimate with certainty. The
assessment also fixes the base-of-flaw elevation used by every downstream node. The flaw probability is the same at every pool level.

## Factors and Risk Factors

The three factors and their risk-factor weights $RF$ are:

- **Wall-fill compaction**, $RF = 3$ — the compaction of the fill against the wall or cliff.
- **Wall slope**, $RF = 2$ — the inclination of the face.
- **Concrete buttress**, $RF = 1$ — whether a concrete buttress is present.

The weights follow the pattern of most CLE scoring models, in which the three factors are weighted 3, 2, and 1 (see
[Factors](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#factors) in the CLE Common Methods manual).

## Weighted Sum

The three scores combine into the weighted sum of
[Flaw Conventions](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#weighted-sum):

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-compaction-screening-eq-1"
  equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_{comp} + 2\,LF_{slope} + LF_{but}"
/>

where $LF_{comp}$, $LF_{slope}$, and $LF_{but}$ are the likelihood factors of the wall-fill compaction, the wall slope, and the concrete buttress.
The attainable weighted sum runs from 6, with every factor at its least likely score, to 22.5, with no formal compaction, a vertical face, and a
buttress present.

A weighted sum of 9 or less gives a negligible raw flaw probability: the curve's lowest point is negligible and sums below it take that end value.
Between 9 and 11 the probability rises steeply from negligible to 1 × 10<sup>-4</sup>. The curve's
highest point, a weighted sum of 24, lies beyond the attainable range; the largest attainable sum of 22.5 gives a raw flaw probability of about
5.6 × 10<sup>-2</sup>. No factor is an incompatible-condition factor — a factor whose least likely score forces the flaw probability to zero (see
[Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
in the CLE Common Methods manual) — so no single score forces the raw flaw probability to zero. The flaw
probability itself can still be negligible from one selection: mechanical compaction slightly wet of optimum makes it negligible through the
flaw-continuity stage (see [Flaw-Continuity Stage](#flaw-continuity-stage)).

## Flaw-Continuity Stage

The raw flaw probability is multiplied by the flaw-continuity probability $P_{cf}$, interpolated in the logarithm of probability from the
wall-fill compaction score on the curve shared with the core and foundation or abutment compaction models (see
[Flaw-Continuity Stage](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-continuity-stage) in the
CLE Common Methods manual, where its engineering basis is requested):

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-compaction-screening-eq-2"
  equation="P_{flaw} = P_{raw}(\Sigma) \times P_{cf}(LF_{comp})"
/>

For the wall-fill compaction categories, $P_{cf}$ takes the values of
<TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-compaction-screening-table-6" />. Every compaction score of this model is a whole number, so no
interpolation between the tabulated points is needed.

The compaction selection therefore enters the flaw probability twice — with the heaviest weight in the weighted sum, and again through the
flaw-continuity probability. Mechanically compacted fill slightly wet of optimum gives a negligible flaw probability whatever the other two factors
score. Without a leakage observation, the largest attainable flaw probability is about 5.6 × 10<sup>-3</sup> (no formal compaction, a vertical face,
and a buttress present); with every selection Unknown, the weighted sum is 17 and the flaw probability is about 6.3 × 10<sup>-6</sup>.

## Worked Example

For fill placed against a wall without formal compaction ($LF_{comp} = 4$), a face battered between 0.1 and 0.25 horizontal per unit of vertical
rise ($LF_{slope} = 2$), and an unknown buttress ($LF_{but} = 2$):

- The weighted sum is $\Sigma = 3(4) + 2(2) + 2 = 18$, a tabulated point, so $P_{raw} = 1 \times 10^{-2}$.
- The flaw-continuity probability for no formal compaction is $P_{cf} = 1 \times 10^{-1}$.
- The flaw probability is $P_{flaw} = (1 \times 10^{-2})(1 \times 10^{-1}) = 1 \times 10^{-3}$.

For a crest elevation of 265 ft and a wall height of 30 ft, the base of the flaw is at 265 − 30 = 235 ft (see
[Base of the Flaw](#base-of-the-flaw)).

## Leakage Observation Override

When leakage adjacent to the wall is reported, the flaw probability is set to 1.0 whatever the factors score, as documented in
[Observation Overrides](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) in the
CLE Common Methods manual. The three selections become optional: when all three are supplied, their scores, weighted sum, raw flaw probability, and
flaw-continuity probability are still computed and reported for reference; when any is omitted, the scoring is not performed. Either way the flaw
probability is 1.0.

The override sets the flaw probability only. The base of the flaw remains at the crest elevation less the wall or cliff height, because it follows
from the geometry of the wall rather than from the scoring; the elevation at which the leakage was observed is not an input.

## Base of the Flaw

The flaw is idealized for initiation as a cylindrical pipe whose invert is the base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$, placed at the crest elevation $z_c$ less
the wall or cliff height $h_w$:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-compaction-screening-eq-3"
  equation="z_f = z_c - h_w"
/>

This is the third of the ways the compaction models resolve the base of the flaw, alongside the normal-pool heuristic and direct input (see
[Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
in the CLE Common Methods manual). The base of the flaw sets the pipe invert of the initiation analysis and the elevation against which the top of an
upstream cutoff wall is compared (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)). The restrictions on where it may lie are listed under
[Wall or Cliff Geometry](./03-input-parameters.mdx#wall-or-cliff-geometry).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-10 — Engineering Basis]
Placing the base of the flaw at the foot of the wall or cliff (<EquationReference equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-compaction-screening-eq-3" />)
requires an engineering basis to state here — why the flaw is evaluated at the lowest elevation of the fill against the face rather than at another
elevation along it, or over a range of elevations.

Proposed (unconfirmed): a poorly compacted zone may extend over the full height of the face, and its lowest point carries the largest head above
the flaw at any pool and is the first to be loaded as the pool rises. The seepage path across the section is also longest at the foot, so the
hydraulic gradient is not necessarily largest there; state whether evaluating initiation at the foot is intended to be conservative. Note also that
the foot is the unconservative location for the upstream cutoff-wall comparison, since a cutoff whose top reaches the foot is credited even where
the poorly compacted zone extends above it. Confirm or replace.
:::

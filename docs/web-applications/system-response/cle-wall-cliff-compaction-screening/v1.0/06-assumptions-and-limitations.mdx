# Assumptions and Limitations

The wall or cliff compaction model applies deliberate simplifications relative to a detailed internal-erosion risk analysis. They reduce input
requirements and are designed to avoid false negatives at the screening level. The model makes every assumption shared by the CLE compaction
models — the eight-node product, the pool-independent flaw probability, the cylindrical-pipe idealization and its default distributions, the
single-elevation failure pathway, and the tabulated progression probabilities — each listed with its basis in
[Assumptions and Limitations](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) in the CLE
Common Methods manual. This chapter lists the assumptions specific to this model. Each states its engineering basis, its effect on results, and the
conditions under which it may not hold; where a basis is still awaiting confirmation, the assumption cross-references the input marker at its
primary location in this document.

## Flaw Assessment

1. **The likelihood of a continuous poorly compacted zone along the face is judged from three factors**: the compaction of the fill against the
   face, the slope of the face, and the presence of a concrete buttress, weighted 3, 2, and 1.
   - *Engineering basis:* The risk-factor and likelihood-factor scheme of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the source of the
     weights and scores is requested at marker IN-06 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#likelihood-factors).
   - *Effect on results:* The compaction of the fill has the largest effect on the weighted sum; the buttress has the smallest.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where other conditions control the quality of the fill against the face — for example an irregular or benched face,
     or a soil against the face that compacts less readily, as its plasticity may make it. The soil of the fill enters the initiation analysis, but
     the scoring does not account for how compactable it is. The risk team's estimate then replaces the screening value.
2. **An Unknown selection is scored 3 for the compaction and the slope of the face, and 2 for the buttress.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-07 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#likelihood-factors).
   - *Effect on results:* Unknown compaction scores as hand compaction in thick layers dry of optimum (3); an unknown slope scores 3, between a
     battered face (2) and a vertical one (3.5); an unknown buttress scores below the midpoint of its Absent and Present scores. With every selection Unknown, the flaw probability is about
     6.3 × 10<sup>-6</sup>.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the available records suggest a condition better or worse than the Unknown score; selecting the indicated
     category, or bracketing the result with the neighboring categories, then shows the sensitivity.
3. **One weighted-sum curve applies at every pool level, with no credit for the pool of record.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see markers IN-08 and IN-09 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#raw-flaw-probability).
   - *Effect on results:* The flaw probability does not depend on how high the reservoir has stood without signs of a flaw.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the embankment has performed satisfactorily under pools close to those of interest; a detailed analysis can then
     credit that performance.
4. **The compaction of the fill enters the flaw probability twice** — through its weight in the weighted sum and through the flaw-continuity
   probability.
   - *Engineering basis:* The flaw-continuity stage shared with the core and foundation or abutment compaction models, whose basis is requested in
     [Flaw-Continuity Stage](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-continuity-stage) in
     the CLE Common Methods manual.
   - *Effect on results:* Mechanically compacted fill slightly wet of optimum gives a negligible flaw probability whatever the slope and buttress
     selections, and each step from hand compaction slightly dry of optimum toward no formal compaction raises the flaw probability by one to two
     orders of magnitude through the flaw-continuity probability alone. The two stages are not independent evidence about the fill — both read the
     same compaction selection — so the double count magnifies the sensitivity of the flaw probability to that one selection.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the compaction records of the fill against the face are uncertain. A single selection of Mechanical, slightly
     wet of optimum then removes the mechanism whatever the face slope and buttress, so a misjudged selection can produce a false negative.
     Bracketing the result with the neighboring compaction categories shows how much the selection controls it.
5. **An observation of leakage adjacent to the wall sets the flaw probability to 1.0 and leaves the base of the flaw unchanged.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Engineering judgment shared by the CLE observation overrides: an observation of leakage demonstrates the flaw (see
     [Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) in
     the CLE Common Methods manual). The base of the flaw follows from the wall geometry and is not an observation.
   - *Effect on results:* The SRP is governed entirely by the downstream nodes, and initiation is still evaluated at the foot of the wall or cliff.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the leakage emerges well above the foot of the face, or has another source; the observed leakage elevation is not
     an input, so a flaw at a higher elevation is evaluated at the foot.

## Flaw Location

6. **The base of the flaw is at the crest elevation less the wall or cliff height.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-02 in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#wall-or-cliff-geometry) for the
     measurement of the height and marker IN-10 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#base-of-the-flaw) for placing the flaw at the foot.
   - *Effect on results:* The taller the wall or cliff, the lower the base of the flaw and the lower the pool at which initiation can begin. Where
     tailwater is below the base of the flaw, a lower base also gives a larger net head across the flaw at a given pool; where tailwater stands
     above the base, the net head is the pool less the tailwater, whatever the wall or cliff height. Where the seepage path length is derived
     rather than supplied, a lower base also lengthens the path, because an embankment section widens downward, so the hydraulic gradient — and with it
     the initiation probability at a given pool — does not necessarily increase with the wall or cliff height. Only the pool at which initiation
     can begin is certain to drop.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the wall or cliff does not reach the crest, where its foot varies in elevation along the embankment, or where the
     fill in contact with it ends above its foot.
7. **The default initial pipe diameter is triangular from 5 to 10 mm with its most likely value at 5 mm.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested in
     [Initial Pipe Diameter](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#initial-pipe-diameter) in the CLE
     Common Methods manual.
   - *Effect on results:* Most realizations carry a diameter near the low end of the range, so the initiation curve is shaped mainly by the
     smaller diameters.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the opening along the face is known to be larger or smaller than the default range; a site-specific
     distribution should then be supplied.

## Failure Pathway

8. **Whether a filter intersects the flaw is stated by the user, with no filter elevation.**
   - *Engineering basis:* The single-elevation pathway convention of the compaction models (see
     [Failure Pathway and Progression](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#the-failure-pathway)
     in the CLE Common Methods manual): the filter either intersects the flaw at its base or does not, at every pool level.
   - *Effect on results:* A stated filter scales the SRP by the continuation probability at every pool level (no change when it is 1.0); no
     filter leaves continuation certain.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where a filter intersects only part of the face, for example only above a certain elevation; the user should then judge
     whether it intersects the pathway at the base of the flaw.
9. **An upstream cutoff wall limits flow through the flaw only if its top reaches the foot of the wall or cliff.**
   - *Engineering basis:* The flow-limitation gating of the compaction models, whose basis is requested in
     [Walls That Stop Below the Crest](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#walls-that-stop-below-the-crest)
     in the CLE Common Methods manual.
   - *Effect on results:* A cutoff wall whose top is below the base of the flaw receives no credit, however effective it is.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the poorly compacted zone extends well above the foot of the face. The cutoff wall is compared only with the foot,
     so a cutoff whose top just reaches the foot receives full credit even though flow can pass over its top into the poorly compacted zone higher
     on the face; the credit is then unconservative. A cutoff whose top is below the foot cannot intersect a zone that sits above it, so giving it
     no credit is consistent.

## Applicability

10. **A wall and a cliff are treated alike.** Every input applies in the same way to fill against a constructed wall and against a natural cliff.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-01 in [Model Overview](./02-model-overview.mdx#purpose).
    - *Effect on results:* The concrete-buttress factor is scored for a cliff as for a wall.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where a cliff face is irregular, fractured, or erodible, conditions the model does not describe.

# Assumptions and Limitations

The BEP screening model applies deliberate simplifications relative to the full toolbox models. They reduce input requirements and are designed
to avoid false negatives at the screening level. Each assumption below states its engineering basis, its effect on results, and the conditions
under which it may not hold. Where an engineering basis is still awaiting confirmation, the assumption cross-references the input marker at its
primary location in this document.

## Seepage Geometry and Hydraulics

1. **The site is idealized as one of two blanket-theory configurations, Case 2 or Case 4.** The configuration is selected from the upstream
   seepage barrier (see [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)).
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): the two cases bracket the screening-relevant configurations — an
     upstream barrier either exists or does not — and automatic selection removes a choice from the user.
   - *Effect on results:* Sites whose stratigraphy matches other blanket-theory cases (semi-pervious strata, more complex layering) are
     approximated by the nearer of the two configurations.
   - *When it may not hold:* Sites with well-characterized semi-pervious strata or geometries that need Cases 1, 3, or 5 through 8; the
     toolbox models apply directly to such sites.
2. **The downstream confining layer carries a linear head profile.** The excess head decays from its value at the toe to zero at the layer's
   downstream termination.
   - *Engineering basis:* Blanket theory <Citation citationKey="USACE1956" />. The landside layer is analyzed as an impervious top stratum
     whatever permeability is selected; the permeability selection affects only the upstream credit.
   - *Effect on results:* Exit heads are reduced by the path-length ratio, which is less conservative than an infinite-blanket idealization.
   - *When it may not hold:* Blankets with strong lateral heterogeneity, or relief wells and toe drains that alter the head profile. A
     semi-pervious landside layer also departs from it: blanket theory replaces a semi-pervious landside stratum with an equivalent impervious
     length that can be shorter or longer than the physical length, depending on the landside boundary condition (see
     [Blanket Theory Case 6](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/blanket-theory-case-6)),
     so the impervious treatment can overstate or understate the exit head.
3. **When the confining-layer extent or presence is unknown and no berm is present, the downstream seepage path spans 1 to 5 embankment heights.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-11 in [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* The wide range spreads the sampled exit heads, gradients, and daylight path lengths, widening the uncertainty carried
     into the blowout, heave, and daylight-progression results.
   - *When it may not hold:* Sites where mapping or borings bound the layer extent outside 1 to 5 embankment heights, where the analyst supplies the extent instead.
4. **Supplied seepage-path lengths are single values.** The upstream blanket length and the upstream and downstream confining-layer extents
   carry no uncertainty; the default range for an unknown downstream extent is the only seepage-path length treated as a distribution.
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): a known length is a geometric site measurement entered as one
     value, and uncertainty about an unknown downstream extent is represented by the wide default range instead.
   - *Effect on results:* The SRP reflects the entered path lengths exactly; uncertainty in a measured length does not spread the result.
   - *When it may not hold:* Sites where a supplied seepage-path length is both highly uncertain and influential; the analyst then brackets
     that uncertainty by running the analysis at candidate lengths.
5. **The downstream control level is interpolated linearly between successive supplied headwater values.** The control level is the higher of the tailwater
   and the exit reference elevation, evaluated at each supplied headwater–tailwater pair.
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening simplification (engineering judgment) with a bounded, documented effect (see
     [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)).
   - *Effect on results:* Within any interval between successive supplied headwater values where the tailwater crosses the reference elevation, the
     interpolated control level slightly overstates the true value, understating the net head there by no more than the change in tailwater
     across that interval (slightly non-conservative).
   - *When it may not hold:* Tailwater that crosses the exit reference within a wide interval between successive supplied headwater values;
     additional headwater–tailwater pairs near the crossing reduce the effect.

## Unfiltered Exit and Initiation

6. **A pre-existing defect fully penetrates the confining layer with a probability given by a Weibull survival function of thickness.** The
   characteristic depth is 3.5 ft and the shape parameter is 1.25 (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-13 in [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* The defect probability sets a pool-independent lower bound on the toe unfiltered-exit probability, because the
     unfiltered-exit probability can never be less than the defect probability. Averaged over the thickness bands, the defect probability is
     about 0.55 for Very Thin, 0.32 for Unknown, 0.09 for Thin, and below 0.01 for Moderate and Thick, so it controls Node 2 whenever the band
     includes thin layers and the blowout probability is lower.
   - *When it may not hold:* Sites with a defect survey or verified intact layers (inspection, construction records) that contradict the
     generic defect population.
7. **The defect and blowout pathways to an unfiltered exit are statistically independent.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-15 in [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* The combined unfiltered-exit probability is at least as large as either mechanism alone.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the two mechanisms share a common cause (both concentrated at thin spots in the layer), the union may misstate
     the combined probability.
8. **A downstream seepage berm is classified by where it ends relative to the confining layer.** A berm within a confirmed layer of known
   greater extent retains the through-layer exit mechanisms at an offset exit point. A berm that reaches or clears the extent, or overlies a
   layer of unknown extent or unknown presence, produces a daylighting exit at the berm toe with a certain unfiltered exit. No berm is credited
   with filtering, and every berm lengthens the toe progression path.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-12 in
     [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx) and marker IN-14 in [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* A berm within a known layer extent keeps the defect and blowout composition, with the head reduced at the offset exit
     point. Over a confirmed layer, a berm that daylights the exit makes the unfiltered-exit and initiation nodes certain, and the toe pathway
     then carries the governing curve. Over a layer of unknown presence, a berm makes the unfiltered-exit node certain (and the initiation node
     too when it reaches or clears a supplied extent), and both pathways are still evaluated (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).
   - *When it may not hold:* Engineered filter berms designed to filter the exit; sites where the berm length relative to the layer extent is
     itself uncertain, since that comparison switches the exit treatment.
9. **A confining layer of unknown presence is evaluated as possibly present.** The defect and blowout analyses run with the Unknown thickness
   band, roof support comes from the embankment materials, and both exit pathways are evaluated.
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design objective (engineering judgment): an unverified layer is neither ignored nor fully credited, avoiding false negatives from
     either extreme.
   - *Effect on results:* Results for unknown-presence sites reflect deliberately wide parameter bands rather than either the confirmed-present
     or the confirmed-absent treatment.
   - *When it may not hold:* Once field verification confirms the layer present or absent, the analysis is re-run with the confirmed answer.

## Progression

10. **A confirmed confining layer provides a certain pipe roof; otherwise the least capable embankment material governs roof support.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Roof-support values follow Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />. Layer-as-roof: engineering
      judgment adopted in the model design — a fine-grained layer able to confine the foundation also sustains the pipe roof.
    - *Effect on results:* With a confirmed layer, no roof-support reduction applies. Otherwise the governing roof-support probability multiplies
      the SRP at every pool level, so a single listed clean sand or gravel (roof-support probability 0.01) lowers the whole curve by two orders
      of magnitude.
    - *When it may not hold:* Layers too thin or brittle to sustain a roof over a growing pipe; heavily fractured or desiccated layers.
11. **The pipe path is horizontal.** No inclination correction is applied.
    - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): pipe inclination is rarely known at the screening stage.
    - *Effect on results:* Neutral for horizontal paths; conservative for downward-inclined paths and non-conservative for upward-inclined
      paths. The size of the effect at shallow angles awaits a basis (marker IN-19 below).
    - *When it may not hold:* Steeply inclined pipe paths (the angle beyond which the effect matters awaits a basis; marker IN-19 below).
12. **The anisotropy correction is set to its reference value.** The correction factor $C_K$ is taken as 1.0, the value the Schmertmann
    relationship $C_K = (1.5/R_{kf})^{0.5}$ gives for a conductivity ratio $R_{kf} = k_h/k_v$ of 1.5, and the seepage path length is not
    transformed for anisotropy (see
    [Field Horizontal Critical Gradient](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#field-horizontal-critical-gradient)).
    - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): the conductivity ratio is rarely characterized at the screening stage.
    - *Effect on results:* Relative to applying the full method with the site ratio, the result is conservative for ratios below 1.5 (at equal
      conductivity the full method gives $C_K \approx 1.22$, a higher critical gradient). For ratios above 1.5 it can be substantially
      non-conservative: at a ratio of 4 the full method gives $C_K \approx 0.61$, partly offset because the full method also shortens the
      transformed path length, which raises the path-length and depth corrections.
    - *When it may not hold:* Strongly layered or stratified foundations, where horizontal conductivity is well above vertical.
13. **No underlayer correction is applied beneath the aquifer.** The correction factor $C_Z$ is taken as 1.0, the Schmertmann method's value
    when no higher-permeability underlayer exists.
    - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): underlayer properties are rarely available at the screening stage.
    - *Effect on results:* A higher-permeability layer beneath the piping sand increases the flow toward the pipe and lowers the critical
      gradient ($C_Z < 1$), so neglecting it is non-conservative where one exists; where none exists, the treatment matches the full method. The
      BEP Progression manual notes that $C_Z$ is approximately 1 when the ratio of piping-layer depth to pipe radius exceeds about 80 (see
      [Field Horizontal Critical Gradient](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#field-horizontal-critical-gradient)).
    - *When it may not hold:* A higher-permeability stratum (for example, coarse sand or gravel) beneath a thin piping sand.
14. **An Unknown fines content is taken as 0%, and the fines correction is a step at 5% fines.** The correction factor is 1.0 below 5% fines and
    1.4 at or above 5%.
    - *Engineering basis:* Taking an Unknown fines content as 0% is a conservative screening posture (engineering judgment): it forgoes the higher fines correction, which would raise the resistance to progression. Step form: basis
      requested — see marker IN-16 in [Delegated Methods](./07-delegated-methods.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* Sands with real fines content receive less progression resistance than the method would assign unless the 5% or
      more category is selected.
    - *When it may not hold:* Sands with significant fines where gradation data is available, where the analyst selects the matching fines
      category.
15. **The progression probability is non-decreasing with pool level.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Physical reasoning: for a fixed seepage geometry and a net head that rises with pool level, a higher pool cannot make progression less likely.
    - *Effect on results:* Sampling noise cannot produce a locally decreasing progression node.
    - *When it may not hold:* Configurations where the net head genuinely falls as the pool rises (tailwater rising faster than headwater); the
      constraint then overrides a real decrease.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-19 — Engineering Basis]
Assumption 11 (horizontal pipe path) needs a basis for two statements about the size of the neglected inclination effect: that it is small at
shallow angles, and that it becomes significant for paths inclined more than about 10° from horizontal. Provide the literature source (for
example, the pipe-path inclination relationship of the Schmertmann method) with full bibliographic details, or the engineering-judgment
attribution, and confirm or replace the 10° threshold.
:::

## Exit Datum and Exit Type

17. **The toe blowout and heave analyses use the daylight datum.** Where a daylight ground-surface elevation is supplied (and the daylighting
    exit is not the berm toe), the net head for the blowout and heave analyses is measured to the daylight reference elevation, although the
    probabilities are evaluated at the toe.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-20 below.
    - *Effect on results:* The difference between the daylight ground and the toe ground is not added to or removed from the uplift at the toe.
      Where the daylight ground lies below the toe, the toe uplift is overstated (conservative); where it lies above, the toe uplift is
      understated (non-conservative).
    - *When it may not hold:* Sites where the daylight ground differs markedly from the toe ground.
18. **The toe exit type follows the presence answer.** The toe exit is hole-type only for a confirmed layer whose exit stays beneath it; for a
    layer of unknown presence it is plane-type (see [Delegated Methods](./07-delegated-methods.mdx)).
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-21 below.
    - *Effect on results:* A plane-type exit takes no exit-geometry reduction, so the toe progression probability under unknown presence is
      lower than it would be with a hole-type exit through a possibly present layer (less conservative).
    - *When it may not hold:* Sites where a confining layer is in fact present, so that toe seepage would emerge through defects as concentrated
      exits.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-20 — Engineering Basis]
Assumption 17: where a daylight ground-surface elevation is supplied, the toe blowout and heave analyses measure the net head to the daylight
reference elevation rather than the toe ground, so the elevation difference between them does not enter the uplift at the toe. Provide the
engineering basis or judgment attribution for this datum choice, or confirm whether the toe ground should be the datum for those analyses.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-21 — Engineering Basis]
Assumption 18: under a layer of unknown presence, the toe exit is treated as plane-type ($C_{3d} = 1.0$), which is less conservative than the
hole-type treatment ($C_{3d} = 0.5$) applied to a confirmed layer. Provide the engineering basis or judgment attribution for this choice, or
confirm whether a possibly present layer should take the hole-type factor.
:::

## Flaw

16. **The flaw probability is independent of pool level.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Definitional: the flaw is a geologic condition whose existence does not depend on the pool.
    - *Effect on results:* The same flaw probability scales the SRP at every pool level.
    - *When it may not hold:* Pool-dependent flaw mechanisms (for example, first-filling collapse features) are outside the model.

## Interpretation Cautions

Three aspects of the screening formulation deserve particular attention when reading results:

- **Unknown-driven breadth.** The Unknown selections (layer presence, thickness, extent) intentionally substitute wide characterizations. A
  high screening SRP at a site with many Unknown answers reflects that breadth as much as the site physics; resolving the unknowns with field
  information and re-running the analysis is the intended response.

- **Defect floor for thin layers.** Because the defect probability does not depend on pool level and the toe unfiltered-exit probability can
  never fall below it (assumption 6), thickness bands that include thin layers keep Node 2 high even at low pools. Where that floor exceeds the
  blowout probability, the pool dependence of the toe SRP comes from the initiation and progression nodes instead.

- **No driving head below the exit reference.** Pools at or below the governing exit reference elevation produce zero net head and therefore
  zero SRP. The reference elevations — and the daylight ground surface in particular — directly position the low-pool end of the
  system response curve.

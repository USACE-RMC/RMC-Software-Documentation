# Output and Interpretation

The model produces two groups of results: probabilities that do not depend on pool level, and series evaluated at each pool level. The
governing SRP series is the system response curve.

## Pool-Independent Results

- **Flaw probability** — the mean posterior probability of a continuous foundation flaw (Node 1), from the Bayesian assessment in
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx).
- **Flaw probability spread** — the 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 95th percentiles of the posterior flaw probability, which show how sharply the
  evidence constrains the flaw judgment.
- **Defect probability** — the mean probability that a pre-existing defect fully penetrates the confining layer; produced only when a confining
  layer may be present.
- **Roof-support probability** — the probability that the material over the developing pipe sustains a roof (Node 4): 1.0 with a confirmed
  confining layer, otherwise the minimum across the listed embankment materials.
- **Intervention and breach probabilities** — the user-specified probabilities for Nodes 6 and 7.

## Pool-Dependent Series

Every pool-dependent result is a series over the evaluated pool levels: every supplied headwater value and additional pool levels distributed
between them (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx)). The daylight-pathway series
are produced only when a confining layer may be present (its presence is Yes or Unknown) *and* the daylighting exit is distinct from the toe
exit. When the berm toe is itself the daylighting exit — a berm over a confirmed layer that reaches or clears its downstream extent, or overlies
a confirmed layer of unknown extent — there are no daylight series and the toe series carry the governing curve (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).

**Hydraulic series:**

- **Pool levels** — the headwater elevations at which the event tree is evaluated.
- **Tailwater** — the tailwater at each pool level, interpolated between the supplied pairs, and blank wherever no tailwater exists.
- **Toe control level** — the downstream control level used by the toe progression pathway: the higher of the tailwater and the toe reference
  elevation.
- **Daylight control level** — the downstream control level used by the blowout, heave, and daylight-progression analyses: the higher of the
  tailwater and the daylight reference elevation.

**Toe pathway series:**

- **Unfiltered-exit probability** — Node 2: the defect–blowout composition with its overrides applied; 1.0 at every pool when no confining layer
  is present.
- **Blowout probability** — the blowout probability alone, before it is combined with the defect probability, so the two contributions to
  Node 2 can be compared; produced only when a confining layer may be present.
- **Initiation probability** — Node 3: the heave probability with the berm and trigger-elevation overrides applied; 1.0 at every pool when no
  confining layer is present.
- **Progression probability** — Node 5: the mean probability of advancement over the toe path, non-decreasing with pool level.
- **SRP** — the product of the seven nodes on the toe pathway.

**Daylight pathway series:**

- **Unfiltered-exit probability** — Node 2: 1.0 at every pool level.
- **Initiation probability** — Node 3: 1.0 at every pool level.
- **Progression probability** — Node 5: the mean probability of advancement over the longer daylight path, non-decreasing with pool level.
- **SRP** — the product of the seven nodes on the daylight pathway.

**Governing series:**

- **Governing SRP** — the system response curve: the larger of the toe and daylight SRP at each pool level, equal to the toe SRP when no
  daylight pathway is evaluated.

## Interpreting the System Response Curve

The governing SRP series is the system response curve — the conditional probability of breach by backward erosion piping as a function of pool
level. While the net head does not decrease as the pool rises, the curve is non-decreasing with pool level: the flaw, roof-support,
intervention, and breach nodes are constant; the blowout and heave probabilities rise with net head; and the progression node is explicitly
constrained to be non-decreasing. Where the tailwater rises faster than the headwater, the net head can fall and the blowout and heave
probabilities with it. Pools at or below the governing exit reference elevation carry zero SRP because no net driving head exists there.

### Which Pathway Governs

The governing curve is the larger of the two pathway SRPs at each pool level, and the two pathways differ in opposite directions:

- The **toe pathway** carries unfiltered-exit and initiation probabilities that can be below one, because the confining layer resists both. Its
  progression analysis runs over a path no longer than the daylight path — shorter with no berm, or with a berm inside a confirmed layer's
  known extent — which raises the average applied gradient, and, when the layer is confirmed and the exit stays beneath it, uses the hole-type
  exit that halves the Schmertmann critical gradient.
- The **daylight pathway** carries certain unfiltered exit and initiation, because nothing confines the exit there. Its progression analysis
  runs over the path to the layer's termination with a plane-type exit.

The daylight pathway governs at a pool level when the reduction the confining layer gives the toe pathway through Nodes 2 and 3 outweighs any
progression advantage the toe pathway gains from its shorter path and exit type; otherwise the toe pathway governs. Under a layer of unknown
presence with a berm, the two paths are equal and both exits are plane-type, so where the two exit datums coincide the daylight pathway governs
(see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)). (The path-length and depth
corrections of the Schmertmann method also change with path length, so the progression comparison itself depends on the site.) Which side of that balance a site falls on depends on the
layer thickness band, the unit weights, the downstream extent, and the foundation sand properties, and it can change with pool level. When both
pathways are evaluated, both are produced, so the balance can be read directly from the results. When the berm toe is itself the daylighting
exit, only the toe pathway is evaluated: it *is* the daylighting exit, with certain unfiltered-exit and initiation probabilities over the
berm-extended progression path.

### How Key Inputs Affect the SRP

The effect of each input depends on the site configuration. The following describes the mechanism by which each key input changes the SRP and
the conditions under which it controls the result:

- **Flaw evidence selections** — the flaw probability multiplies the SRP at every pool level, so the effect of the geologic environment, the
  strength of evidence, and the observed-seepage selections on the flaw likelihood scales the whole curve by the same factor, whatever the other
  inputs. A sand-boil or concentrated-seepage observation also enables the seepage-through-layer and trigger-elevation inputs, which can set
  Nodes 2 and 3 to certainty and so change the curve beyond that factor (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).
- **Confining-layer thickness category** — thickness enters the defect probability (pool-independent, and rising steeply as the layer thins)
  and the vertical exit gradient in the blowout and heave analyses (a thinner layer gives a larger gradient for the same head). It affects the
  toe pathway only, and only where a confining layer may be present. Where no layer is present, or where a berm daylights the exit over a
  confirmed layer, Nodes 2 and 3 are certain and thickness has no effect. Under a layer of unknown presence with a berm, Node 2 is certain but
  Node 3 stays at the heave probability (unless the berm reaches or clears a supplied extent), so thickness still affects it.
- **Downstream confining-layer extent** — with no berm, a longer downstream path $L_3$ leaves a larger share of the net head at the toe
  (raising the toe blowout and heave probabilities) and lengthens the daylight progression path (lowering the daylight progression
  probability). With a berm ending inside a confirmed layer's known extent, the blowout and heave are evaluated at the offset exit point,
  where the head is the net head times the berm length divided by the total path length, so a longer extent lowers them. When no berm is
  present and the extent or the layer's presence is unknown, the 1 to 5 embankment-height default spreads these effects across a wide range.
  With a berm, the extent also decides which exit treatment applies (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).
- **Relative density category** — denser sand raises the Schmertmann critical gradient through the density correction and lowers the
  progression probability on both pathways. It has little effect at pool levels where the progression probability is already close to 0 or 1.
- **Coefficient of uniformity, effective grain size, and fines content** — these set the base critical gradient, the grain-size correction,
  and the fines-content correction of the Schmertmann analysis, and they act on Node 5 of both pathways in the same way as relative density.
- **Embankment materials** — when the confining layer is not confirmed, the minimum roof-support probability multiplies the SRP at every pool
  level, so a single listed material with a low roof-support probability lowers the whole curve by that factor. With a confirmed layer, the
  materials have no effect.
- **Reference elevations** — the toe and daylight ground surfaces set the pool level below which there is no net head, and so position the
  low-pool end of the curve.

### Checking the Result

The documented method implies several relationships that every result must satisfy. A result that breaks one of the identities below
indicates a computation error in the model, not a property of the site:

- **Product of nodes** — at each pool level, each pathway SRP equals the product of its seven node probabilities, all of which are reported.
- **Governing value** — the governing SRP equals the larger of the toe and daylight SRP at each pool level, or the toe SRP when no daylight
  pathway is evaluated.
- **Upper bound** — no SRP exceeds the product of the pool-independent nodes: the flaw, roof-support, unsuccessful-intervention, and breach
  probabilities.
- **Unfiltered-exit floor** — on the toe pathway, the unfiltered-exit probability is never less than either the defect probability or the
  blowout probability.
- **Certain nodes** — on the daylight pathway, the unfiltered-exit and initiation probabilities are 1.0 at every pool level; on the toe pathway
  they are 1.0 at every pool level when the confining-layer presence is No.
- **Zero-head threshold** — each pathway's SRP is zero at pool levels where its progression analysis has no net head: at or below the higher
  of the tailwater and the toe reference elevation for the toe pathway, and of the tailwater and the daylight reference elevation for the
  daylight pathway.
- **Monotonic progression** — the progression probabilities never decrease with pool level.
- **Trigger elevation** — where a trigger elevation is supplied, the toe initiation probability is 1.0 at every pool level at or above it.

Two further checks follow from the method and help judge whether a result is reasonable for the site:

- **Flaw ceiling** — the governing SRP never exceeds the flaw probability, so a curve that approaches the flaw probability at high pools means
  every other node is close to certain there; the analyst can confirm that this matches the site's confining layer, materials, and berm.
- **Low-pool unfiltered exit** — for a confirmed layer with no berm, the blowout probability falls toward zero as the net head falls, so at low
  pools the toe unfiltered-exit probability should approach the defect probability; a much larger value at low pools points to a forcing
  condition (for example, observed seepage through the layer) that the analyst can confirm.

### Screening Versus Detailed Analysis

A high screening SRP does not by itself indicate a high risk of failure — it indicates that the structure warrants analysis with the full
toolbox models, where:

- the blanket-theory case matching the actual stratigraphy can be selected from all eight configurations (see the
  [General Overview](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/general-overview) of the BEP Initiation manual);
- site-specific blanket geometry replaces the screening defaults and unknown-value ranges;
- the anisotropy and underlayer corrections (see
  [Field Horizontal Critical Gradient](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#field-horizontal-critical-gradient)) and the pipe-inclination correction (see
  [Pipe Path Inclination](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#pipe-path-inclination)) can be applied to the Schmertmann analysis;
- the Sellmeijer method is available as an alternative progression formulation (see
  [Sellmeijer](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/sellmeijer)); and
- the flaw judgment can be refined by targeted subsurface investigation rather than screening-level evidence categories.

A low screening SRP provides reasonable confidence that backward erosion piping is not a critical concern at the screening level, subject to
the assumptions and limitations documented in this reference.

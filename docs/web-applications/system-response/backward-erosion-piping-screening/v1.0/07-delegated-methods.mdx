# Delegated Methods

The screening model hands two of its event-tree calculations to established analysis methods, each documented in a dedicated technical manual:

- **Blanket theory** evaluates the blowout and heave probabilities behind Node 2 (Flaw: Unfiltered Exit) and Node 3 (Initiation) on the toe
  pathway.
- **The Schmertmann critical-gradient method** evaluates the probability of pipe advancement behind Node 5 (Progression: Hydraulic Condition) on
  both pathways.

The theory of each method lives in its manual. Each section below states the node the method serves and the question it answers there, links
the chapter of the manual that covers it, and then documents only what the screening model supplies, what it uses from the result, and every
screening-level adjustment relative to the manual. The flaw assessment (Node 1) and the defect-survival relationship (part of Node 2) are the
screening model's own calculations, documented in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx) and [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx).

## Blanket Theory — Blowout and Heave

**Nodes served:** Node 2 (Flaw: Unfiltered Exit) and Node 3 (Initiation), on the toe pathway, wherever a confining layer may be present and the
toe exit remains beneath it.

**Questions answered:** At Node 2, is the uplift pressure at the base of the confining layer large enough to rupture the layer (blowout), opening
an unfiltered exit? At Node 3, is the vertical gradient through a defect in the layer large enough to fluidize the underlying sand (heave),
starting erosion at the exit? Both questions compare the vertical exit gradient through the layer with a critical gradient set by a saturated
unit weight, which is what blanket theory computes <Citation citationKey="USACE1956" form="parenthetical" />.

### Blanket-Theory Configuration

The model evaluates one of two blanket-theory configurations, selected from the upstream seepage barrier by the rule documented in
[Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx#blanket-theory-configuration-selection):

- **Case 2** — impervious top strata on both the riverside and the landside, used when an upstream seepage barrier is credited with an
  upstream path length. The theory,
  including the exit head at the landside toe and at a given distance beyond it, is in
  [Blanket Theory Case 2](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/blanket-theory-case-2)
  of the BEP Initiation manual.
- **Case 4** — no riverside top stratum and an impervious landside top stratum, used otherwise. The theory, including the effective entrance
  length of $0.43\,d$ for an open riverside entrance, is in
  [Blanket Theory Case 4](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/blanket-theory-case-4)
  of the BEP Initiation manual.

### Blowout and Heave Evaluations

The model evaluates the selected configuration **twice with identical sampled realizations** of the foundation thickness, confining-layer
thickness, and downstream path length:

- **Blowout** — the critical gradient is computed from the *confining layer's* saturated unit weight $\gamma_{bl}$, representing rupture
  of the layer against its buoyant weight. The result feeds Node 2.
- **Heave** — the critical gradient is computed from the *foundation sand's* saturated unit weight $\gamma_{sand}$, representing
  fluidization of the sand column at a defect through the layer. The result feeds Node 3.

Because the two evaluations share their sampled realizations, the blowout and heave probabilities differ only through the unit weight entering
the critical gradient.

### Governing Relationships

Within each Monte Carlo realization, and at each pool level with net driving head $\Delta H$ above the exit level $z_{ex}$ (both defined in
[Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)):

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-19" equation="h_o = \Delta H \cdot \frac{L_3}{L_{total}}" />

<Equation
  equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-20"
  equation="h_x = \begin{cases} h_o \cdot \dfrac{L_3 - x}{L_3} & x < L_3 \\[6pt] 0 & x \geq L_3 \end{cases}"
/>

The head in the sand beneath the layer at the evaluation point is $\min(h, z_{ex}) + h_x$, and the net head across the layer, $h_{net}$, is that head
less the water standing on the layer there (<EquationReference equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-10" />). The gradient and factor of safety follow:

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-21" equation="i_{cv} = \frac{\gamma_{sat} - \gamma_w}{\gamma_w}" />

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-22" equation="i_v = \frac{h_{net}}{d_{bl}}, \qquad FS = \frac{i_{cv}}{i_v}" />

where $h_o$ is the excess head above the exit level at the embankment toe, from flow beneath the layer (ft); $L_3$ and $L_{total}$ are the
downstream and total seepage path lengths (ft), with $L_3$ sampled per realization when no berm is present and the extent or the layer's
presence is unknown; $h_x$ is the excess head above the exit level at the exit evaluation point, a distance $x$ (ft) downstream of the toe
(ft); $h_{net}$ is the net head across the confining layer at the exit evaluation point (ft); $i_{cv}$ is the critical vertical gradient;
$\gamma_{sat}$ is the saturated unit weight ($\gamma_{bl}$ for blowout, $\gamma_{sand}$ for heave) (lb/ft³); $\gamma_w$ is the unit weight of
water, 62.4 lb/ft³; $i_v$ is the vertical exit gradient through the confining layer; $d_{bl}$ is the sampled confining-layer thickness (ft);
and $FS$ is the factor of safety against the vertical gradient.

The blowout or heave probability at each pool level is the fraction of Monte Carlo realizations in which $FS < 1$. Realizations with no
upward gradient (no positive net head across the layer) contribute no failure.

### Screening-Level Adjustments

Relative to the blanket-theory chapters of the BEP Initiation manual, the screening model fixes the following:

- **Finite-blanket head loss always applies** — the excess head at the toe is always reduced by the path-length ratio
  (<EquationReference equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-19" />). The idealization of an infinite downstream blanket carrying the full net head at
  the toe is not used.
- **The exit and the evaluation point may sit at different ground elevations** — the blanket-theory chapters measure the exit head against a
  single landside ground surface. The screening model measures the driving head to the exit level at the daylighting exit and the net head
  against the water standing on the layer at the evaluation point, so a daylighting exit above the toe adds the difference to the uplift and
  one below the toe reduces it. With a pool below the exit level, no seepage flows and the foundation sand stands at pool level beneath the
  layer. Where the two ground surfaces coincide, the net head equals the blanket-theory excess head $h_x$.
- **Downstream blanket length follows the resolved downstream path** — $L_3$ enters as the supplied downstream extent, or is drawn per
  realization from the wide default range when no berm is present and the extent or the layer's presence is unknown
  (see [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)).
- **Confining-layer thickness comes from the category band** — the sampled $d_{bl}$ follows the qualitative thickness category (or the custom
  distribution) documented in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx).
- **The unit weight is mechanism-specific** — $\gamma_{bl}$ for blowout and $\gamma_{sand}$ for heave, each defaulting per
  [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx).
- **Foundation permeability does not influence the screening result** — hydraulic conductivity affects only the seepage-quantity results of
  blanket theory, which the screening model does not use; the exit-gradient computations are independent of it.

## Schmertmann Method — Progression

**Node served:** Node 5 (Progression: Hydraulic Condition), on the toe pathway and, when it is evaluated, the daylight pathway.

**Question answered:** Once erosion has initiated at the exit and a roof is available, is the average hydraulic gradient along the seepage path
large enough to keep advancing the pipe upstream through the foundation sand until it reaches the reservoir? The Schmertmann
method <Citation citationKey="Schmertmann2000" form="parenthetical" /> answers it by comparing the average applied gradient with a field critical gradient derived from
laboratory piping tests and corrected for site conditions. The complete method is documented in the
[Schmertmann](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann) chapter of the BEP
Progression manual; the subsections below link the part of that chapter each step follows.

### Base Critical Gradient

The base critical gradient derives from laboratory piping-model tests, represented as a family of quantile curves linear in the coefficient of
uniformity (see
[Laboratory Horizontal Critical Gradient](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#laboratory-horizontal-critical-gradient)):

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-23" equation="i_{pmt}(\xi) = m(\xi)\,C_u + b(\xi)" />

where $i_{pmt}(\xi)$ is the laboratory critical gradient at cumulative probability $\xi$, $C_u$ is the sampled coefficient of uniformity of
the foundation sand, and $m(\xi)$ and $b(\xi)$ are the quantile regression coefficients listed in
<TableReference tableKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-table-20" />. The screening model uses these laboratory relationships without modification.

The quantile family represents the uncertainty in the laboratory critical gradient. <Citation citationKey="Schmertmann2000" form="narrative" />
corrected the results of more than 100 laboratory flume tests to common reference conditions and proposed a single default line for use when no
flume tests are available, but because many of the plotted points are averages of several tests, the spread of the underlying data is not
evident from that line. <Citation citationKey="Robbins2016" form="narrative" /> compiled the corrected data and fit quantile regression
lines from the 10 percent to the 90 percent quantile, and <Citation citationKey="Robbins2020" form="narrative" /> added 0 percent
and 100 percent bounding lines by linear extrapolation of the slopes and intercepts of the regression lines. The screening model uses the
complete family, from the 0 percent to the 100 percent line (<FigReference figKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-fig-4" />).

### Field Critical Gradient and Correction Factors

The field critical gradient scales the laboratory gradient by the correction factors (see
[Field Horizontal Critical Gradient](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#field-horizontal-critical-gradient)):

<Equation
  equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-24"
  equation="i_{ch}(\xi) = i_{pmt}(\xi) \cdot \frac{C_D \, C_L \, C_S \, C_K \, C_\gamma \, C_Z}{C_R} \cdot C_\alpha \cdot C_{OF}"
/>

where $i_{ch}(\xi)$ is the field critical gradient at cumulative probability $\xi$ and the remaining symbols are the correction factors of
<TableReference tableKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-table-21" />. In the screening configuration, five factors remain active and four are fixed at unity.

The active factors take their values from the screening inputs:

- $C_D$ — from the sampled foundation thickness $d$ and the pathway's seepage path length
- $C_L$ — from the pathway's seepage path length
- $C_S$ — from the sampled effective grain size $D_{10}$
- $C_\gamma$ — from the sampled relative density $D_r$ (per the category mapping or custom distribution in
  [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx))
- $C_{OF}$ — the product of the fines-content correction and the exit-geometry factor, given below

The four geometric and material factors follow the <Citation citationKey="Schmertmann2000" form="narrative" /> relationships, which
normalize each field condition to the reference test conditions (seepage path length 5 ft, piping-layer depth to length ratio 0.2, $D_{10}$ of
0.20 mm, and relative density of 60 percent):

<Equation
  equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-25"
  equation="C_D = \frac{\left(d/L\right)^{0.2/\left[(d/L)^2 - 1\right]}}{0.2^{\,0.2/\left(0.2^2 - 1\right)}}"
/>

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-26" equation="C_L = \left(\frac{5}{L}\right)^{0.2}" />

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-27" equation="C_S = \left(\frac{D_{10}}{0.2}\right)^{0.2}" />

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-28" equation="C_\gamma = 1 + 0.4\left(\frac{D_r}{100} - 0.6\right)" />

where $C_D$, $C_L$, $C_S$, and $C_\gamma$ are the depth, path-length, grain-size, and density corrections; $d$ is the sampled foundation sand
thickness (ft); $L$ is the seepage path length of the pathway under evaluation, $L_{prog,toe}$ or $L_{prog,day}$ (ft), used without
transformation because the anisotropy correction is not applied; $D_{10}$ is the sampled effective grain size (mm); and $D_r$ is the sampled
relative density (percent).

The fixed factors reflect information that is rarely available at the screening stage:

- $C_K$ — taken as 1.0, because the effect of anisotropy on the critical gradient is not well understood. This is the value the BEP
  Progression manual's anisotropy relationship, $C_K = (1.5/R_{kf})^{0.5}$, gives for the Schmertmann reference conductivity ratio
  $R_{kf} = k_h/k_v$ of 1.5; the seepage path length is not transformed for anisotropy (assumption 12 in
  [Assumptions and Limitations](./08-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx))
- $C_Z$ — taken as 1.0, the value for no higher-permeability underlayer; no stratum beneath the aquifer is characterized (assumption 13)
- $C_\alpha$ — the pipe path is fixed horizontal (inclination 0°), so no inclination correction applies (see
  [Pipe Path Inclination](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#pipe-path-inclination))
- $C_R$ — the axis is treated as straight, as in the BEP Progression manual

### Exit Geometry and Fines Corrections

The overall field correction is:

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-29" equation="C_{OF} = C_{FC} \cdot C_{3d}" />

where $C_{OF}$ is the overall field correction, $C_{FC}$ is the fines-content correction, and $C_{3d}$ is the exit-geometry factor.

The exit-geometry factor $C_{3d}$ distinguishes concentrated (hole-type) exits from distributed (plane-type) exits. Van Beek et
al. (2015) <Citation citationKey="vanBeek2015" /> found that three-dimensional flow converging toward a single exit point produces critical
gradients roughly half those predicted for the two-dimensional configuration. Their comparison was made against the Dutch (Sellmeijer) piping
model, and the BEP Progression manual notes that the reduction was not developed and calibrated for the Schmertmann method; the screening model
applies it to the Schmertmann critical gradient by engineering judgment, as the manual does, so a hole-type exit takes $C_{3d} = 0.5$ and a
plane-type exit $C_{3d} = 1.0$. The screening model assigns the exit type as follows:

- **Toe pathway** — hole-type ($C_{3d} = 0.5$) when the confining layer is confirmed, *unless* a berm is present and either the layer's
  downstream extent is unknown or the berm reaches or clears that extent (the exit then daylights beyond the layer and is plane-type). When
  the layer's presence is No or Unknown, the toe exit is plane-type (assumption 18 in
  [Assumptions and Limitations](./08-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)).
- **Daylight pathway** — always plane-type ($C_{3d} = 1.0$); the exit is an exposed sand surface.

The hole-type factor for a confined toe exit is conservative. It represents seepage converging on a single defect in the layer; where many boils
form, the flow converging on any one pipe head is reduced, and so is the reduction in the critical gradient.

The fines-content correction is a step function of the foundation sand's fines content:

<Equation
  equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-30"
  equation="C_{FC} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{fines} < 5\% \\[4pt] 1.4 & \text{fines} \geq 5\% \end{cases}"
/>

where $C_{FC}$ is the fines-content correction. The higher factor applies at exactly 5 percent fines and above; a fines content below
5 percent takes no correction.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-16 — Engineering Basis]
The fines-content correction applied in the Schmertmann analysis — a step from 1.0 to 1.4, with the higher factor applying at exactly 5 percent
fines and above — requires an engineering basis. Provide the source or engineering-judgment attribution for the step form, the 5 percent
threshold, and the 1.4 value, and add the term to the Schmertmann chapter of the BEP Progression manual, which currently omits it.
:::

### Probability of Advancement

At each pool level, the applied gradient is the net head over the pathway's seepage path length (see
[Likelihood of Backward Erosion Piping Progression](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#likelihood-of-backward-erosion-piping-progression)):

<Equation equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-31" equation="i_{adv}(h) = \frac{\Delta H(h)}{L_{prog}}" />

where $i_{adv}(h)$ is the average horizontal gradient at headwater $h$, $\Delta H(h)$ is the net driving head of
<EquationReference equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-4" /> (ft), and $L_{prog}$ is the toe or daylight progression path
length (ft) from [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx) (sampled per realization on the daylight pathway when no berm is
present and the downstream extent or the layer's presence is unknown).

This is the global, or average, horizontal gradient in the foundation sand along the potential pipe path: the net head across the path divided
by its length, evaluated for the ambient seepage conditions before a pipe forms (<FigReference figKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-fig-5" />). The Schmertmann
method compares the field critical gradient with this pre-pipe gradient rather than with the gradient near an advancing pipe tip.

<Figure
  figKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-fig-5"
  src="/figures/web-applications/system-response/backward-erosion-piping-screening/v1.0/figures/average-horizontal-gradient-geometry.png"
  alt="Seepage model cross-section of an embankment on a sand foundation with a landside ditch exit, showing the net head difference and the horizontal path length between the riverside entrance and the exit."
  caption="Geometry of the average horizontal gradient: the net head difference divided by the horizontal seepage path length to the exit."
/>

Within each realization, the correction factors scale the entire family of laboratory quantile curves
(<EquationReference equationKey="backward-erosion-piping-screening-eq-24" />), and the probability of advancement is the cumulative probability that the corrected
critical gradient is at or below the applied gradient — interpolated from the scaled quantile family, and limited to 0 and 1 beyond its ends.
A pool producing no positive net head yields no probability of advancement.

The Node 5 probability at each pool level is the mean probability of advancement across the Monte Carlo realizations, then constrained to be
non-decreasing with pool level as described in [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx).

# Delegated Methods

The screening model hands two of its event-tree calculations to established analysis methods, each documented in a dedicated technical manual:

- **Blanket theory** evaluates the blowout and heave probabilities behind Node 2 (Flaw: Unfiltered Exit) and Node 3 (Initiation) on the toe
  pathway.
- **The Schmertmann critical-gradient method** evaluates the probability of pipe advancement behind Node 5 (Progression: Hydraulic Condition) on
  both pathways.

The theory of each method lives in its manual. Each section below states the node the method serves and the question it answers there, links
the chapter of the manual that covers it, and then documents only what the screening model supplies, what it uses from the result, and every
screening-level adjustment relative to the manual. The flaw assessment (Node 1) and the defect-survival relationship (part of Node 2) are the
screening model's own calculations, documented in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx) and [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx).

## Blanket Theory — Blowout and Heave

**Nodes served:** Node 2 (Flaw: Unfiltered Exit) and Node 3 (Initiation), on the toe pathway, wherever a confining layer may be present and the
toe exit remains beneath it.

**Questions answered:** At Node 2, is the uplift pressure at the base of the confining layer large enough to rupture the layer (blowout), opening
an unfiltered exit? At Node 3, is the vertical gradient through a defect in the layer large enough to fluidize the underlying sand (heave),
starting erosion at the exit? Both questions compare the vertical exit gradient through the layer with a critical gradient set by a saturated
unit weight, which is what blanket theory computes<Citation citationKey="USACE1956" />.

### Blanket-theory configuration

The model evaluates one of two blanket-theory configurations, selected from the upstream seepage barrier by the rule documented in
[Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx#blanket-theory-configuration-selection):

- **Case 2** — impervious top strata on both the riverside and the landside, used when an upstream seepage barrier is present. The theory,
  including the exit head at the landside toe and at a given distance beyond it, is in
  [Blanket Theory Case 2](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/blanket-theory-case-2)
  of the BEP Initiation manual.
- **Case 4** — no riverside top stratum and an impervious landside top stratum, used otherwise. The theory, including the effective entrance
  length of $0.43\,d$ for an open riverside entrance, is in
  [Blanket Theory Case 4](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/blanket-theory-case-4)
  of the BEP Initiation manual.

### Blowout and heave evaluations

The model evaluates the selected configuration **twice with identical sampled realizations** of the foundation thickness, confining-layer
thickness, and downstream path length:

- **Blowout** — the critical gradient is computed from the *confining layer's* saturated unit weight $\gamma_{bl}$, representing rupture
  of the layer against its buoyant weight. The result feeds Node 2.
- **Heave** — the critical gradient is computed from the *foundation sand's* saturated unit weight $\gamma_{sand}$, representing
  fluidization of the sand column at a defect through the layer. The result feeds Node 3.

Because the two evaluations share their sampled realizations, the blowout and heave probabilities differ only through the unit weight entering
the critical gradient.

### Governing relationships

Within each Monte Carlo realization, and at each pool level with net head $H$ (defined in
[Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)):

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-15" equation="h_o = H \cdot \frac{L_3}{L_{total}}" />

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-16"
  equation="h_x = \begin{cases} h_o \cdot \dfrac{L_3 - x}{L_3} & x < L_3 \\[6pt] 0 & x \geq L_3 \end{cases}"
/>

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-17" equation="i_{cv} = \frac{\gamma_{sat} - \gamma_w}{\gamma_w}" />

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-18" equation="i_v = \frac{h_x}{z_{bl}}, \qquad FS = \frac{i_{cv}}{i_v}" />

where:

> $h_o$ = excess head above the exit datum at the embankment toe  
> $h_x$ = excess head at the exit evaluation point, offset $x$ from the toe  
> $L_3$, $L_{total}$ = downstream and total seepage path lengths ($L_3$ sampled per realization when no berm is present and the extent or the layer's presence is unknown)  
> $i_{cv}$ = critical vertical gradient  
> $\gamma_{sat}$ = saturated unit weight ($\gamma_{bl}$ for blowout, $\gamma_{sand}$ for heave)  
> $\gamma_w$ = unit weight of water (62.4 pcf)  
> $i_v$ = vertical exit gradient through the confining layer  
> $z_{bl}$ = sampled confining-layer thickness  
> $FS$ = factor of safety against the vertical gradient

The blowout or heave probability at each pool level is the fraction of Monte Carlo realizations in which $FS < 1$. Realizations with no exit
gradient (zero net head) contribute no failure.

### Screening-level adjustments

Relative to the blanket-theory chapters of the BEP Initiation manual, the screening model fixes the following:

- **Finite-blanket head loss always applies** — the excess head at the toe is always reduced by the path-length ratio
  (<EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-15" />). The idealization of an infinite downstream blanket carrying the full net head at
  the toe is not used.
- **Downstream blanket length follows the resolved downstream path** — $L_3$ enters as the supplied downstream extent, or is drawn per
  realization from the wide default range when no berm is present and the extent or the layer's presence is unknown
  (see [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)).
- **Confining-layer thickness comes from the category band** — the sampled $z_{bl}$ follows the qualitative thickness category (or the custom
  distribution) documented in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx).
- **The unit weight is mechanism-specific** — $\gamma_{bl}$ for blowout and $\gamma_{sand}$ for heave, each defaulting per
  [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx).
- **Foundation permeability does not influence the screening result** — hydraulic conductivity affects only the seepage-quantity results of
  blanket theory, which the screening model does not use; the exit-gradient computations are independent of it.

## Schmertmann Method — Progression

**Node served:** Node 5 (Progression: Hydraulic Condition), on the toe pathway and, when it is evaluated, the daylight pathway.

**Question answered:** Once erosion has initiated at the exit and a roof is available, is the average hydraulic gradient along the seepage path
large enough to keep advancing the pipe upstream through the foundation sand until it reaches the reservoir? The Schmertmann
method<Citation citationKey="Schmertmann2000" /> answers it by comparing the average applied gradient with a field critical gradient derived from
laboratory piping tests and corrected for site conditions. The complete method is documented in the
[Schmertmann](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann) chapter of the BEP
Progression manual; the subsections below link the part of that chapter each step follows.

### Base critical gradient

The base critical gradient derives from laboratory piping-model tests, represented as a family of quantile curves linear in the coefficient of
uniformity (see
[Laboratory Horizontal Critical Gradient](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#laboratory-horizontal-critical-gradient)):

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-19" equation="i_{pmt}(\xi) = m(\xi)\,C_u + b(\xi)" />

where $i_{pmt}(\xi)$ is the laboratory critical gradient at cumulative probability $\xi$, and $m(\xi)$ and $b(\xi)$ are the quantile regression
coefficients from the BEP Progression manual. The screening model uses these laboratory relationships without modification.

### Field critical gradient and correction factors

The field critical gradient scales the laboratory gradient by the correction factors (see
[Field Horizontal Critical Gradient](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#field-horizontal-critical-gradient)):

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-20"
  equation="i_{ch}(\xi) = i_{pmt}(\xi) \cdot \frac{C_D \, C_L \, C_S \, C_K \, C_\gamma \, C_Z}{C_R} \cdot C_\alpha \cdot C_{OF}"
/>

In the screening configuration, five factors remain active and four are fixed at unity
(<TableReference tableKey="bep-screening-table-22" />).

The active factors take their values from the screening inputs:

- $C_D$ — from the sampled foundation thickness $d$ and the pathway's seepage path length
- $C_L$ — from the pathway's seepage path length
- $C_S$ — from the sampled effective grain size $D_{10}$
- $C_\gamma$ — from the sampled relative density $D_r$ (per the category mapping or custom distribution in
  [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx))
- $C_{OF}$ — the product of the fines-content correction and the exit-geometry factor, given below

The fixed factors reflect information that is rarely available at the screening stage:

- $C_K$ — taken as 1.0, which the BEP Progression manual's anisotropy relationship, $C_K = (1.5/R_{kf})^{0.5}$, gives for a reference
  conductivity ratio $R_{kf} = k_h/k_v$ of 1.5; the seepage path length is not transformed for anisotropy (assumption 12 in
  [Assumptions and Limitations](./08-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx))
- $C_Z$ — taken as 1.0, the value for no higher-permeability underlayer; no stratum beneath the aquifer is characterized (assumption 13)
- $C_\alpha$ — the pipe path is fixed horizontal (inclination 0°), so no inclination correction applies (see
  [Pipe Path Inclination](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#pipe-path-inclination))
- $C_R$ — the axis is treated as straight, as in the BEP Progression manual

### Exit geometry and fines corrections

The overall field correction is:

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-21" equation="C_{OF} = C_{FC} \cdot C_{3d}" />

The exit-geometry factor $C_{3d}$ distinguishes concentrated (hole-type) exits from distributed (plane-type) exits. Van Beek et
al. (2015)<Citation citationKey="vanBeek2015" /> found that three-dimensional flow converging toward a single exit point produces critical
gradients roughly half those predicted for the two-dimensional configuration. Their comparison was made against the Dutch (Sellmeijer) piping
model, and the BEP Progression manual notes that the reduction was not developed and calibrated for the Schmertmann method; the screening model
applies it to the Schmertmann critical gradient by engineering judgment, as the manual does, so a hole-type exit takes $C_{3d} = 0.5$ and a
plane-type exit $C_{3d} = 1.0$. The screening model assigns the exit type as follows:

- **Toe pathway** — hole-type ($C_{3d} = 0.5$) when the confining layer is confirmed, *unless* a berm is present and either the layer's
  downstream extent is unknown or the berm reaches or clears that extent (the exit then daylights beyond the layer and is plane-type). When
  the layer's presence is No or Unknown, the toe exit is plane-type (assumption 18 in
  [Assumptions and Limitations](./08-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx)).
- **Daylight pathway** — always plane-type ($C_{3d} = 1.0$); the exit is an exposed sand surface.

The fines-content correction is a step function of the foundation sand's fines content:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-22"
  equation="C_{FC} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{fines} < 5\% \\[4pt] 1.4 & \text{fines} \geq 5\% \end{cases}"
/>

The higher factor applies at exactly 5% fines and above; a fines content below 5% takes no correction.

and above — requires an engineering basis. Provide the source or engineering-judgment attribution for the step form, the 5% threshold, and the

### Probability of advancement

At each pool level, the applied gradient is the net head over the pathway's seepage path length (see
[Likelihood of Backward Erosion Piping Progression](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann#likelihood-of-backward-erosion-piping-progression)):

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-23" equation="i_{adv}(h) = \frac{H(h)}{L_{prog}}" />

where $L_{prog}$ is the toe or daylight progression path length from
[Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx) (sampled per realization on the daylight pathway when no berm is
present and the downstream extent or the layer's presence is unknown). Within each realization, the correction factors scale the entire family of laboratory quantile curves
(<EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-20" />), and the probability of advancement is the cumulative probability that the corrected
critical gradient is at or below the applied gradient — interpolated from the scaled quantile family, and limited to 0 and 1 beyond its ends.
A pool producing no positive net head yields no probability of advancement.

The Node 5 probability at each pool level is the mean probability of advancement across the Monte Carlo realizations, then constrained to be
non-decreasing with pool level as described in [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx).

# Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths

Before the event tree is assembled, the model resolves the hydraulic loading and the seepage-path geometry from the site inputs: the downstream
reference elevations that control net driving head, the treatment of tailwater, the three seepage path components $L_1$, $L_2$, and $L_3$, the
selection between the two blanket-theory configurations, the location of the exit evaluation point, and the head that acts across the confining
layer there. This chapter documents each resolution rule in calculation order.

## Downstream Reference Elevations

Two reference elevations define the downstream boundary of the head computations:

- **Toe reference elevation** $E_{toe,ref}$ — the controlling ground surface at the downstream limit of the embankment section. When a seepage
  berm is present and its toe ground-surface elevation is specified, the berm toe controls; otherwise the downstream embankment toe controls
  (the ground beneath a berm is then assumed flat, so the berm toe shares the embankment toe elevation).

- **Daylight reference elevation** $E_{day,ref}$ — the controlling ground surface at the downstream termination of the confining layer, where
  the foundation sand daylights. When the daylight ground-surface elevation is supplied, it controls; otherwise the daylight exit is assumed to
  share the toe reference elevation. The daylight ground may sit above or below the embankment toe. One exception applies: when the daylighting
  exit is the berm toe itself — a berm that reaches or clears a confirmed layer's downstream extent, overlies a confirmed layer of unknown
  extent, or overlies a layer of unknown presence — the toe reference is the exit's datum, and a separately supplied daylight ground-surface
  elevation would describe the same location a second time. The supplied value is then not used.

The two elevations play different roles. Seepage beneath the confining layer leaves the foundation sand at the daylighting exit, so the
daylight reference elevation sets the exit level that controls the driving head for the blowout, heave, and daylight progression analyses.
The toe reference elevation sets the level of the water standing on the confining layer at the exit evaluation point, against which the
blowout and heave net head is measured (see [Head Beneath the Confining Layer](#head-beneath-the-confining-layer)), and it controls the toe
progression pathway. Both elevations therefore enter the blowout and heave analyses whenever they differ.

## Tailwater and Net Driving Head

At each pool level, the downstream control level is the higher of the tailwater and the applicable reference elevation. Where no tailwater
exists — the tailwater series was omitted, or an individual stage has no tailwater — the control level falls directly to the reference
elevation. The net driving head is then:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-4"
  equation="H(h) = \begin{cases} 0 & h < E_{ref} \\[4pt] \max\bigl(h - \max(t,\, E_{ref}),\; 0\bigr) & h \geq E_{ref} \end{cases}"
/>

where:

> $h$ = headwater elevation  
> $t$ = tailwater elevation at that pool level (where one exists)  
> $E_{ref}$ = the applicable reference elevation ($E_{day,ref}$ for the blowout, heave, and daylight-progression analyses; $E_{toe,ref}$ for  
> the toe-progression analysis)

A pool at or below the exit reference elevation produces zero net driving head: no seepage flows out of that exit, so the progression analysis
along that pathway is not loaded. Zero driving head does not by itself mean zero load on the confining layer. Where the daylight ground sits
above the toe, a pool between the two elevations fills the foundation sand to pool level and still acts upward on the layer at the toe; the
blowout and heave analyses account for this static case (see [Head Beneath the Confining Layer](#head-beneath-the-confining-layer)).

The control level is evaluated at each supplied headwater–tailwater pair and varies linearly between successive supplied headwater values.
Within any interval between successive supplied headwater values where the supplied tailwater crosses the reference elevation, this linear treatment slightly overstates the
control level (and so slightly understates the net head); the effect is bounded by the change in tailwater across that interval and is noted in
[Assumptions and Limitations](./08-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx).

## Seepage Path Components

Blanket theory idealizes the seepage path in three segments <Citation citationKey="USACE1956" />: the upstream entry length $L_1$ through or
beneath the upstream top stratum, the path $L_2$ beneath the embankment, and the downstream path $L_3$ from the embankment toe to the seepage
exit. The screening model resolves each from the site inputs.

### Upstream Path Length L₁

The upstream path length is resolved by precedence:

1. **Engineered upstream blanket present** — $L_1$ equals the supplied blanket length.
2. **Confining layer confirmed and Very Thin** — $L_1 = 0$. A very thin upstream extension is credited with no head loss.
3. **Confining layer confirmed, impervious, with a supplied upstream extension** — $L_1$ equals the supplied upstream confining-layer
   length.
4. **Otherwise** — $L_1 = 0$. A layer whose upstream extension is No or Unknown, or a layer that is semi-pervious or of unknown permeability,
   is credited with no upstream head loss.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-10 — Engineering Basis]
Two judgments in the upstream-path crediting rules require an engineering basis to state here:

1. A confirmed Very Thin confining layer contributes no upstream path length ($L_1 = 0$), taking precedence over the impervious-extension
   credit. Proposed (unconfirmed): a layer thinner than about 5 ft cannot be relied on to remain intact and carry head loss over its upstream
   extent; confirm or replace.
2. Only a layer characterized as Impervious is credited; Semi-pervious and Unknown permeability contribute nothing.

Provide the citation or engineering-judgment attribution for each.
:::

### Embankment Path Length L₂

The embankment path length $L_2$ is the embankment base width — the crest width plus the horizontal projections of the
upstream and downstream faces (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx)). A downstream seepage berm is not part of $L_2$; its length
extends the downstream path instead.

### Downstream Path Length L₃

The downstream path length $L_3$ — from the embankment toe to the seepage exit — is resolved from the berm and confining-layer inputs
(<TableReference tableKey="bep-screening-table-19" />). A supplied downstream extent enters as its single value; the default for no berm with an
unknown extent or unknown layer presence is the only downstream-path characterization carried as a distribution.

## Blanket-Theory Configuration Selection

The blowout and heave analyses run under one of two blanket-theory configurations, selected from the upstream seepage barrier:

- **Blanket Theory Case 2** — impervious top strata on both the riverside and the landside (see
  [Blanket Theory Case 2](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/blanket-theory-case-2)
  in the BEP Initiation manual).
  Selected whenever the upstream path length is credited ($L_1 > 0$): an engineered upstream blanket is present, or the confining layer is
  confirmed, impervious, not Very Thin, and has a supplied upstream extension length. The total seepage path is:

  <Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-5" equation="L_{total} = L_1 + L_2 + L_3" />

- **Blanket Theory Case 4** — no riverside top stratum, impervious landside top stratum only (see
  [Blanket Theory Case 4](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/blanket-theory-case-4)
  in the BEP Initiation manual).
  Selected otherwise. With an open seepage entrance, the effective entry length is approximated from the foundation thickness:

  <Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-6" equation="L_{total} = 0.43\,d + L_2 + L_3" />

where $d$ is the sampled foundation sand thickness. A confirmed Very Thin layer is credited with $L_1 = 0$ per the crediting rules above, so it
routes to Case 4 and retains the open-entrance length $0.43\,d$; crediting neither an upstream path nor an entrance length would understate the
head loss upstream of the embankment.

## Exit Evaluation Point

The blowout and heave probabilities are evaluated at an exit point along the downstream blanket. By default the evaluation point is the
downstream toe (zero offset), where the excess head is highest. One site condition shifts it: when a berm overlies a confirmed confining layer
whose supplied downstream extent exceeds the berm length — the configuration in which the toe exit remains beneath the layer and the
through-layer defect and blowout mechanisms are retained (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)) — the evaluation point moves to the berm toe,
the first exposed ground beyond the berm:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-7"
  equation="x = L_b"
/>

where:

> $x$ = distance of the exit evaluation point downstream of the embankment toe  
> $L_b$ = berm length

The confining layer then extends $L_3 - x$ beyond the evaluation point, and the excess head available there follows the linear blanket-theory
head profile, reduced by the factor $(L_3 - x)/L_3$ relative to the toe (see [Head Beneath the Confining Layer](#head-beneath-the-confining-layer)).
When the berm instead reaches or clears the layer's downstream extent, no offset arises — the exit daylights at the berm toe and the
confined-exit nodes are set to certainty rather than computed (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).

## Head Beneath the Confining Layer

Blowout and heave depend on the net head across the confining layer at the exit evaluation point: the head in the foundation sand beneath the
layer less the head of the water standing on the layer there. The two heads are referenced to different ground surfaces. The seepage beneath
the layer exits at the daylighting exit, so the exit level is

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-24"
  equation="E = \max(t,\, E_{day,ref}) \qquad T = \max(t,\, E_{check})"
/>

where:

> $E$ = exit level, the control level at the daylighting exit  
> $T$ = level of the water standing on the confining layer at the exit evaluation point  
> $E_{check}$ = ground surface at the exit evaluation point: $E_{toe,ref}$ (the berm-toe ground when a berm is present)

The head in the sand beneath the layer at the evaluation point combines the exit level with the share of the driving head that remains after
the head loss along the path upstream of that point <Citation citationKey="USACE1956" />:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-25"
  equation="h_{under} = \min(h,\, E) + \max(h - E,\, 0)\,\frac{L_3 - x}{L_{total}}"
/>

and the net head across the layer is

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-26" equation="h_{net} = h_{under} - T" />

where $L_{total}$ is the total seepage path of the selected configuration (<EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-5" /> or
<EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-6" />), evaluated per Monte Carlo realization, and $x$ is the evaluation-point distance of
<EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-7" />. A distance $x$ at or beyond $L_3$ leaves no head from flow at the evaluation point.

The first term covers two loading states. When the pool stands above the exit level, seepage flows and $\min(h, E) = E$: the head beneath the
layer is the exit level plus the remaining share of the driving head. When the pool stands at or below the exit level, no seepage flows, the
second term vanishes, and the foundation sand fills to pool level, so $h_{under} = h$. A daylight ground above the toe therefore adds the
difference between the two ground surfaces to the uplift at the toe, and a daylight ground below the toe reduces it. Where the daylight ground
equals the toe ground, <EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-26" /> reduces to the excess head of the blanket-theory toe solution.

The blowout analysis compares the upward gradient $h_{net}/z_{bl}$ with the critical gradient of the confining layer, and the heave analysis
with the critical gradient of the foundation sand (see [Delegated Methods](./07-delegated-methods.mdx)). A realization with no positive net head
does not fail.

## Progression Path Lengths

The Schmertmann progression analysis uses the full geometric path from the upstream limit of the seepage barrier to the exit under evaluation:

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-8" equation="L_{prog,toe} = L_1 + L_2 + L_b" />

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-9" equation="L_{prog,day} = L_1 + L_2 + L_3" />

where the berm length $L_b$ is included only when a berm is present. $L_3$ in the daylight path is sampled per Monte Carlo realization only
when no berm is present and the downstream extent or the layer's presence is unknown (the one-to-five-embankment-height default); otherwise it
enters as a single value. A longer path reduces the average applied gradient. When the presence is Unknown and a berm is present, $L_3 = L_b$,
so the two progression paths are equal.

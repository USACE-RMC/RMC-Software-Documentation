# Model Overview

## Purpose

The BEP screening model computes a system response curve for the backward erosion piping failure mode as a function of headwater elevation.
The system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level represents the conditional probability of dam or levee breach due to backward
erosion piping, given that pool level. The model produces a curve of SRP values across a range of headwater elevations that
characterizes the structure's vulnerability to this failure mode.

Backward erosion piping is the progressive removal of cohesionless foundation soil by seepage, beginning at an unfiltered downstream exit and
eroding backward (upstream) beneath the embankment until a continuous pipe connects the impounded water to the exit. The failure mode requires a
continuous path of erodible material, an unfiltered exit, sufficient gradient to initiate erosion, a soil roof that keeps the developing pipe
open, and sufficient gradient to advance the pipe to the reservoir.

## Intended Use

The model is a screening-level assessment of backward erosion piping risk. It makes simplifying and generally conservative assumptions with two
goals:

- **Preventing false negatives** — The model is designed to avoid underestimating risk at the screening level, so that structures with
  potential BEP concerns are flagged for further analysis.
- **Reducing input burden** — The model requires fewer inputs than the full BEP toolbox models. Qualitative selections (confining-layer
  thickness category, relative density category, geologic environment) substitute for site-specific distributions, and default distributions
  cover material properties that are typically unavailable at the screening stage. Where a site characteristic is genuinely unknown — the
  presence of a confining layer, or the extent of that layer beyond the embankment — the model accepts Unknown as an answer and substitutes a
  deliberately wide characterization rather than requiring the user to guess.

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments. For higher-level analyses, the full BEP Initiation and BEP
Progression toolbox models provide the complete methodologies with full parameterization (see
[Comparison with Detailed Models](#comparison-with-detailed-models)).

## How the Model Works

The screening model executes three stages:

1. **Flaw assessment** — A Bayesian analysis estimates the probability that a continuous path of fine to medium, uniform sand exists in the
   foundation. A prior probability based on the geologic depositional environment is updated with subsurface-data evidence and observed-seepage
   evidence when available. The mean posterior probability populates the first event-tree node and is independent of pool level
   (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).

2. **Hydraulic and seepage-geometry resolution** — The model resolves the downstream reference elevations that control net driving head, the
   upstream seepage path length $L_1$, the embankment base width $L_2$, and the downstream seepage path length $L_3$ from the site inputs, and
   selects the blanket-theory configuration — Case 2 when an upstream seepage barrier is present, Case 4 otherwise
   (see [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)).

3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the model composes seven node probabilities and multiplies them to produce the SRP. Blanket
   theory computes the blowout and heave probabilities feeding the unfiltered-exit and initiation nodes, a defect-survival relationship gives the
   probability that a pre-existing defect already penetrates the confining layer, and the Schmertmann critical-gradient method computes the
   probability of pipe progression. The blanket-theory and Schmertmann analyses are evaluated by Monte Carlo simulation over the uncertain
   material and geometric inputs (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx) and [Delegated Methods](./07-delegated-methods.mdx)).

## Physics Basis

The model combines the following engineering methods:

- **Bayesian updating in log-odds form** for the probability of a continuous foundation flaw, combining a geology-based prior with likelihood
  ratios for the site evidence (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx); the source of the formulation is requested at marker IN-18)
- **Blanket theory** for the excess head beneath the downstream confining layer and the vertical exit gradient through it, from which the
  blowout and heave probabilities follow<Citation citationKey="USACE1956" />
- **A Weibull defect-survival relationship** for the probability that a pre-existing defect fully penetrates the confining layer (see
  [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx))
- **Roof-support probabilities by soil classification** for the mechanical condition of pipe progression<Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />
- **The Schmertmann critical-gradient method** for the hydraulic condition of pipe progression<Citation citationKey="Schmertmann2000" />, with
  an exit-geometry factor that distinguishes concentrated from distributed exits<Citation citationKey="vanBeek2015" />

## Exit Pathways

The model evaluates up to two seepage exit pathways:

- **Toe pathway** — Seepage exits at the downstream toe of the embankment, or at the toe of the downstream seepage berm when one is present.
  This pathway is always evaluated. When a confining layer may be present and the toe exit remains beneath it — no berm, or a berm ending
  within the layer's known extent — the unfiltered-exit probability at the toe is composed from two mechanisms (a pre-existing defect through
  the layer, and hydraulic blowout of the layer), and the initiation probability is the blanket-theory heave probability. Where a berm instead
  places the exit at or beyond the layer's downstream termination, the exit daylights at the berm toe and the unfiltered-exit and initiation
  probabilities are certain there.

- **Daylight pathway** — Seepage exits at the downstream termination of the confining layer, where the underlying sand daylights at the ground
  surface. This pathway is evaluated whenever a confining layer may be present (its presence is confirmed or unknown) *and* the daylighting
  exit is distinct from the toe exit. At the daylight exit there is no confining layer to penetrate, so the unfiltered-exit and initiation
  probabilities are both certain. With no berm, or with a berm inside a confirmed layer's known extent, the daylight pathway is also
  distinguished by its longer seepage path to the exit. When a berm over a confirmed layer reaches or clears the layer's downstream extent — or
  overlies a confirmed layer whose extent is unknown — the berm toe is itself the daylighting exit: the toe pathway then represents that exit
  directly, and no separate daylight pathway is evaluated. A layer of unknown presence always retains both pathways; with a berm, both then
  share the berm-extended path length and differ in the toe initiation node and the exit datum (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).

The governing SRP at each headwater level is the larger of the two pathway SRPs, so the more critical exit condition controls the screening
result. When only the toe pathway is evaluated, it governs directly.

## Relationship to Toolbox Models

The screening model uses the same blanket-theory and Schmertmann methods as the standalone toolbox models for its blowout, heave, and
progression computations. The screening model differs only in what it supplies to
them: it builds their geometry and material inputs from the screening selections and fixes several choices that the toolbox models leave to
the analyst (<TableReference tableKey="bep-screening-table-1" />). [Delegated Methods](./07-delegated-methods.mdx) documents each fixed choice
and its basis.

# Model Overview

## Purpose

Each CLE screening model computes a system response curve for one concentrated leak erosion mechanism as a function of headwater elevation. The
system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level is the conditional probability of breach of the embankment by concentrated leak erosion
through a flaw of that mechanism, given that pool level. The curve of SRP values across the range of headwater levels characterizes the
embankment's vulnerability to the mechanism.

Concentrated leak erosion is erosion of the walls of an open crack, gap, or poorly compacted zone by water flowing through it. The failure mode
requires a continuous flaw through the embankment, a hydraulic shear stress on the flaw walls large enough to initiate erosion, an unfiltered exit
that lets the eroded soil leave the embankment, an enlarging pipe that stays open and is neither filled nor starved of flow, unsuccessful
detection and intervention, and finally an uncontrolled release of the impounded water.

## Intended Use

The CLE screening models are screening-level assessments of concentrated leak erosion risk. They make simplifying and generally conservative
assumptions with two goals:

- **Preventing false negatives** — The models are designed to avoid underestimating risk at the screening level, so that embankments with potential
  concentrated leak erosion concerns are flagged for further analysis.
- **Reducing input burden** — Qualitative selections (compaction categories, climate categories, conduit and wall descriptions, the embankment
  type) substitute for site-specific measurements, and default distributions cover the erodibility and initial-opening properties that are
  typically unavailable at the screening stage.

The models are not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments. For higher-level analyses, the full concentrated leak erosion
toolbox methods and the internal-erosion probability methods they draw on provide the complete methodologies with full parameterization (see
[Comparison with Detailed Models](#comparison-with-detailed-models)).

## How the Model Works

Every CLE screening model executes the same three stages:

1. **Flaw assessment** — The model estimates the probability that a continuous flaw of its mechanism exists through the embankment, and the
   elevation of that flaw. The probability comes from scoring qualitative and geometric site factors (or, for cross-valley settlement, from a
   settlement-based relationship), and an observation of the flaw — leakage, wet spots, or observed continuous cracking, depending on the model —
   can override it to certainty. The flaw probability is the same at every pool level (see [Flaw Conventions](./05-flaw-conventions.mdx)).

2. **Pathway resolution** — The model resolves the failure pathway through the embankment at the flaw elevation: whether a filter intersects it
   and where the filter's top lies, which materials must hold a roof over the enlarging pipe, and which upstream seepage-limiting elements still
   reach the flaw (see [Failure Pathway and Progression](./07-failure-pathway-and-progression.mdx)).

3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the model composes the eight node probabilities and multiplies them to produce the SRP.
   Initiation is either a gate on the headwater rising above the bottom of an open crest crack, or a Monte Carlo cylindrical-pipe analysis of the
   hydraulic shear stress at the flaw (see [Initiation](./06-initiation.mdx) and [Event Tree](./04-event-tree.mdx)).

## Physics Basis

The family combines the following engineering methods:

- **Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring** for the probability of a continuous flaw, in which each site factor is assigned a likelihood
  factor, weighted by a risk factor, and the weighted sum is mapped to a flaw probability <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> (see
  [Flaw Conventions](./05-flaw-conventions.mdx))
- **Hydraulic shear stress on the walls of a cylindrical pipe** compared with the soil's critical shear stress for the initiation of erosion in a
  compaction flaw, as documented in the
  [Cylindrical Pipe](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/cylindrical-pipe) chapter
  of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation manual
- **Roof-support probabilities by soil classification** for the ability of the enlarging pipe to stay open, and **crack-filling probabilities by
  embankment type** for the chance that upstream material fills the pipe <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />
- **Flow-limitation probabilities by upstream element** for the chance that a cutoff wall or upstream facing restricts the flow through the
  flaw <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />
- **Filter evaluation** for the probability that erosion continues past a filter intersecting the failure pathway, applied through the shared
  continuation method documented in the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening Technical Reference Manual and drawing on the
  [Filter Evaluation (Continuation)](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/general-overview)
  toolbox methods

## Comparison with Detailed Models

The detailed counterparts of the screening models are the concentrated leak erosion toolbox methods and the internal-erosion probability methods
of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> applied in full. The Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation manual covers four flaw
geometries — a cylindrical pipe, a horizontal crack, a vertical rectangular crack, and a vertical triangular crack (see its
[General Overview](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/general-overview)) — of
which the compaction models use the cylindrical pipe. <TableReference tableKey="cle-common-screening-table-1" /> summarizes the differences.

## The Two Model Groups

The eight models fall into two groups that share their flaw geometry and therefore their initiation treatment and their pathway rules
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-common-screening-table-2" />):

- **Crest-crack models** — cross-valley differential settlement, differential settlement adjacent to a wall or cliff, desiccation cracking, and
  frost cracking. The flaw is a transverse crack open at the crest and extending down to a bottom-of-crack elevation. Every node is
  deterministic except continuation, which the shared continuation method may evaluate by Monte Carlo simulation when a filter intercepts the
  pathway (its route through the USCS classes of the base and filter soils samples their gradation parameters).
- **Compaction models** — poor compaction within the core, adjacent to a conduit, adjacent to a wall or cliff, and along the foundation or
  abutment contact. The flaw is a zone of poorly compacted soil at a fixed base-of-flaw elevation, idealized for initiation as a cylindrical pipe.
  Initiation is evaluated by Monte Carlo simulation, and continuation may be as well, by the same continuation route as the crest-crack models;
  every other node is deterministic.

The flaw elevation is the one flaw quantity every downstream node uses: it sets where initiation can begin, where the failure pathway runs, and
which filter and upstream elements the pathway meets. [Flaw Conventions](./05-flaw-conventions.mdx#flaw-elevation) documents how each model
resolves it.

## Relationship to Toolbox Models

The compaction models use the same cylindrical-pipe initiation analysis as the standalone Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox. They
differ mainly in what they supply to it: the embankment section and flaw elevation come from the screening inputs, and the critical shear stress and
initial pipe diameter default to screening ranges that the user may replace. [Initiation](./06-initiation.mdx#delegated-method-cylindrical-pipe-initiation)
documents each supplied quantity and its basis. The crest-crack models use no toolbox method for initiation. All eight models hand the continuation node to the
shared continuation method whenever a filter intersects the failure pathway
(see [Failure Pathway and Progression](./07-failure-pathway-and-progression.mdx#node-3-continuation)).

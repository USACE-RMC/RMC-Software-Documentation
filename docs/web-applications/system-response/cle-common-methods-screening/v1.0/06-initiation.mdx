# Initiation

Node 2 of every CLE screening model asks whether the hydraulic shear stress exerted on the walls of the flaw by the water flowing through it is
large enough to begin eroding them. The two model groups answer it differently. The crest-crack models apply a headwater gate at the bottom of the
open crack. The compaction models hand the question to the cylindrical-pipe initiation analysis of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation
toolbox, evaluated by Monte Carlo simulation over the soil's critical shear stress and the initial pipe diameter.

## Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate

**Models:** cross-valley differential settlement, settlement adjacent to a wall or cliff, desiccation cracking, and frost cracking.

The flaw is a transverse crack open from the crest down to the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$
(see [Flaw Conventions](./05-flaw-conventions.mdx#crest-crack-models-bottom-of-the-crack)). Water enters the crack only once the pool rises above
its bottom, and once it does, initiation is taken as certain:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-common-screening-eq-8"
  equation="P_{init}(h) = \begin{cases} 1.0 & h > z_{bc} \\[4pt] 0 & h \le z_{bc} \end{cases}"
/>

The gate turns on strictly above the bottom of the crack: at a pool exactly at $z_{bc}$, initiation is zero. No shear stress is computed, no
cylindrical-pipe analysis is performed, and tailwater plays no part. In the desiccation and frost-cracking models, an inapplicable mechanism (see
[Flaw Conventions](./05-flaw-conventions.mdx#inapplicable-mechanism)) holds initiation at zero at every pool level.

The gate makes the crack depth the controlling quantity of the crest-crack models' initiation: the deeper the crack, the lower the pool at which
the SRP steps from negligible to its full value. Above $z_{bc}$, the SRP of a crest-crack model varies with pool level only through the
continuation, roof-support, and flow-limitation nodes (see [Failure Pathway and Progression](./07-failure-pathway-and-progression.mdx)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-06 — Engineering Basis]
The initiation probability of 1.0 applied once the pool rises above the bottom of an open crest crack
(<EquationReference equationKey="cle-common-screening-eq-8" />) requires an engineering basis to state here. The model records two different
attributions for it: one describes it as a conservative screening-level convention that is not an explicit finding of Pierson (2023), while
others attribute the certain initiation of a transverse crest crack to Pierson (2023). Resolve which is intended. If Pierson (2023) is to be
cited, provide its full bibliographic details (authors, title, publisher or journal, and report number) so that it can be added to the
bibliography.

Proposed (unconfirmed): an open transverse crack at the crest is wide enough that the shear stress of any flow through it exceeds the critical
shear stress of the crack walls, so initiation is taken as certain once water can enter the crack — a conservative screening posture under which a
site needing a lower value would adjust the SRP. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Delegated Method: Cylindrical Pipe Initiation

**Node served:** Node 2 (Initiation) of the compaction models — poor compaction within the core, adjacent to a conduit, adjacent to a wall or
cliff, and along the foundation or abutment.

**Question answered:** At each pool level, does the flow through a pipe of initial diameter $D$ at the base of the flaw exert a hydraulic shear
stress $\tau$ on the pipe wall that exceeds the critical shear stress $\tau_c$ of the soil? A poorly compacted zone is idealized as such a pipe,
and the cylindrical-pipe analysis compares the two stresses over the uncertainty in $\tau_c$ and $D$. The theory is in the
[Cylindrical Pipe](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/cylindrical-pipe) chapter
of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation manual; this section documents what the screening models supply to it, what they take from it, and
each screening-level choice.

### Governing relationships

The pipe invert is the base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$ resolved by the flaw assessment
(see [Flaw Conventions](./05-flaw-conventions.mdx#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)). At each pool level $h$ with tailwater $h_t$, the upstream
and downstream heads above the invert are each limited to be no less than zero:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-common-screening-eq-9"
  equation="H_1 = \max(h - z_f,\, 0), \qquad H_2 = \max(h_t - z_f,\, 0), \qquad i = \frac{H_1 - H_2}{L}"
/>

where $L$ is the seepage path length and $i$ the average hydraulic gradient along the pipe. A pool at or below the invert gives no flow and no
initiation; a tailwater below the invert, or no tailwater, gives no submergence. The hydraulic shear stress on the wall of a cylindrical pipe
carrying this gradient is, as derived in the toolbox manual's
[Hydraulic Shear Stress](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/cylindrical-pipe#hydraulic-shear-stress)
section,

<Equation equationKey="cle-common-screening-eq-10" equation="\tau = \gamma_w\, i\, \frac{D}{4}" />

with $\gamma_w$ the unit weight of water (62.4 lb/ft³) and $D$ converted from millimeters to feet. Erosion initiates in a realization when
$\tau > \tau_c$. The initiation probability at each pool level is the fraction of Monte Carlo realizations in which it does, as in the toolbox
manual's
[Likelihood of Initiation](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/cylindrical-pipe#likelihood-of-initiation-of-concentrated-leak-erosion)
section:

<Equation equationKey="cle-common-screening-eq-11" equation="P_{init}(h) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \mathbf{1}\bigl[\tau_n(h) > \tau_{c,n}\bigr]" />

where each of the $N$ realizations draws one critical shear stress $\tau_{c,n}$ and one initial pipe diameter $D_n$ and holds them at every pool
level. Because the same draws are carried across pool levels, the initiation probability does not decrease wherever the gradient rises with pool
level: it rises, or stays flat at 0, at 1, or at a plateau between them. It can fall only where a rising tailwater reduces the net head across the
pipe faster than the pool raises it.

### Seepage path length

Unless the user supplies a seepage path length, the model measures the pipe as a horizontal line at the invert elevation across the embankment
section — the core section when one is supplied, the shell section otherwise (see
[Common Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#embankment-description)):

<Equation equationKey="cle-common-screening-eq-12" equation="L = W_t + S_{us}(z_f) + S_{ds}(z_f)" />

where $W_t$ is the top width of the section ($W_{t,c}$ for the core, $W_{t,s}$ for the shell) and $S_{us}(z_f)$ and $S_{ds}(z_f)$ are the
horizontal spans of the upstream and downstream faces from the top of the section down to the invert. Each span is the sum, over the face
segments, of the vertical extent of each segment above the invert (only the part above it, for the segment the invert cuts) times the segment's
slope. This is the measurement the toolbox manual prescribes in its
[Core Geometry](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/cylindrical-pipe#core-geometry)
and Hydraulic Shear Stress sections: across the embankment slopes for a homogeneous embankment, and across the impervious core for a zoned
embankment whose outer zones are very permeable. As the manual recommends, a user-supplied length replaces the derivation for a sloping core or a
pipe that does not run perpendicular to the embankment centerline; a surveyed path length may be supplied the same way. The length is the same at every pool level.

### Screening-level adjustments

Relative to the toolbox method, the compaction models:

1. **Use the cylindrical-pipe geometry only.** The horizontal, vertical rectangular, and vertical triangular crack geometries of the toolbox are
   not offered; a poorly compacted zone is idealized as a pipe.
2. **Always run the probabilistic analysis.** The toolbox's deterministic option, which compares the most likely values only, is not used, so the
   initiation probability always reflects the uncertainty in the critical shear stress and the initial pipe diameter.
3. **Supply default distributions** for the critical shear stress and the initial pipe diameter, which the user may replace.
4. **Derive the seepage path length** from the screening section at the flaw elevation, which the user may replace.

The toolbox's further results — the critical pipe diameter, the headwater level for initiation, and the steady flow through the pipe — do not
enter the event tree.

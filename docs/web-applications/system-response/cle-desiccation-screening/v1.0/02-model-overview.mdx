# Model Overview

## Purpose

The CLE desiccation screening model computes a system response curve for concentrated leak erosion through a desiccation crack at the embankment
crest, as a function of headwater elevation. The system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level is the conditional probability of breach
of the embankment by concentrated leak erosion through such a crack, given that pool level.

Desiccation cracking is the opening of cracks in the soil near the crest as it dries and shrinks. Where a desiccation crack runs transversely
across the crest and is continuous from the upstream face to the downstream face, it opens a path through the embankment from the crest down to
the bottom of the crack. When the pool rises above the bottom of the crack, water flows through it and can erode its walls. The failure mode then
proceeds as for any concentrated leak: the erosion must find an unfiltered exit, the enlarging pipe must stay open and be neither filled nor starved
of flow, intervention must fail, and an uncontrolled release of the impounded water must follow.

## Intended Use

The model is a screening-level assessment of concentrated leak erosion through desiccation cracking at the crest. Like the other CLE screening
models, it makes simplifying and generally conservative assumptions with two goals
(see the [Model Overview](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#intended-use) of the CLE
Common Methods manual):

- **Preventing false negatives** — The model is designed to avoid underestimating risk at the screening level, so that embankments with a
  potential desiccation-cracking concern are flagged for further analysis.
- **Reducing input burden** — Three qualitative site descriptions — the surface layer over the core, the climate, and the soil classes at the crest
  — stand in for an assessment of crack occurrence and crack depth from site investigation. A measured average annual net evaporation may replace
  the climate description where it is available.

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments (see
[Comparison with Detailed Models](#comparison-with-detailed-models)).

## How the Model Works

The model executes the three stages common to the CLE screening models
(see [How the Model Works](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#how-the-model-works) in the CLE
Common Methods manual), with a desiccation-specific flaw assessment:

1. **Flaw assessment** — The model scores three site factors on a likelihood scale of 1 to 4: the surface layer over the core, the climate (or
   the measured net evaporation), and the plasticity of the soils the crack would cross. The weighted sum of the scores is converted to the
   probability that a continuous desiccation crack exists. A weighted sum below 13 gives a zero probability, and a report that continuous transverse
   cracking caused by desiccation has been observed sets the probability to 1.0 (see [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx)). From the same
   descriptions the model takes a tabulated crack depth and computes the bottom-of-crack elevation (see [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx)).

2. **Pathway resolution** — From the user's description of the embankment along the crack, the model takes the top of the first filter the
   failure pathway meets, the soils along the pathway, the embankment type, and the upstream seepage-limiting elements, following the conventions
   for cracks open at the crest
   (see [The Failure Pathway](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#the-failure-pathway)
   in the CLE Common Methods manual).

3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level the model multiplies the eight node probabilities. When the mechanism applies, initiation is
   certain once the pool rises above the bottom of the crack and zero at or below it; when every listed crest soil is a clean or nearly clean sand
   or gravel, it is zero at every pool level; continuation, roof support, and flow limitation follow the pool level relative to the top
   of the filter and the top of any upstream wall (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).

Every node is evaluated directly from the inputs and tabulated values. The model performs no Monte Carlo simulation of its own; the continuation
node is the only one that may be a Monte Carlo estimate, when the shared continuation method evaluates the filter by sampling
(see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx#node-3-continuation)).

## Physics Basis

The model combines the following engineering methods:

- **Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring** for the probability of a continuous flaw, in which each site factor is assigned a likelihood
  factor, weighted by a risk factor, and the weighted sum is mapped to a flaw probability <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> (see
  [Risk-Factor and Likelihood-Factor Scoring](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
  in the CLE Common Methods manual). The desiccation factors express that a crack is more likely where the crest is unprotected, the climate is
  drier, and the soil is more plastic (see [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx)).
- **Tabulated desiccation crack depths** by climate, soil, and surface layer, adapted from Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />
  (see [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx)).
- **The headwater gate at the bottom of an open crest crack** for initiation, as documented in
  [Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
  in the CLE Common Methods manual.
- **Tabulated roof-support, crack-filling, and flow-limitation probabilities** for the progression nodes <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, and
  **filter evaluation** for the continuation node through the shared continuation method, both applied as documented in
  [Failure Pathway and Progression](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression) in
  the CLE Common Methods manual.

The screening model does not estimate the width of the crack. Crack width enters a detailed shear stress analysis of initiation, but the screening
model's initiation gate depends only on the bottom-of-crack elevation, so no width is needed.

## Place Among the CLE Screening Models

The desiccation model is one of the four crest-crack models of the CLE family, together with cross-valley differential settlement, differential
settlement adjacent to a wall or cliff, and frost cracking (see
[The Two Model Groups](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#the-two-model-groups) in the CLE
Common Methods manual). Its flaw is a transverse crack open at the crest, and it shares with the other crest-crack models the headwater initiation
gate, the pool-dependent failure-pathway gates, and the form of its results. It differs from them in its flaw assessment:

- Its three factors describe the crest surface, the climate, and the crest soils rather than settlement or geometry.
- Its crack depth comes from a table by climate, soil, and surface layer rather than from the settlement of the embankment.
- It is one of the two models, with frost cracking, that apply a lower threshold to the weighted sum and that treat the mechanism as inapplicable
  when every crest soil is a clean or nearly clean sand or gravel.

# Flaw Probability

Node 1 of the event tree is the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous transverse desiccation crack exists across the crest. The model estimates
it by the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, which seven of the CLE
screening models share and which is documented in
[Risk-Factor and Likelihood-Factor Scoring](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
of the CLE Common Methods manual. This chapter documents the desiccation-specific parts of that scoring: the three factors and their scores, the
curve that converts the weighted sum to a probability, the lower threshold, the observation override, and the case in which the mechanism does not
apply. The scored probability is used directly; the model applies no flaw-continuity stage.

## Factors

The model scores three factors, each on a likelihood scale on which 1 is the least likely condition for a continuous desiccation crack and 4 the
most likely (<TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-3" />).

Across their full ranges, the surface layer can move the weighted sum by up to 9, the climate by up to 6, and the crest soil by up to 3. A
particular change can still move it less than a change of another factor: going from a gravel road to no road or pathway adds 4.5, less than the
6 added by going from a temperate to an arid climate. The climate likelihood factor can take any value between 1 and 4 when it is interpolated
from a measured net evaporation; the other two take only the tabulated values.

### Surface layer

The surface-layer likelihood factor $LF_s$ is 1 for a paved crest, 2.5 for a gravel road, and 4 for a crest with no road or pathway (see
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#surface-layer-over-the-core)).

### Climate

When no net evaporation is supplied, the climate likelihood factor $LF_{cl}$ is 4 for an arid climate, 3 for a monsoonal climate, 2 for a seasonal
climate, and 1 for a temperate climate (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#climate)).

When a measured average annual net evaporation $E_n$ is supplied, $LF_{cl}$ is interpolated linearly in $E_n$ between the points of
<TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-4" />, and takes the end value beyond the tabulated range.

The result is a stepped relationship with short transitions:

- $LF_{cl} = 1$ for a net evaporation of 425 mm or less.
- $LF_{cl}$ rises linearly from 1 to 2 between 425 and 450 mm, and is 2 from 450 to 900 mm.
- $LF_{cl}$ rises linearly from 2 to 3 between 900 and 925 mm, and is 3 from 925 to 1500 mm.
- $LF_{cl}$ steps from 3 to 4 at 1500 mm and is 4 above it.

A net evaporation within one of the two short transitions therefore gives a fractional climate score, for example 1.5 at 437.5 mm.

### Crest soil

Each listed crest soil class is scored per <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-5" />. The soil likelihood factor $LF_{cs}$
is the highest score among the scored classes in the list, so the most crack-prone soil the crack would cross governs. When the list is empty — the
crest soils are unknown — $LF_{cs} = 2$.

## Weighted Sum

The three scores combine into the weighted sum of the common scheme (see
[Weighted sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#weighted-sum) in the CLE Common Methods
manual), here

<Equation equationKey="cle-desiccation-screening-eq-1" equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_s + 2\,LF_{cl} + 1\,LF_{cs}" />

The weighted sum ranges from 6 (paved crest, temperate climate, least crack-prone soil) to 24 (no road or pathway, arid climate, fat clay). A
paved crest limits it to at most 15.

## Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum

The weighted sum is converted to the raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$ by interpolating the logarithm of the probability linearly between the points
of <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-6" />, as described in
[Flaw probability from the weighted sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
of the CLE Common Methods manual. A weighted sum above 24 cannot occur, so the upper end of the curve is reached only at the most adverse
combination of all three factors.

### Lower threshold

A weighted sum below 13 gives a raw flaw probability of zero; a weighted sum of exactly 13 takes the first tabulated value, 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup>.
This is the lower-threshold rule of the common scheme (see
[Flaw probability from the weighted sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)).
No single factor score forces the probability to zero on its own. Apart from the inapplicable-mechanism rule — every listed crest soil a clean or
nearly clean sand or gravel (see [Inapplicable Mechanism](#inapplicable-mechanism)), which also sets the raw flaw probability to zero — the
threshold is the only rule that does. Whether a crest reaches the threshold depends first on its surface layer:

- **On a paved crest**, the weighted sum is 13 or more only when $2\,LF_{cl} + LF_{cs} \ge 10$ — for example, an arid climate with a soil scored 2
  or more (including an unknown soil, scored 2), or a monsoonal climate with a fat clay. A paved crest in an arid climate with a soil scored 1 has a
  weighted sum of 12 and falls below the threshold. A paved crest in a seasonal or temperate climate always falls below the threshold.
- **On a gravel road**, the weighted sum is below 13 only when $2\,LF_{cl} + LF_{cs} < 5.5$. With the climate given as a category, these are a
  temperate climate with a soil scored 1, 2, or 3 (sums of 10.5, 11.5, and 12.5) and a seasonal climate with a soil scored 1 (a sum of 12.5). A
  measured net evaporation within the 425 to 450 mm or the 900 to 925 mm transition gives a fractional climate score, and some of those values also
  fall below 13; the general condition governs.
- **On a crest with no road or pathway**, the weighted sum is at least 15 and always reaches the threshold.

A raw flaw probability of zero is carried into the event tree as the negligibly small value described in
[SRP Computation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation) of the CLE Common Methods
manual, unless the observation override applies.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-07 — Engineering Basis]
The weighted-sum curve of <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-6" /> (13, 16, 20, 24 → 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup>,
1 × 10<sup>-2</sup>, 1 × 10<sup>-1</sup>, 0.5) and the lower threshold at a weighted sum of 13 require an engineering basis. Cite the figure or
table of Fell et al. (2024) from which they are taken, or provide the engineering-judgment attribution, including why the probability is zero
rather than continuing to fall below a weighted sum of 13. The interpolation form is the family-wide question of marker IN-03 of the CLE Common
Methods manual.
:::

## Observation Override

When continuous desiccation cracking is reported as observed — transverse cracking at the crest, attributed to desiccation, and judged continuous
(see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#continuous-desiccation-cracking-observed)) — the flaw probability is 1.0 whatever the factors
score, including when the weighted sum is below the threshold. This is the observation override of the common scheme (see
[Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) of the CLE
Common Methods manual). The flaw probability is then

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-desiccation-screening-eq-2"
  equation="P_{flaw} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{continuous desiccation cracking observed} \\[4pt] P_{raw}(\Sigma) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}"
/>

The override sets only the flaw probability. The crack depth, and with it the pool level at which initiation begins, is still the tabulated value
of [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx); the observation does not supply a measured depth.

## Inapplicable Mechanism

When every listed crest soil is one of the six clean or nearly clean sands and gravels, the model treats desiccation cracking as unable to occur in
the crest (see
[Inapplicable Mechanism](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#inapplicable-mechanism) of the
CLE Common Methods manual):

- the surface-layer and climate likelihood factors are still evaluated and reported, but the soil likelihood factor, the weighted sum, and the
  raw flaw probability are set to zero;
- the flaw probability is negligibly small;
- the observation override is not consulted, because cracking observed in such a crest cannot be desiccation cracking;
- the crack depth is zero, so the bottom of the crack is at the crest, and initiation is zero at every pool level.

The SRP is then negligible at every pool level. An empty list is not this case: it states that the crest soils are unknown and is scored as
described under [Crest soil](#crest-soil).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-08 — Engineering Basis]
The treatment of the six classes SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP as soils in which desiccation cracking does not occur — passed over when listed
with other soils, and making the mechanism inapplicable when listed alone — requires an engineering basis. Provide the literature source or the
engineering-judgment attribution, and state whether the silty classes SM, GM, and ML, which can also be non-plastic, are deliberately retained
with the lowest score.

Proposed (unconfirmed): clean and nearly clean sands and gravels, with less than 12 percent fines, have too little plasticity to shrink on drying
and hold open a crack, so a crest built only of them cannot develop a desiccation crack. Confirm or replace.
:::

In the first example the weighted sum of 13.5 lies between the tabulated points 13 and 16, so

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-desiccation-screening-eq-3"
  equation="\log_{10} P_{raw} = \log_{10}\left(10^{-3}\right) + \frac{13.5 - 13}{16 - 13}\left[\log_{10}\left(10^{-2}\right) - \log_{10}\left(10^{-3}\right)\right] = -2.833"
/>

and $P_{flaw} = 10^{-2.833} = 1.47 \times 10^{-3}$. The second example is the most adverse paved crest: an arid climate and a fat clay core reach a
weighted sum of 15, two thirds of the way from 13 to 16, and a flaw probability of $10^{-2.333} = 4.64 \times 10^{-3}$. The third is the most
adverse combination of all three factors and takes the top of the curve. The fourth falls below the threshold of 13 and has a negligible flaw
probability; an observation of continuous desiccation cracking would raise it to 1.0.

# Assumptions and Limitations

The desiccation model applies deliberate simplifications relative to a detailed assessment of desiccation cracking. They reduce input requirements
and are designed to avoid false negatives at the screening level. This chapter lists the assumptions specific to desiccation cracking. The
assumptions the model shares with the other CLE screening models — the event-tree product, certain initiation above the bottom of a crest crack,
the crest-crack pathway gates, and the tabulated progression probabilities — are listed in
[Assumptions and Limitations](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) of the CLE
Common Methods manual and apply here unchanged. Each assumption below states its engineering basis, its effect on results, and the conditions under
which it may not hold. Where an engineering basis is still awaiting confirmation, the assumption cross-references the input marker at its primary
location in this document.

## Flaw Probability

1. **Three site factors — the surface layer, the climate, and the crest soil, weighted 3, 2, and 1 — determine the probability of a continuous
   desiccation crack.** (See [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#factors).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the specific
     weights and scores are requested at marker IN-06 in [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#crest-soil).
   - *Effect on results:* Across their full ranges, the surface layer can move the weighted sum by up to 9, the climate by up to 6, and the soil
     by up to 3. These are limits, not a ranking of every change: going from a gravel road to no road or pathway adds 4.5, less than the 6 added
     by going from a temperate to an arid climate.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where other conditions control cracking at the site — for example the moisture content at which the crest was
     compacted, vegetation, or the exposure of the crest to sun and wind — that the three factors do not describe.
2. **When several crest soils are listed, the most crack-prone of them governs.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): the crack needs to form in only one of the soils it crosses, so the
     soil most able to crack is taken as controlling, the conservative choice at the screening level.
   - *Effect on results:* Once the list holds a scored soil, adding a less plastic soil never lowers the flaw probability or the crack depth;
     adding a more plastic one can raise both.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the most plastic soil occupies only a small part of the crest that a crack could not cross continuously.
3. **An unknown crest soil is scored 2.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-05 in [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#crest-soil).
   - *Effect on results:* An unknown soil gives a flaw probability no higher than a known high-plasticity silt or fat clay, and no lower than a
     known silty sand, silty gravel, low-plasticity silt, clayey sand, or clayey gravel; the probabilities are equal when both weighted sums fall
     below 13, or when an observation of cracking sets both to 1.0. Its crack is always shallower than that of the more plastic soils and deeper
     than that of the less plastic ones.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the crest is likely to be built of a high-plasticity soil but its classification has not been confirmed;
     listing the probable class then gives the more conservative result.
4. **A measured average annual net evaporation, when supplied, replaces the climate selection for both the climate score and the crack depth.**
   (See [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#climate).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see markers IN-04 in [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#climate) and IN-11 in
     [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#crack-depth-from-measured-net-evaporation).
   - *Effect on results:* Outside the two short transitions at 425 to 450 mm and 900 to 925 mm, the measured value gives the same result as the
     climate category it falls in; within them it gives an intermediate result.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the climate is better described by the timing of drying periods than by their annual total, for example a
     climate with long dry seasons but moderate annual net evaporation.
5. **A weighted sum below 13 gives a zero flaw probability, and, other than a crest of clean sands and gravels alone (Assumption 7), no factor
   forces the probability to zero on its own.**
   (See [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#lower-threshold).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-07 in [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#lower-threshold).
   - *Effect on results:* A paved crest is not ruled out in every case: it reaches the threshold in an arid climate with a soil scored 2 or more
     (or an unknown soil), or in a monsoonal climate with a fat clay.
     Below the threshold the SRP is negligible at every pool level unless cracking has been observed. At the threshold the flaw probability
     drops from 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup> to negligible across a single step of the weighted sum.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where a crest scoring just below 13 has conditions, not captured by the factors, that favor cracking.
6. **An observation of continuous desiccation cracking sets the flaw probability to 1.0.** The observation counts only when transverse cracking
   has been observed, is attributed to desiccation, and is judged continuous across the crest.
   - *Engineering basis:* Engineering judgment, following the observation-override convention of the CLE screening models (see
     [Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) in
     the CLE Common Methods manual): an observed continuous crack demonstrates the flaw directly.
   - *Effect on results:* The flaw node no longer limits the curve; the downstream nodes, and the crack depth through the initiation gate, still
     do.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the observed cracking is shallow surface cracking that is not continuous through the embankment; the
     observation should then not be reported as continuous.
7. **Desiccation cracking does not occur in the clean and nearly clean sands and gravels SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP.** Such soils are passed
   over when listed with others, and make the mechanism inapplicable when listed alone.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-08 in [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#inapplicable-mechanism).
   - *Effect on results:* A crest of these soils alone gives a negligible SRP at every pool level, whatever the surface layer, the climate, or an
     observation of cracking.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the classification of the crest soil is uncertain and a plastic fraction may be present; listing the possible
     plastic class, or leaving the list empty, keeps the mechanism in the analysis.

## Crack Depth

8. **The crack depth is a tabulated value by climate, surface layer, and crest-soil score.** (See
   [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#tabulated-crack-depths).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Adapted from Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the adaptation is requested at marker IN-09 in
     [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#tabulated-crack-depths).
   - *Effect on results:* The crack depth, measured down from the crest elevation, sets the pool level at which the SRP first rises above
     negligible: the deeper the tabulated crack, the lower that pool level. Further steps can follow where the pool crosses the top of a filter or
     of a wall.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the depth of desiccation cracking has been measured, for example in test pits or trenches across the crest, or
     where the crest has been rebuilt or treated so that the tabulated depth does not apply.
9. **A paved crest takes the crack depths tabulated for a gravel roadway.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-10 in [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#tabulated-crack-depths).
   - *Effect on results:* A paved crest and a gravel road with the same climate and soil have the same bottom of crack, although the paved crest
     has the lower flaw probability.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where a sealed pavement keeps the crest soil moist enough to limit cracking to a shallower depth than under a gravel
     road.
10. **The crack depth is always the tabulated value, including when continuous cracking has been observed.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-12 in [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#cracks-of-zero-depth).
    - *Effect on results:* An observed crack in a crest whose tabulated depth is zero contributes nothing to the SRP below the crest.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where the observed crack is deeper than the tabulated value.
11. **The tabulated crack depths are in feet and are subtracted from the crest elevation without conversion.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-03 in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#units).
    - *Effect on results:* With elevations in feet, the bottom of the crack is as tabulated. With elevations in another unit, it is misplaced.
    - *When it may not hold:* Whenever elevations are not in feet.
12. **The width of the crack is not estimated.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Follows from the headwater initiation gate of the crest-crack models (see
      [Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
      in the CLE Common Methods manual), which depends only on the bottom-of-crack elevation.
    - *Effect on results:* None within the model; the width of a crack plays no part in its initiation, continuation, or progression nodes.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where a narrow crack is expected to seal or to carry too little flow to erode; a detailed shear stress analysis of
      the crack geometry is then needed.

## Limitations

- **Screening scope.** The model screens a single mechanism — transverse cracking at the crest by drying and shrinkage. Cracking by frost,
  settlement, or hydraulic fracture is screened by the other CLE models or lies outside the family.
- **Qualitative description of the crest.** The flaw probability and the crack depth rest on three qualitative descriptions. Two site-specific
  inputs can refine them: a measured average annual net evaporation, which replaces the climate category in both results, and the observation of
  continuous desiccation cracking, which sets the flaw probability. Other evidence of cracking — measured crack depths, the condition of the crest
  after dry seasons, or test-pit logs — cannot be entered and does not refine either result.

# Input Parameters

This chapter documents the inputs specific to the desiccation model — the five flaw inputs that describe the crest — and summarizes the inputs the
model shares with the other CLE screening models, which are documented in full in
[Common Input Parameters](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) of the CLE Common Methods
manual. The model takes no distribution inputs: every input is a single value, a selection, or a list of soil classes.

## Flaw Inputs

The flaw inputs describe the crest of the embankment where a desiccation crack would form
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-2" />). They set the probability of a continuous crack (see
[Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx)) and its depth (see [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx)).

### Surface layer over the core

The surface layer is what covers the core soil at the crest. It sets the surface-layer likelihood factor and selects the row of the crack-depth
table. The choices are:

- **Paved** — a concrete or otherwise sealed roadway or pathway over the core. It is the least likely condition for a desiccation crack (likelihood
  factor 1) and takes the crack depths tabulated for a gravel roadway.
- **Gravel road** — a gravel roadway over the core (likelihood factor 2.5), with the crack depths tabulated for a gravel roadway.
- **No road or pathway** — the core soil is exposed at the crest, or carries no roadway or pathway (likelihood factor 4), with the crack depths
  tabulated for a crest with no road or pathway.

There is no Unknown choice. When no surface layer is given, the model takes a crest with no road or pathway, the most crack-prone of the three:
it carries the highest surface-layer likelihood factor and the deeper of the two crack-depth rows. Its basis is requested together with that of
the climate default under [Climate](#climate).

The model sets no flaw probability to zero on account of the surface layer alone, so a paved crest lowers the flaw probability without ruling
the mechanism out in every case. In a seasonal or temperate climate, however, a paved crest always falls below the lower threshold of the scoring,
so in practice the mechanism is ruled out there; in an arid or monsoonal climate it can reach the threshold, depending on the crest soil (see
[Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#lower-threshold)).

### Climate

The climate describes the precipitation and temperature regime at the dam. It sets the climate likelihood factor and selects the climate of the
crack-depth table, unless a measured net evaporation is supplied, in which case the climate selection is not used. The choices are:

- **Arid** — less than 10 in of annual rainfall and high summer temperatures (likelihood factor 4).
- **Monsoonal** — monsoonal or other distinct wet and dry periods, with high summer temperatures (likelihood factor 3).
- **Seasonal** — annual rainfall greater than 20 in, with no prolonged hot, dry periods (likelihood factor 2).
- **Temperate** — rainfall distributed uniformly throughout the year (likelihood factor 1).

There is no Unknown choice. When no climate is given, the model takes a temperate climate, the least crack-prone of the four: it carries the
lowest climate likelihood factor and crack depths no deeper than those of any other climate. This default is not conservative — for the other
inputs given, it gives a flaw probability no higher and a bottom of crack no lower than any other climate would — so a result computed with the
climate defaulted can understate both the flaw probability and the crack depth. When a measured net evaporation is supplied, the climate
selection, given or defaulted, is not used.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-01 — Definition]
The four climate descriptions above need a source and a rule for sites they do not clearly cover. As stated, the descriptions overlap and leave
gaps: a site with 10 to 20 in of annual rainfall and no distinct wet and dry periods fits none of them, and a site with less than 10 in of rainfall
and distinct wet and dry periods fits two. Cite the table of Fell et al. (2024) from which the descriptions are taken, and state how the user should
classify a site that falls between them (for example, by selecting the drier category, or by supplying a measured net evaporation instead).
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-13 — Engineering Basis]
The defaults of the two selections require an engineering basis. When no surface layer is given the model takes a crest with no road or pathway,
the most crack-prone surface; when no climate is given it takes a temperate climate, the least crack-prone climate (likelihood factor 1, and
crack depths no deeper than those of any other climate). Provide the engineering basis or the engineering-judgment attribution for each default
— in particular for the temperate default, which is the least conservative of the four climates — or state that both selections are always to be
supplied and the defaults are not to be relied on.
:::

### Average annual net evaporation

The average annual net evaporation $E_n$ is an optional measured alternative to the climate selection, in millimeters regardless of the length unit
used for elevations. When it is supplied, it replaces the climate selection in both places the climate is used: the climate likelihood factor is
interpolated from it (see [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#climate)), and the crack depth is interpolated between the depths of the
four climates (see [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#crack-depth-from-measured-net-evaporation)). It must be zero or positive. A measured value
suits a site whose climate falls between the categories, or where a published evaporation record is available.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Definition]
State how the average annual net evaporation is to be determined — in particular whether it is the annual potential (pan or lake) evaporation less
the annual rainfall, and over what period of record it is averaged — and cite the source that defines it for use with the desiccation crack-depth
tables.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the annual evaporation less the annual rainfall, averaged over the period of record, as used by Fell et al. (2024) to
characterize the climate in its desiccation crack-depth tables. Confirm or replace.
:::

### Crest soil classifications

The crest soil classifications are the classes in the Unified Soil Classification System (USCS) of every material, other than a drain or filter,
that the crack would cross at the crest. Only the fifteen classes named below are accepted. Other dual symbols, such as SC-SM, GC-GM, SW-SM, or
GW-GC, and the organic soils have no entry of their own, so the user has to map each such soil to one of the fifteen before entering it. The
classifications set the soil likelihood factor and select the column of the crack-depth table:

- **Plastic and silty soils** — the nine classes SM, GM, ML, SC, GC, CL-ML, CL, MH, and CH are scored, and the most crack-prone of the listed soils
  governs (see [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#crest-soil)).
- **Clean and nearly clean sands and gravels** — the six classes SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP are accepted in the list but are not scored,
  because desiccation cracking is not modeled in them. When every listed soil is one of these six, the mechanism does not apply (see
  [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#inapplicable-mechanism)).
- **Unknown** — leaving the list empty states that the crest soils are unknown. The model then assigns an intermediate soil score (see
  [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#crest-soil)).

### Continuous desiccation cracking observed

This input reports an observation of the crack itself. It is answered yes only when all three of the following hold: transverse cracking has been
observed at the crest, the cracking is attributed to desiccation, and it is judged continuous across the crest. A yes sets the flaw probability to
1.0 (see [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#observation-override)); it does not change the crack depth. The default, no, takes no
observation into account.

## Shared Inputs

The model also takes the following inputs in the form documented for the crest-crack models in
[Common Input Parameters](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) of the CLE Common Methods
manual. None of them is modified for desiccation cracking.

- **Hydraulic loading** — the headwater series and the optional tailwater series (see
  [Hydraulic Loading](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#hydraulic-loading)). The model
  reports the tailwater with its results but does not use it.
- **Crest elevation** $z_c$ and **embankment type** (see
  [Embankment Description](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description)). The
  crack depth is measured down from the crest elevation, and the embankment type sets the crack-filling probability. The model takes no
  slope-segment description of the embankment section.
- **Failure-pathway inputs** — the top-of-filter elevation $z_{tf}$, the full-pathway and below-filter roof materials, and the continuation inputs
  of the shared continuation method (see
  [Failure-Pathway Inputs](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#failure-pathway-inputs) and the
  [Input Parameters](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) chapter of the Continuation (Filter
  Evaluation) manual). The below-filter roof materials are the materials the user describes along the below-filter pathway, cut at the bottom of
  the crack. The user therefore has to work out the bottom-of-crack elevation — the crest elevation less the tabulated crack depth of
  [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#bottom-of-crack-elevation) — before entering them.
- **Upstream seepage-limiting elements** and the **top-of-wall elevation** $z_{tw}$ (see
  [Upstream Seepage-Limiting Elements](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#upstream-seepage-limiting-elements)).
- **Probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach**, each defaulting to 1.0 (see
  [Intervention and Breach](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#intervention-and-breach)).

The model takes none of the initiation material inputs of the compaction models — no critical shear stress, initial pipe diameter, or seepage path
length — because its initiation node is a headwater gate rather than a shear stress analysis.

## Units

Elevations — the headwater, tailwater, crest, top-of-filter, and top-of-wall elevations — are on one common datum and in one length unit, as
described in [Units and Datum](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#units-and-datum) of the
CLE Common Methods manual. Two quantities carry fixed units: the net evaporation is in millimeters, and the tabulated crack depths are in feet. The
crack depth is subtracted from the crest elevation without conversion (see [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#bottom-of-crack-elevation)), so the
bottom-of-crack elevation is correct only when elevations are in feet.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Applicability]
The tabulated crack depths are in feet and are subtracted from the crest elevation with no unit conversion, so a crest elevation in meters
produces a bottom-of-crack elevation that is wrong by the factor between the two units. State the length unit the model is intended to be used in,
and the instruction to give a user working in SI units. This is the desiccation-model instance of the family-wide question raised at marker IN-01
of the CLE Common Methods manual.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the model is intended for elevations in feet; a user working in SI units converts the elevations to feet before running
the model. Confirm or replace.
:::

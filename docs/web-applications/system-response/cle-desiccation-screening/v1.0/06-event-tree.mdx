# Event Tree

The model computes the SRP at each headwater level from the eight-node event tree shared by the CLE screening models, documented in
[Event Tree](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree) of the CLE Common Methods manual. The tree is
evaluated at every supplied headwater value and at additional pool levels between them, as described in
[Headwater Grid](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#headwater-grid). This chapter states how
each node is populated for desiccation cracking. Only the flaw node is specific to the mechanism; the other seven follow the crest-crack
conventions of the shared manual without change.

## Node 1: Flaw

*Question:* Does a continuous transverse desiccation crack exist across the crest?

The flaw probability $P_{flaw}$ is the result of [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx): the scored probability from the surface layer,
climate, and crest soil, or 1.0 when continuous desiccation cracking has been observed, or a negligibly small value when the mechanism is
inapplicable or the weighted sum is below 13 without an observation. It is the same at every pool level.

## Node 2: Initiation

*Question:* Does water flowing through the crack erode its walls?

Initiation is the headwater gate of the crest-crack models (see
[Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
in the CLE Common Methods manual), applied at the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$ of [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#bottom-of-crack-elevation).
At a headwater (pool) elevation $h$, the initiation probability is

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-desiccation-screening-eq-5"
  equation="P_{init}(h) = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{the mechanism applies and } h > z_{bc} \\[4pt] 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}"
/>

At a pool exactly at the bottom of the crack, initiation is zero. When the mechanism is inapplicable, initiation is zero at every pool level. No
shear stress is computed and tailwater plays no part. Certain initiation above the bottom of the crack is a shared assumption of the crest-crack
models (Assumption 9 in
[Assumptions and Limitations](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations#initiation) of
the CLE Common Methods manual).

## Node 3: Continuation

*Question:* Does an unfiltered exit allow erosion to continue?

Continuation follows the crest-crack convention of
[Node 3: Continuation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-3-continuation)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. The continuation probability $P_{cont}(h)$ at pool level $h$ is set as follows. With no filter on the failure
pathway, $P_{cont} = 1.0$ at every pool level. With a filter whose top is at $z_{tf}$, $P_{cont}$ is the probability of continuing erosion
$P_{ce}$ at pool levels at or below $z_{tf}$, and 1.0 above it, where the water in the upper part of the crack passes over the filter's top. A
filter caps the pathway when its top reaches the crest and no full-pathway roof materials are given (see
[The Failure Pathway](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#the-failure-pathway)
in the CLE Common Methods manual); such a filter gives $P_{ce}$ at every pool level. The value of $P_{ce}$
is evaluated once, by the shared continuation method, from the filter and the base soils upstream of it (see
[Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil)
in the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) manual).

Because initiation is zero at or below the bottom of the crack, a filter that does not cap the pathway and whose top lies at or below $z_{bc}$
never affects the SRP: at every pool level where initiation occurs, the pool is above the filter's top and continuation is 1.0. A filter that caps
the pathway is the exception at any crack depth: its continuation value $P_{ce}$ and the below-filter roof-support value apply at every pool level,
including, for a crack of zero depth, the pool levels above the crest at which initiation occurs.

## Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

*Question:* Does the material over the enlarging pipe hold a roof, so that the pipe stays open?

The roof-support probability $P_{roof}(h)$ is the minimum roof-support probability over the material set that applies at pool level $h$ (see
[Node 4: Progression — Roof Support](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-4-progression--roof-support)
in the CLE Common Methods manual): the below-filter materials, taken at the bottom-of-crack elevation, at pool levels at or below $z_{tf}$ (and at
every pool level when the filter caps the pathway), and the full-pathway materials above $z_{tf}$ (and at every pool level when no filter is
present). Roof support therefore changes at the filter's top in step with continuation.

The crest soils of the flaw assessment and the roof materials are separate inputs. The crest soils describe the material the crack crosses at the
crest and set the likelihood and depth of cracking; the roof materials describe the materials along the failure pathway and set the probability
that the enlarging pipe stays open.

## Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

*Question:* Is the enlarging pipe left unfilled by material carried in from upstream?

The probability of no crack filling $P_{nf}$ depends only on the embankment type, per
[Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-5-progression--no-crack-filling)
in the CLE Common Methods manual, following Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />. It is the same at every pool level.

## Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

*Question:* Does the flow into the enlarging pipe remain unrestricted by upstream seepage-limiting elements?

The probability of no flow limitation $P_{nl}(h)$ is the minimum over the upstream elements that reach the pool level, per
[Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. When a top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ is supplied, every listed element applies at pool levels at or below
$z_{tw}$; above it the walls drop out and only an upstream facing element remains. With no element listed, $P_{nl} = 1.0$.

## Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

*Questions:* Do detection and intervention fail? Does an uncontrolled release of impounded water occur?

The probabilities $P_{int}$ and $P_{br}$ are the user-specified values, each applied at every pool level and each defaulting to 1.0 (see
[Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#nodes-7-and-8-unsuccessful-intervention-and-breach)
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each pool level is the product of the eight node probabilities:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-desiccation-screening-eq-6"
  equation="\mathrm{SRP}(h) = P_{flaw} \times P_{init}(h) \times P_{cont}(h) \times P_{roof}(h) \times P_{nf} \times P_{nl}(h) \times P_{int} \times P_{br}"
/>

as in [SRP Computation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation) of the CLE Common
Methods manual. Wherever the product is zero — for example at and below the bottom of the crack, or where a user-specified probability is zero —
the SRP is reported as a negligibly small value rather than zero. Above the bottom of the crack the SRP changes with pool level only where the pool crosses the top of a filter or the top of a wall that lies
above $z_{bc}$.

<TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-9" /> shows which nodes vary with pool level. Every node is evaluated directly from the
inputs, so the curve can be reproduced exactly from the reported node values, apart from any sampling in the continuation probability $P_{ce}$.

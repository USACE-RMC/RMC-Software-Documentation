# Event Tree

The model computes the system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level from the eight-node event tree shared by the concentrated leak
erosion (CLE) screening models, documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree">Event Tree</DocLink>
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual. The tree is evaluated at every supplied headwater value and at additional pool levels between them, as
described in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="headwater-grid">Headwater Grid</DocLink>
section of that manual. This chapter states how each node is populated for desiccation cracking. Only the flaw node is specific to the
mechanism; the other seven follow the crest-crack conventions of the shared manual without change.

## Node 1: Flaw

*Question:* Does a continuous transverse desiccation crack exist across the crest?

The flaw probability $P_{flaw}$ is the result of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-probability.mdx): the scored probability from the surface layer,
climate, and crest soil, or 1.0 when continuous desiccation cracking has been observed, or a negligibly small value when the mechanism is
inapplicable or the weighted sum is below 13 without an observation. It is the same at every pool level.

## Node 2: Initiation

*Question:* Is the hydraulic shear stress on the walls of the crack large enough to initiate concentrated leak erosion?

The model applies the headwater gate of the crest-crack models (<EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-8" fallback="Equation 8" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="crest-crack-models-headwater-gate">Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual) at the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$ of
[Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#bottom-of-crack-elevation): initiation is 1.0 at pool levels above $z_{bc}$ and zero at or below it. When the
mechanism is inapplicable, initiation is zero at every pool level, as that section states. No shear stress is computed and tailwater plays no
part. Certain initiation above the bottom of the crack is a shared assumption of the crest-crack models (see the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="assumptions-and-limitations" anchor="initiation">Initiation</DocLink>
assumptions of the CLE Common Methods manual).

## Node 3: Continuation

*Question:* Does an unfiltered seepage exit exist that allows erosion to continue?

Continuation follows the crest-crack pathway convention (<EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-14" fallback="Equation 14" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-3-continuation">Node 3: Continuation</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual). With no filter on the failure pathway, $P_{cont} = 1.0$ at every pool level. With a filter whose top is
at $z_{tf}$, the node takes the probability of continuing erosion $P_{ce}$, the node result of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening
model (written $P_{cont}$ in that manual; see its
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="output-and-interpretation" anchor="node-result">Node Result</DocLink>
section), at pool levels at or below $z_{tf}$, and 1.0 above it, where the water in the upper part of the crack passes over the filter's top. A
filter caps the pathway when its top reaches the crest and no full-pathway roof materials are given (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="the-failure-pathway">The Failure Pathway</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual); such a filter gives $P_{ce}$ at every pool level. The value of $P_{ce}$ is evaluated once, from the filter and
the base soils upstream of it (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="method-selection-and-governing-base-soil">Method Selection and Governing Base Soil</DocLink>
in the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual).

Because initiation is zero at or below the bottom of the crack, a filter that does not cap the pathway and whose top lies at or below $z_{bc}$
never affects the SRP: at every pool level where initiation occurs, the pool is above the filter's top and continuation is 1.0. A filter that caps
the pathway is the exception at any crack depth: its continuation value $P_{ce}$ and the below-filter roof-support value apply at every pool level,
including, for a crack of zero depth, the pool levels above the crest at which initiation occurs.

## Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

*Question:* Does a continuous stable roof form over the enlarging pipe, so that it stays open?

The roof-support probability $P_{roof}(h)$ is the minimum roof-support probability over the material set that applies at pool level $h$ (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-4-progression--roof-support">Node 4: Progression — Roof Support</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual): the below-filter materials, taken at the bottom-of-crack elevation, at pool levels at or below $z_{tf}$ (and at
every pool level when the filter caps the pathway), and the full-pathway materials above $z_{tf}$ (and at every pool level when no filter is
present). Roof support therefore changes at the filter's top in step with continuation.

The crest soils of the flaw assessment and the roof materials are separate inputs. The crest soils describe the material the crack crosses at the
crest and set the likelihood and depth of cracking; the roof materials describe the materials along the failure pathway and set the probability
that the enlarging pipe stays open.

## Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

*Question:* Does the enlarging pipe remain unfilled, rather than being filled and healed by material carried in from upstream?

The probability of no crack filling $P_{nf}$ depends only on the embankment type, per
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-5-progression--no-crack-filling">Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual, following <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" form="narrative" />. It is the same at every pool level.

## Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

*Question:* Does the flow into the enlarging pipe remain unrestricted by upstream seepage-limiting elements?

The probability of no flow limitation $P_{nl}(h)$ is the probability of the upstream facing element times that of the governing wall (the
most effective of the walls listed), counting only the elements that reach the pool level; an element that is absent or does not reach it
contributes 1.0 (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation">Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual). When a top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ is supplied, the walls apply at pool levels at or below $z_{tw}$;
above it the walls drop out and only an upstream facing element remains. With no element listed, $P_{nl} = 1.0$.

## Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

*Questions:* Do detection and intervention fail (Node 7)? Does an uncontrolled release of impounded water occur (Node 8)?

Both nodes are the shared intervention and breach inputs, documented with their defaults in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="nodes-7-and-8-unsuccessful-intervention-and-breach">Nodes 7 and 8</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. The model applies them without change.

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each headwater (pool) elevation $h$ is the product of the eight node probabilities, by <EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-1" fallback="Equation 1" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="srp-computation">SRP Computation</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. In this model $P_{init}$, $P_{cont}$, $P_{roof}$, and $P_{nl}$ vary with $h$, and the other nodes are
the same at every pool level.

Wherever the product is zero — for example at and below the bottom of the crack, or where an intervention or breach probability is zero — the
SRP is reported as a negligibly small value rather than zero. Above the bottom of the crack the SRP changes with pool level only where the pool
crosses the top of a filter or the top of a wall that lies above $z_{bc}$. Every node is evaluated directly from the inputs, so the curve can be
reproduced exactly from the reported node values, apart from any sampling in the continuation probability $P_{ce}$.

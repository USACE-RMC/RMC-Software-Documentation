# Crack Depth

The flaw assessment passes one elevation to the downstream nodes along with the flaw probability: the bottom of the desiccation crack. Water can
enter the crack only when the pool rises above it, so it sets the pool level at which initiation begins. It is also the elevation at which the user cuts the
below-filter pathway when describing the below-filter roof materials (see
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#shared-inputs)); the model does not cut the pathway itself (see
[Crest-crack models: bottom of the crack](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#crest-crack-models-bottom-of-the-crack)
in the CLE Common Methods manual). This chapter documents how the model obtains the crack depth from the climate, the crest soil, and the surface
layer.

The crack depth is computed whatever the flaw probability, including when the flaw probability is negligible or when the observation override sets
it to 1.0. The exception is an inapplicable mechanism — every listed crest soil a clean or nearly clean sand or gravel — when the crack depth is
zero rather than tabulated (see [Inapplicable Mechanism](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#inapplicable-mechanism)).

## Tabulated Crack Depths

The crack depth $d_c$, in feet, is read from <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-8" /> or
<TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-11" />, adapted from Tables 5.10 to 5.13 of Fell et al.
(2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />. The tables are entered with three of the flaw inputs:

- **Surface layer** — a crest with no road or pathway takes <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-8" />; a gravel road and a
  paved crest both take the gravel-roadway depths of <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-11" />.
- **Climate** — the climate selection selects the row.
- **Soil column** — the governing crest-soil likelihood factor $LF_{cs}$ of [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#crest-soil), 1 to 4,
  selects the column. An unknown crest soil, scored 2, takes the column $LF_{cs} = 2$.

The tabulated depth never decreases with a drier climate, a more plastic soil, or an unprotected crest, although several neighboring entries are
equal — for example, the temperate and seasonal depths for a soil scored 1, and the arid depths with no road or pathway for soils scored 3 and 4.
The conditions that raise the flaw probability therefore never raise the bottom of the crack. Paving, however, lowers the flaw probability
without changing the depth, because a paved crest and a gravel road share the gravel-roadway depths. The deepest crack, 20 ft, occurs in an
arid climate on a crest with no road or pathway in a soil scored 3 or 4.

## Crack Depth from Measured Net Evaporation

When a measured average annual net evaporation $E_n$ is supplied, the crack depth is not read for one climate. Instead the model reads the four
depths for the user's surface layer and soil column — one for each climate — and interpolates linearly between them in $E_n$ on the same points as
the climate likelihood factor (<TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-4" /> in
[Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#climate)):

- $E_n$ of 425 mm or less takes the temperate depth.
- Between 425 and 450 mm, the depth moves linearly from the temperate to the seasonal depth; from 450 to 900 mm it is the seasonal depth.
- Between 900 and 925 mm, the depth moves linearly from the seasonal to the monsoonal depth; from 925 to 1500 mm it is the monsoonal depth.
- Above 1500 mm, the depth is the arid depth.

Measured net evaporation therefore resolves to the depth of one climate category except within the two short transitions. For example, on a crest
with no road or pathway and a lean clay core (soil score 2), a net evaporation of 437.5 mm gives a depth halfway between the temperate 3 ft and the
seasonal 5 ft, $d_c = 4$ ft.

:::

## Bottom-of-Crack Elevation

The bottom of the crack is the crest elevation less the crack depth:

<Equation equationKey="cle-desiccation-screening-eq-4" equation="z_{bc} = z_c - d_c" />

where $z_c$ is the crest elevation. This is the crest-crack relationship of the CLE Common Methods manual (see
[Crest-crack models: bottom of the crack](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#crest-crack-models-bottom-of-the-crack)).
The crack depth is in feet and is subtracted without conversion, so the crest elevation must be in feet for $z_{bc}$ to be correct (see
[Units](./03-input-parameters.mdx#units) in Input Parameters). When the mechanism is inapplicable — every listed crest soil a clean or nearly clean
sand or gravel — the crack depth is zero and $z_{bc} = z_c$.

## Cracks of Zero Depth

Three entries of <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-11" /> are zero: a soil scored 1 under a paved crest or a gravel road in
a temperate, seasonal, or monsoonal climate. For these crests — and, when a net evaporation is supplied, for any value of 1500 mm or less on such a crest — the
bottom of the crack is at the crest. Because initiation requires the pool to rise above the bottom of the crack
(<EquationReference equationKey="cle-desiccation-screening-eq-5" /> in [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation)), the SRP is then
negligible at every pool level up to the crest, whatever the flaw probability. This holds even when continuous desiccation cracking has been
observed: the observation sets the flaw probability to 1.0 but supplies no crack depth.

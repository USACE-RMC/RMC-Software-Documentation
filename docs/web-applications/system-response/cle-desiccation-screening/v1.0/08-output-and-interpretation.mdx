# Output and Interpretation

The model reports the SRP at each evaluated pool level together with the probability of each of the eight nodes, in the form shared by the
crest-crack models and documented in
[Output and Interpretation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) of the CLE Common
Methods manual. This chapter documents the flaw-assessment results specific to desiccation cracking and how to read the curve for this mechanism.
When a filter intersects the failure pathway, the results of the shared continuation method are also reported, as described in
[Output and Interpretation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) of the Continuation
(Filter Evaluation) manual.

## Flaw-Assessment Results

The flaw assessment reports the quantities of [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx) and [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx):

- **Surface-layer, climate, and crest-soil likelihood factors** — $LF_s$, $LF_{cl}$, and $LF_{cs}$. The climate factor is the interpolated value
  when a net evaporation was supplied.
- **Governing crest soil** — the listed soil class whose score set $LF_{cs}$. It is absent when the crest soils are unknown and when the mechanism
  is inapplicable.
- **Whether the mechanism applies** — no only when every listed crest soil is a clean or nearly clean sand or gravel. The likelihood factor of the
  soil, the weighted sum, and the raw flaw probability are then reported as zero.
- **Weighted sum** $\Sigma$.
- **Raw flaw probability** $P_{raw}$ — the value from the weighted-sum curve, before the observation override; zero when the weighted sum is below
  13.
- **Flaw probability** $P_{flaw}$ — the value used in the event tree: 1.0 when the observation override applied, otherwise $P_{raw}$, with a zero
  replaced by a negligibly small value.
- **Crack depth** $d_c$, in feet, and the **bottom-of-crack elevation** $z_{bc}$.

## Interpreting the System Response Curve

The curve has the shape described for the crest-crack models in
[Interpreting the System Response Curve](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#interpreting-the-system-response-curve)
of the CLE Common Methods manual:

- **At and below the bottom of the crack** $z_{bc}$, the SRP is negligible, because initiation is zero.
- **Just above the bottom of the crack**, the SRP steps up to the product of the flaw probability and the continuation, progression, intervention,
  and breach nodes that apply at that pool level.
- **Further steps** occur only where the pool crosses the top of a filter $z_{tf}$ or the top of a wall $z_{tw}$ that lies above $z_{bc}$.

Two features are particular to desiccation cracking:

- **The height of the curve depends strongly on the crest description.** The flaw probability spans from negligible to 0.5 across the attainable
  weighted sums, so the surface layer, the climate, and the crest soil can move the curve by several orders of magnitude. The downstream nodes
  can control the height instead: a low continuation probability below a filter, or a strongly flow-limiting upstream element such as a modern
  cutoff wall (a probability of no flow limitation of 0.001), lowers the curve by up to three orders of magnitude at the pool levels where it
  applies.
- **The pool level at which the curve rises is also set by the crest description.** The same three descriptions select the crack depth, from zero
  to 20 ft below the crest. A crest whose tabulated depth is zero has a negligible SRP at every pool level up to the crest, even when continuous
  cracking has been observed (see [Crack Depth](./05-crack-depth.mdx#cracks-of-zero-depth)).

## Example

<TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-10" /> shows the node values for a homogeneous earthfill embankment with a crest at
elevation 100 ft, no road or pathway on the crest, a seasonal climate, and a lean clay (CL) crest soil. No filter intersects the failure pathway,
the full-pathway roof material is lean clay, a sheet pile wall upstream has its top at elevation 98 ft, and intervention and breach take their
defaults of 1.0. The flaw assessment gives $\Sigma = 3(4) + 2(2) + 1(2) = 18$, a flaw probability halfway in the logarithm between
1 × 10<sup>-2</sup> and 1 × 10<sup>-1</sup>, $P_{flaw} = 10^{-1.5} = 0.0316$, and a crack depth of 5 ft, so $z_{bc} = 95$ ft.

The flaw probability is 0.0316 at every pool level. Continuation, roof support, and crack filling are 1.0 at every pool level, because no filter
intersects the pathway, a lean clay roof material has a roof-support probability of 1.0, and the embankment is homogeneous earthfill.
Above the top of the wall the flow entering the upper part of the crack passes over the wall, so flow limitation no longer applies and the SRP
doubles. Had the crest carried a gravel road instead, the weighted sum would have been 13.5, the flaw probability 1.47 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, and the
crack depth 1.5 ft: the curve would be lower by more than an order of magnitude and would not rise until the pool exceeded elevation 98.5 ft.

## Checking the Result

The checks of
[Checking the Result](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#checking-the-result) in
the CLE Common Methods manual apply. For this model in particular:

- **Flaw probability** — the reported likelihood factors should match the inputs per [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-probability.mdx), and, when the
  mechanism applies, the weighted sum should equal $3\,LF_s + 2\,LF_{cl} + LF_{cs}$; when it is inapplicable the weighted sum and the soil
  likelihood factor are reported as zero (see [Inapplicable Mechanism](./04-flaw-probability.mdx#inapplicable-mechanism)). A flaw probability of 1.0 should appear only when the observation override applied.
- **Bottom of the crack** — when the mechanism applies, the bottom-of-crack elevation should equal the crest elevation less the tabulated depth of
  <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-8" /> or <TableReference tableKey="cle-desiccation-screening-table-11" />; when it is inapplicable, it equals the crest elevation. A bottom of crack on a different datum from the headwater series, or a crest
  elevation not in feet, misplaces the step of the curve (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#units)).
- **Plateau** — above the bottom of the crack, the SRP equals the product of the reported node values at that pool level.

## Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis

The model's results indicate whether concentrated leak erosion through desiccation cracking at the crest warrants detailed analysis; they are not a
substitute for one. Where the result is significant to a decision, the flaw probability and the crack depth — the two quantities the crest
description controls — should be re-examined with site evidence of cracking, such as test pits across the crest and observations after dry
seasons, and the methods of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> and the judgment of the risk team should replace the screening
values.

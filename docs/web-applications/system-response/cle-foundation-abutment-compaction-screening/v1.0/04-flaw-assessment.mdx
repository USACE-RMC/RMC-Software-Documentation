# Flaw Assessment

The flaw assessment populates Node 1 of the event tree with the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous zone of poorly compacted fill exists along
the foundation or abutment contact, and passes the base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$ to the downstream nodes. It follows the conventions shared by the
CLE family (see the [Flaw Conventions](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions) chapter of the
common methods manual): risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring of three factors, conversion of the weighted sum to a raw flaw probability,
and a flaw-continuity stage. This chapter documents the factors, tables, and rules specific to the mechanism. The assessment is deterministic and
the same at every pool level.

## Base-of-Flaw Elevation

The flaw is idealized for initiation as a cylindrical pipe whose invert is the base of the flaw. Its elevation follows the contact location the
user selects (see [Flaw Location](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-location)):

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-eq-1"
  equation="z_f = \begin{cases} z_{fc} & \text{foundation contact} \\[4pt] z_{np} + \tfrac{1}{3}\,\bigl(z_c - z_{np}\bigr) & \text{abutment contact} \end{cases}"
/>

where $z_{fc}$ is the embankment–foundation contact elevation, $z_{np}$ the normal pool elevation, and $z_c$ the crest elevation. A foundation
flaw sits on the contact itself, where the fill was placed on the foundation. An abutment contact spans a range of elevations up the valley wall,
and the model places the flaw on it by the normal-pool heuristic that the core compaction model also uses (see
[Compaction models: base of the flaw](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
in the common methods manual, where the basis of the heuristic is addressed). The crest must lie above the normal pool.

The base of the flaw sets the pool level above which initiation can occur, the elevation of the seepage path through the section, and the level
against which the top of an upstream wall is checked (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Engineering Basis]
The placement of an abutment-contact flaw at one third of the way from the normal pool to the crest requires an engineering basis specific to the
abutment case: why a single elevation on the abutment contact is representative of a flaw that can lie anywhere up the valley wall, and why the
normal-pool heuristic, rather than for example the lowest abutment elevation below normal pool or a user-supplied elevation, is used. The basis
should also address that the placement is unconservative for a flaw lower on the abutment, because it gives a negligible SRP up to the heuristic
elevation above normal pool (see Assumption 2 in
[Assumptions and Limitations](./06-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#flaw-location)).
:::

### Foundation preparation

The likelihood factor $LF_{fdn}$ of the foundation preparation factor is read from the material and preparation selections
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-7" />). The two materials have separate tables, which differ only in the
well-prepared choice each allows.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-7"
  alt="Likelihood factor of the foundation preparation factor"
  caption="Likelihood factor of the foundation preparation factor."
  colAlign={['left', 'center', 'center']}
  headers={[[{ value: 'Foundation Preparation' }, { value: 'Soil' }, { value: 'Rock' }]]}

    ['1', 'Not accepted', '3.5', '3.5'],

/>

No preparation choice scores 2, 3, or 4: the factor takes the value 1 for a well-prepared surface and 3.5 otherwise, and an Unknown preparation is
scored as an irregular or poorly prepared one. A likelihood factor of 1 on this factor forces the flaw probability to a negligible value (see
[Well-Prepared Surface](#well-prepared-surface)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-07 — Engineering Basis]
The likelihood factors of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-7" /> require an engineering basis: 1 for compacted
soil and for a regular or treated rock surface, 3.5 for an irregular or poorly prepared surface, and 3.5 for an Unknown preparation (scored the
same as irregular or poorly prepared, rather than at an intermediate value). Cite the source table in Fell et al. (2024), or provide the
engineering-judgment attribution, including why the factor has no intermediate categories between 1 and 3.5.
:::

### Section geometry

The section geometry factor scores the base width $W$ and the height $H_s$ of the embankment section — the core section when one is supplied,
otherwise the shell — as derived in
[Section geometry used in scoring](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#section-geometry-used-in-scoring)
in the common methods manual, or the values the user supplies in their place. The base width and the width-to-height ratio are each placed in a
band (<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-9" />), and the less favorable of the two governs:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-eq-2"
  equation="LF_{geom} = \max\bigl(LF_W(W),\; LF_{W/H}(W/H_s)\bigr)"
/>

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-9"
  alt="Banding of the section base width and width-to-height ratio"
  caption="Banding of the section base width and width-to-height ratio."
  colAlign={['center', 'center', 'center']}
  headers={[[{ value: 'Likelihood Factor' }, { value: 'Base Width W' }, { value: 'Width-to-Height Ratio W/H<sub>s</sub>' }]]}
  columns={[
    ['1', '2', '3', '4'],
    ['Greater than 50', 'Greater than 30, up to 50', 'Greater than 15, up to 30', '15 or less'],
    ['Greater than 2', 'Greater than 0.75, up to 2', 'Greater than 0.25, up to 0.75', '0.25 or less'],
  ]}
/>

A narrow section scores a higher likelihood factor than a wide one. A value exactly on a threshold takes the higher likelihood factor (the
band of the narrower section): a base width of exactly 50 scores 2, and a ratio of exactly 2 scores 2. The base width is banded at fixed numerical thresholds in the length unit of the inputs (see
[Units and Datum](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#units-and-datum) in the common
methods manual). The common methods manual writes the base width as $W = W_t + S_{us} + S_{ds}$, where $W_t$ is the top width of the section and
$S_{us}$ and $S_{ds}$ are the horizontal spans of its upstream and downstream faces. For a section whose faces each have a single segment
extending over the full height $H_s$, the spans are $S_{us} = m_{us} H_s$ and $S_{ds} = m_{ds} H_s$, where $m_{us}$ and $m_{ds}$ are the upstream
and downstream face slopes (horizontal per vertical), so the ratio is $W/H_s = W_t/H_s + m_{us} + m_{ds}$: the top width over the height plus the
two face slopes. Such a section whose two face slopes sum to more than 2 always scores 1 on the ratio, and its geometry factor is then set by the
base width alone. The ratio band governs when its likelihood factor exceeds that of the base width, as it can for
steep-sided sections such as a narrow central core. The identity holds only for dimensions derived
from the section; where the user supplies the base width, the height, or both in their place, the ratio is the ratio of the values used and need
not follow the face slopes.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-09 — Engineering Basis]
The banding of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-9" /> requires an engineering basis: the base width thresholds of
50, 30, and 15, the width-to-height ratio thresholds of 2, 0.75, and 0.25, and the rule that the larger of the two likelihood factors governs.
State the length unit the base width thresholds assume (they are applied without unit conversion), and cite the source table in Fell et al. (2024)
or provide the engineering-judgment attribution.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-10 — Applicability]
The section geometry factor scores the embankment section supplied for the analysis, which also sets the seepage path length of initiation.
For an abutment contact, the section through the flaw lies toward the side of the valley and is lower and narrower than the maximum section. State
whether the user should supply the section at the flaw location, or supply the maximum section and enter the base width and height at the flaw
location in the optional flaw base width and flaw height inputs.
:::

## Weighted Sum and Raw Flaw Probability

The three likelihood factors combine into the weighted sum of
[Flaw Conventions](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#weighted-sum) in the common
methods manual, with this model's risk factors:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-eq-3"
  equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_{fdn} + 2\,LF_{comp} + LF_{geom}"
/>

The weighted sum is converted to the raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$ by interpolating the logarithm of the probability linearly between the points
of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-10" />, as described in
[Flaw probability from the weighted sum](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
in the common methods manual. The model has no lower threshold on the weighted sum.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-10"
  alt="Raw flaw probability by weighted sum"
  caption="Raw flaw probability by weighted sum."
  colAlign={['center', 'center']}
  headers={[[{ value: 'Weighted Sum' }, { value: 'Raw Flaw Probability' }]]}
  columns={[
    ['9', '11', '13', '18', '24'],
    ['Negligible', '1 × 10<sup>-4</sup>', '7 × 10<sup>-4</sup>', '1 × 10<sup>-2</sup>', '1 × 10<sup>-1</sup>'],
  ]}
/>

Unless the surface is well prepared, the foundation preparation factor scores 3.5, so the weighted sum lies between 13.5 (every other factor at 1)
and 22.5 (every other factor at 4). Only the part of the curve between 13 and 24 is therefore used, and the raw flaw probability ranges from about
9.1 × 10<sup>-4</sup> to about 5.6 × 10<sup>-2</sup>. A well-prepared surface does not consult the curve.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-11 — Engineering Basis]
The weighted-sum curve of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-10" /> (weighted sums 9, 11, 13, 18, 24 → negligible,
1 × 10<sup>-4</sup>, 7 × 10<sup>-4</sup>, 1 × 10<sup>-2</sup>, 1 × 10<sup>-1</sup>) requires an engineering basis. Cite the figure or table in
Fell et al. (2024) from which it is read for a flaw along the foundation or abutment contact, or provide the engineering-judgment attribution,
including the value of 7 × 10<sup>-4</sup> at a weighted sum of 13, where the wall or cliff compaction model, on the same weighted sums,
uses 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup>.
:::

## Well-Prepared Surface

The foundation preparation factor is an incompatible-condition factor in the sense of
[Flaw probability from the weighted sum](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
in the common methods manual: when it scores 1 — compacted soil on a soil foundation, or a regular or treated rock surface on a rock foundation —
the raw flaw probability is zero whatever the other two factors score, and the final flaw probability is negligible. Such a surface is taken as
incompatible with a continuous zone of poor compaction along the contact.

The model has no observation override: no observation of leakage or wet spots sets the flaw probability to 1.0 (see
[Observation Overrides](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) in the
common methods manual). The well-prepared-surface rule is the only rule that sets the flaw probability without scoring it.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-12 — Engineering Basis]
Two decisions require an engineering basis: (1) a well-prepared foundation or abutment surface (a foundation preparation likelihood factor of 1)
forces the flaw probability to a negligible value regardless of how poorly the contact zone was compacted or how narrow the section is; and (2) the
model accepts no observation — such as seepage, wet spots, or sinkholes along the contact — that sets the flaw probability to 1.0, unlike the core,
conduit, and wall or cliff compaction models. Provide the literature source or the engineering-judgment attribution for each.
:::

## Flaw-Continuity Stage

The raw flaw probability is multiplied by the flaw-continuity probability $P_{cf}$, read from the curve shared by the three compaction models that
apply the stage (see
[Flaw-Continuity Stage](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-continuity-stage) in the
common methods manual, where the basis of the curve is addressed). In this model the curve is keyed to the likelihood factor of the contact-zone
compaction method, the factor with a risk factor of 2, and not to the foundation preparation factor, which carries the highest risk factor:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-eq-4"
  equation="P_{flaw} = \max\bigl(P_{raw}(\Sigma) \times P_{cf}(LF_{comp}),\; \text{negligible}\bigr)"
/>

The flaw-continuity probability is negligible for special methods with good documentation, 1 × 10<sup>-4</sup> for special methods,
1 × 10<sup>-3</sup> for bulldozer compaction or an Unknown method, and 1 × 10<sup>-1</sup> for poor compaction in thick layers. The final flaw
probability is never reported as exactly zero: where the product is zero or smaller than the negligible value, the negligible value is reported, as
for the SRP (see
[SRP Computation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation) in the common methods
manual).

Across the table, the compaction method moves the flaw probability far more than the section geometry does: each step from one compaction method to
the next poorer one raises it by more than an order of magnitude, through both the weighted sum and the flaw-continuity probability, while the
section geometry moves it by a factor of about 3 to 5 across its full range. The largest flaw probability any combination of inputs can produce is about 5.6 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, for poor compaction in
thick layers on a section that scores 4 on geometry, so the SRP of the model never exceeds that value.

### Example

For a soil foundation of Unknown preparation ($LF_{fdn} = 3.5$), an Unknown compaction method ($LF_{comp} = 3$), and a section with a base width
greater than 50 and a width-to-height ratio greater than 2 ($LF_{geom} = 1$):

- The weighted sum is $\Sigma = 3 \times 3.5 + 2 \times 3 + 1 = 17.5$ (<EquationReference equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-eq-3" />).
- Interpolating the logarithm of the probability nine tenths of the way from 7 × 10<sup>-4</sup> at 13 to 1 × 10<sup>-2</sup> at 18 gives
  $P_{raw} \approx 7.7 \times 10^{-3}$.
- The flaw-continuity probability for $LF_{comp} = 3$ is 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, so
  $P_{flaw} \approx 7.7 \times 10^{-6}$ (<EquationReference equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-eq-4" />).

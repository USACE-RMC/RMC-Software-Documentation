# Flaw Assessment

The flaw assessment populates Node 1 of the concentrated leak erosion (CLE) event tree with the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous zone of
poorly compacted fill exists along the foundation or abutment contact, and passes the base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$ to the downstream nodes. It
follows the conventions shared by the CLE family (see the
[Flaw Conventions](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions)
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual): risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring of three factors, conversion of the weighted sum to a raw flaw
probability, and a flaw-continuity stage. This chapter documents the factors, tables, and rules specific to the mechanism. The assessment is
deterministic and the same at every pool level.

## Base-of-Flaw Elevation

The flaw is idealized for initiation as a cylindrical pipe whose invert is the base of the flaw $z_f$. Its elevation follows the contact location
(see [Flaw Location](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-location)):

- **Foundation contact** — $z_f$ is the embankment–foundation contact elevation $z_{fc}$. A foundation flaw sits on the contact itself, where the
  fill was placed on the foundation.
- **Abutment contact** — an abutment contact spans a range of elevations up the valley wall, and the model places the flaw on it by the
  normal-pool heuristic documented in
  [Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
  in the CLE Common Methods manual: one third of the way from the normal pool elevation $z_{np}$ to the crest elevation $z_c$. The crest must lie
  above the normal pool.

The base of the flaw sets the pool level above which initiation can occur, the elevation of the seepage path through the section, and the level
against which the top of an upstream wall is checked (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Engineering Basis]
The placement of an abutment-contact flaw at one third of the way from the normal pool to the crest requires an engineering basis specific to the
abutment case: why a single elevation on the abutment contact is representative of a flaw that can lie anywhere up the valley wall, and why the
normal-pool heuristic, rather than for example the lowest abutment elevation below normal pool or a user-supplied elevation, is used. The basis
should also address that the placement is unconservative for a flaw lower on the abutment, because it gives a negligible SRP up to the heuristic
elevation above normal pool (see Assumption 2 in
[Assumptions and Limitations](./06-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#flaw-location)).
:::

## Factor Scores

Three factors are scored. Each factor $j$ carries a risk factor $RF_j$ and a likelihood factor $LF_j$ on the scale of 1 (least likely condition
for a flaw) to 4 (most likely), following the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scheme of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-5" />).

### Foundation Preparation

The likelihood factor $LF_{fdn}$ of the foundation preparation factor is read from the material and preparation selections
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-6" />). The two materials have separate columns, which differ only in
the well-prepared choice each allows.

No preparation choice scores 2, 3, or 4: the factor takes the value 1 for a well-prepared surface and 3.5 otherwise, and an Unknown preparation is
scored as an irregular or poorly prepared one. A likelihood factor of 1 on this factor forces the flaw probability to a negligible value (see
[Well-Prepared Surface](#well-prepared-surface)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-07 — Engineering Basis]
The likelihood factors of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-6" /> require an engineering basis: 1 for
compacted soil and for a regular or treated rock surface, 3.5 for an irregular or poorly prepared surface, and 3.5 for an Unknown preparation
(scored the same as irregular or poorly prepared, rather than at an intermediate value). Cite the source table in Fell et al. (2024), or provide the
engineering-judgment attribution, including why the factor has no intermediate categories between 1 and 3.5.
:::

### Section Geometry Factor

The section geometry factor scores the dimensions of the embankment section through which a poorly compacted zone would have to run.

The likelihood factor $LF_{geom}$ of the section geometry factor is the larger of two banded scores, one of the base width $W$ and one of the
width-to-height ratio $W/H_s$:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-eq-1"
  equation="LF_W = \begin{cases} 1 & W > 50 \\ 2 & 30 < W \le 50 \\ 3 & 15 < W \le 30 \\ 4 & W \le 15 \end{cases} \qquad LF_{W/H} = \begin{cases} 1 & W/H_s > 2 \\ 2 & 0.75 < W/H_s \le 2 \\ 3 & 0.25 < W/H_s \le 0.75 \\ 4 & W/H_s \le 0.25 \end{cases}"
/>

<Equation equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-eq-2" equation="LF_{geom} = \max\left(LF_W,\ LF_{W/H}\right)" />

where $LF_W$ is the base-width score, $LF_{W/H}$ is the width-to-height score, $W$ is the base width of the embankment section, and $H_s$ is the
height of the section, both in the length unit of the elevations. $W$ and $H_s$ are derived from the core section when one is supplied, otherwise
from the shell section, as described in
[Section Geometry Used in Scoring](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#section-geometry-used-in-scoring)
in the CLE Common Methods manual, unless the flaw base width and flaw height are supplied in their place (see
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-factor-selections)). <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-8" /> lists
the bands.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-8"
  alt="Section geometry likelihood factor bands"
  caption="Section geometry likelihood factor bands; the larger of the two scores governs."
  colAlign={['center', 'center', 'center']}
  headers={[[{ value: 'Likelihood Factor' }, { value: 'Base Width W' }, { value: 'Width-to-Height Ratio W/H<sub>s</sub>' }]]}
  columns={[
    ['1', '2', '3', '4'],
    ['Greater than 50', 'Greater than 30, up to 50', 'Greater than 15, up to 30', '15 or less'],
    ['Greater than 2', 'Greater than 0.75, up to 2', 'Greater than 0.25, up to 0.75', '0.25 or less'],
  ]}
/>

Taking the larger of the two scores means that either a narrow section or a slender one is enough to make a continuous flaw more likely: a section
scores 1 only when it is both wider than 50 and more than twice as wide as it is high. A value exactly on a band boundary takes the higher
likelihood factor of the narrower band; for example, a base width of exactly 50 scores 2, and a ratio of exactly 2 scores 2. The base-width
boundaries are applied to the base width as entered, with no unit conversion (see [Unit System](./03-input-parameters.mdx#unit-system)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-09 — Engineering Basis]
The section geometry bands of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-8" /> (base width boundaries 50, 30, and 15;
width-to-height ratio boundaries 2, 0.75, and 0.25) and the rule that the larger of the two scores governs require an engineering basis. Provide
the source table of Fell et al. (2024) or the engineering-judgment attribution.
:::

Because a derived base width is the top width of the section plus the horizontal spans of both faces, a section whose faces each run in a single
segment over the full height, and whose two face slopes (horizontal per vertical) sum to more than 2, always scores 1 on the ratio; its section
geometry factor is then set by the base width alone. The ratio band governs when its likelihood factor exceeds that of the base width, as it can
for steep-sided sections such as a narrow central core. This holds only for dimensions derived from the section; where the base width, the height,
or both are supplied in their place, the ratio is the ratio of the values used and need not follow the face slopes.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-10 — Applicability]
The section geometry factor scores the embankment section supplied for the analysis, which also sets the seepage path length of initiation.
For an abutment contact, the section through the flaw lies toward the side of the valley and is lower and narrower than the maximum section. State
whether the user should supply the section at the flaw location, or supply the maximum section and enter the base width and height at the flaw
location in the optional flaw base width and flaw height inputs.
:::

## Weighted Sum

The three likelihood factors combine into the weighted sum described in
[Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#weighted-sum)
in the CLE Common Methods manual, which for this model is:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-eq-3"
  equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_{fdn} + 2\,LF_{comp} + LF_{geom}"
/>

where $\Sigma$ is the weighted sum, $LF_{fdn}$ is the likelihood factor of the foundation preparation, $LF_{comp}$ is the likelihood factor of the
contact-zone compaction method, and $LF_{geom}$ is the likelihood factor of the section geometry, all dimensionless.

## Raw Flaw Probability

The weighted sum is converted to the raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$ by interpolating the logarithm of the probability linearly between the points
of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-9" />, as described in
[Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. The model has no lower threshold on the weighted sum.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-9"
  alt="Raw flaw probability by weighted sum"
  caption="Raw flaw probability by weighted sum."

/>

Unless the surface is well prepared, the foundation preparation factor scores 3.5, so the weighted sum lies between 13.5 (every other factor at 1)
and 22.5 (every other factor at 4). Only the part of the curve between 13 and 24 is therefore used, and the raw flaw probability ranges from about
9.1 × 10<sup>-4</sup> to about 5.6 × 10<sup>-2</sup>. A well-prepared surface does not consult the curve.

The curve is the one Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> assign to pool levels above the pool of record. The model applies it
at every pool level, whatever the headwater, and takes no pool-of-record input; the companion curve for pool levels at or below the pool of record
is not used. The flaw probability therefore takes no credit for the embankment's past performance up to its pool of record. This is a conservative
screening simplification; past performance can be credited in a detailed analysis.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-11 — Engineering Basis]
The weighted-sum curve of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-9" /> (weighted sums 9, 11, 13, 18, 24 →
negligible, 1 × 10<sup>-4</sup>, 7 × 10<sup>-4</sup>, 1 × 10<sup>-2</sup>, 1 × 10<sup>-1</sup>) requires an engineering basis. Cite the figure or
table in Fell et al. (2024) from which it is read for a flaw along the foundation or abutment contact, or provide the engineering-judgment
attribution, including the value of 7 × 10<sup>-4</sup> at a weighted sum of 13.
:::

## Well-Prepared Surface

The foundation preparation factor is an incompatible-condition factor in the sense of
[Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
in the CLE Common Methods manual: when it scores 1 — compacted soil on a soil foundation, or a regular or treated rock surface on a rock foundation —
the raw flaw probability is zero whatever the other two factors score, and the final flaw probability is negligible. Such a surface is taken as
incompatible with a continuous zone of poor compaction along the contact. This is an engineering-judgment screening rule: where the foundation
or abutment was prepared for the first lift of fill by compacting the soil surface, or by regularizing or treating the rock surface, poor
compaction along the contact is not considered further at the screening level. The well-prepared-surface rule is the only rule that sets the flaw
probability without scoring it.

## Flaw-Continuity Stage

The raw flaw probability is multiplied by the flaw-continuity probability $P_{cf}$, read from the shared flaw-continuity curve (see
[Flaw-Continuity Stage](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-continuity-stage)
in the CLE Common Methods manual, where the basis of the curve is addressed). In this model the curve is keyed to the likelihood factor of the
contact-zone compaction method, the factor with a risk factor of 2, and not to the foundation preparation factor, which carries the highest risk
factor. The final flaw probability is never reported as exactly zero:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-eq-4"
  equation="P_{flaw} = \max\bigl(P_{raw}(\Sigma) \times P_{cf}(LF_{comp}),\; \text{negligible}\bigr)"
/>

where $P_{raw}(\Sigma)$ is the raw flaw probability at the weighted sum, $P_{cf}(LF_{comp})$ is the flaw-continuity probability at the compaction
likelihood factor, and "negligible" is the negligibly small value at which the SRP is also floored (see
[SRP Computation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation)
in the CLE Common Methods manual). The flaw-continuity probability is negligible for special methods with good documentation,
1 × 10<sup>-4</sup> for special methods, 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup> for bulldozer compaction or an Unknown method, and 1 × 10<sup>-1</sup> for poor
compaction in thick layers.

## Observation Override

The model has no observation override: no observation of leakage or wet spots sets the flaw probability to 1.0 (see
[Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides)
in the CLE Common Methods manual). Observed leakage through the embankment exiting the downstream face is attributed to poor compaction within the
core, where it demonstrates the flaw; it is not applied to the foundation or abutment contact as well, to avoid counting the same observed leakage
twice.

Across the table, the compaction method moves the flaw probability far more than the section geometry does: each step from one compaction method
to the next poorer one raises it by more than an order of magnitude, through both the weighted sum and the flaw-continuity probability, while the
section geometry moves it by a factor of about 3 to 5 across its full range. The largest flaw probability any combination of inputs can produce is
about 5.6 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, for poor compaction in thick layers on a section that scores 4 on section geometry, so the SRP of the model never
exceeds that value.

## Worked Example

For a soil foundation of Unknown preparation ($LF_{fdn} = 3.5$), an Unknown compaction method ($LF_{comp} = 3$), and a section with a base width
greater than 50 ft and a width-to-height ratio greater than 2 ($LF_{geom} = 1$):

- The weighted sum is $\Sigma = 3 \times 3.5 + 2 \times 3 + 1 = 17.5$ (<EquationReference equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-eq-3" />).
- Interpolating the logarithm of the probability nine tenths of the way from 7 × 10<sup>-4</sup> at 13 to 1 × 10<sup>-2</sup> at 18 gives
  $P_{raw} \approx 7.7 \times 10^{-3}$.
- The flaw-continuity probability for $LF_{comp} = 3$ is 1 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, so
  $P_{flaw} \approx 7.7 \times 10^{-6}$ (<EquationReference equationKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-eq-4" />).

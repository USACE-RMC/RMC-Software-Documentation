# Input Parameters

This chapter documents the inputs specific to the concentrated leak erosion (CLE) foundation or abutment compaction model: the unit system, the
flaw location and the elevations it requires, the three flaw-factor selections, the optional section dimensions used in scoring, and the
filter-presence selection. The model also takes the inputs shared by the CLE family, documented in the
[Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters)
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual and listed under [Shared Inputs](#shared-inputs) below. Each group gives the short facts for its inputs in
a table, followed by a description of each input.

## Unit System

The model takes a unit system input, US customary (the default) or SI. All elevations and lengths are entered in the length unit of that system
(feet or meters) and share one vertical datum. The input changes no formula in this model, and quantities with fixed units (critical shear stress
in lb/ft², initial pipe diameter in millimeters) are stated in those units regardless of the unit system. The base-width bands of the section
geometry factor are applied to the base width as entered, with no unit conversion (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#section-geometry-factor)). The datum convention shared by the CLE family is documented in
[Unit System](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#unit-system)
in the CLE Common Methods manual.

## Flaw Location

The flaw lies along the foundation contact or along an abutment contact. The contact location decides how the base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$ is
resolved (<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-2" />).

The contact location is one of two values. For a foundation contact — fill placed on the foundation along the valley floor — the base of the flaw
is the embankment–foundation contact elevation $z_{fc}$, the elevation of the base of the embankment on the foundation at the section analyzed. For
an abutment contact — fill placed against an abutment along the valley wall — the base of the flaw is placed one third of the way from the normal
pool elevation $z_{np}$ to the crest (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#base-of-flaw-elevation)); the normal pool elevation is the
normal operating pool, and the crest must lie above it.

In either case the resolved base of the flaw must satisfy the conditions common to the compaction models: it must be a positive elevation, no
higher than the top of the embankment section used for initiation and no lower than the lowest segment bottom of either face of that section (see
[Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-01 — Applicability]
The model treats the foundation and abutment contacts as two alternative locations of one flaw, selected by the user, and evaluates one of them
per run. State how the user should choose between them for a given embankment — for example, whether an abutment contact is any contact against a
valley wall steeper than some inclination, and whether an embankment whose foundation and abutment contacts are both of concern should be
evaluated once for each location, with the larger result or both results carried forward.
:::

## Flaw-Factor Selections

Three selections describe the contact, and two optional dimensions replace the section width and height in scoring
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-3" />). How each selection is scored is documented in
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx).

The foundation material is the material the embankment is placed against at the contact, soil or rock. No combined soil-and-rock choice is
offered: a contact that is partly soil and partly rock, such as a valley floor of alluvium against rock abutments, is evaluated by separate
analyses, one with the soil material for the part of the contact on soil and one with the rock material for the part on rock.

The foundation preparation is the condition of the foundation or abutment surface on which the fill was placed: compacted soil (soil only), a
regular or treated rock surface (rock only), irregular or poorly prepared (soil or rock), or Unknown (soil or rock), with the likelihood factors of
<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-6" />. The well-prepared choice depends on the material, and the
well-prepared choice of the other material is not accepted. A compacted soil surface, or a regular rock surface or one treated to make it so,
forces the flaw probability to a negligible value (see [Well-Prepared Surface](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#well-prepared-surface)). The choices that
score above 1 — irregular or poorly prepared, and Unknown — score the same for both materials, so the material decides only which well-prepared
choice is available. Unknown is scored as irregular or poorly prepared.

The contact-zone compaction method is how the embankment fill in the zone against the foundation or abutment was compacted, with the likelihood
factors of <TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-compaction-screening-table-7" />: special methods with good documentation (special
compaction methods were used in the contact zone, and their use is well documented); special methods (without good documentation); bulldozer, layer
at the limit of compaction (the fill was placed and compacted by bulldozer, without compaction by rollers, in layers at the limit of the compaction
equipment); poor compaction, thick layers (poor compaction methods were used in thick layers, leaving the soil poorly compacted or allowing it to
segregate against the foundation surface); or Unknown, scored as bulldozer compaction.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Definition]
The foundation preparation choices (Compacted soil; Regular or treated rock surface; Irregular or poorly prepared) and the contact-zone
compaction method choices (Special methods with good documentation; Special methods; Bulldozer, layer at the limit of compaction; Poor
compaction, thick layers) are described above only by their names. Define what each covers so the user can select between them — for example,
what treatment makes a rock surface "regular or treated" (dental concrete, slush grouting, removal of overhangs, shaping of steps), what counts as
"special methods" of contact-zone compaction (hand-operated or rubber-tired compaction, moisture conditioning, placement of a selected contact
fill), and what layer thickness separates the bulldozer and poor-compaction choices — or cite the table in Fell et al. (2024) that defines them.
:::

The flaw base width and flaw height are optional replacements for the base width $W$ and height $H_s$ of the embankment section, which the model
otherwise derives from the section geometry (see
[Section Geometry Used in Scoring](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#section-geometry-used-in-scoring)
in the CLE Common Methods manual). Each may be supplied alone. A supplied value replaces the derived value in the scoring of the section geometry
factor only; the initiation analysis keeps the section geometry.

When a filter is present, the continuation inputs are required, and the continuation node is evaluated by the shared continuation method. When it
is absent, the exit is unfiltered, continuation is certain, and the continuation inputs are not used. The continuation inputs — the filter
description and the base soils it must protect — are documented in the
[Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/input-parameters)
chapter of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-04 — Definition]
State what counts as a filter intercepting the pathway along a foundation or abutment contact — for example, a filter or drain zone placed on the
foundation downstream of the core, a chimney filter reaching the foundation, or a filter blanket on an abutment — and which soils the user should
supply as the base soils to the continuation method: the contact-zone fill, the core soil, the foundation soil, or each of them.
:::

## Shared Inputs

The model also takes the following inputs, each documented in the CLE Common Methods manual:

- **Hydraulic loading** — the headwater series and optional tailwater series (see
  [Hydraulic Loading](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#hydraulic-loading)).
- **Embankment description** — the crest elevation, the embankment type, the shell section, and the optional core section (see
  [Embankment Description](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description)).
  The crest also serves as the top of dam in the abutment heuristic.
- **Embankment materials** — the soil classifications of the materials the pipe passes through, which govern roof support (see
  [Failure-Pathway Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#failure-pathway-inputs)).
- **Upstream seepage-limiting elements** — the elements present and the optional top-of-wall elevation, which is checked against the base of the
  flaw (see
  [Upstream Seepage-Limiting Elements](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#upstream-seepage-limiting-elements)).
- **Initiation material inputs** — the core soil classification, and the optional critical shear stress, initial pipe diameter, and seepage path
  length (see
  [Initiation Material Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#initiation-material-inputs)).
  Left blank, the initial pipe diameter takes this model's default distribution, listed in
  [Initial Pipe Diameter](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#initial-pipe-diameter)
  in the CLE Common Methods manual (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation)); a site-specific distribution replaces the default.
- **Intervention and breach** — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach (see
  [Intervention and Breach](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#intervention-and-breach)).

The model assigns one critical shear stress to the wall of the pipe, from the core soil classification or the supplied distribution (see
[Critical Shear Stress](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#critical-shear-stress)
in the CLE Common Methods manual). Only one soil is classified, although the pipe lies against both the contact-zone fill and the foundation or
abutment surface. The clean and nearly clean sand and gravel classes — SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP — are not accepted as the classification,
even when a critical shear stress distribution is supplied in place of the default range, so a contact whose erodible material is a clean sand or
gravel, such as fill placed on a clean sand foundation, cannot be represented by its own classification.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-14 — Applicability]
State which soil the core soil classification (and any user-supplied critical shear stress distribution) should describe for a flaw along the
foundation or abutment contact: the contact-zone fill, an erodible soil foundation forming the floor of the pipe, or the more erodible of the
two. State also how the user should proceed where the erodible material at the contact is a clean or nearly clean sand or gravel, which the model
does not accept as the classification.
:::

# Input Parameters

This chapter documents the inputs specific to the foundation or abutment compaction model: the flaw location and the elevations it requires, the
three flaw-factor selections, the optional section dimensions used in scoring, the default initial pipe diameter, and the filter-presence
selection. The model also takes the inputs shared by the CLE family, documented in the
[Common Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) chapter of the common
methods manual and listed under [Inputs Shared with the Family](#inputs-shared-with-the-family) below. Each group gives the short facts for its
quantities in a table, followed by a description of each input and, for selections, a list of the choices and what each implies.

## Flaw Location

The user states whether the flaw lies along the foundation contact or along an abutment contact. The choice decides how the base-of-flaw
elevation $z_f$ is resolved (<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-2" />).

- **Contact location** — the contact along which the poorly compacted zone lies:
  - **Foundation** — the fill placed on the foundation along the valley floor. The base of the flaw is the embankment–foundation contact
    elevation $z_{fc}$.
  - **Abutment** — the fill placed against an abutment along the valley wall. The base of the flaw is placed one third of the way from the
    normal pool elevation $z_{np}$ to the crest (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#base-of-flaw-elevation)).
- **Embankment–foundation contact elevation** — the elevation of the base of the embankment on the foundation at the section analyzed. It is
  used only for a foundation contact and is ignored for an abutment contact.
- **Normal pool elevation** — the normal operating pool. It is used only for an abutment contact, where the crest must lie above it, and is
  ignored for a foundation contact.

In either case the resolved base of the flaw must satisfy the conditions common to the compaction models: it must be a positive elevation, no
higher than the top of the embankment section used for initiation and no lower than the lowest segment bottom of either face of that section (see
[Compaction models: base of the flaw](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
in the common methods manual).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-01 — Applicability]
The model treats the foundation and abutment contacts as two alternative locations of one flaw, selected by the user, and evaluates one of them
per run. State how the user should choose between them for a given embankment — for example, whether an abutment contact is any contact against a
valley wall steeper than some inclination, and whether an embankment whose foundation and abutment contacts are both of concern should be
evaluated once for each location, with the larger result or both results carried forward.
:::

## Flaw-Factor Selections

Three selections describe the contact, and two optional dimensions replace the section width and height in scoring
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-3" />). How each selection is scored is documented in
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx).

    ['Soil or rock', 'One of four, consistent with the material', 'One of five', '> 0', '> 0'],
    ['Required', 'Required', 'Required', 'Derived from the section', 'Derived from the section'],
  ]}
/>

- **Foundation material** — the material the embankment is placed against at the contact:
  - **Soil** — a soil foundation or abutment.
  - **Rock** — a rock foundation or abutment.

  No combined soil-and-rock choice is offered. The preparation choices that score a likelihood factor above 1 — Irregular or poorly prepared, and
  Unknown — score the same for both materials, so the material selection decides only which well-prepared choice is available.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Applicability]
The foundation material choice is either soil or rock; no choice describes a contact that is partly soil and partly rock, such as a valley floor of
alluvium against rock abutments, or weathered rock grading to soil. State how the user should select the material for such a contact.
:::

- **Foundation preparation** — the condition of the foundation or abutment surface on which the fill was placed. The well-prepared choice depends
  on the material, and selecting the well-prepared choice of the other material is not accepted:
  - **Compacted soil** (soil only) — the soil surface on which the fill was placed was compacted. Forces the flaw probability to a negligible value
    (see [Well-prepared surface](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#well-prepared-surface)).
  - **Regular or treated rock surface** (rock only) — a rock surface that is regular, or has been treated to make it so. Forces the flaw
    probability to a negligible value.
  - **Irregular or poorly prepared** (soil or rock) — a surface that is irregular or was not well prepared.
  - **Unknown** (soil or rock) — the preparation is not known; scored as irregular or poorly prepared.
- **Contact-zone compaction method** — how the embankment fill in the zone against the foundation or abutment was compacted:
  - **Special methods with good documentation** — special compaction methods were used in the contact zone, and their use is well documented.
  - **Special methods** — special compaction methods were used, without good documentation.
  - **Bulldozer, layer at the limit of compaction** — the fill was compacted by bulldozer, in layers at the limit of compaction.
  - **Poor compaction, thick layers** — the fill was poorly compacted in thick layers.
  - **Unknown** — the compaction method is not known; scored as bulldozer compaction.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Definition]
The foundation preparation choices (Compacted soil; Regular or treated rock surface; Irregular or poorly prepared) and the contact-zone
compaction method choices (Special methods with good documentation; Special methods; Bulldozer, layer at the limit of compaction; Poor
compaction, thick layers) are described above only by their names. Define what each covers so the user can select between them — for example,
what treatment makes a rock surface "regular or treated" (dental concrete, slush grouting, removal of overhangs, shaping of steps), what counts as
"special methods" of contact-zone compaction (hand-operated or rubber-tired compaction, moisture conditioning, placement of a selected contact
fill), and what layer thickness separates the bulldozer and poor-compaction choices — or cite the table in Fell et al. (2024) that defines them.
:::

- **Flaw base width and flaw height** — optional replacements for the base width $W$ and height $H_s$ of the embankment section, which the model
  otherwise derives from the section geometry (see
  [Section geometry used in scoring](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#section-geometry-used-in-scoring)
  in the common methods manual). Each may be supplied alone. A supplied value replaces the derived value in the scoring of the geometry factor
  only; the initiation analysis keeps the section geometry.

## Initiation Default

The model takes the initiation material inputs of the compaction models — the core soil classification, and optional critical shear stress,
initial pipe diameter, and seepage path length — as documented in
[Initiation Material Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#initiation-material-inputs)
in the common methods manual. Only the default of the initial pipe diameter is specific to the model
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-4" />).

- **Initial pipe diameter** — the diameter of the cylindrical pipe idealizing the poorly compacted contact zone. Left blank, it is a triangular
  distribution with a low value of 2 mm, a most likely value of 3 mm, and a high value of 5 mm, the same range as the core compaction model. A
  site-specific distribution replaces the default. The basis of the default ranges of the four compaction models is addressed in
  [Initial pipe diameter](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#initial-pipe-diameter) in the
  common methods manual.

The model assigns one critical shear stress to the wall of the pipe, from the core soil classification or the user's distribution (see
[Critical shear stress](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#critical-shear-stress) in the
common methods manual). Only one soil is classified, although the pipe lies against both the contact-zone fill and the foundation or
abutment surface. The clean and nearly clean sand and gravel classes — SP-SM, SW, SP, GP-GM, GW, and GP — are not accepted as the
classification, even when the user supplies a critical shear stress distribution in place of the default range, so a contact whose erodible material is a clean sand or gravel, such as fill placed on a clean sand foundation, cannot be
represented by its own classification.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-14 — Applicability]
State which soil the core soil classification (and any user-supplied critical shear stress distribution) should describe for a flaw along the
foundation or abutment contact: the contact-zone fill, an erodible soil foundation forming the floor of the pipe, or the more erodible of the
two. State also how the user should proceed where the erodible material at the contact is a clean or nearly clean sand or gravel, which the model
does not accept as the classification.
:::

## Filter Presence and Continuation

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-foundation-abutment-screening-table-5"

- **Filter presence at the contact** — whether a filter intercepts the failure pathway along the contact at the base of the flaw:
  - **Present** — the continuation inputs are required, and the continuation node is evaluated by the shared continuation method.
  - **Absent** — the exit is unfiltered and continuation is certain. The continuation inputs are not used.
- **Continuation inputs** — the filter description and the base soils it must protect, as documented in the
  [Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) chapter of the continuation manual.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-04 — Definition]
State what counts as a filter intercepting the pathway along a foundation or abutment contact — for example, a filter or drain zone placed on the
foundation downstream of the core, a chimney filter reaching the foundation, or a filter blanket on an abutment — and which soils the user should
supply as the base soils to the continuation method: the contact-zone fill, the core soil, the foundation soil, or each of them.
:::

## Inputs Shared with the Family

The model also takes the following inputs, each documented in the common methods manual:

- **Hydraulic loading** — the headwater series and optional tailwater series (see
  [Hydraulic Loading](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#hydraulic-loading)), on the
  common datum and in the units of [Units and Datum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#units-and-datum).
- **Embankment description** — the crest elevation, the embankment type, the shell section, and the optional core section (see
  [Embankment Description](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description)).
  The crest also serves as the top of dam in the abutment heuristic.
- **Embankment materials** — the soil classifications of the materials the pipe passes through, which govern roof support (see
  [Failure-Pathway Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#failure-pathway-inputs)).
- **Upstream seepage-limiting elements** — the elements present and the optional top-of-wall elevation, which is checked against the base of the
  flaw (see
  [Upstream Seepage-Limiting Elements](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#upstream-seepage-limiting-elements)).
- **Intervention and breach** — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach (see
  [Intervention and Breach](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#intervention-and-breach)).

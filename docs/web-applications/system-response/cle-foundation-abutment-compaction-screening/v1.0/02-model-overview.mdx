# Model Overview

## Purpose

The model computes a system response curve for concentrated leak erosion through poorly compacted fill along the foundation or abutment contact,
as a function of headwater elevation. The system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level is the conditional probability of breach of
the embankment by this mechanism, given that pool level.

The mechanism is a continuous zone of loose or poorly compacted embankment fill against the surface the embankment is built on — the foundation
along the valley floor, or the abutment along the valley walls. Such a zone forms where the foundation surface was left irregular or poorly
prepared, or where the fill against it could not be, or was not, compacted as well as the body of the embankment. Water from the reservoir flowing
along the zone can erode its walls; if the exit is unfiltered and the enlarging pipe stays open, the erosion can progress to breach.

## Intended Use

The model is a screening-level assessment of concentrated leak erosion along the foundation or abutment contact. Like the other CLE screening
models, it makes simplifying and generally conservative assumptions to prevent false negatives and to reduce input burden (see
[Intended Use](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#intended-use) in the common methods
manual). The placement of an abutment flaw by the normal-pool heuristic is an exception: for a flaw that lies lower on the abutment it is
unconservative, because it gives a negligible SRP from the actual flaw elevation up to the heuristic elevation above normal pool (see Assumption 2
in [Assumptions and Limitations](./06-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#flaw-location)). For this mechanism the input burden is reduced to three qualitative selections describing the contact — the foundation material, how its
surface was prepared, and how the fill against it was compacted — together with the embankment section and the screening inputs shared by the
family.

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments (see
[Comparison with Detailed Models](#comparison-with-detailed-models)).

## How the Model Works

The model executes the three stages common to the CLE family (see
[How the Model Works](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#how-the-model-works) in the
common methods manual), with the following mechanism-specific content:

1. **Flaw assessment** — The model places the base of the flaw at the embankment–foundation contact elevation for a foundation contact, or at
   one third of the way from normal pool to the crest for an abutment contact. It scores three factors: the preparation of the foundation or
   abutment surface, the method used to compact the fill in the contact zone, and the width and width-to-height ratio of the embankment section.
   The weighted score is converted to a raw flaw probability, which is multiplied by a flaw-continuity probability keyed to the compaction method.
   A well-prepared surface forces the flaw probability to a negligible value. The model has no observation override
   (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).

2. **Pathway resolution** — The failure pathway runs through the embankment section at the base of the flaw. The user states whether a filter
   intercepts it; the materials it passes through set roof support; and the upstream seepage-limiting elements are checked against the base of the
   flaw (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the model multiplies the eight node probabilities. Initiation is the cylindrical-pipe Monte
   Carlo analysis of the common methods, with this model's default initial pipe diameter; every other node is the same at every pool level
   (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

## Physics Basis

The model combines the methods of the CLE family (see
[Physics Basis](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#physics-basis) in the common methods
manual). Specific to this mechanism:

- **Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring** of the probability of a continuous flaw along the contact <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, with
  three factors that describe the contact surface, the compaction of the contact zone, and the embankment section, and a flaw-continuity stage
  keyed to the compaction method (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).
- **Hydraulic shear stress on the walls of a cylindrical pipe** at the base of the flaw, compared with the critical shear stress assigned
  through the core soil classification, by the cylindrical-pipe initiation analysis shared by the four compaction models (see
  [Cylindrical Pipe Initiation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#delegated-method-cylindrical-pipe-initiation)
  in the common methods manual). Which soil along the contact that classification describes is requested at marker IN-14 in
  [Initiation Default](./03-input-parameters.mdx#initiation-default).
- **Tabulated progression probabilities** for roof support, crack filling, and flow limitation <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, applied as for
  every compaction model, and **filter evaluation** by the shared continuation method when a filter intercepts the pathway.

## Relationship to the Shared Methods

The model shares everything except its flaw assessment with the other CLE compaction models:

- **Common CLE methods** — the event tree and SRP product, the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring and flaw-continuity conventions, the
  cylindrical-pipe initiation analysis with its critical shear stress table, and the roof-support, crack-filling, and flow-limitation tables are
  documented in the
  [Concentrated Leak Erosion Common Methods Screening Technical Reference Manual](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/preface).
  [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx) links each node to its section there.
- **Continuation** — when a filter intercepts the pathway, the continuation node is the result of the shared continuation method documented in the
  [Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening Technical Reference Manual](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/preface)
  (see [Node 3: Continuation](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-3-continuation)).

Within the family, the model is closest to the core compaction model: both band the width and width-to-height ratio of the embankment section,
both place a flaw by the normal-pool heuristic (here only for an abutment contact), both apply the flaw-continuity stage, and both use the same
default initial pipe diameter range. It differs in having a foundation-preparation factor in place of a core-compaction category, a
well-prepared-surface rule in place of an observation override, and in the factor that keys the flaw-continuity stage: both key it to their
compaction factor, but here that factor carries a risk factor of 2, while in the core compaction model it is the factor with the highest risk
factor, 3.

# Output and Interpretation

The model reports the results common to the CLE compaction models — the pool-independent node probabilities, the series evaluated at each pool
level, and the supporting results of the initiation analysis — documented in the
[Output and Interpretation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) chapter of the
common methods manual. This chapter describes the flaw-assessment results specific to the model and how they bear on reading and checking the
system response curve.

## Results Common to the Compaction Models

- **Pool-independent results** — the flaw, continuation, roof-support, crack-filling, and flow-limitation probabilities (see
  [Pool-Independent Results](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#pool-independent-results)).
  The flow-limitation probability is the value after the walls are checked against the base of the flaw.
- **Pool-dependent series** — the pool levels, the tailwater, the initiation probability, the flow-limitation, unsuccessful-intervention, and
  breach probabilities, and the SRP (see
  [Pool-Dependent Series](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#pool-dependent-series)).
- **Initiation analysis** — the heads above the pipe invert, the hydraulic gradient, the shear stress statistics, and the seepage path length (see
  [Supporting Results](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#supporting-results)).
- **Continuation results** — when a filter is present, the results of the continuation method (see the
  [Output and Interpretation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) chapter of the
  continuation manual). When no filter is present, the continuation probability is 1.0 and no continuation results are reported.

## Flaw-Assessment Results

The flaw assessment reports each quantity of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx), so that the flaw probability can be traced from the
selections:

- **Likelihood factors** — the foundation preparation factor $LF_{fdn}$, the contact-zone compaction factor $LF_{comp}$, and the section geometry
  factor $LF_{geom}$.
- **Section dimensions** — the base width $W$, the height $H_s$, and their ratio $W/H_s$ used in scoring: the user's values where supplied,
  otherwise those derived from the section.
- **Weighted sum** $\Sigma$ and **raw flaw probability** $P_{raw}$ — the raw flaw probability is zero when the well-prepared-surface rule applies.
- **Flaw-continuity probability** $P_{cf}$ — keyed to the contact-zone compaction factor.
- **Flaw probability** $P_{flaw}$ — the Node 1 value, never reported as exactly zero.
- **Base-of-flaw elevation** $z_f$ — the contact elevation, or the abutment heuristic elevation.
- **Well-prepared surface** — whether the foundation preparation factor scored 1 and forced the flaw probability to a negligible value.

## Interpreting the System Response Curve

Only initiation varies with pool level, so the curve is the initiation curve scaled by the product of the other seven nodes
(see [SRP Computation](./05-event-tree.mdx#srp-computation)). It is negligible at and below the base of the flaw $z_f$, rises with pool level as the
gradient through the pipe grows, and approaches the product of the other seven nodes as initiation approaches 1.0. The general reading of a
compaction-model curve — its shape, the effect of tailwater, and a plateau below the product of the other nodes — is described in
[Interpreting the System Response Curve](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#interpreting-the-system-response-curve)
in the common methods manual.

Two features are specific to the mechanism:

- **Among the flaw selections, the compaction method moves the ceiling most when the surface is not well prepared.** The flaw probability, and
  so the ceiling of the curve, takes one of the values of
  [Range of the Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-flaw-probability). For a surface that is not well prepared, changing the
  compaction method moves the ceiling by more than an order of magnitude per step, while changing the section geometry moves it by a factor of
  about 3 to 5 across its full range; on a well-prepared surface the ceiling is negligible whatever the compaction method. The other nodes that
  scale the curve — continuation, roof support, and flow limitation — can move the ceiling as much or more. The flaw-assessment results show
  which selection drives a result.
- **For an abutment contact, the curve starts above normal pool.** The base of the flaw lies one third of the way from normal pool to the crest,
  so the curve is negligible at every pool level up to that elevation, and the headwater series must extend above it for the curve to rise.

## Checking the Result

In addition to the checks common to the compaction models (see
[Checking the Result](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#checking-the-result) in
the common methods manual):

- **Flaw probability** — the reported flaw probability should be one of the values of
  [Range of the Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-flaw-probability), or negligible for a well-prepared surface or for
  special methods with good documentation. It can be reproduced by hand from the reported likelihood factors.
- **Upper bound** — the SRP at every pool level is no larger than about 5.6 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, the largest flaw probability the model can produce.
- **Lower end** — the curve is negligible at and below the reported base-of-flaw elevation. For a foundation contact, that elevation should match
  the contact elevation supplied; for an abutment contact, it should lie one third of the way from normal pool to the crest.
- **Wall credit** — the flow-limitation probability excludes the walls only when the top-of-wall elevation is below the reported base of the
  flaw.

Three causes of a negligible curve at every pool level are specific to the model: a well-prepared surface, special compaction methods with good
documentation, and, for an abutment contact, a headwater series that does not rise above the heuristic base of the flaw. The flaw-assessment
results and the base-of-flaw elevation show whether one of them applies.

## Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis

The model is a screening tool. Its result indicates whether concentrated leak erosion along the foundation or abutment contact warrants detailed
analysis; it is not a substitute for one. Where the result is significant to a decision — and in particular where the flaw probability rests on
Unknown selections, or where seepage has been observed along the contact, which the model does not take as evidence — the flaw should be
re-examined with construction records, foundation geology, and performance, following Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> and
the judgment of the risk team (see
[Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#screening-results-relative-to-detailed-analysis)
in the common methods manual).

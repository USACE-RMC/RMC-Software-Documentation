# Assumptions and Limitations

The model shares the assumptions of the CLE screening family — the event-tree product, the pool-independent flaw, the cylindrical-pipe
idealization and its defaults, and the progression tables — listed in the
[Assumptions and Limitations](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) chapter of
the common methods manual, and the assumptions of the continuation method listed in the
[Assumptions and Limitations](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) chapter of the
continuation manual. This chapter lists the assumptions specific to the foundation or abutment compaction mechanism. Each states its engineering
basis, its effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold. Where an engineering basis is awaiting confirmation, the assumption
cross-references the input marker at its primary location in this document.

## Flaw Location

1. **A foundation flaw lies on the embankment–foundation contact.** Its base is the contact elevation supplied by the user (see
   [Base-of-Flaw Elevation](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#base-of-flaw-elevation)).
   - *Engineering basis:* Definition of the mechanism: the poorly compacted zone is the fill placed on the foundation surface.
   - *Effect on results:* Initiation is zero, and the SRP negligible, at pool levels at or below the contact. The seepage path through a section whose
     base is at the contact spans its full base width, which gives the lowest gradient the section allows for a given net head.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the contact steps or slopes across the section, so that the poorly compacted zone lies above the contact
     elevation supplied.
2. **An abutment flaw lies one third of the way from the normal pool to the crest.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-05 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#base-of-flaw-elevation); the
     heuristic itself is addressed in
     [Compaction models: base of the flaw](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
     in the common methods manual.
   - *Effect on results:* Initiation is zero, and the SRP negligible, at pool levels at or below that elevation, which lies above normal pool,
     however low on the abutment the contact runs. For a flaw that lies lower on the abutment, the placement is unconservative: the model gives a
     negligible SRP at every pool level from the actual flaw up to $z_f$, including pool levels between normal pool and $z_f$, and above $z_f$ it
     applies less net head over the pipe invert than the lower flaw would carry. The higher placement also shortens the seepage path, which steepens
     the gradient for a given net head, so above $z_f$ whether initiation is underestimated or overestimated depends on the section. Walls are
     judged against the same elevation.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where construction records or performance locate the poorly compacted zone on the abutment, such as at a steep or
     overhanging section of the valley wall below normal pool.
3. **One run evaluates one contact location.** The foundation and abutment contacts are alternative flaw locations, selected by the user.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-01 in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-location).
   - *Effect on results:* A site with concerns at both contacts needs a run for each to describe both.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where one continuous poorly compacted zone runs from the valley floor up the abutment, so that neither the foundation
     contact elevation nor the abutment heuristic elevation represents the flaw on its own.

## Flaw Scoring

4. **The flaw probability is scored from three factors, weighted 3, 2, and 1.** The factors are the foundation preparation, the contact-zone
   compaction method, and the section geometry.
   - *Engineering basis:* The risk-factor and likelihood-factor scheme of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the factor
     weights and tables are requested at markers IN-06 to IN-09 and IN-11 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#scored-factors).
   - *Effect on results:* The flaw probability takes one of the values of
     [Range of the Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-flaw-probability), no larger than about 5.6 × 10<sup>-3</sup>, which
     caps the SRP.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where other evidence — continuous rock defects, open joints, or erodible foundation soils against the fill — bears on
     a flaw along the contact more directly than the three factors do; a detailed analysis then estimates the flaw probability from that evidence.
5. **An Unknown selection is scored as a specific known condition.** An Unknown foundation preparation is scored as irregular or poorly prepared
   (3.5), and an Unknown compaction method as bulldozer compaction at the limit of compaction (3).
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see markers IN-07 and IN-08 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#foundation-preparation).
   - *Effect on results:* An Unknown preparation receives the least favorable score of its factor. An Unknown compaction method does not: poor
     compaction in thick layers scores 4 and, for a surface that is not well prepared, raises the flaw probability more than two orders of
     magnitude above the Unknown value. On a well-prepared surface both selections give a negligible flaw probability.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the construction era or practice indicates poor compaction of the contact zone; poor compaction in thick layers
     should then be selected.
6. **The section geometry factor scores the width and height of the section supplied.** The core section governs when one is supplied, and the
   user may replace both dimensions.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see markers IN-09 and IN-10 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#section-geometry).
   - *Effect on results:* A section scores 1 only if its base width is greater than 50 and its width-to-height ratio is greater than 2. Across
     its full range, the factor changes the flaw probability by a factor of about 3 to 5, unless the surface is well prepared or special methods
     with good documentation were used, when the flaw probability is negligible whatever the geometry.
   - *When it may not hold:* For an abutment flaw, where the section at the flaw is lower and narrower than the section supplied; the flaw base
     width and height inputs should then carry the section at the flaw.
7. **A well-prepared foundation or abutment surface rules out the flaw.** Compacted soil on a soil foundation, or a regular or treated rock
   surface, forces the flaw probability to a negligible value whatever the other factors score.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-12 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#well-prepared-surface).
   - *Effect on results:* The SRP is negligible at every pool level.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the contact zone was poorly compacted on a well-prepared surface, or where the surface preparation is
     recorded but not verified.
8. **No observation sets the flaw probability to 1.0.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-12 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#well-prepared-surface).
   - *Effect on results:* Observed seepage or wet spots along the contact do not change the screening result.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where such observations are judged to demonstrate a flaw along the contact; the screening flaw probability then
     understates the condition, and a detailed analysis should take the observation as evidence.
9. **The flaw-continuity stage is keyed to the contact-zone compaction method.** The scored probability is multiplied by the flaw-continuity
   probability of the compaction method's likelihood factor.
   - *Engineering basis:* The flaw-continuity curve shared by three compaction models (see
     [Flaw-Continuity Stage](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-continuity-stage) in
     the common methods manual, where its basis is addressed).
   - *Effect on results:* Special methods with good documentation give a negligible flaw probability whatever the surface preparation and
     geometry, and the compaction method enters the flaw probability twice, so the result is especially sensitive to that selection.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the continuity of a poorly compacted zone along the contact depends on the surface preparation rather than on
     the compaction method, such as a continuous open joint or overhang in a rock abutment.

## Initiation and Pathway

10. **The default initial pipe diameter is triangular with 2, 3, and 5 mm**, the same range as the core compaction model.
    - *Engineering basis:* The default ranges of the compaction models are addressed in
      [Initial pipe diameter](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#initial-pipe-diameter) in the
      common methods manual.
    - *Effect on results:* The shear stress is proportional to the diameter, so the range sets how high above the base of the flaw the pool must
      rise before initiation becomes likely.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where the contact has open defects, such as open joints or a gap against an overhanging rock surface, larger than a
      few millimeters; a site-specific distribution should then be supplied.
11. **Filter presence at the contact is a user statement, independent of the base-of-flaw elevation.**
    - *Engineering basis:* The user resolves the failure pathway against the section and supplies the result (see
      [The Failure Pathway](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#the-failure-pathway)
      in the common methods manual); what counts as a filter at the contact is requested at marker IN-04 in
      [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#filter-presence-and-continuation).
    - *Effect on results:* A filter stated present multiplies the SRP by the probability of continuing erosion $P_{ce}$ at every pool level.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where a filter covers only part of the contact, such as a foundation filter that does not extend up the abutment.
12. **Walls are judged against the base of the flaw.** A wall whose top is below the base of the flaw gives no flow-limitation credit.
    - *Engineering basis:* The flow-limitation convention of the compaction models (see
      [Walls that stop below the crest](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#walls-that-stop-below-the-crest)
      in the common methods manual).
    - *Effect on results:* A cutoff wall reaching at or above the foundation contact limits flow into a foundation flaw; for an abutment flaw, the wall
      top must reach the heuristic elevation above normal pool.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where a cutoff wall does not extend along the abutment contact, even though its top is above the base of the flaw.

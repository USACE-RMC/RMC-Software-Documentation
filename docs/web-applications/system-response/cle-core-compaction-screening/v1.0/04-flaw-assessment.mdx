# Flaw Assessment

Node 1 of the event tree is the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous zone of poorly compacted soil exists through the embankment core. The flaw
assessment also fixes the base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$, which sets the pipe invert of the initiation analysis (Node 2) and is the elevation the
top of any upstream wall is compared with (Node 6); the other nodes do not use it. The assessment is deterministic and does not depend on pool level, so $P_{flaw}$ and $z_f$ are the same at every headwater.

The assessment follows the conventions shared by the CLE family, documented in the
[Flaw Conventions](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions) chapter of the CLE Common Methods
manual. It runs in two stages:

1. **Scoring** — three factors are scored and combined into a weighted sum, which is converted to a raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$.
2. **Flaw continuity** — $P_{raw}$ is multiplied by a flaw-continuity probability $P_{cf}$ keyed to the compaction score, giving $P_{flaw}$.

An observation of embankment leakage or wet spots overrides both stages and the base-of-flaw heuristic (see
[Observation Override](#observation-override)).

## Factors

The model scores the three factors of <TableReference tableKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-table-3" /> by the risk-factor and likelihood-factor
scheme of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, described in
[Risk-Factor and Likelihood-Factor Scoring](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. Each factor carries a risk factor $RF$, its weight, and a likelihood factor $LF$ on a scale on which 1 is the least
likely condition for a flaw and 4 the most likely.

score selects in the second stage (see [Flaw Continuity](#flaw-continuity)).

### Continuity factor

The continuity factor scores the dimensions of the embankment section through which a poorly compacted zone would have to run. Its likelihood
factor $LF_{cont}$ is the larger of two banded scores, one of the base width $W$ and one of the width-to-height ratio $W/H$:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-eq-1"
  equation="LF_W = \begin{cases} 1 & W > 50 \\ 2 & 30 < W \le 50 \\ 3 & 15 < W \le 30 \\ 4 & W \le 15 \end{cases} \qquad LF_{W/H} = \begin{cases} 1 & W/H > 2 \\ 2 & 0.75 < W/H \le 2 \\ 3 & 0.25 < W/H \le 0.75 \\ 4 & W/H \le 0.25 \end{cases}"
/>

<Equation equationKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-eq-2" equation="LF_{cont} = \max\left(LF_W,\ LF_{W/H}\right)" />

where $W$ and $H$ are the base width and height of the embankment section — the core section when one is supplied, otherwise the shell — or the
user-supplied flaw base width and flaw height (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-base-width-and-flaw-height)). The section
height $H$ is the quantity written $H_s$ in
[Section Geometry Used in Scoring](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#section-geometry-used-in-scoring)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. The base width is in the length unit of the elevations. <TableReference tableKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-table-5" /> lists the bands.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-table-5"
  alt="Continuity likelihood factor bands"
  caption="Continuity likelihood factor bands; the larger of the two scores governs."
  colAlign={['center', 'center', 'center']}
  headers={[[{ value: 'Likelihood Factor' }, { value: 'Base Width W' }, { value: 'Width-to-Height Ratio W/H' }]]}

    ['Greater than 2', 'Greater than 0.75, up to 2', 'Greater than 0.25, up to 0.75', '0.25 or less'],

/>

Taking the larger of the two scores means that either a narrow section or a slender one is enough to make a continuous flaw more likely: a section
scores 1 only when it is both wider than 50 and more than twice as wide as it is high. A band boundary itself belongs to the band below it; for
example, a base width of exactly 50 scores 2.

require an engineering basis. Provide the source table or the engineering-judgment attribution, and state the unit of the base-width boundaries:

Proposed (unconfirmed): the base-width boundaries are in feet, so a user working in meters should convert the section before running the model.

:::

### Borrow variability factor

The borrow variability factor has a fixed likelihood factor $LF_{var} = 2.5$. It is not a user selection and does not vary between sites.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-06 — Engineering Basis]
The borrow variability factor and its fixed likelihood factor of 2.5 require a definition and an engineering basis. State what the factor represents
physically (for example, the variability of the borrow material placed in the core, which can leave zones of more erodible or less compactable
soil), why it is fixed rather than selected by the user, and the basis for the value of 2.5, between likelihood factors 2 and 3.
:::

## Weighted Sum and Raw Flaw Probability

The three scores combine into the weighted sum

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-eq-3"
  equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_{comp} + 2\,LF_{cont} + 1\,LF_{var}"
/>

with $LF_{var} = 2.5$, which ranges from 7.5 (well compacted, continuity score 1) to 22.5 (very poorly compacted, continuity score 4). The raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$ is
interpolated from <TableReference tableKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-table-6" />, linearly in the logarithm of the probability between
tabulated points, as described in
[Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
in the CLE Common Methods manual.

<TableVertical
  tableKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-table-6"
  alt="Raw flaw probability by weighted sum"
  caption="Raw flaw probability by weighted sum."
  colAlign={['center', 'center']}
  headers={[[{ value: 'Weighted Sum' }, { value: 'Raw Flaw Probability' }]]}
  columns={[
    ['6', '9', '11', '13', '18', '24'],
    ['Negligible', '1 × 10<sup>-4</sup>', '5 × 10<sup>-4</sup>', '2 × 10<sup>-3</sup>', '4 × 10<sup>-2</sup>', '5 × 10<sup>-1</sup>'],
  ]}
/>

Every attainable weighted sum lies inside the tabulated range, so the end values are never held. Because the lowest point carries a negligible
probability, the raw probability falls steeply below a weighted sum of 9; at the lowest attainable sum of 7.5 it is several orders of magnitude
below 1 × 10<sup>-4</sup>. That range matters only for a well-compacted core, whose flaw probability is negligible after the flaw-continuity stage
in any case. The model applies no lower threshold and no incompatible-condition factor, so no score forces $P_{raw}$ to zero.

## Flaw Continuity

The second stage multiplies the raw probability by the flaw-continuity probability $P_{cf}$, interpolated log-linearly from the compaction score
on the curve the model shares with the wall or cliff and the foundation or abutment compaction models (see
[Flaw-Continuity Stage](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-continuity-stage) in the CLE
Common Methods manual):

<Equation equationKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-eq-4" equation="P_{flaw} = P_{raw}(\Sigma) \times P_{cf}(LF_{comp})" />

The value for each category is listed in <TableReference tableKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-table-4" />. The Unknown score of 3.5 is
interpolated between the points at 3 and 4, giving 1 × 10<sup>-2</sup>. The point of the curve at a score of 2 (1 × 10<sup>-4</sup>) is not used
by this model, because no category scores 2.

The compaction category therefore enters the flaw probability twice: through its weight of 3 in the weighted sum, and again through $P_{cf}$. The
section geometry enters only through the weighted sum.

A well-compacted core has a negligible flaw probability whatever the section geometry, because its flaw-continuity
probability is negligible. For the other categories, each step in the continuity score raises the flaw probability by a factor of about 2 to 3.5,
while moving from Poorly compacted to Very poorly compacted raises it by a factor of about 350 to 600, with Unknown in between.

### Example

An embankment section with a base width of 350 and a height of 60 (from its base at elevation 205 to a crest at elevation 265), a core of Unknown
compaction, and a normal pool at elevation 235 scores as follows:

- $W = 350$ is greater than 50, so $LF_W = 1$; $W/H = 5.83$ is greater than 2, so $LF_{W/H} = 1$; hence $LF_{cont} = 1$.
- Unknown compaction gives $LF_{comp} = 3.5$.
- $\Sigma = 3 \times 3.5 + 2 \times 1 + 2.5 = 15$.
- Between the tabulated points at 13 and 18, $P_{raw} = 2 \times 10^{-3} \times (20)^{0.4} \approx 6.6 \times 10^{-3}$.
- $P_{cf}(3.5) = 1 \times 10^{-2}$, so $P_{flaw} \approx 6.6 \times 10^{-5}$.
- The base of the flaw is $z_f = 235 + (265 - 235)/3 = 245$.

## Observation Override

When embankment leakage or wet spots are reported as observed, the flaw is taken as demonstrated:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-eq-5"
  equation="P_{flaw} = 1.0, \qquad z_f = z_{lk}"
/>

where $z_{lk}$ is the observed leakage or wet-spot elevation. The override replaces the scored probability and the normal-pool heuristic together.
It is the only CLE compaction override that also moves the flaw elevation (see
[Observation Overrides](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) in the CLE
Common Methods manual). If a compaction category is supplied with the observation, the factors are still scored and reported, but they do not
affect the result; without a category, no scores are reported. The override sets only Node 1 and the flaw elevation: every downstream node is
evaluated as usual at the observed elevation.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-08 — Definition]
State which elevation the user should enter as the leakage or wet-spot elevation $z_{lk}$ — for example, the elevation of the observed seepage exit
on the downstream face, or the lowest observed wet spot when several are observed — and the basis for taking it as the invert of a horizontal pipe
through the core. The pipe is analyzed at this elevation from the upstream face to the downstream face, so an exit that the flaw reaches by a
sloping path would place the pipe at the exit elevation rather than at the elevation of the flaw within the core.
:::

## Base of the Flaw

Without an observation, the base of the flaw is placed by the normal-pool heuristic shared with the abutment case of the foundation or abutment
compaction model, one third of the way from the normal pool elevation $z_{np}$ to the crest elevation $z_c$:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-core-compaction-screening-eq-6"
  equation="z_f = z_{np} + \tfrac{1}{3}\,\bigl(z_c - z_{np}\bigr)"
/>

The heuristic is documented in
[Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. The upper reference is always the crest of the embankment, even when a core section with a lower top is supplied.

Whichever way it is resolved, the base of the flaw is the invert of the pipe in the initiation analysis and must lie within the embankment section
used for initiation: at or below the top of that section (the core top when a core section is supplied, otherwise the crest), at or above the lowest
segment bottom of each face, and at a positive elevation. An analysis whose base of the flaw falls outside these limits — for example, a core top
below the heuristic elevation — is not evaluated, because its inputs are inconsistent.

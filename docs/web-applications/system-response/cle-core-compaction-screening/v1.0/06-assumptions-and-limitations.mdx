# Assumptions and Limitations

The core compaction model makes every assumption shared by the CLE family — the product form of the event tree, the cylindrical-pipe idealization
of a compaction flaw, the default critical shear stress ranges, the fixed failure pathway of a compaction flaw, and the tabulated progression
probabilities — each listed with its basis, effect, and limits in the
[Assumptions and Limitations](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) chapter of the
CLE Common Methods manual. This chapter lists the assumptions the core compaction model adds or makes specific. Each states its engineering basis,
its effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold. Where a basis is still awaiting confirmation, the assumption
cross-references the input marker at its primary location.

## Flaw Assessment

1. **The likelihood of a continuous poorly compacted zone is captured by three factors: core compaction, the continuity of the section, and borrow
   variability.** No other influence on the flaw is scored (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#factors)).
   - *Engineering basis:* The risk-factor and likelihood-factor scheme of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the basis of the
     factors and weights is requested at marker IN-03 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#factors).
   - *Effect on results:* The core compaction factor, with a weight of 3, moves the weighted sum by up to 9; the continuity factor, with a weight of
     2, by up to 6; borrow variability adds a constant 2.5.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where site information bears on the flaw in ways the three factors do not represent; the risk team's estimate then
     replaces the screening value.
2. **The compaction of the core is described by one of four qualitative categories, and Unknown is scored between Poorly compacted and Very poorly
   compacted.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Requested at markers IN-01 in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#core-compaction-category) and IN-04 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#compaction-factor).
   - *Effect on results:* Selecting Unknown gives a flaw probability about 19 to 25 times that of Poorly compacted for the same section (see
     [Resulting Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#resulting-flaw-probability)).
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the core's compaction varies along its length or with depth; one category represents the whole core.
3. **The continuity of a poorly compacted zone is scored from the base width and width-to-height ratio of the section, and the less favorable of the
   two governs.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Requested at marker IN-05 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#continuity-factor).
   - *Effect on results:* Without an observation, a narrow or slender section raises the flaw probability by a factor of about 2 to 3.5 per band
     for a Poorly compacted, Very poorly compacted, or Unknown core. A well-compacted core stays negligible whatever the geometry, and under the
     observation override the geometry does not affect the flaw probability.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the section is described in a length unit other than the one the base-width bands assume (see marker IN-05), or
     where the low-permeability part of the section differs from the section described by the slope-segment tables; the flaw base width and height
     can then be supplied (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-base-width-and-flaw-height)).
4. **Borrow variability is fixed at a likelihood factor of 2.5 for every site.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Requested at marker IN-06 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#borrow-variability-factor).
   - *Effect on results:* Borrow variability contributes the same 2.5 to every weighted sum, so it never distinguishes between sites.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the borrow sources are known to be uniform, the fixed 2.5 overstates the flaw probability; where they are known
     to be highly variable, it understates it.
5. **The compaction category enters the flaw probability twice** — through its weight in the weighted sum and again through the flaw-continuity
   probability (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-continuity)).
   - *Engineering basis:* The flaw-continuity stage shared with the wall or cliff and foundation or abutment compaction models, documented in the
     [Flaw-Continuity Stage](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-continuity-stage)
     section of the CLE Common Methods manual.
   - *Effect on results:* A well-compacted core has a negligible flaw probability whatever the section geometry, and the flaw probability spans
     many orders of magnitude across the four categories.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where compaction records conflict with field performance, such as observed seepage through a core rated well
     compacted; the observation override then applies.
6. **An observation of embankment leakage or wet spots demonstrates the flaw and locates it.** The flaw probability is set to 1.0 and the base of the
   flaw to the observed elevation (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#observation-override)).
   - *Engineering basis:* Engineering judgment that an observation demonstrates the flaw, as for every CLE observation override (see the
     [assumptions of the CLE Common Methods manual](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations#flaw));
     the meaning of the observed elevation is requested at marker IN-08 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#observation-override).
   - *Effect on results:* The SRP is governed entirely by the downstream nodes, and initiation, flow limitation, and the user's filter judgment are
     all evaluated at the observed elevation.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the leakage or wet spots have another source, such as seepage through the foundation or along a conduit, or where
     the observed exit lies below or above the flaw within the core.
7. **Without an observation, the base of the flaw is one third of the way from the normal pool to the crest, measured to the crest even when a lower
   core top is described.**
   - *Engineering basis:* The normal-pool heuristic shared with the abutment case of the foundation or abutment compaction model, documented in
     [Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
     in the CLE Common Methods manual.
   - *Effect on results:* The SRP is negligible at pool levels at or below this elevation, and a wall is credited only if its top reaches it. A
     core top below this elevation leaves the inputs inconsistent, and the analysis is not evaluated.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where construction records or performance locate the poorly compacted zone. A zone lower in the core carries more
     head at every pool level and begins to initiate at a lower pool, so the heuristic is unconservative there: the SRP at normal pool, for
     example, is negligible even if the zone lies below normal pool. When leakage or wet spots locate the zone, the observation override should be
     used.

## Failure Pathway and Initiation

8. **The user decides whether a filter intercepts the pathway, and the continuation probability is one value at every pool level.** Supplying the
   filter and base-soil description states that a filter is present; the model does not compare the filter with the base of the flaw (see
   [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-3-continuation)).
   - *Engineering basis:* Engineering judgment: a compaction flaw is at one elevation, so the filter it meets does not change with the pool (see
     the [assumptions of the CLE Common Methods manual](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations#failure-pathway-and-progression)).
   - *Effect on results:* A supplied filter multiplies the SRP at every pool level by the continuation probability $P_{ce}$, which lowers it
     whenever $P_{ce}$ is below 1.0.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the filter's top lies near the base of the flaw, or where the base of the flaw is uncertain; supplying a filter
     that does not reach the flaw credits it wrongly, so the user should confirm that it reaches the base of the flaw.
9. **When a core section is supplied, it governs both the flaw scoring and the initiation analysis, whatever the embankment type.**
   - *Engineering basis:* The pipe-length measurement of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox, which measures across the impervious core
     of a zoned embankment with very permeable outer zones (see
     [Seepage Path Length](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#seepage-path-length) in the CLE
     Common Methods manual); for the flaw scoring, the dimensions the continuity factor should measure are requested at marker IN-02 in
     [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-base-width-and-flaw-height).
   - *Effect on results:* A core narrower than the shell gives a shorter seepage path, a steeper gradient, and a higher initiation probability, and
     can raise the continuity score.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the shells are not free draining and share the head loss with the core; the shell section or a supplied seepage
     path length then describes the flaw better.
10. **The default initial pipe diameter is a triangular distribution from 2 mm to 5 mm, most likely 3 mm.**
    - *Engineering basis:* See
      [Initial Pipe Diameter](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#initial-pipe-diameter) in the CLE
      Common Methods manual.
    - *Effect on results:* The hydraulic shear stress is proportional to the diameter, so the range sets how high the pool must rise before
      initiation becomes likely; larger diameters (a range shifted upward) lower that pool level. The critical shear stress and the seepage path
      length also set that pool level.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where the poorly compacted zone is expected to carry a larger or smaller initial opening; a site-specific distribution
      should then be supplied.

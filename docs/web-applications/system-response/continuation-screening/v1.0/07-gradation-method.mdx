# Gradation Method

**Step served:** the probability of continuing erosion $P_{cont,j}$ for a pair in which both the base soil and the filter have gradation
envelopes (see [Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](./04-method-selection-and-governing-base-soil.mdx)).

**Question answered:** How much erosion of the base soil must occur before the filter seals, and how likely is it that the filter never seals?
A filter that satisfies the no-erosion particle retention criterion stops erosion with little or none; a coarser filter may still seal after some
or excessive erosion, or may allow erosion to continue. The Foster and Fell method divides the range of filter $D_{15}$ into those outcomes from
continuing erosion filter tests <Citation citationKey="FosterFell2001" />, and is applied to filters that do not satisfy the no-erosion
criterion.

The method runs the soil-to-soil filter evaluation of the Filter Evaluation (Continuation) Toolbox. The theory of each step lives in that manual.
Each section below links the chapter the step follows and documents only what the screening model supplies, what it uses from the result, and
every screening-level adjustment relative to the manual.

## Step 1: Particle-Size Analysis

The model analyzes each band of the base soil and filter gradation envelopes as described in the
[Filter Gradation](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/filter-gradation#filter-gradation)
chapter. For each band it determines the gravel content (75 mm to 4.75 mm), the fines content $FC$ (finer than 0.075 mm), the characteristic
sizes $D_{10}$, $D_{15}$, $D_{30}$, $D_{60}$, $D_{85}$, $D_{90}$, and $D_{95}$, and the coefficients of uniformity and curvature:

<Equation equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-6" equation="C_u = \frac{D_{60}}{D_{10}}, \qquad C_c = \frac{(D_{30})^2}{D_{10} \cdot D_{60}}" />

$C_u$ and $C_c$ are undetermined for a band that does not extend to 10 percent passing.

## Step 2: Base Soil Regrading Characteristics

The need to regrade the base soil is assessed for each band from four characteristics, following the
[Needs Assessment for Regrading](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/base-gradation#needs-assessment-for-regrading)
section of the Base Gradation chapter. In the toolbox the analyst answers each characteristic; the screening model derives three of them from the
particle-size analysis of the base soil:

- **Particles larger than the No. 4 sieve** — present when the band's gravel content is greater than zero; absent when it is zero or
  undetermined.
- **Fines content less than 15 percent** — when the band's $FC$ is less than 15 percent or undetermined.
- **Broadly graded** — derived from the band's coefficients by the ASTM D2487 limits <Citation citationKey="ASTM2487" />, treating the band as a
  gravel when its gravel content exceeds 50 percent and as a sand otherwise:

<Equation
  equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-7"
  equation="\text{broadly graded} = \begin{cases} C_u \ge 6 \ \text{and} \ 1 \le C_c \le 3 & \text{sand} \\ C_u \ge 4 \ \text{and} \ 1 \le C_c \le 3 & \text{gravel} \end{cases}"
/>

The gravel-or-sand test is the model's own simplification. ASTM D2487 treats a coarse-grained soil as a gravel when more than half of its coarse
fraction is gravel, that is, when its gravel content exceeds its sand content <Citation citationKey="ASTM2487" />; the model instead compares the
gravel content with 50 percent of the whole band. A band with more gravel than sand but no more than 50 percent gravel is therefore treated as a
sand and needs $C_u \ge 6$, rather than 4, to be broadly graded.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-17 — Engineering Basis]
The broad-grading assessment treats a band as a gravel when its gravel content exceeds 50 percent of the band, rather than when its gravel
content exceeds its sand content as in ASTM D2487. A gravelly band with a substantial sand or fines content is then held to the sand limit
$C_u \ge 6$; with less than 15 percent fines it is more often taken as not broadly graded and not regraded, which gives a larger $D_{85B}$ and a
less restrictive no-erosion limit. Provide the engineering basis or the engineering-judgment attribution for the 50 percent rule, or confirm
that the ASTM D2487 comparison of gravel with sand is intended.
:::

When a band's fines content is undetermined, because its gradation does not extend down to 0.075 mm (and does not end at zero percent
passing), the model treats the band as having less than 15 percent fines. When a band's gravel content is undetermined, because its gradation
does not cover the 75 mm and 4.75 mm sizes, the model treats the band as having no particles larger than the No. 4 sieve. When the fine band's
fines content is undetermined, the particle retention check also places the base soil in category 4 (see
[Particle retention check](#particle-retention-check)). These defaults can skip regrading, and they can change the no-erosion limit in either
direction: an unregraded band keeps a larger $D_{85B}$, while category 4 can give a larger or smaller limit than the category of the soil's actual
fines content.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-18 — Engineering Basis]
When a base soil band's fines content is undetermined, the model treats it as less than 15 percent; when its gravel content is undetermined, the
model treats the band as having no particles larger than the No. 4 sieve; and when the fine band's fines content is undetermined, the particle
retention check uses base soil category 4. These defaults can skip regrading and can raise or lower the no-erosion limit. Provide the engineering
basis or the engineering-judgment attribution for these defaults, or state how an undetermined fines or gravel content should be handled (for
example, rejecting the input).
:::

The fourth characteristic, **gap graded**, requires visual examination of the gradation curve and judgment, so the user supplies it with the
missing particle size $d_{gap}$ (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#base-soils)). A user-supplied broadly graded answer replaces the
derived value.

When $C_u$ and $C_c$ are undetermined and no broadly graded answer is supplied, the band is treated as not broadly graded. The toolbox manual
instead directs the analyst to extrapolate the gradation curve and calculate the coefficients externally.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-11 — Engineering Basis]
When the broad-grading assessment of a band is undetermined (its $C_u$ and $C_c$ cannot be calculated) and the user supplies no answer, the
model treats the band as not broadly graded. For a band containing gravel with less than 15 percent fines, this skips regrading on the No. 4
sieve, leaving a larger $D_{85B}$ and a less restrictive no-erosion limit. Provide the engineering basis or the engineering-judgment attribution
for this default, or confirm that the opposite (broadly graded, and therefore regraded) default is intended.
:::

## Step 3: Filter Evaluation

### Filter segregation adjustment

When segregation parameters are supplied for a filter band (see
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#filter-segregation-adjustment)), the model forms an adjusted filter gradation in which that band is
reshaped after segregation or washout of its finer fraction, following the
[Filter Gradation After Segregation or Washout](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/filter-gradation#filter-gradation-after-segregation-or-washout)
section. A band without parameters keeps its original curve in the adjusted gradation. The adjusted gradation enters only the particle retention
check below; see [Use of the adjusted filter gradation](#use-of-the-adjusted-filter-gradation).

### Base soil regrading

Each band of the base soil gradation is regraded according to its characteristics, following the
[Base Soil Gradation After Regrading](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/base-gradation#base-soil-gradation-after-regrading)
section <Citation citationKey="Sherard1989" />:

1. A gap-graded band is regraded on its missing particle size $d_{gap}$.
2. Otherwise, a band with no particles larger than the No. 4 sieve is not regraded.
3. Otherwise, the band is regraded to 100 percent passing the No. 4 sieve (4.75 mm), unless its fines content is less than 15 percent and it is not
   broadly graded, in which case it is not regraded.

Regrading divides the percent passing at each size at or below the regrading size by the percent passing at the regrading size (interpolated on
the band), multiplied by 100 percent, and sets the percent passing at larger sizes to 100 percent.

### Particle retention check

The coarsest filter $D_{15}$ (the $D_{15}$ of the coarse band) is compared with the maximum allowable $D_{15F}^{max}$ of the no-erosion criterion
(<EquationReference equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-3" /> and <EquationReference equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-4" />, including
the category 3 floor), evaluated from the $D_{85}$ and fines content of the fine band of the regraded base soil — the finest base soil, placed in
category 4 when its fines content is undetermined — following the
[Particle Retention Criterion](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/design-for-particle-retention#particle-retention-criterion)
section <Citation citationKey="FEMA2011" />. When an adjusted filter gradation exists, its coarsest $D_{15}$ is checked against the same limit.

If the original filter gradation, and the adjusted gradation when there is one, both satisfy the criterion, the filter is taken to stop erosion
with no or practically no erosion of the base soil. The Foster and Fell evaluation is not performed, and the pair takes

<Equation equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-8" equation="P_{NE} = 1, \qquad P_{SE} = P_{EE} = P_{CE} = 0, \qquad P_{cont,j} = 0" />

Otherwise, the Foster and Fell evaluation follows.

### Representative base soil gradations

The Foster and Fell evaluation uses three representative gradations of the regraded base soil — coarse, average, and fine — selected from the
envelope with the confidence interval $CI$, following the
[Base Soil Characterization](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/foster-and-fell#base-soil-characterization)
section of the Foster and Fell chapter <Citation citationKey="Toolbox2008" />. The toolbox manual denotes the percentage of the gradations
considered representative by $N$; this document calls it the confidence interval $CI$ and keeps $N$ for the number of Monte Carlo realizations
of the soil classification method. With the tail percentage

<Equation equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-9" equation="t = \frac{100 - CI}{2}" />

the representative coarse gradation moves the coarse band of the regraded envelope toward the fine band by $t$ percent of the distance between
them at each particle size of the coarse band, and the representative fine gradation moves the fine band toward the coarse band by the same
fraction at each particle size of the fine band:

<Equation
  equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-10"
  equation="F_{rc} = F_c + \frac{t}{100}\left(F_f - F_c\right), \qquad F_{rf} = F_f - \frac{t}{100}\left(F_f - F_c\right)"
/>

where:

> $F_c$, $F_f$ = percent passing of the coarse and fine bands of the regraded base soil envelope at the particle size  
> $F_{rc}$, $F_{rf}$ = percent passing of the representative coarse and fine gradations  
> $t$ = percentage of the envelope excluded at each limit

With the default $CI$ of 90 percent, $t = 5$ percent.

The representative average gradation is formed by averaging the representative coarse and fine gradations point by point: the percent passing
at each listed size of the coarse gradation is averaged with the percent passing at the same position in the list of the fine gradation, and
each average is assigned the particle size of the coarse band. This assumes that both bands of the base soil gradation are reported at the same
sieve sizes (Assumption 19 in [Assumptions and Limitations](./08-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#gradation-method)). The fines content $FC$ and
the fine-to-medium sand content $fm$ of the average gradation are the means of those of the representative coarse and fine gradations.

### Erosion boundaries

For each representative gradation, the model evaluates the filter $D_{15}$ boundaries between the erosion conditions, following the
[No-Erosion Boundary](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/foster-and-fell#no-erosion-boundary),
[Continuing Erosion Boundary](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/foster-and-fell#continuing-erosion-boundary),
and
[Excessive Erosion Boundary](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/foster-and-fell#excessive-erosion-boundary)
sections <Citation citationKey="FosterFell2001" /> <Citation citationKey="Toolbox2008" />:

- **No-erosion boundary** $(D_{15F})_{NE}$ — the no-erosion criterion of
  <EquationReference equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-3" /> or <EquationReference equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-4" />, evaluated
  from the representative gradation's $D_{85B}$ and $FC$. Unlike the particle retention check, the category 3 boundary is interpolated without
  the floor at the category 2 limit.
- **Continuing erosion boundary**:

<Equation equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-11" equation="(D_{15F})_{CE} = 9\,D_{95B}" />

- **Excessive erosion boundary**, by the base soil grouping of the representative gradation:

<Equation
  equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-12"
  equation="(D_{15F})_{EE} = \begin{cases} 9\,D_{95B} & D_{95B} \le 0.3 \text{ mm} \\ 9\,D_{90B} & 0.3 < D_{95B} \le 2 \text{ mm} \\ 9\,D_{85B} & D_{95B} > 2 \text{ mm},\ FC \le 15 \\ 2.5\left[\dfrac{(4\,D_{85B} - 0.7)(35 - FC)}{20} + 0.7\right] & D_{95B} > 2 \text{ mm},\ 15 < FC \le 35 \\ 0.34 \cdot 1.07^{fm} & D_{95B} > 2 \text{ mm},\ FC > 35 \end{cases}"
/>

where:

> $D_{85B}$, $D_{90B}$, $D_{95B}$ = characteristic sizes of the representative base soil gradation (mm)  
> $FC$ = fines content of the representative gradation (%)  
> $fm$ = fine-to-medium sand content of the representative gradation, the percentage between 1.18 mm and 0.075 mm (%)

The excessive erosion boundary is the same for a dispersive and a non-dispersive base soil; dispersivity enters the evaluation only through the
no-erosion boundary.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-12 — Decision]
The Excessive Erosion Boundary section of the Filter Evaluation (Continuation) Toolbox manual states that the excessive erosion boundary depends on
whether the base soil is non-dispersive or dispersive, but the model's excessive erosion boundary
(<EquationReference equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-12" />) does not depend on dispersivity. Confirm which is intended, and whether this
document or the toolbox manual should be corrected.
:::

### Erosion probabilities

For each representative gradation, the range of filter $D_{15}$ from the finest filter $D_{15}$ (the fine band of the original filter gradation)
to the coarsest (the coarse band) is divided among the four erosion conditions, following the
[Likelihood of Erosion](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/foster-and-fell#likelihood-of-erosion)
section. The proportion of the range coarser than $(D_{15F})_{CE}$ is the continuing erosion proportion; the proportion between
$(D_{15F})_{EE}$ and $(D_{15F})_{CE}$ is the excessive erosion proportion; the proportion between $(D_{15F})_{NE}$ and $(D_{15F})_{EE}$ is the
some-erosion proportion; and the remainder is the no-erosion proportion. Proportions are measured on a logarithmic scale of particle size.

The division needs both filter $D_{15}$ values. When a filter band's smallest measured percent passing is greater than 15 percent, so that the
gradation does not extend down to that size, its $D_{15}$ is undetermined (see
[Gradation Envelopes](./03-input-parameters.mdx#gradation-envelopes)). An undetermined coarse-band $D_{15}$ makes the particle retention check
fail, so the Foster and Fell evaluation runs. In the division, an undetermined $D_{15}$ is taken as zero, and the proportions then follow:

- When the fine band's $D_{15}$ is undetermined, every proportion except no erosion is zero, so $P_{cont,j} = 0$ however coarse the filter is.
- When only the coarse band's $D_{15}$ is undetermined, the continuing erosion proportion of each representative gradation is 1 if the fine
  band's $D_{15}$ is at or above that gradation's $(D_{15F})_{CE}$, and every proportion except no erosion is zero otherwise.

The first case can report no continuing erosion for a filter that is far too coarse.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-16 — Decision]
When the $D_{15}$ of a filter band is undetermined because the band's smallest measured percent passing is greater than 15 percent, the Foster and Fell division takes it as
zero. With the fine band's $D_{15}$ undetermined, every erosion proportion except no erosion is zero and $P_{cont,j} = 0$, even for a coarse
filter whose coarse band fails the particle retention check; with only the coarse band's $D_{15}$ undetermined, the result depends only on
whether the fine band's $D_{15}$ reaches the continuing erosion boundary of each representative gradation. Confirm whether this treatment is intended and supply the wording of
its basis, or state how an undetermined filter $D_{15}$ should be handled (for example, rejecting the input or taking $P_{cont,j} = 1.0$).
:::

The probabilities of the four conditions weight the proportions of the three representative gradations, giving the average gradation the
weight $CI/100$ and the coarse and fine gradations $(100 - CI)/200$ each:

<Equation
  equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-13"
  equation="P_X = \frac{100 - CI}{200}\,X_{coarse} + \frac{CI}{100}\,X_{average} + \frac{100 - CI}{200}\,X_{fine}, \qquad X \in \{NE,\ SE,\ EE,\ CE\}"
/>

where $P_{NE}$, $P_{SE}$, $P_{EE}$, and $P_{CE}$ are the probabilities of no, some, excessive, and continuing erosion, and $X_{coarse}$,
$X_{average}$, and $X_{fine}$ are the proportions of condition $X$ for the representative coarse, average, and fine gradations.

## Step 4: Screening Probability of Continuing Erosion

The screening model folds the probability of excessive erosion into the probability of continuing erosion:

<Equation equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-14" equation="P_{cont,j} = P_{CE} + P_{EE}" />

Excessive erosion is erosion extensive enough to damage the embankment before the filter seals (see the
[Foster and Fell](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/foster-and-fell) chapter), so the
screening model treats it as continuing erosion. Some erosion, after which the filter quickly seals, is not counted.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-13 — Engineering Basis]
The screening probability of continuing erosion is the sum of the Foster and Fell probabilities of continuing and excessive erosion
(<EquationReference equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-14" />). Provide a literature citation (full bibliographic details for the
bibliography) or the engineering-judgment attribution for treating excessive erosion as continuing erosion at the screening level.
:::

## Use of the Adjusted Filter Gradation

The adjusted filter gradation from the segregation adjustment affects only whether the Foster and Fell evaluation runs. When the original
filter gradation satisfies the particle retention check but the adjusted gradation does not, the Foster and Fell evaluation is performed — but
its probabilities, and therefore $P_{cont,j}$, are computed from the original filter gradation. The toolbox procedure instead evaluates the
adjusted gradation in parallel with the original one, as a separate event-tree branch for a segregated or internally unstable filter.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-14 — Decision]
The screening probability of continuing erosion always comes from the Foster and Fell evaluation of the original filter gradation; the adjusted
(segregated or washed-out) filter gradation only decides whether that evaluation runs. Confirm that this is the intended screening treatment, and
supply the wording of its basis, or state how the adjusted gradation should enter the probability (for example, the larger of the two results).
:::

## Permeability

The toolbox's soil-to-soil evaluation also checks the permeability (drainage) criterion of the filter, documented in the
[Permeability](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/permeability) chapter. The screening
model does not use the permeability result: $P_{cont,j}$ depends on particle retention alone.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-15 — Engineering Basis]
The probability of continuing erosion does not consider the permeability criterion of the filter. Provide the engineering basis or the
engineering-judgment attribution for excluding drainage from the continuation node.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the continuation node addresses whether the filter retains the eroding base soil; whether the filter drains freely
bears on other aspects of performance and is outside this node.
:::

## Screening-Level Adjustments

Relative to the soil-to-soil filter evaluation of the Filter Evaluation (Continuation) Toolbox, the screening gradation method fixes the
following:

- **Regrading characteristics are derived** — the No. 4, fines content, and broad-grading characteristics come from the particle-size analysis,
  with a user override for broad grading; only gap grading is supplied by the user (Step 2).
- **The confidence interval defaults to 90 percent** for the representative base soil gradations.
- **One probability is reported** — the sum of the continuing and excessive erosion probabilities (Step 4).
- **The adjusted filter gradation decides only whether the Foster and Fell evaluation runs** (see
  [Use of the Adjusted Filter Gradation](#use-of-the-adjusted-filter-gradation)).
- **Permeability is not used** (see [Permeability](#permeability)).
- **Constricted exits are not evaluated** — the toolbox's separate
  [Constricted Exit](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/constricted-exit) evaluation, for
  base soil eroding into a pipe, conduit, or crack, is not part of the screening model.

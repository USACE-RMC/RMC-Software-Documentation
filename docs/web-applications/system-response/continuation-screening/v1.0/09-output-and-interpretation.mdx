# Output and Interpretation

The model produces one probability of continuing erosion for the continuation node, the method and base soil that produced it, and the result of
each base soil pair. None of the results depends on the pool level.

## Node Result

- **Probability of continuing erosion** $P_{cont}$ — the continuation-node probability: the largest of the pair probabilities
  (see [Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](./04-method-selection-and-governing-base-soil.mdx)).
- **Governing method** — the method that produced $P_{cont}$ for the governing pair: the gradation method, the soil classification method, the
  year-of-construction method, or the no-information default.
- **Governing base soil** — the base soil whose pair produced $P_{cont}$. When pairs tie, the first of them in the order entered.

The governing pair's details, listed under Pair Results, also describe the node result.

## Pair Results

For each base soil, in the order entered:

- **Probability of continuing erosion** $P_{cont,j}$ and the **method** that produced it.
- **Foster and Fell probabilities** (gradation method only) — $P_{NE}$, $P_{SE}$, $P_{EE}$, and $P_{CE}$, the probabilities of no, some,
  excessive, and continuing erosion before they are combined into $P_{cont,j} = P_{CE} + P_{EE}$. When the filter satisfied the particle
  retention check, they are 1, 0, 0, and 0 (see [Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx)).

How the regrading characteristics and the particle sizes used by each method are resolved from the inputs is described in
[Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx#step-2-base-soil-regrading-characteristics) and
[Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx#input-resolution).

## Reading the Result

**Which method governs.** The governing method shows how much information stands behind $P_{cont}$. A result from the gradation method rests on
measured gradations of both materials; a result from the soil classification method rests on classification ranges for at least one of them; a
result from the year-of-construction method rests on the construction period alone; and the default, $P_{cont} = 1.0$, rests on no information.
When the governing pair used a less informative method than the other pairs, the least-characterized base soil may be controlling the node:
supplying its gradation or classification is the most direct way to refine the result.

**Which base soil governs.** Because the worst pair governs, adding base soils never lowers $P_{cont}$. A base soil entered with no
classification, no gradation, and no year of construction makes $P_{cont} = 1.0$.

**Gradation method results.** $P_{cont,j} = 0$ results either when the filter satisfied the no-erosion criterion for the finest regraded base
soil with every filter gradation considered, or when the whole filter range falls in the no-erosion and some-erosion zones of every
representative gradation. It also results when the fine band of the filter gradation does not extend down to 15 percent passing, so that its
$D_{15}$ is undetermined, and when only the coarse band's $D_{15}$ is undetermined and the fine band's $D_{15}$ is below the continuing erosion
boundary of every representative gradation (see
[Erosion probabilities](./07-gradation-method.mdx#erosion-probabilities) in the Gradation Method chapter). A positive result is the share of the filter $D_{15}$ range, weighted over the representative base soil
gradations, that lies in the excessive or continuing erosion zones; comparing $P_{EE}$ and $P_{CE}$ shows whether the filter is expected to seal
after damaging erosion or not at all. Because $P_{SE}$ is not counted, a filter range lying entirely between the no-erosion and excessive erosion
boundaries of every representative gradation gives zero even though the filter fails the no-erosion criterion.

**Soil classification method results.** The result is the fraction of Monte Carlo realizations in which the filter is coarser than the
no-erosion limit. It is sensitive to the classification and category ranges: a filter category whose $D_{15}$ range lies entirely above the
limits of a fine-grained base soil gives a result of 1.0 (see [Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx)). Because every
failing realization counts as continuing erosion, the result does not credit a filter that seals after some erosion.

**Reasonableness checks.**

- A gradation-method result of zero alongside a supplied filter segregation adjustment does not show that the adjusted filter gradation
  satisfied the criterion. When the original gradation passes and the adjusted one fails, the Foster and Fell evaluation runs on the original
  gradation, which returns zero whenever the original filter range lies at or below the excessive erosion boundary of every representative
  gradation. For very fine clays ($D_{95B}$ below about 0.022 mm), $9\,D_{95B}$ falls below the 0.2 mm no-erosion limit, so an original filter
  that passes the check can still lie in the continuing erosion zone and return a positive result (see Assumption 16 in
  [Assumptions and Limitations](./08-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#gradation-method)).
- A large difference between pairs using different methods can reflect the methods rather than the soils; supplying gradations for the
  less-characterized base soils puts the pairs on a common footing.

## Screening Versus Detailed Analysis

The model gives a screening-level estimate. Its no-information default and its governing-pair rule resolve incomplete information in the
direction of continuing erosion, but several of its defaults can lower the result (see [Intended Use](./02-model-overview.mdx#intended-use)). It fixes or derives steps that a
detailed evaluation leaves to the analyst, reports a single probability rather than the separate erosion conditions, and does not evaluate the
segregation branch, the permeability criterion, or constricted exits on their own terms (see
[Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx#screening-level-adjustments)). Where the continuation node is important to the risk estimate, a
detailed evaluation with the Filter Evaluation (Continuation) Toolbox — beginning with representative gradations selected by the analyst from all
available gradation tests — provides the basis for a higher-level analysis.

# Soil Classification Method

**Step served:** the probability of continuing erosion $P_{cont,j}$ for a pair whose base soil and filter lack a complete pair of gradation
envelopes, but whose base soil particle sizes and filter particle size can each be resolved from a classification, a category, or a single
gradation (see [Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](./04-method-selection-and-governing-base-soil.mdx)).

**Question answered:** Is the filter fine enough to satisfy the no-erosion particle retention criterion for the base soil? The criterion compares
the filter's $D_{15}$ with a maximum allowable $D_{15}$ set by the base soil's $D_{85}$ and fines content <Citation citationKey="FEMA2011" />. Where the particle sizes are known only as ranges for a soil classification or filter category, the method
answers the question over Monte Carlo realizations of those sizes and reports the fraction of realizations in which the filter is too coarse. The
criterion itself is documented in the
[Particle Retention Criterion](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/design-for-particle-retention#particle-retention-criterion)
section of the Filter Evaluation (Continuation) Toolbox manual; this chapter documents how the screening model supplies its inputs and uses its
result.

## Input Resolution

The method needs three quantities for each realization: the filter's $D_{15F}$, and the base soil's $D_{85B}$ and fines content $FC$ (percent
finer than the No. 200 sieve, 0.075 mm). Each comes either from a supplied gradation, as a single fixed value, or from a range, as a sampled
value:

- **Filter $D_{15F}$** — the $D_{15}$ of the coarse band of the filter gradation envelope when a filter gradation is supplied and that size is
  determined. This is the coarsest filter $D_{15}$, the value the particle retention check in the toolbox compares with the criterion. Otherwise,
  $D_{15F}$ is sampled from the range of the filter category (<TableReference tableKey="continuation-screening-table-11" />).
- **Base soil $D_{85B}$ and $FC$** — the $D_{85}$ and fines content of the fine band of the base soil gradation envelope when a base soil
  gradation is supplied and both values are determined. These describe the finest base soil, the gradation the toolbox's particle retention check
  uses. Otherwise, both are sampled from the ranges of the base soil classification
  (<TableReference tableKey="continuation-screening-table-10" />).

$D_{85B}$ and $FC$ always come from the same source. They describe the same fine fraction of one soil, so the model never pairs a measured
$D_{85B}$ with a sampled $FC$ or the reverse: when the base soil gradation determines only one of them, both are taken from the classification
ranges. A gradation supplied for only one material is used without the regrading of the gradation method (see
[Assumption 11](./08-assumptions-and-limitations.mdx#soil-classification-method) in Assumptions and Limitations).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Engineering Basis]
When the soil classification method uses a value from a single supplied gradation, it uses the gradation as entered, without the mathematical
regrading on the No. 4 sieve or on a missing particle size that the gradation method applies. Where the gradation method would regrade the
band — a gap-graded band, or a band containing gravel unless it has less than 15 percent fines and is not broadly graded — the unregraded
$D_{85B}$ is larger than the regraded value and the maximum allowable $D_{15F}$ is correspondingly larger. Provide the engineering basis or the
engineering-judgment attribution for using the unregraded gradation, or confirm that regrading should be applied in this case.
:::

## Monte Carlo Evaluation

In each Monte Carlo realization, every quantity resolved from a range is drawn from a uniform distribution between the range's bounds,
independently of the others, and every quantity resolved from a gradation keeps its fixed value.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-08 — Engineering Basis]
Each range-resolved quantity is sampled from a uniform distribution, and $D_{85B}$, $FC$, and $D_{15F}$ are sampled independently of one another
— including $D_{85B}$ and $FC$ of the same base soil, which describe the same fine fraction. Provide the engineering basis or the
engineering-judgment attribution for the uniform distribution and for sampling $D_{85B}$ and $FC$ independently.

Proposed (unconfirmed): with only a classification or category known, a uniform distribution takes no position on where within the range the
soil lies.
:::

### Base soil category

The realization's base soil category $BSC$ follows from its fines content <Citation citationKey="FEMA2011" />:

<Equation
  equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-2"
  equation="BSC = \begin{cases} 1 & FC > 85 \\ 2 & 40 < FC \le 85 \\ 3 & 15 < FC \le 40 \\ 4 & FC \le 15 \end{cases}"
/>

A classification whose fines content range spans a category boundary — the silty and clayey sands and gravels span categories 2, 3, and 4, and
the silts and clays span categories 1 and 2 — produces realizations in more than one category.

### Maximum allowable filter D15

The maximum allowable filter $D_{15}$ for the no-erosion condition, $D_{15F}^{max}$, depends on the category and on whether the base soil is
dispersive <Citation citationKey="FEMA2011" />. For a non-dispersive base soil:

<Equation
  equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-3"
  equation="D_{15F}^{max} = \begin{cases} \max(9\,D_{85B},\ 0.2) & BSC = 1 \\ 0.7 & BSC = 2 \\ \dfrac{40 - FC}{40 - 15}\left(4\,D_{85B} - 0.7\right) + 0.7 & BSC = 3 \\ 4\,D_{85B} & BSC = 4 \end{cases}"
/>

For a dispersive base soil:

<Equation
  equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-4"
  equation="D_{15F}^{max} = \begin{cases} \max(6.5\,D_{85B},\ 0.2) & BSC = 1 \\ 0.5 & BSC = 2 \\ \dfrac{40 - FC}{40 - 15}\left(4\,D_{85B} - 0.5\right) + 0.5 & BSC = 3 \\ 4\,D_{85B} & BSC = 4 \end{cases}"
/>

where:

> $D_{15F}^{max}$ = maximum allowable filter $D_{15}$ for the no-erosion condition (mm)  
> $D_{85B}$ = base soil $D_{85}$ (mm)  
> $FC$ = base soil fines content (%)

In category 3, the criterion interpolates linearly in fines content between the category 2 limit at 40 percent fines and the category 4 limit
$4\,D_{85B}$ at 15 percent fines. When $4\,D_{85B}$ is smaller than the category 2 limit (0.7 mm, or 0.5 mm for a dispersive soil), the model uses
the category 2 limit for category 3 instead of interpolating, so that a category 3 soil, which has fewer fines than a category 2 soil, is never
given a more restrictive limit than a category 2 soil. The floor raises the allowable $D_{15F}$ above the interpolated value, so it is the less
conservative of the two choices: it can lower $P_{cont,j}$.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-09 — Engineering Basis]
The category 3 floor — using the category 2 limit (0.7 mm, or 0.5 mm for a dispersive base soil) whenever $4\,D_{85B}$ is smaller than it —
requires a basis. Provide the literature citation or engineering-judgment attribution. Note also that the no-erosion boundary of the Foster and
Fell evaluation in the gradation method interpolates category 3 without this floor (see
[Erosion boundaries](./07-gradation-method.mdx#erosion-boundaries) in the Gradation Method chapter); confirm
whether the two should agree.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the floor corresponds to the instruction in published filter criteria to use the category 2 limit when $4\,D_{85B}$ is less
than it.
:::

### Probability of continuing erosion

The filter fails the criterion in a realization when $D_{15F} > D_{15F}^{max}$. The probability of continuing erosion is the fraction of
realizations in which it fails:

<Equation
  equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-5"
  equation="P_{cont,j} = \frac{N_{fail}}{N}"
/>

where $N_{fail}$ is the number of realizations in which the filter fails the no-erosion criterion and $N$ is the number of realizations. The
method makes no distinction between a filter that just fails the no-erosion criterion and one that is coarse enough for continuing erosion: every
failing realization counts as continuing erosion.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-10 — Engineering Basis]
The method counts every realization that fails the no-erosion criterion (<EquationReference equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-3" /> and
<EquationReference equationKey="continuation-screening-eq-4" />) as continuing erosion, although a filter coarser than the no-erosion limit may
still seal after some or excessive erosion (see the
[Foster and Fell](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/foster-and-fell) chapter). Provide the
engineering basis or the engineering-judgment attribution for this treatment.

Proposed (unconfirmed): without gradation envelopes, the some-erosion and excessive-erosion boundaries cannot be evaluated, so the screening
method resolves the uncertainty conservatively by treating any filter coarser than the no-erosion limit as allowing continuing erosion.
:::

## Behavior of the Result

- When the filter $D_{15F}$ is fixed by a gradation and the base soil is sampled, $P_{cont,j}$ is the fraction of the base soil's classification
  range for which that filter is too coarse. When the base soil is fixed by a gradation and the filter is sampled, it is the fraction of the
  filter category's $D_{15}$ range that is too coarse for that base soil.
- With the classification ranges of the silts and clays (categories 1 and 2), $D_{15F}^{max}$ lies between 0.2 mm and 0.7 mm (0.5 mm for a
  dispersive soil). A filter whose $D_{15F}$ lies entirely above the upper limit — any Gravel with sand or Gravel filter — then fails in every
  realization, giving $P_{cont,j} = 1.0$, and a filter whose $D_{15F}$ lies entirely below 0.2 mm passes in every realization, giving zero.
- A dispersive base soil never raises $D_{15F}^{max}$ — it lowers or leaves unchanged the category 1, 2, and 3 limits and leaves category 4
  unchanged — so it does not reduce the likelihood that the filter fails the criterion. In category 1, both criteria give 0.2 mm when $D_{85B}$
  is about 0.022 mm or less, which covers the whole CL, MH, and CH ranges, so dispersivity has no effect there.
- The result is a single value for the pair; it does not depend on the pool level.

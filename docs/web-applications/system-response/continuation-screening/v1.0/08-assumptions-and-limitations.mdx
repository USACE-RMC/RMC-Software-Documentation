# Assumptions and Limitations

The continuation screening model applies deliberate simplifications relative to a full filter evaluation. They let the model accept whatever
filter information is available. Several resolve missing information in the direction of continuing erosion, but not all: Assumptions 11, 12,
15, 16, and 20 can each lower the probability, the gradation method does not count some erosion (Assumption 14), and a filter gradation whose
fine band does not extend down to 15 percent passing gives zero in the gradation method (see Gradation coverage under
[Limitations](#limitations)). Each assumption below states
its engineering basis, its effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold. Where an engineering basis is still awaiting confirmation, the
assumption cross-references the input marker at its primary location in this document.

## Method Selection and Combination

1. **Each base soil is evaluated by the most informative method its inputs support.** Inputs for a lower-priority method are ignored when a
   higher-priority method applies (see [Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](./04-method-selection-and-governing-base-soil.mdx)).
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): measured gradations describe the base soil and filter more
     completely than classification ranges, which in turn describe them more completely than a construction period.
   - *Effect on results:* Supplying better data changes the method and can move the result in either direction. A year of construction
     supplied alongside a gradation or classification has no effect on that pair only when the gradation method applies (both materials have
     gradation envelopes), or when both sides of the pair resolve for the soil classification method: the filter side through a filter
     gradation that determines $D_{15}$ or a filter category other than Unknown, and the base side through a supported classification or a base
     soil gradation whose fine band determines both $D_{85}$ and fines content. Otherwise the pair falls to the year-of-construction method, and
     the year decides the result — for example, a base soil classification or gradation with no filter gradation and no filter category (or an
     Unknown category).
   - *When it may not hold:* Gradations that are not representative of the materials in place (for example, a few tests from a borrow area
     rather than the constructed zone), where a less detailed method could be the better indicator.
2. **The base soil the filter is least able to retain governs.** The node probability is the largest pair probability.
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): the filter must stop erosion of every base soil it protects, so
     the worst pair controls.
   - *Effect on results:* Adding a base soil can only raise or leave unchanged the node probability. An entry with no information takes
     $P_{cont} = 1.0$ and governs.
   - *When it may not hold:* A base soil listed that cannot in fact reach the filter along the erosion path under consideration, which then
     controls the result without physical justification.
3. **When no method can be selected for a pair and no year of construction is supplied, continuation is certain.** No method can be selected
   when neither the gradation method nor the soil classification method has its inputs: the pair lacks either envelope, and the filter
   $D_{15}$ or the base soil $D_{85}$ and fines content cannot be resolved. This covers a pair with no information, and also a pair with a full
   base soil gradation but no filter information.
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): without evidence that the filter can retain the base soil, the
     filter cannot be credited with stopping erosion.
   - *Effect on results:* $P_{cont} = 1.0$ for such pairs, the most conservative value.
   - *When it may not hold:* Filters known from construction records or inspection to be well designed, for which supplying a gradation,
     classification, or construction year gives a more realistic result.
4. **Continuation does not depend on the pool level or on the erosion mechanism.** The same inputs give the same probability at every
   headwater, for any internal-erosion failure mode.
   - *Engineering basis:* Particle retention is governed by the relationship between the particle-size distributions of the base soil and the
     filter <Citation citationKey="FEMA2011" />, and the Foster and Fell erosion boundaries were derived from continuing erosion filter tests under
     high water pressures <Citation citationKey="FosterFell2001" />.
   - *Effect on results:* The calling screening model applies one continuation probability across its range of pool levels.
   - *When it may not hold:* Flow conditions at the filter face well beyond those of the filter tests on which the criteria rest, such as much
     higher hydraulic gradients or flow velocities, where the head could affect whether and how quickly the filter seals.

## Year-of-Construction Method

5. **The construction period alone sets the probability of continuing erosion when nothing else is known.** The probability is 0.9 before 1950,
   0.5 from 1950 to 1970, 0.25 from 1971 to 1985, and 0.1 after 1985.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-04 in [Year-of-Construction Method](./05-year-of-construction-method.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* Every pair evaluated by this method receives the same probability, whatever its base soil.
   - *When it may not hold:* Embankments whose filters were designed ahead of, or behind, the practice of their period, and zones that were
     never designed as filters.

## Soil Classification Method

6. **The ranges for each soil classification represent the base soil's $D_{85}$ and fines content.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-06 in [Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* The ranges set the spread of maximum allowable filter $D_{15}$ and therefore the fraction of realizations that fail.
   - *When it may not hold:* Base soils whose measured particle sizes fall outside the ranges for their classification; a base soil gradation
     then gives a better result.
7. **The ranges for each filter category represent the filter's $D_{15}$.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see markers IN-07 in [Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx) and IN-01
     (category definitions) in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* A category range that lies wholly above the base soil's maximum allowable $D_{15F}$ gives $P_{cont,j} = 1.0$, and one
     that lies wholly below it gives zero, whatever the actual filter $D_{15}$ is within that range. The Gravel range (10 to 50 mm) lies above
     every limit the base soil classification ranges can produce, so a Gravel filter gives 1.0 against any base soil evaluated from its
     classification ranges.
   - *When it may not hold:* Filters near a category boundary or outside their category's range; a filter gradation then gives a better
     result.
8. **Range-resolved quantities are uniform and independent.** $D_{85B}$, $FC$, and $D_{15F}$ are each drawn from a uniform distribution, with no
   correlation between them.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-08 in [Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* Realizations can pair a coarse $D_{85B}$ with a high fines content, combinations that may be rare in real soils of
     that classification.
   - *When it may not hold:* Soils in which the $D_{85}$ and fines content are strongly related, as they are within one gradation curve.
9. **Only silty and clayey sands and gravels, silts, and clays carry classification ranges.** The clean and slightly silty sands and gravels
   (GP, GW, GP-GM, SP, SW, SP-SM) have no ranges, and an input set that pairs one of these classes with a filter category other than Unknown is
   rejected.
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-06 in [Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx).
   - *Effect on results:* Such base soils need a gradation for any method other than the year-of-construction method or the default. With a
     filter category other than Unknown, the input set is rejected even when a gradation is supplied, unless the classification is left out
     (see [Base Soils](./03-input-parameters.mdx#base-soils) in Input Parameters).
   - *When it may not hold:* Embankments with clean sand or gravel zones upstream of the filter and no gradations available.
10. **Failing the no-erosion criterion counts as continuing erosion.** The method does not distinguish some or excessive erosion from
    continuing erosion.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-10 in [Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* For the same materials, the probability can exceed the gradation method's, which credits filters that seal after
      some erosion.
    - *When it may not hold:* Filters only moderately coarser than the no-erosion limit, which the Foster and Fell evaluation would place in the
      some-erosion or excessive-erosion zone.
11. **A single supplied base soil gradation is used without regrading.** The base soil $D_{85}$ and fines content are taken from the gradation
    as entered.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-05 in [Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* Where the full evaluation would regrade the band — a gap-graded band, or a band containing gravel unless it has less
      than 15 percent fines and is not broadly graded — the unregraded $D_{85}$ is larger than the regraded value, which raises the allowable
      filter $D_{15}$ and can lower the probability.
    - *When it may not hold:* Gravelly or gap-graded base soils, for which regrading would apply in a full evaluation.
12. **The category 3 limit is not allowed to fall below the category 2 limit.** When $4\,D_{85B}$ is smaller than 0.7 mm (0.5 mm for a dispersive
    soil), the category 3 criterion uses the category 2 limit.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-09 in [Soil Classification Method](./06-soil-classification-method.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* Fine base soils in category 3 receive the same limit as category 2 soils rather than a more restrictive one. The floor
      raises the allowable filter $D_{15}$, so it can lower the probability. The Foster and Fell no-erosion boundary in the gradation method does
      not apply the floor, so the particle retention check and the Foster and Fell evaluation can use different no-erosion limits for the same
      soil.
    - *When it may not hold:* Category 3 soils whose fine fraction is fine enough that the interpolated limit lies well below the category 2
      value, for which the floored limit may be coarser than needed to retain them.

## Gradation Method

13. **A filter that satisfies the no-erosion criterion with every filter gradation considered stops erosion.** The Foster and Fell evaluation is
    not performed, and the probability of continuing erosion is zero.
    - *Engineering basis:* Filters meeting the no-erosion particle retention criterion seal with no or practically no erosion of the base soil <Citation citationKey="FEMA2011" /> <Citation citationKey="FosterFell2001" />; see the
      [Design for Particle Retention](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/design-for-particle-retention)
      chapter.
    - *Effect on results:* The result is zero while the coarsest filter $D_{15}$ is within the limit for the finest regraded base soil, unless
      an adjusted filter gradation fails the check. The Foster and Fell evaluation then runs on the original gradation with no-erosion
      boundaries that do not apply the category 3 floor, so in edge cases the result can be positive. Beyond the limit, the Foster and Fell
      evaluation sets the result, which is positive only when part of the filter range lies in the excessive or continuing erosion zone.
    - *When it may not hold:* Filters whose gradation in place is coarser than the gradations supplied, through segregation or internal
      instability not captured by the segregation adjustment.
14. **Excessive erosion counts as continuing erosion.** The screening probability is the sum of the Foster and Fell probabilities of continuing
    and excessive erosion.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-13 in [Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* The probability is at least the Foster and Fell probability of continuing erosion, and larger whenever part of the
      filter range lies in the excessive erosion zone.
    - *When it may not hold:* Embankments that can tolerate the erosion and leakage that occur before an excessive-erosion filter seals.
15. **An undetermined broad-grading assessment is taken as not broadly graded.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-11 in [Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* A gravelly band with less than 15 percent fines is then not regraded, giving a larger $D_{85B}$ and a less
      restrictive no-erosion limit.
    - *When it may not hold:* Base soil gradations that do not extend to 10 percent passing; supplying the broad-grading answer removes the
      default.
16. **The adjusted filter gradation decides only whether the Foster and Fell evaluation runs.** The probabilities are computed from the original
    filter gradation.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-14 in [Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* The adjustment matters only when the original gradation passes the particle retention check and the adjusted one
      fails it. The original gradation's Foster and Fell result is then zero only when its filter range lies at or below the excessive erosion
      boundary of every representative gradation. Passing the particle retention check does not ensure this: for very fine clays ($D_{95B}$
      below about 0.022 mm), $9\,D_{95B}$ falls below the 0.2 mm no-erosion limit, so a filter that passes the check can still lie in the
      continuing erosion zone and give a positive result.
    - *When it may not hold:* Filters for which segregation or washout is likely, where the adjusted gradation's own Foster and Fell result
      would be larger.
17. **The permeability criterion does not enter the probability.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-15 in [Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* A filter that retains the base soil but drains poorly is credited fully at the continuation node.
    - *When it may not hold:* Filters whose limited permeability allows pressures to build up and the filter or the base soil to be bypassed.
18. **The excessive erosion boundary does not depend on dispersivity.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Decision requested — see marker IN-12 in [Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx).
    - *Effect on results:* For a dispersive base soil, the excessive erosion zone is the same as for a non-dispersive soil of the same gradation.
    - *When it may not hold:* Dispersive base soils, if the excessive erosion boundary should be stricter for them.
19. **Both bands of the base soil gradation are reported at the same sieve sizes.** The representative average gradation is formed by averaging
    the representative coarse and fine gradations point by point.
    - *Engineering basis:* The average gradation is obtained for each input sieve size across the envelope (see
      [Base Soil Characterization](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/foster-and-fell#base-soil-characterization)
      in the Foster and Fell chapter of the toolbox manual).
    - *Effect on results:* With matching sieve sizes, the average gradation lies midway between the representative coarse and fine gradations at
      every size, and its characteristic sizes set the average gradation's erosion boundaries, which carry the weight $CI/100$.
    - *When it may not hold:* Bands reported at different sieve sizes or in a different number of sizes, for which the averaged points do not
      correspond to a common particle size and the average gradation, and therefore the result, is distorted.
20. **A band is treated as a gravel in the broad-grading assessment when its gravel content exceeds 50 percent.** Otherwise it is treated as a
    sand.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-17 in [Gradation Method](./07-gradation-method.mdx#step-2-base-soil-regrading-characteristics).
    - *Effect on results:* A band with more gravel than sand but no more than 50 percent gravel must reach $C_u \ge 6$, not 4, to be broadly
      graded. With less than 15 percent fines, it is then more often taken as not broadly graded and not regraded, which leaves a larger
      $D_{85B}$ and a less restrictive no-erosion limit.
    - *When it may not hold:* Gravelly base soils with a substantial sand or fines content, which the classification standard would call a
      gravel.

## Limitations

- **One filter boundary per evaluation.** The model evaluates one filter against its base soils. For a multi-stage filter, each zone boundary is
  a separate evaluation, from the finest to the coarsest filter material, with the finer filter acting as the base soil for the next (see the
  [Background](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/background) chapter of the toolbox
  manual).
- **Gradation coverage.** A characteristic size outside the range of a supplied gradation is undetermined. A filter gradation whose smallest
  measured percent passing is greater than 15 percent, or a base soil gradation whose smallest measured percent passing is greater than 85
  percent, does not extend down to that size and cannot supply it; the representative gradations
  and erosion boundaries of the Foster and Fell evaluation also depend on the bands covering a common range of particle sizes. In the gradation
  method, a filter band that does not extend down to 15 percent passing gives an unconservative result: when the fine band's $D_{15}$ is undetermined,
  $P_{cont,j} = 0$ however coarse the filter (see
  [Erosion probabilities](./07-gradation-method.mdx#erosion-probabilities) in the Gradation Method chapter and marker IN-16).
- **Soil filters only.** Erosion into a concentrated exit such as an open joint, defect, or crack in a pipe or conduit is not evaluated. The
  toolbox evaluates it separately as a
  [Constricted Exit](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/filter-evaluation-continuation/v1.0/constricted-exit) rather than as
  a soil filter.
- **Very high fine-to-medium sand content.** For a representative base soil gradation with more than 35 percent fines and a very high
  fine-to-medium sand content, the excessive erosion boundary can exceed the continuing erosion boundary. The some-erosion and continuing erosion
  zones then overlap and the proportions no longer divide the filter range consistently. The literature does not address this combination.
- **Screening level.** The result is a screening-level estimate. A detailed filter evaluation with the Filter Evaluation (Continuation) Toolbox
  gives the analyst control over the representative gradations, the regrading assessment, the segregation branch, and the permeability check.

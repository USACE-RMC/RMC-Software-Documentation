# Flaw Assessment

Node 1 of the event tree is the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous zone of poorly compacted earthfill exists along the conduit, through the
embankment. The model estimates it by the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />,
applied to four conduit factors. The scheme — the factors, the weighted sum, and its log-linear conversion to a probability — is documented in the
[Risk-Factor and Likelihood-Factor Scoring](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. This chapter documents the conduit model's factors, its likelihood-factor tables, its flaw-probability
curve, its observation override, and its flaw elevation. The assessment does not depend on pool level: $P_{flaw}$ applies across the whole system
response curve.

## Factors and Risk Factors

The model scores four factors, each with a fixed risk factor $RF$ that weights its influence
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-5" />). The conduit type and surround carries the largest weight; the other three
are weighted equally.

## Weighted Sum

The likelihood factors combine into the weighted sum described in the
[Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#weighted-sum) section of the CLE Common
Methods manual, which for the four conduit factors is:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-eq-1"
  equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_{cd} + 2\,LF_{col} + 2\,LF_{cmp} + 2\,LF_{tr}"
/>

Because the trench factor scores at least 2, the weighted sum ranges from 11 (every factor at its most favorable choice) to 34 (every factor at its
least favorable choice). A selection of Unknown for all four factors gives $\Sigma = 27$.

## Flaw Probability

The weighted sum is converted to the raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$ by interpolating the logarithm of the probability linearly between the points
of <TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-10" />, as described in the
[Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
section of the CLE Common Methods manual.

Over the attainable range of the weighted sum, the flaw probability runs from $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ at $\Sigma = 11$ to about
$4.8 \times 10^{-2}$ at $\Sigma = 34$; the last tabulated point, $\Sigma = 36$, cannot be reached and serves only to interpolate above
$\Sigma = 28$. With every factor Unknown ($\Sigma = 27$), the flaw probability is about $8.2 \times 10^{-3}$.

The model applies none of the rules that can force the raw flaw probability to zero in other CLE models: no factor is marked as physically
incompatible with a flaw, and no lower threshold on the weighted sum applies, since the lowest attainable sum is the first tabulated point. Nor does
the model apply a flaw-continuity stage (see the
[Flaw-Continuity Stage](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-continuity-stage) section of
the CLE Common Methods manual). Without an observation, the flaw probability is the scored value:

<Equation equationKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-eq-2" equation="P_{flaw} = P_{raw}(\Sigma)" />

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-10 — Engineering Basis]
The flaw-probability curve of <TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-10" /> is recorded in the model as the curve for one
loading condition, described as the pool of record plus 20 percent, and the model applies it at every pool level. Provide: (1) the table or figure
of Fell et al. (2024) from which the six points are taken; (2) a definition of the "pool of record plus 20 percent" loading condition; and (3) the
engineering basis for using the curve of that one loading condition for the flaw probability at every pool level, given that the source may
tabulate other loading conditions (for example, pools below the pool of record, or first filling) with different probabilities.
:::

## Leakage Observation Override

When leakage adjacent to the conduit has been observed, the flaw is taken as demonstrated and the flaw probability is set to 1.0:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-eq-3"
  equation="P_{flaw} = 1.0 \quad \text{when leakage adjacent to the conduit is observed}"
/>

The observation makes the four factor selections optional. When all four factors are given, the weighted sum and raw flaw probability remain
available for comparison, but the flaw probability is 1.0. The override sets only
Node 1: it does not change the flaw elevation, and every downstream node is evaluated as usual, as described in the
[Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) section
of the CLE Common Methods manual.

## Flaw Elevation

The base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$ is the bottom-of-conduit elevation the user supplies (see
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-inputs)). It is used directly — no heuristic is applied — and it is not changed by the leakage
observation. It fixes the invert of the idealized pipe for initiation, the elevation at which the seepage path length is measured, and the
elevation of the failure pathway that the diaphragm filter and the embankment materials describe (see
[Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw)
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-11 — Engineering Basis]
Two choices about the flaw elevation require an engineering basis to state here: (1) placing the flaw at the bottom of the conduit, rather than at
another elevation along the conduit's height such as its haunches or springline; and (2) keeping the flaw at the bottom of the conduit when
leakage has been observed, whatever the elevation at which the leakage emerges, where the core compaction model instead moves its flaw to the
observed leakage elevation. Provide the literature source or the engineering-judgment attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the bottom of the conduit is the lowest elevation a flaw along the conduit can occupy, so initiation can begin at the
lowest pool level and the net head across the flaw is largest there (the seepage path is also longest there, which partly offsets the larger
head); leakage along a conduit is taken to follow the conduit, so its emergence point does not locate the flaw better than the conduit itself.
Confirm or replace.
:::

# Flaw Assessment

Node 1 of the concentrated leak erosion (CLE) event tree is the probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous zone of poorly compacted earthfill exists
along the conduit, through the embankment. The model estimates it by the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring of
<Citation citationKey="Fell2024" form="narrative" />, applied to four conduit factors. The scheme — the factors, the weighted sum, and its log-linear conversion to
a probability — is documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="flaw-conventions" anchor="risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring">Risk-Factor and Likelihood-Factor Scoring</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. This chapter documents the conduit model's factors, its likelihood-factor tables, its flaw-probability
curve, its observation override, and its flaw elevation. The assessment does not depend on pool level: $P_{flaw}$ applies across the whole system
response curve.

## Factor Scores

The model scores four factors. Each factor $j$ carries a fixed risk factor $RF_j$ that weights its influence and a likelihood factor $LF_j$ on the
scale of 1 (least likely to produce a flaw) to 4 (most likely) (<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-6" />). The conduit
type and surround carries the largest weight; the other three are weighted equally.

A conduit with cutoff collars scores higher than one without. The method of <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" form="narrative" /> scores poorly
detailed collars as more likely to produce a flaw, with a likelihood factor of 3. The model assumes that every collar present is poorly detailed, so
it uses no intermediate score for well-detailed collars. When the presence of collars is not known, the model assumes that they exist, so an Unknown
selection scores the same as Present and a missing record does not lower the screening result. Where the collars are known to be well detailed and
the fill around them was compacted to the standard of the rest of the fill, the score of 3 overstates the likelihood of a flaw.

An Unknown trench is scored as a trench of medium width, depth, and slope. The trench factor has no choice scored 1: the most favorable trench,
wide with slopes flatter than 1H:1V, scores 2. The trench factor therefore contributes at least 4 to the weighted sum (twice its risk factor), which
sets the minimum attainable sum at 11.

## Weighted Sum

The likelihood factors combine into the weighted sum described in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="flaw-conventions" anchor="weighted-sum">Weighted Sum</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual, which for the four conduit factors is:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-eq-1"
  equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_{cd} + 2\,LF_{col} + 2\,LF_{comp} + 2\,LF_{tr}"
/>

where $\Sigma$ is the weighted sum, $LF_{cd}$ is the likelihood factor of the conduit type and surround, $LF_{col}$ is the likelihood factor of the
cutoff collars, $LF_{comp}$ is the likelihood factor of the compaction adjacent to the conduit, and $LF_{tr}$ is the likelihood factor of the
conduit trench details, all dimensionless. Because the trench factor scores at least 2, the weighted sum ranges from 11 (every factor at its most
favorable choice) to 34 (every factor at its least favorable choice). A selection of Unknown for all four factors gives $\Sigma = 27$.

The weighted sum is converted to the raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$ by interpolating the logarithm of the probability linearly between the points
of <TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-11" />, as described in the

Over the attainable range of the weighted sum, the raw flaw probability runs from $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ at $\Sigma = 11$ to about
$4.8 \times 10^{-2}$ at $\Sigma = 34$; the last tabulated point, $\Sigma = 36$, cannot be reached and serves only to interpolate above
$\Sigma = 28$. With every factor Unknown ($\Sigma = 27$), the raw flaw probability is about $8.2 \times 10^{-3}$.

The model applies none of the rules that can force the raw flaw probability to zero: no factor is marked as physically incompatible with a flaw,
and no lower threshold on the weighted sum applies, since the lowest attainable sum is the first tabulated point. Nor does the model apply a
flaw-continuity stage (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="flaw-conventions" anchor="flaw-continuity-stage">Flaw-Continuity Stage</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual). Without an observation, the flaw probability $P_{flaw}$ is therefore the raw flaw probability $P_{raw}(\Sigma)$.

The flaw-probability curve of <TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-11" /> is recorded in the model as the curve for one
loading condition, described as the pool of record plus 20 percent, and the model applies it at every pool level. Provide: (1) the table or figure
of Fell et al. (2024) from which the six points are taken; (2) a definition of the "pool of record plus 20 percent" loading condition; and (3) the
engineering basis for using the curve of that one loading condition for the flaw probability at every pool level, given that the source may
tabulate other loading conditions (for example, pools below the pool of record, or first filling) with different probabilities.
:::

## Observation Override

When leakage adjacent to the conduit has been observed, the flaw is taken as demonstrated and the flaw probability is set to 1.0, as described in
the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="flaw-conventions" anchor="observation-overrides">Observation Overrides</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. The observation makes the four factor selections optional. When all four factors are given, the weighted
sum and raw flaw probability remain available for comparison, but the flaw probability is 1.0. The override sets only Node 1: it does not change
the flaw elevation, and every downstream node is evaluated as usual.

## Base-of-Flaw Elevation

The base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$ is the supplied bottom-of-conduit elevation (see
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-location)). It is used directly — no heuristic is applied — and it is not changed by the leakage
observation. It fixes the invert of the idealized pipe for initiation, the elevation at which the seepage path length is measured, and the
elevation of the failure pathway that the diaphragm filter and the embankment materials describe (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="flaw-conventions" anchor="compaction-models-base-of-the-flaw">Compaction Models: Base of the Flaw</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-11 — Engineering Basis]
Two choices about the flaw elevation require an engineering basis to state here: (1) placing the flaw at the bottom of the conduit, rather than at
another elevation along the conduit's height such as its haunches or springline; and (2) keeping the flaw at the bottom of the conduit when
leakage has been observed, whatever the elevation at which the leakage emerges. Provide the literature source or the engineering-judgment
attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the bottom of the conduit is the lowest elevation a flaw along the conduit can occupy, so initiation can begin at the
lowest pool level and the net head across the flaw is largest there (the seepage path is also longest there, which partly offsets the larger
head); leakage along a conduit is taken to follow the conduit, so its emergence point does not locate the flaw better than the conduit itself.
Confirm or replace.
:::

# Assumptions and Limitations

The conduit compaction model shares the assumptions of the CLE screening family — the product form of the event tree, the cylindrical-pipe
idealization and its default critical shear stress, the horizontal seepage path at the flaw elevation, and the tabulated progression probabilities
— listed with their bases in the
[Assumptions and Limitations](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) chapter of the
CLE Common Methods manual. The assumptions of the continuation method are listed in the
[Assumptions and Limitations](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) chapter of the
Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual. This chapter lists the assumptions specific to a conduit flaw. Each states its engineering
basis, its effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold.

## Flaw

1. **The flaw is a continuous zone of poorly compacted earthfill along the conduit.** Low-stress zones over or beside the conduit, and erosion of
   embankment soil into the conduit through open joints or defects, are not part of the flaw the model scores.
   - *Engineering basis:* Scope requested — see marker IN-01 in [Model Overview](./02-model-overview.mdx#intended-use).
   - *Effect on results:* The flaw probability reflects only the factors that govern how well the fill against the conduit was compacted.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where differential settlement, arching over a rigid conduit, or conduit deterioration is the more likely cause of a
     flaw; those conditions call for a separate assessment.
2. **The flaw probability is scored from four conduit factors weighted 3, 2, 2, and 2, with no flaw-continuity stage.** (See
   [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#factors-and-risk-factors).)
   - *Engineering basis:* The risk-factor and likelihood-factor scheme of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the source of the
     conduit factors and weights, and the basis for applying no flaw-continuity stage, are requested at marker IN-07 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#factors-and-risk-factors).
   - *Effect on results:* Across its choices, the conduit type and surround moves the weighted sum by up to 9, the compaction adjacent to the
     conduit by up to 6, and the cutoff collars and the trench details by up to 4 each. The compaction category enters the flaw probability only
     once, through its weight, unlike the three compaction models that also apply a flaw-continuity probability.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where construction records or inspection give direct evidence of the fill condition along the conduit; the risk
     team's estimate of the flaw probability then replaces the screening value.
3. **Cutoff collars raise the likelihood of a flaw, and an unknown collar condition is scored as collars present.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-08 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#cutoff-collars).
   - *Effect on results:* Collars present, or unknown, add 4 to the weighted sum relative to collars absent, which multiplies the flaw
     probability by about 2 to 4, depending on where the sum lies on the curve.
   - *When it may not hold:* Conditions requested with the basis at marker IN-08 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#cutoff-collars).
4. **Unknown selections for the conduit type, the compaction adjacent to the conduit, and the trench details score 3, and the trench factor has no
   choice scored 1.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-09 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#conduit-trench-details).
   - *Effect on results:* With every factor Unknown, the weighted sum is 27 and the flaw probability is about $8.2 \times 10^{-3}$, about 55
     times the value for the most favorable conduit ($1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ at a weighted sum of 11). No conduit scores below a weighted sum of 11.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the conduit, its fill, or its trench can be characterized from drawings or records; the known choice should then
     be selected.
5. **The flaw-probability curve for the pool-of-record-plus-20-percent loading condition applies at every pool level.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-10 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability).
   - *Effect on results:* The flaw probability does not reflect whether the embankment has experienced the pool levels being evaluated.
   - *When it may not hold:* For first filling, or for pools well beyond the pool of record, where a loading-specific flaw probability may differ.
6. **An observation of leakage adjacent to the conduit sets the flaw probability to 1.0 without changing the flaw elevation.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Engineering judgment for the override, as for the other CLE models (see
     [Observation Overrides](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides) in
     the CLE Common Methods manual); the basis for keeping the flaw elevation is requested at marker IN-11 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-elevation), and the qualifying observation at marker IN-04 in
     [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#flaw-inputs).
   - *Effect on results:* With leakage observed, the SRP is governed by the downstream nodes, and initiation still begins at the bottom of the
     conduit.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the leakage has another explanation, such as seepage along a different path or leakage from the conduit
     itself.
7. **The flaw lies at the bottom of the conduit.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-11 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-elevation).
   - *Effect on results:* Initiation is zero, and the SRP negligible, until the pool rises far enough above the bottom of the conduit for the
     hydraulic shear stress to reach the lowest critical shear stress, which can be well above the conduit bottom. While the tailwater is below
     the bottom of the conduit, the bottom of the conduit gives the largest net head across the flaw, but it also measures the seepage path where
     the embankment section is widest, so the two effects on the hydraulic gradient oppose each other. With the tailwater above the bottom of the
     conduit, the net head is the headwater minus the tailwater whatever the flaw elevation, as long as that elevation is below the tailwater, and
   only the path-length effect remains; for a flaw elevation $z$ above the tailwater, the net head would instead be $h - z$.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the poorly compacted zone is known to lie at another elevation along the conduit, such as at its haunches.

## Initiation

8. **The poorly compacted zone is idealized as a cylindrical pipe with a default initial diameter between 5 and 12.5 mm, most likely 10 mm.**
   - *Engineering basis:* The cylindrical-pipe idealization of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox (see
     [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation)); the basis of the default range is requested at marker IN-06 in
     [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#initial-pipe-diameter).
   - *Effect on results:* The shear stress is proportional to the diameter, so the default range, larger than the core compaction model's, places
     initiation at lower pool levels than the core model's default would.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the opening along the conduit is better represented as a gap or crack against the conduit wall, or where its
     size can be estimated from cracking analysis; a site-specific diameter distribution should then be supplied.
9. **The seepage path is measured horizontally across the embankment section at the bottom of the conduit.**
   - *Engineering basis:* The pipe-length measurement of the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation toolbox, as adopted by every compaction model
     (see [Seepage Path Length](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#seepage-path-length) in the CLE
     Common Methods manual).
   - *Effect on results:* For a conduit that crosses the embankment on a skew, the measured path is shorter than the true path along the
     conduit, so the hydraulic gradient and the initiation probability are overstated, or unchanged where initiation is 0 or 1 (conservative).
     For a conduit whose grade changes elevation across the section, the single bottom-of-conduit elevation also misstates the net head: taken
     at the higher upstream invert, it understates the head to the lower exit, so the net effect on the gradient can go either way.
   - *When it may not hold:* For a conduit that crosses the embankment on a skew, or one whose grade changes elevation across the section; the
     length along the conduit should then be supplied.

## Continuation and Progression

10. **Continuation is credited only for a diaphragm filter around the conduit; with none, continuation is certain.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Engineering judgment: with no filter credited, the screening takes continuation as certain, to avoid false negatives
      (conservative; see
      [Node 3: Continuation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-3-continuation)
      in the CLE Common Methods manual); the treatment of other filters is requested at marker IN-05 in
      [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#diaphragm-filter).
    - *Effect on results:* A diaphragm filter multiplies the SRP by the continuation probability at every pool level; without one, the continuation
      node does not reduce the SRP.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where another filter intercepts the zone along the conduit, or where erosion exits into an open defect of the
      conduit itself upstream of the diaphragm. Such erosion bypasses the filter, so crediting $P_{ce}$ there is unconservative; the continuation
      method does not evaluate exits into the conduit.
11. **No credit is taken for flow limitation by upstream elements.**
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-12 in
      [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation).
    - *Effect on results:* An upstream facing or cutoff wall does not reduce the SRP of a conduit flaw (conservative).
    - *When it may not hold:* Where an upstream element demonstrably limits flow into the zone around the conduit; a detailed analysis
      can then assess the credit.

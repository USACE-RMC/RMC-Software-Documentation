# Event Tree

The concentrated leak erosion (CLE) conduit compaction model computes the system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level as the
product of the eight-node CLE event tree, whose structure, node questions, and headwater grid are documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree">Event Tree</DocLink>
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual. This chapter documents how each node is populated for a conduit flaw.

The flaw is at one elevation, the bottom of the conduit, so the filter it meets and the materials it passes through do not change with pool level.
Only initiation varies with pool level, as for every compaction model (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="pool-dependence">Pool Dependence</DocLink> in the CLE Common Methods
manual).

## Node 1: Flaw

*Question:* Does a continuous zone of poorly compacted earthfill exist along the conduit, through the embankment?

$P_{flaw}$ is the result of the four-factor scoring, or 1.0 when leakage adjacent to the conduit has been observed, as documented in
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx). It is the same at every pool level.

## Node 2: Initiation

*Question:* At each pool level, does the flow through the poorly compacted zone at the bottom of the conduit exert a hydraulic shear
stress on the walls of the zone that exceeds the critical shear stress of the soil?

The zone is idealized as a cylindrical pipe and the question is answered by the cylindrical-pipe initiation analysis shared by the compaction
models, documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="delegated-method-cylindrical-pipe-initiation">Delegated Method: Cylindrical Pipe Initiation</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual and, for its theory, in the
[Cylindrical Pipe](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/concentrated-leak-erosion-initiation/v1.0/cylindrical-pipe) chapter of
the Concentrated Leak Erosion Initiation manual. At each pool level $h$ with tailwater $h_t$, the analysis takes the net head across the pipe above
its invert, divides it by the seepage path length $L$ to obtain the average hydraulic gradient, computes the hydraulic shear stress on the wall of
a pipe of initial diameter $D$, and reports the initiation probability $P_{init}(h)$ as the fraction of Monte Carlo realizations, each drawing one
critical shear stress $\tau_c$ and one diameter $D$, in which the shear stress exceeds the critical shear stress.

The conduit model supplies the analysis with the following:

- **Pipe invert** — the bottom-of-conduit elevation $z_f$. A pool at or below the bottom of the conduit gives no flow through the pipe and zero
  initiation.
- **Seepage path length** — unless a length is supplied, the horizontal width of the embankment section at the bottom of the conduit,
  measured across the core section when one is supplied and across the shell otherwise, as described in the
  <DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="seepage-path-length">Seepage Path Length</DocLink> section of the CLE
  Common Methods manual. A supplied length suits a conduit that does not run perpendicular to the embankment centerline or a surveyed path along
  the conduit.
- **Critical shear stress** — unless a distribution is supplied, the triangular range for the core soil classification from the shared table
  in the <DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="critical-shear-stress">Critical Shear Stress</DocLink>
  section of the CLE Common Methods manual. The classifications SP, SW, GP, GW, SP-SM, and GP-GM are not accepted, because non-plastic soils are
  not modeled (see [Shared Inputs](./03-input-parameters.mdx#shared-inputs)).
- **Initial pipe diameter** — unless a distribution is supplied, this model's default triangular distribution with low 5 mm, most likely 10 mm,
  and high 12.5 mm, listed in
  <DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="initial-pipe-diameter">Initial Pipe Diameter</DocLink>
  in the CLE Common Methods manual. Because the shear stress is proportional to the diameter, a larger diameter shifts initiation to lower pool
  levels for the same soil and section.

The model applies the screening-level adjustments common to the compaction models, listed in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="screening-level-adjustments">Screening-Level Adjustments</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual, and makes none of its own.

## Node 3: Continuation

*Question:* Does an unfiltered seepage exit exist that allows erosion along the conduit to continue?

The model answers the question with one input: whether a diaphragm filter surrounds the conduit. With no diaphragm filter, the exit is unfiltered
and continuation is certain, $P_{cont} = 1.0$. With a diaphragm filter, $P_{cont}$ is the probability of continuing erosion $P_{ce}$, the node
result of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening model (written $P_{cont}$ in that manual), evaluated from the description of the filter
and the base soils upstream of it.

The continuation method selects, for each base-soil and filter pair, the most informative method whose inputs are available (or certain
continuation when none is), and the worst base soil governs, as documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="method-selection-and-governing-base-soil">Method Selection and Governing Base Soil</DocLink>
chapter of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual; the value it returns is described in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="output-and-interpretation" anchor="node-result">Node Result</DocLink> section of that manual.
With no diaphragm filter the method is not evaluated. Either way, $P_{cont}$ is one value applied at every pool level: the gate is a statement
about the embankment, not an elevation compared with the pool. This is the compaction-model form of the continuation node described in
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-3-continuation">Node 3: Continuation</DocLink>
of the CLE Common Methods manual. The applicability of the diaphragm-filter question to other filters is requested at marker IN-05 in
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#diaphragm-filter).

## Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

*Question:* Does the material over the enlarging pipe along the conduit hold a roof, so that the pipe stays open?

$P_{roof}$ is the minimum roof-support probability across the embankment materials the pipe passes through, from the table of

in
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-4-progression--roof-support">Node 4: Progression — Roof Support</DocLink>

## Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

*Question:* Does the enlarging pipe remain unfilled by material carried in from upstream?

$P_{nf}$ depends only on the embankment type, from the table given in
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-5-progression--no-crack-filling">Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling</DocLink>
of the CLE Common Methods manual: 1.0 for homogeneous and zoned earthfill, and 0.5 for central-core earth and rockfill.

## Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

*Question:* Does the flow into the enlarging pipe remain unrestricted by upstream seepage-limiting elements?

The model represents no upstream seepage-limiting element for a conduit flaw: it takes no input of upstream facings or cutoff walls, and the
probability of no flow limitation $P_{nl}$ is fixed at

<Equation equationKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-eq-2" equation="P_{nl} = 1.0" />

at every pool level. No upstream element reduces the SRP of this model, whereas the general form of the node credits an upstream facing or a
cutoff wall that reaches the flaw (see
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation">Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual, where the engineering basis of taking no flow-limitation credit for a conduit flaw is requested with the other
flow-limitation conventions).

## Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

*Questions:* Do detection and intervention fail (Node 7)? Does an uncontrolled release of impounded water occur (Node 8)?

$P_{int}$ and $P_{br}$ are the user-specified values, applied at every pool level, as documented in
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="intervention-and-breach">Intervention and Breach</DocLink> in the
CLE Common Methods manual.

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each pool level $h$ is the product of the eight node probabilities, as documented in
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="srp-computation">SRP Computation</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual, with the flow-limitation probability fixed at 1.0. Only $P_{init}(h)$ varies with pool level; the other seven
factors are constant. Where the product is zero, the SRP is reported as a negligibly small value instead. The product is zero at and below the
bottom of the conduit; above it, until the hydraulic gradient is large enough for the shear stress to reach the critical shear stress in some
realizations; wherever the tailwater reaches the headwater, so that there is no net head across the pipe; and at every pool level where the
probability of unsuccessful intervention or of breach is set to 0. Where the tailwater is absent
or does not rise as fast as the headwater, the curve therefore rises with pool level from that negligible value toward the product of the seven
constant factors, which it reaches only if every realization initiates; a rising tailwater can flatten or lower it, and it stays negligible at
every pool level if any constant factor is zero (see [Output and Interpretation](./07-output-and-interpretation.mdx)).

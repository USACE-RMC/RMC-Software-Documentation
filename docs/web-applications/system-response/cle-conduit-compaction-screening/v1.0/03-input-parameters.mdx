# Input Parameters

The concentrated leak erosion (CLE) conduit compaction model takes most of the inputs shared by the CLE compaction models, documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters">Input Parameters</DocLink>
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual, and adds the inputs specific to a conduit flaw. This chapter states the unit system, documents the
conduit-specific inputs — the four flaw factors, the bottom-of-conduit elevation, the leakage observation, and the diaphragm filter — and then
lists which shared inputs apply. Each group gives the short facts for its inputs in a table, followed by a description of each input.

## Unit System

The model takes a unit system input, US customary (the default) or SI. All elevations and lengths are entered in the length unit of that system
(feet or meters) and share one vertical datum. The input changes no formula in this model, and quantities with fixed units (critical shear stress
in lb/ft², initial pipe diameter in millimeters) are stated in those units regardless of the unit system. The datum convention shared by the CLE
family, including the requirement that the compaction models place the embankment above zero elevation, is documented in
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="unit-system">Unit System</DocLink>
in the CLE Common Methods manual.

The four conduit factors are required unless leakage adjacent to the conduit is observed. Each is a choice among the options listed with their
likelihood factors in <TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-7" /> (conduit type and surround: concrete-encased round
pipe with sloping sides, concrete-encased round pipe with vertical sides, masonry or brick with vertical sides, round pipe not encased, or Unknown),
<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-8" /> (cutoff collars: Absent, Present, or Unknown),
<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-9" /> (compaction adjacent to the conduit: mechanical, slightly wet of optimum;
by hand, slightly dry of optimum; by hand in thick layers, dry of optimum; no formal compaction; or Unknown), and
<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-10" /> (conduit trench details: wide, with slopes flatter than 1H:1V; medium
width, depth, and slope; narrow and deep, with near-vertical sides; or Unknown). A round pipe not encased includes corrugated metal pipe. Cutoff
collars are also called seepage collars. No formal compaction covers fill against the conduit that was not formally compacted or was poorly
compacted. A wide trench is one whose side slopes are flatter than one horizontal to one vertical. Unknown applies wherever the conduit type, the
presence of collars, the method of compaction, or the trench geometry is not known.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Definition]
The conduit-factor categories are named without fuller definitions. State what each covers so that a user can select among them, in particular:
(1) whether "round pipe not encased" includes corrugated metal and other flexible pipes, and which choice applies to conduits not listed, such as
a cast-in-place rectangular or horseshoe conduit or a concrete box culvert; (2) whether "no formal compaction" also covers poor compaction in very
thick lifts without moisture control; and (3) the trench widths, depths, and side slopes that separate the wide, medium, and narrow trench
categories, given that only the wide category carries a stated slope limit (flatter than 1H:1V). Cite the table of Fell et al. (2024) that
defines the categories, if they are taken from it.

Proposed (unconfirmed): "round pipe not encased" includes corrugated metal pipe; "no formal compaction" covers poor compaction with very thick
lifts and no moisture control; and the narrow, deep trench is one backfilled with soil. Confirm or replace.
:::

The bottom-of-conduit elevation is the elevation of the bottom of the conduit, which the model uses as the base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$: the
invert of the idealized pipe, and the elevation of the failure pathway (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#base-of-flaw-elevation)). It must be a positive elevation on the common datum, no higher than the top
of the embankment section used for initiation (the core top when a core section is supplied, otherwise the crest), and no lower than the lowest
segment bottom of each face of that section. It is required even when leakage is observed, and the observation does not change it.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-04 — Definition]
State what observation qualifies as "leakage adjacent to the conduit" for the override — for example, seepage emerging along the outside of the
conduit at its outlet, wet areas over the conduit alignment on the downstream slope, or flow carrying fines — and whether leakage into the conduit
through its joints qualifies.
:::

## Diaphragm Filter

The model states the presence of a filter on the failure pathway as the presence of a diaphragm filter around the conduit
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-5" />).

:::note[Availability]
The continuation inputs apply only when a diaphragm filter is present.
:::

With no diaphragm filter, the exit is taken as unfiltered and continuation is certain. The continuation inputs describe the diaphragm filter and the
base soils upstream of it, as documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="base-soils">Base Soils</DocLink>
and <DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="filter">Filter</DocLink>
sections of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual. With no diaphragm filter they are not used, even if supplied.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Applicability]
The model recognizes only a diaphragm filter around the conduit as a filter on the failure pathway. State whether the user should answer yes for
other filters that intercept the zone along the conduit — for example, a chimney or blanket filter that the conduit passes through, or a filter
envelope placed around the downstream portion of the conduit — or whether those are intended to be left uncredited at the screening level.
:::

## Shared Inputs

The model takes the following shared inputs, each as documented in the CLE Common Methods manual:

- **<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="hydraulic-loading">Hydraulic loading</DocLink>**
  — the headwater elevation series $h$ and the optional tailwater series $h_t$. Tailwater, when it stands above the bottom of the conduit, reduces
  the net head across the pipe in the initiation analysis.
- **<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="embankment-description">Embankment description</DocLink>**
  — the crest elevation $z_c$, the embankment type, the shell top width and face segments, and the optional core section. When a core section is
  supplied it is used for the seepage path length and for the limits on the bottom-of-conduit elevation, whatever the embankment type; otherwise
  the shell section is used. The model scores no section geometry in its flaw assessment.
- **<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="failure-pathway-inputs">Failure-pathway inputs</DocLink>**
  of the compaction models — the filter presence, stated here as the presence of a diaphragm filter around the conduit (see
  [Diaphragm Filter](#diaphragm-filter)), the embankment materials the pipe passes through, and the continuation inputs.
- **<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="initiation-material-inputs">Initiation material inputs</DocLink>**
  — the core soil classification, and the optional critical shear stress distribution, initial pipe diameter distribution, and seepage path
  length. The core soil classification may not be SP, SW, GP, GW, SP-SM, or GP-GM, because non-plastic soils are not modeled; these
  classifications are refused even when a critical shear stress distribution is supplied. Left blank, the initial pipe diameter takes this model's
  default distribution, listed in
  <DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="initial-pipe-diameter">Initial Pipe Diameter</DocLink>
  in the CLE Common Methods manual (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation)); a site-specific distribution, of any of the five
  distribution forms the compaction models accept, replaces the default.
- **<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="intervention-and-breach">Intervention and breach</DocLink>**
  — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention $P_{int}$ and of breach $P_{br}$.

The model does not take the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="input-parameters" anchor="upstream-seepage-limiting-elements">upstream seepage-limiting elements</DocLink>
or a top-of-wall elevation: it has no flow-limitation input, and its Node 6 is fixed at 1.0 (see
[Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Definition]
The core soil classification selects the default critical shear stress range of the initiation analysis. For this model it is not stated which
soil it describes: the earthfill compacted adjacent to the conduit, in which the flaw would erode, or the core of the embankment. State which soil
the user should classify, and how to choose when the fill adjacent to the conduit differs from the core.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the classification of the earthfill adjacent to the conduit, since that is the soil whose walls the concentrated leak
erodes. Confirm or replace.
:::

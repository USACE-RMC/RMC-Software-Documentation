# Input Parameters

The conduit compaction model takes most of the inputs shared by the CLE compaction models, documented in the
[Common Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) chapter of the CLE Common
Methods manual, and adds the inputs specific to a conduit flaw. This chapter lists which shared inputs apply, then documents the conduit-specific
inputs: the four flaw factors, the leakage observation, the bottom-of-conduit elevation, the diaphragm filter, and the default initial pipe
diameter. Each group gives the short facts for its quantities in a table, followed by a description of each input and, for selections, the list of
available choices.

## Shared Inputs

The model takes the following shared inputs, each as documented in the CLE Common Methods manual:

- **[Hydraulic loading](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#hydraulic-loading)** — the
  headwater elevation series $h$ and the optional tailwater series $h_t$. Tailwater, when it stands above the bottom of the
  conduit, reduces the net head across the pipe in the initiation analysis.
- **[Units and datum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#units-and-datum)** — every
  elevation on one common datum, with the embankment above zero elevation.
- **[Embankment description](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description)**
  — the crest elevation $z_c$, the embankment type, the shell top width and face segments, and the optional core section. When a core section is
  supplied it is used for the seepage path length and for the limits on the bottom-of-conduit elevation, whatever the embankment type; otherwise
  the shell section is used. The model scores no section geometry in its flaw assessment.
- **[Failure-pathway inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#failure-pathway-inputs)**
  of the compaction models — the filter presence, stated here as the presence of a diaphragm filter around the conduit (see
  [Diaphragm Filter](#diaphragm-filter)), the embankment materials the pipe passes through, and the continuation inputs.
- **[Initiation material inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#initiation-material-inputs)**
  — the core soil classification, and the optional critical shear stress distribution, initial pipe diameter distribution, and seepage path
  length. The core soil classification may not be SP, SW, GP, GW, SP-SM, or GP-GM, because non-plastic soils are not modeled; these
  classifications are refused even when a critical shear stress distribution is supplied. The initial pipe diameter default specific to this model is given under [Initial Pipe Diameter](#initial-pipe-diameter).
- **[Intervention and breach](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#intervention-and-breach)**
  — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention $P_{int}$ and of breach $P_{br}$, each defaulting to 1.0.

The model does not take the
[upstream seepage-limiting elements](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#upstream-seepage-limiting-elements)
or a top-of-wall elevation: it has no flow-limitation input, and its Node 6 is fixed at 1.0 (see
[Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation)).

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Definition]
The core soil classification selects the default critical shear stress range of the initiation analysis. For this model it is not stated which
soil it describes: the earthfill compacted adjacent to the conduit, in which the flaw would erode, or the core of the embankment. State which soil
the user should classify, and how to choose when the fill adjacent to the conduit differs from the core.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the classification of the earthfill adjacent to the conduit, since that is the soil whose walls the concentrated leak
erodes. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Flaw Inputs

The flaw inputs describe the conduit and the fill around it, and fix the elevation of the flaw
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-2" />). How the model scores them is documented in

The four conduit factors are required unless leakage adjacent to the conduit is observed.

- **Conduit type and surround** — the form of the conduit and of any concrete encasing it. The choices are:
  - **Concrete-encased round pipe with sloping sides** — a round pipe in a concrete encasement whose sides slope.
  - **Concrete-encased round pipe with vertical sides** — a round pipe in a concrete encasement whose sides are vertical.
  - **Masonry or brick with vertical sides** — a masonry or brick conduit with vertical sides.
  - **Round pipe not encased** — a round pipe with no concrete encasement.
  - **Unknown** — the conduit type or its surround is not known.
- **Cutoff collars** — whether cutoff collars (also called seepage collars) project from the conduit into the surrounding fill. The choices are
  **Absent**, **Present**, and **Unknown**.
- **Compaction adjacent to the conduit** — how the earthfill against the conduit was compacted. The choices are:
  - **Mechanical, slightly wet of optimum** — compacted by mechanical equipment at a water content slightly wet of optimum.
  - **By hand, slightly dry of optimum** — compacted by hand at a water content slightly dry of optimum.
  - **By hand in thick layers, dry of optimum** — compacted by hand in thick layers at a water content dry of optimum.
  - **No formal compaction** — the fill against the conduit was not formally compacted.
  - **Unknown** — the method of compaction is not known.
- **Conduit trench details** — the geometry of the trench in which the conduit was laid. The choices are:
  - **Wide, with slopes flatter than 1H:1V** — a wide trench whose side slopes are flatter than one horizontal to one vertical.
  - **Medium width, depth, and slope** — a trench of intermediate width, depth, and side slope.
  - **Narrow and deep, with near-vertical sides** — a narrow, deep trench with near-vertical sides.
  - **Unknown** — the trench geometry is not known.
- **Leakage adjacent to the conduit observed** — whether leakage has been observed adjacent to the conduit. A yes sets the flaw probability to
  1.0 and makes the four factor selections optional; the bottom-of-conduit elevation is still required and is not changed (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#leakage-observation-override)).
- **Bottom-of-conduit elevation** — the elevation of the bottom of the conduit, which the model uses as the base-of-flaw elevation $z_f$: the
  invert of the idealized pipe, and the elevation of the failure pathway (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-elevation)). It must be a positive elevation on the common datum, no higher than the top of the
  embankment section used for initiation (the core top when a core section is supplied, otherwise the crest), and no lower than the lowest
  segment bottom of each face of that section.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Definition]
The conduit-factor categories are named without fuller definitions. State what each covers so that a user can select among them, in particular:
(1) whether "round pipe not encased" includes corrugated metal and other flexible pipes, and which choice applies to conduits not listed, such as
a cast-in-place rectangular or horseshoe conduit or a concrete box culvert; (2) whether "no formal compaction" also covers poor compaction in very
thick lifts without moisture control; and (3) the trench widths, depths, and side slopes that separate the wide, medium, and narrow trench
categories, given that only the wide category carries a stated slope limit (flatter than 1H:1V). Cite the table of Fell et al. (2024) that
defines the categories, if they are taken from it.

Proposed (unconfirmed): "round pipe not encased" includes corrugated metal pipe; "no formal compaction" covers poor compaction with very thick
lifts and no moisture control; and the narrow, deep trench is one backfilled with soil. Confirm or replace.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-04 — Definition]
State what observation qualifies as "leakage adjacent to the conduit" for the override — for example, seepage emerging along the outside of the
conduit at its outlet, wet areas over the conduit alignment on the downstream slope, or flow carrying fines — and whether leakage into the conduit
through its joints qualifies.
:::

## Diaphragm Filter

The model states the presence of a filter on the failure pathway as the presence of a diaphragm filter around the conduit
(<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-3" />).

- **Diaphragm filter around the conduit** — whether a diaphragm filter surrounds the conduit and intercepts the failure pathway. With no
  diaphragm filter, the exit is taken as unfiltered and continuation is certain.
- **Continuation inputs** — the description of the diaphragm filter and of the base soils upstream of it, as documented in the
  [Base Soils](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#base-soils) and
  [Filter](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#filter) sections of the Continuation (Filter
  Evaluation) Screening manual. They are required, and checked, only when a diaphragm filter is present; with no diaphragm filter they are not
  used, even if supplied.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-05 — Applicability]
The model recognizes only a diaphragm filter around the conduit as a filter on the failure pathway. State whether the user should answer yes for
other filters that intercept the zone along the conduit — for example, a chimney or blanket filter that the conduit passes through, or a filter
envelope placed around the downstream portion of the conduit — or whether those are intended to be left uncredited at the screening level.
:::

## Initial Pipe Diameter

The initial pipe diameter $D$ of the idealized cylindrical pipe is an optional distribution input, shared with the other compaction models (see
[Initiation Material Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#initiation-material-inputs)
in the CLE Common Methods manual). Left blank, it defaults to the triangular distribution of
<TableReference tableKey="cle-conduit-compaction-screening-table-4" />, which is specific to this model. A site-specific distribution, of any of
the five distribution forms the compaction models accept, replaces the default.

The default initial pipe diameter of the conduit compaction model (triangular, low 5 mm, most likely 10 mm, high 12.5 mm) requires an engineering
basis, including why it is larger than the core compaction model's default (triangular, 2, 3, and 5 mm). Provide a literature source or the

(its marker IN-11), so the two should be resolved together.

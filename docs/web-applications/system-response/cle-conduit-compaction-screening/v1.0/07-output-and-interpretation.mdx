# Output and Interpretation

The model reports the SRP at each evaluated pool level together with the probability of each event-tree node, in the form common to the CLE
compaction models described in the
[Output and Interpretation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) chapter of the CLE
Common Methods manual. This chapter documents what is specific to the conduit compaction model's results and how to read them.

## Pool-Independent Results

- **Flaw probability** — Node 1: the scored flaw probability, or 1.0 when leakage adjacent to the conduit has been observed (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).
- **Continuation probability** — Node 3: the continuation method's result when a diaphragm filter surrounds the conduit, otherwise 1.0. The
  continuation method's own results — the method used for each base soil and the governing base soil — are reported only when a diaphragm filter
  is present, and are described in the
  [Output and Interpretation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) chapter of the
  Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual.
- **Roof-support probability** — Node 4: the minimum across the embankment materials.
- **Crack-filling probability** — Node 5: the probability of no crack filling for the embankment type.

No flow-limitation result is reported: the node is fixed at 1.0 (see
[Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-6-progression--no-flow-limitation)).

## Pool-Dependent Series

- **Pool levels and tailwater** — the headwater elevations at which the tree is evaluated and the tailwater at each, as described in the
  [Pool-Dependent Series](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#pool-dependent-series)
  section of the CLE Common Methods manual. Where there is no tailwater, the downstream head above the bottom of the conduit is taken as zero.
- **Initiation probability** — Node 2 at each pool level.
- **Unsuccessful-intervention and breach probabilities** — Nodes 7 and 8 at each pool level.
- **SRP** — the system response curve.

## Supporting Results

- **Flaw assessment** — the likelihood factors of the four conduit factors, the weighted sum $\Sigma$, the raw flaw probability $P_{raw}$, the
  final flaw probability, and the base-of-flaw elevation (the bottom of the conduit).
- **Initiation analysis** — at each pool level, the upstream and downstream heads above the bottom of the conduit, the hydraulic gradient, and the
  mean and 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles of the hydraulic shear stress; the seepage path length; and the low, most likely, and high values of the
  critical shear stress and initial pipe diameter distributions used, which confirm the critical shear stress and diameter ranges that governed
  the result.

## Interpreting the System Response Curve

The curve stays negligible from the bottom of the conduit up to the pool level at which the hydraulic shear stress first reaches the lowest
critical shear stress, then rises with pool level as the hydraulic gradient along the pipe grows, where the tailwater does not offset the
headwater rise, following the initiation probability. Because every other node is constant, the curve rises toward the product of the constant
nodes (Node 6 being 1.0),

<EquationNoRef equation="\mathrm{SRP}_{max} = P_{flaw} \times P_{cont} \times P_{roof} \times P_{nf} \times P_{int} \times P_{br}" />

and reaches it only where every realization initiates. The node breakdown shows which node sets that ceiling:

- **The flaw probability** enters it at between $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ and about $4.8 \times 10^{-2}$, or at 1.0 when leakage has
  been observed. The flaw-assessment results show which conduit factors drive it; the conduit type and surround carries the largest weight.
- **A diaphragm filter** multiplies it by the continuation probability.
- **A cohesionless material on the pathway** multiplies it by its roof-support probability, which can lower it by up to two orders of magnitude.
- **A central-core earth and rockfill embankment** halves it through the crack-filling node.
- **No upstream element** lowers it: a cutoff wall or upstream facing has no effect on this model's curve.

The pool level at which the curve begins to rise, and how steeply it rises, are set by the critical shear stress and the initial pipe diameter
relative to the hydraulic gradient through the section at the bottom of the conduit. A curve that levels off below the ceiling reflects
realizations that do not initiate within the evaluated pool range, as discussed in
[Reading the Node Breakdown](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#reading-the-node-breakdown)
in the CLE Common Methods manual. A rising tailwater that reduces the net head across the pipe can flatten or lower the curve over that range.

## Checking the Result

The checks of
[Checking the Result](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#checking-the-result) in
the CLE Common Methods manual apply. For this model in particular:

- **Lower end** — the SRP is negligible at every pool level at or below the bottom of the conduit, because there is no upstream head above the
  flaw. A curve that never rises within the pool range can point to a bottom-of-conduit elevation on a different datum from the headwater
  series.
- **Upper bound** — the SRP never exceeds $\mathrm{SRP}_{max}$ above, with no flow-limitation factor in the product, except where
  $\mathrm{SRP}_{max}$ is zero, when the reported SRP is the negligible floor value.
- **Flaw probability** — without a leakage observation, the reported flaw probability can be reproduced from the four likelihood factors with the
  weighted sum and the curve of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability); it lies between $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ and about
  $4.8 \times 10^{-2}$.
- **Continuation** — a continuation probability of 1.0 is expected when no diaphragm filter is stated. With a diaphragm filter stated present,
  a value of 1.0 means that the continuation method itself returned certain continuation; the governing method reported in its
  [Node Result](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#node-result) section shows why.

## Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis

The model is a screening tool. Its result indicates whether concentrated leak erosion along a conduit warrants detailed analysis; it is not a
substitute for one. The screening simplifications — the four-factor flaw score, the bottom-of-conduit flaw elevation, the default initial pipe
diameter and critical shear stress, and the absence of flow-limitation credit — are chosen to avoid false negatives. Where the result is
significant to a decision, the governing nodes should be re-examined with site-specific information, such as construction records of the conduit
backfill, inspection of the conduit and its outlet, and erodibility testing of the adjacent fill, and the methods of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> and the
judgment of the risk team should replace the screening values.

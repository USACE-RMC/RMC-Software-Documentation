# Model Overview

## Purpose

The conduit compaction screening model computes a system response curve for concentrated leak erosion through poorly compacted earthfill adjacent
to a conduit, as a function of headwater elevation. The system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level is the conditional probability
of breach of the embankment by concentrated leak erosion along the conduit, given that pool level.

The flaw is a continuous zone of loose or poorly compacted earthfill along the conduit, through which water from the reservoir can flow and erode
the surrounding soil. The model scores the likelihood of such a flaw from four factors that describe the conduit and the placement of the fill
around it, places the flaw at the bottom of the conduit, and carries it through the eight-node CLE event tree.

## Intended Use

The model is a screening-level assessment of concentrated leak erosion along a conduit. Like the other CLE screening models (see the
[Intended Use](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#intended-use) section of the CLE Common
Methods manual), it makes simplifying and generally conservative assumptions to avoid false negatives, and it substitutes qualitative selections
for site-specific measurements to reduce input burden. Each of the four conduit factors accepts Unknown where the information is not available.

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments. A detailed analysis of a conduit flaw assesses the flaw, its
location, and each progression condition directly (see [Comparison with Detailed Models](#comparison-with-detailed-models)).

## How the Model Works

The model executes the three stages common to the CLE screening models
(see [How the Model Works](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#how-the-model-works) in the
CLE Common Methods manual), with these conduit-specific elements:

1. **Flaw assessment** — Four conduit factors (the conduit type and surround, cutoff collars, the compaction of the fill adjacent to the conduit,
   and the conduit trench details) are each scored with a likelihood factor, weighted, and summed, and the weighted sum is converted to a flaw
   probability. An observation of leakage adjacent to the conduit sets the flaw probability to 1.0. The flaw elevation is the bottom of the
   conduit, which the user supplies (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).

2. **Pathway resolution** — The failure pathway runs through the embankment at the bottom of the conduit. The user states whether a diaphragm
   filter surrounds the conduit, and lists the soils the pipe passes through. The model has no upstream seepage-limiting elements to resolve (see
   [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the model multiplies the eight node probabilities. Initiation is evaluated by Monte Carlo
   simulation of a cylindrical pipe at the bottom of the conduit, continuation is evaluated by the shared continuation method only when a diaphragm
   filter is present, and the flow-limitation node is held at 1.0 (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

## Physics Basis

The model combines the following engineering methods:

- **Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring** of four conduit factors for the probability of a continuous flaw <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />,
  following the scheme documented in the
  [Flaw Conventions](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
  chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx))
- **Hydraulic shear stress on the walls of a cylindrical pipe** at the bottom of the conduit, compared with the critical shear stress of the
  adjacent soil, following the
  [Initiation](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#delegated-method-cylindrical-pipe-initiation)
  chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual
- **Filter evaluation** of a diaphragm filter around the conduit, through the shared continuation method documented in the
  [Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil)
  chapter of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual
- **Roof-support probabilities by soil classification** and **crack-filling probabilities by embankment type** for the progression of the pipe <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, as tabulated
  in the
  [Failure Pathway and Progression](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-4-progression--roof-support)
  chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual

## Relationship to the Shared CLE Methods

The model is one of the four compaction models of the CLE family (see
[The Two Model Groups](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#the-two-model-groups) in the CLE
Common Methods manual). It shares with the other three the cylindrical-pipe initiation analysis, the critical shear stress table, and the rule
that every node except initiation is the same at every pool level. It differs from them in three ways:

- **Four flaw factors, weighted 3, 2, 2, and 2,** where the other compaction models score three, and **no flaw-continuity stage**: the scored
  probability is the flaw probability.
- **A directly supplied flaw elevation**, the bottom of the conduit. The core compaction model and the abutment case of the foundation and
  abutment compaction model place the flaw by a normal-pool heuristic, the foundation case of that model takes the embankment-foundation contact
  elevation directly, and the wall and cliff compaction model takes the crest elevation minus the wall or cliff height.
- **No flow-limitation node input**: Node 6 is fixed at 1.0, so no upstream cutoff or facing reduces the SRP.

Two further features are shared with only some of the other compaction models. The observation override leaves the flaw elevation unchanged, as
in the wall and cliff compaction model; the core compaction model's observation also relocates the flaw, and the foundation and abutment
compaction model has no observation override. Like the wall and cliff and the foundation and abutment compaction models, the model gates
continuation on an explicit yes-or-no answer about the filter and ignores the continuation inputs when the answer is no; the core compaction model
instead evaluates continuation whenever a filter description is supplied. The form of the filter, a diaphragm around the conduit, is specific to
this model.

It also carries its own default initial pipe diameter range, larger than that of the core compaction model (see
[Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation)).

## Relationship to the Typical Event Tree

The [embedded-structure](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/risk-calculations-suite/typical-event-tree-database/v1.5/Concentrated-Leak-Erosion#along-embedded-structure)
tree of the Typical Event Tree Database, written for a crack along a structure such as a conduit, carries eight events that correspond to those of
the screening tree: a transverse crack along the structure, initiation, continuation, crack stability, flow limitation by upstream zones, crack filling by
upstream zones, unsuccessful intervention, and breach. The screening model differs from it in three respects, so the database tree is not
presented here as the model's structure:

- The database flaw node allows a low-stress zone or poor compaction; the screening flaw is poor compaction only.
- The screening model does not evaluate flow limitation: the node is held at 1.0.
- The screening model orders crack filling before flow limitation. Because the SRP is a product of the node probabilities, the order does not
  affect the result.

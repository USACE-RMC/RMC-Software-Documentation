# Assumptions and Limitations

The wall or cliff settlement model applies deliberate simplifications relative to a detailed concentrated leak erosion risk analysis. They reduce
input requirements and are designed to avoid false negatives at the screening level. This chapter lists the assumptions specific to this model.
Each assumption states its engineering basis, its effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold. Where an engineering basis is
still awaiting confirmation, the assumption cross-references the input marker at its primary location in this document.

## Shared Assumptions

The model also makes the assumptions common to the CLE screening models, documented with their bases in the
[Assumptions and Limitations](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/assumptions-and-limitations) chapter of
the common methods manual. Those that apply to this model are the event-tree assumptions (the SRP as the product of the nodes, the single-valued
intervention and breach probabilities, and the headwater grid), the pool-independent flaw probability, the scoring of site factors into a flaw
probability, the certain initiation of an open crest crack above its bottom, and the crest-crack pathway, roof-support, crack-filling, and
flow-limitation conventions. The compaction-model assumptions of that chapter do not apply.

## Flaw Probability

1. **The likelihood of a continuous crack is scored from three factors: the bench-width ratio, the normalized settlement, and the abutment slope
   below the wall or cliff, with risk factors 3, 2, and 1.** (See [Likelihood Factors](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#likelihood-factors).)
   - *Engineering basis:* The risk-factor and likelihood-factor method of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the source of the
     bands and weights is requested at marker IN-05 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#likelihood-factors).
   - *Effect on results:* One step in the cross-valley profile score moves the weighted sum three times as far as one step in the abutment slope
     score, so the bench-width ratio carries the largest risk-factor weight. A score changes only where its input crosses a band limit, and the
     abutment slope, like the bench-width ratio, forces the flaw probability to zero on its own when it scores 1.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where cracking against the wall or cliff is controlled by conditions the three factors do not describe, such as
     irregularities in the face of the wall or cliff or compressible foundation soils beneath the fill. The risk team's estimate then replaces the
     screening value.
2. **A bench-width ratio of exactly 1 is scored in the band for a likelihood factor of 3.**
   - *Engineering basis:* Screening design decision (engineering judgment): in the source, the cross-valley profile bands for likelihood factors of 2
     and 3 both include a ratio of 1, and the model assigns that ratio to the more conservative of the two (see marker IN-05 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#likelihood-factors)).
   - *Effect on results:* At a ratio of exactly 1 the weighted sum is 3 higher than it would be in the band for 2, which raises the flaw
     probability (conservative).
   - *When it may not hold:* Not applicable as a site condition; it affects only a ratio at the boundary itself.
3. **A bench wider than 2.5 times the wall or cliff height, or an abutment slope of 25° or flatter, rules out a continuous crack.** Either sets the
   flaw probability to zero, whatever the settlement (see
   [Incompatible-Condition Factors](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#incompatible-condition-factors)).
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-06 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#incompatible-condition-factors).
   - *Effect on results:* The SRP is negligible at every pool level for such a geometry.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where a large settlement or poor compaction against the wall or cliff could crack the embankment despite a wide
     bench or a flat abutment; other CLE mechanisms, such as poor compaction adjacent to a wall or cliff, should then be considered (see the CLE Wall/Cliff Compaction Screening Technical Reference Manual).
4. **When the settlement is not known, the normalized settlement defaults to 0.5 percent of the dam height for a dam completed before 1950 and
   0.25 percent for a dam completed in 1950 or later.** (See [Normalized Settlement](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#normalized-settlement).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-04 in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#normalized-settlement).
   - *Effect on results:* The later-era default scores a settlement likelihood factor of 1 and gives a crack depth of 65 percent of the effective
     wall height; the earlier-era default scores 2 and gives 90 percent. Moving the year of construction across 1950 therefore always changes the
     crack depth. It also changes the flaw probability unless an incompatible-condition factor already forces it to zero.
   - *When it may not hold:* For embankments with settlement known or suspected to be larger than the default — compressible foundations, poorly
     compacted fill, or a history of crest repairs — where a measured settlement should be supplied.
5. **The weighted sum is converted to a flaw probability by a log-linear curve with a negligible point at a weighted sum of 9.** (See
   [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the source of the curve is requested at marker IN-07 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability).
   - *Effect on results:* The lowest reachable weighted sum, 10, gives a negligible flaw probability, many orders of magnitude below
     $1 \times 10^{-3}$, and an effectively negligible SRP; the highest, 24, gives 0.2.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where site investigation supports a flaw probability more directly than the factor scores do.
6. **An observation of cracking does not override the scored flaw probability.** (See
   [No Observation Override](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#no-observation-override).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-08 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#no-observation-override).
   - *Effect on results:* An embankment with an observed transverse crack adjacent to the wall or cliff receives the same flaw probability as one
     without it, which may understate the SRP.
   - *When it may not hold:* Wherever such a crack has been observed; a detailed assessment should then weigh the observation directly.

## Crack Geometry

7. **The crack depth is a tabulated fraction of an effective wall height, which is itself a tabulated fraction of the wall or cliff height.** (See
   [Crack Depth](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />; the source of the relationships is requested at marker IN-10 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth).
   - *Effect on results:* The crack depth never exceeds the wall or cliff height, reaches the full effective wall height at 1 percent normalized
     settlement, and is 15 percent of the effective wall height at zero settlement. A deeper crack lowers the pool level at which the SRP steps up.
   - *When it may not hold:* Where the crack depth is known from investigation, such as test pits or trenches at the crest; a detailed analysis
     then uses the observed depth.
8. **The crack width, crack depth, and effective wall height relationships take their edge values beyond their tabulated ranges.** (See
   [Range of the Tabulated Relationships](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-tabulated-relationships).)
   - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see marker IN-11 in
     [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#range-of-the-tabulated-relationships).
   - *Effect on results:* A normalized settlement above 2 percent gives the crack depth of 2 percent, the full effective wall height; an abutment
     slope steeper than 60° gives the values of 60°.
   - *When it may not hold:* For settlements or slopes well outside the tabulated range, where the edge values may not represent the crack.
9. **The crack width does not enter the event tree.** Initiation depends only on whether the pool stands above the bottom of the crack, even
   where the crack width is zero.
   - *Engineering basis:* The headwater gate of the crest-crack models, documented with its basis in the
     [Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
     section of the common methods manual.
   - *Effect on results:* A narrow crack and a wide crack of the same depth give the same SRP. The assumption is conservative for narrow cracks:
     it overstates initiation where a narrow crack would not erode. At a bench-width ratio of exactly 2.5 the crack width is zero, yet the flaw
     probability and the crack depth are not, so initiation is certain above the bottom of a zero-width crack (see
     [Crack Width](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-width)).
   - *When it may not hold:* For narrow cracks in erosion-resistant soils; a crack-geometry initiation analysis then applies.
10. **One wall or cliff geometry is evaluated per run, and the inputs are not checked against one another.** The model does not require the wall or
    cliff height to be less than the dam height or the bottom of the crack to lie above the base of the embankment.
    - *Engineering basis:* Basis requested — see markers IN-01 and IN-03 in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#dam-and-wall-or-cliff-geometry).
    - *Effect on results:* A wall or cliff height that exceeds the embankment height can place the bottom of the crack below the base of the
      embankment, so that initiation is certain at every pool level above that elevation.
    - *When it may not hold:* Where the embankment abuts several walls or cliffs, or a wall or cliff taller than the embankment; each geometry and
      the height in contact with the fill should then be considered (see marker IN-01).

## Limitations

- **Scope of the mechanism** — The model addresses cracking by differential settlement against a steep wall or cliff at an abutment. Poor
  compaction of the fill against the wall or cliff is a separate CLE mechanism with its own screening model (see the CLE Wall/Cliff Compaction Screening Technical Reference Manual).
- **Screening-level geometry** — The flaw is described by four measurements and a settlement. Irregular abutment profiles, several benches, or a
  wall or cliff whose height varies along the embankment are represented only through the values the user chooses to supply.
- **Deterministic flaw assessment** — The flaw probability and crack depth carry no uncertainty in the inputs. Where an input is uncertain, the
  effect can be bracketed by running the model with alternative values.

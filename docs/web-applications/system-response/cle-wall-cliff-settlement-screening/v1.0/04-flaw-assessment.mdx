# Flaw Assessment

The flaw assessment answers two questions about the transverse crack that differential settlement against the wall or cliff can open near the
crest. How likely is a continuous crack through the embankment (Node 1 of the event tree)? And how deep is it, which sets the pool level above
which the crack can carry a concentrated leak (Node 2)? Both answers come from the same three quantities — the bench-width ratio, the normalized
settlement, and the abutment slope below the wall or cliff — and both are the same at every pool level. The calculation proceeds in the order of
this chapter:

1. Form the bench-width ratio and the normalized settlement.
2. Score the three factors as likelihood factors and form their weighted sum.
3. Convert the weighted sum to the flaw probability.
4. Compute the crack width.
5. Compute the effective wall height and the crack depth, and from the crack depth the bottom-of-crack elevation.

Steps 2 and 3 apply the risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring scheme shared by seven of the CLE screening models, documented in the
[Risk-Factor and Likelihood-Factor Scoring](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
section of the common methods manual; this chapter documents the factors, bands, and curve of this model. The flaw probability is used directly as
Node 1: the model applies no flaw-continuity stage and no observation override.

## Normalized Ratios

### Bench-width ratio

The bench-width ratio compares the width of the bench at the base of the wall or cliff with the height of the wall or cliff:

<Equation equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-1" equation="r_b = \frac{W_b}{H_w}" />

where $W_b$ is the bench width and $H_w$ is the wall or cliff height. A ratio of zero describes fill placed directly against the wall or cliff
with no bench; a large ratio describes a wide bench that sets the fill well back from the wall or cliff.

### Normalized settlement

The normalized settlement $r_s$ is the post-construction settlement expressed as a percentage of the dam height. When the maximum
post-construction settlement $s$ is supplied,

<Equation equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-2" equation="r_s = 100\,\frac{s}{H}" />

with $s$ and $H$ in the same length unit. When no settlement is supplied, the normalized settlement takes a default value set by the year of
construction $Y$:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-3"
  equation="r_s = \begin{cases} 0.5\ \% & Y < 1950 \\[4pt] 0.25\ \% & Y \ge 1950 \end{cases}"
/>

A dam completed in 1950 itself takes the 0.25 percent default. A supplied settlement always takes precedence over the default, whatever the year.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-04 — Engineering Basis]
The default normalized settlement used when no settlement is supplied
(<EquationReference equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-3" />: 0.5 percent of the dam height for dams completed before 1950,
0.25 percent for dams completed in 1950 or later, with the year 1950 itself classed with the later dams) requires an engineering basis. Provide the
literature source for the two values and the 1950 boundary (with full bibliographic details if it is not Fell et al. (2024), and the table or
section if it is), or the engineering-judgment attribution to state here.

Proposed (unconfirmed): the two values represent typical post-construction crest settlements of embankments built before and after the general
adoption of controlled compaction of earthfill, with older, less well compacted embankments settling more. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Likelihood Factors

Each of the three flaw factors is scored as a likelihood factor from 1, the condition least likely to produce a continuous crack, to 4, the most
likely, by placing its input in one of four bands (<TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-table-4" />). Each factor carries
a risk factor that weights its score (<TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-table-5" />), following Fell et al.
(2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />:

- **Cross-valley profile** — scored on the bench-width ratio $r_b$. A narrow bench, or none, scores high; a wide bench scores low.
- **Settlement** — scored on the normalized settlement $r_s$. Larger settlement relative to the dam height scores higher.
- **Abutment slope** — scored on the abutment slope below the wall or cliff $\beta_1$. A steeper slope scores higher.

the normalized settlement fall in the two lowest settlement bands: the 0.25 percent default of a dam completed in 1950 or later scores 1, and the

### Weighted sum

The three scores combine into the weighted sum defined in the
[Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#weighted-sum) section of the common
methods manual:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-4"
  equation="\Sigma = 3\,LF_{xv} + 2\,LF_{s} + 1\,LF_{\beta}"
/>

where $LF_{xv}$, $LF_{s}$, and $LF_{\beta}$ are the likelihood factors of the cross-valley profile, settlement, and abutment slope. Every
likelihood factor of this model is a whole number, so the weighted sum is a whole number between 6 and 24.

### Incompatible-condition factors

The cross-valley profile and the abutment slope are incompatible-condition factors (see the
[Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
section of the common methods manual). When either scores 1 — a bench wider than 2.5 times the wall or cliff height, or an abutment slope of
25° or flatter — the flaw probability is zero whatever the other two factors score. The settlement factor is not an incompatible-condition factor:
a settlement score of 1 lowers the weighted sum but does not by itself rule out a crack.

Once neither geometric factor scores 1, each scores at least 2, so every weighted sum that reaches the flaw-probability curve lies between 10 and
24, and every whole number in that range can occur.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-06 — Engineering Basis]
The rule that a cross-valley profile score of 1 (bench-width ratio greater than 2.5) or an abutment slope score of 1 (slope of 25° or flatter)
forces the flaw probability to zero, while a settlement score of 1 does not, requires an engineering basis. Provide the literature source (the table
or section of Fell et al. (2024), if it is the source) or the engineering-judgment attribution.

Proposed (unconfirmed): a bench that wide sets the fill far enough from the wall or cliff, and an abutment that flat gives a gradual enough
transition, that the geometry cannot concentrate differential settlement into a continuous transverse crack, whereas some settlement is always
present. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Flaw Probability

The weighted sum is converted to the flaw probability $P_{flaw}$ by the curve of
<TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-table-6" />, interpolated with the logarithm of the probability linear in the
weighted sum, as documented in the
[Flaw Probability from the Weighted Sum](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#flaw-probability-from-the-weighted-sum)
section of the common methods manual.
The point at a weighted sum of 9 carries a negligible probability. Because no reachable weighted sum is less than 10 and none exceeds 24, the
curve is never held at either end value.

## No Observation Override

The model has no observation override: an observed transverse crack at the crest does not set the flaw probability to 1.0, and no observation is
taken as an input. The flaw probability is always the scored value. The common methods manual lists the overrides of the other models in its
[Observation Overrides](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#observation-overrides)
section.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-08 — Engineering Basis]
The absence of an observation override in this model — where the cross-valley settlement, desiccation, and frost-cracking models each accept an
observation of continuous transverse cracking that sets the flaw probability to 1.0 — requires an engineering basis or a documentation decision to
state here. State why an observed crack adjacent to the wall or cliff is not used to override the scored probability, and what a user who has
observed such a crack should do.
:::

The crack width is a supporting result. It does not enter the crack depth or any node of the event tree: in this model, initiation depends only on
whether the pool stands above the bottom of the crack (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-2-initiation)).

At a bench-width ratio of exactly 2.5 the crack width is zero at every settlement and slope, and between ratios of 2 and 2.5 it falls toward zero.
The other results do not follow it to zero. A ratio of 2.5 still scores a cross-valley profile likelihood factor of 2, so the flaw probability
is not zero unless the abutment slope scores 1, and the effective wall height ratio is held at its value for a ratio of 2, so the crack depth is
not zero either. Initiation is then certain above the bottom of a crack that the width relationship gives as zero width.

## Crack Depth

The crack depth is a fraction of an effective wall height $H_e$, which is itself a fraction of the wall or cliff height. The effective wall
height ratio $r_e = H_e/H_w$ is interpolated bilinearly in the abutment slope and the bench-width ratio from
<TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-table-11" /> and <TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-table-12" /> <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, which together form one
table split by bench-width ratio, and the effective wall height is

<Equation equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-6" equation="H_e = r_e(\beta_1,\, r_b)\; H_w" />

there.

## Bottom of the Crack

The bottom-of-crack elevation is the crest elevation less the crack depth, as for every crest-crack model. The crack depth is measured down from
the crest whatever the elevation of the top of the wall or cliff; the model takes no top-of-wall or top-of-cliff elevation (see marker IN-01 in
[Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#dam-and-wall-or-cliff-geometry)). The convention is documented in the
[Crest-Crack Models: Bottom of the Crack](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#crest-crack-models-bottom-of-the-crack)
section of the common methods manual:

<Equation equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-8" equation="z_{bc} = z_c - d_c" />

It is the initiation gate of the event tree, and the elevation at which the below-filter roof materials are taken (see
[Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)). It is computed whenever the inputs are valid, including when an incompatible-condition factor forces the flaw
probability to zero; the SRP is then negligible at every pool level regardless of the crack depth.

## Range of the Tabulated Relationships

The crack width, effective wall height, and crack depth relationships are tabulated over normalized settlements from 0 to 2 percent, bench-width
ratios from 0 to 2.5 (0 to 2 for the effective wall height), and abutment slopes from 15° to 60°. Beyond the tabulated range of an input, each
relationship takes its value at the nearest tabulated edge of that input; it is not extrapolated. In particular:

- A normalized settlement above 2 percent gives the crack width and depth of 2 percent: the crack depth is the full effective wall height.
- An abutment slope steeper than 60° gives the values of 60°, and a slope flatter than 15° gives the values of 15° (such a slope also forces the
  flaw probability to zero through the abutment slope factor).
- A bench-width ratio above 2 gives the effective wall height ratio of 2; above 2.5 the cross-valley profile factor also forces the flaw
  probability to zero.

The likelihood-factor bands of <TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-table-4" /> have no such limit: their outer bands
are open-ended.

## Worked Example

For an embankment of height $H = 100$ ft against a wall of height $H_w = 40$ ft with a bench width $W_b = 10$ ft, a measured post-construction
settlement $s = 0.5$ ft, and an abutment slope $\beta_1 = 50°$:

1. **Normalized ratios** — $r_b = 10 / 40 = 0.25$ and $r_s = 100 \times 0.5 / 100 = 0.5$ percent.
2. **Likelihood factors** — the cross-valley profile scores 3 ($0.25 \le r_b \le 1$), the settlement scores 2 ($0.25 < r_s \le 0.75$), and the
   abutment slope scores 3 ($45° < \beta_1 \le 60°$). Neither incompatible-condition factor scores 1.
3. **Flaw probability** — $\Sigma = 3 \times 3 + 2 \times 2 + 1 \times 3 = 16$, which gives $P_{flaw} = 1.0 \times 10^{-2}$.
4. **Crack width** — $w_{45} = 20$ mm at $r_s = 0.5$ percent and $r_b = 0.25$, and $n_\beta = 1.0$ at 50°, so $w = 20$ mm.
5. **Crack depth** — the effective wall height ratio $r_e$ at 50° and $r_b = 0.25$ is interpolated between 0.75 at 45° and 0.57 at 60° as 0.69, so
   $H_e = 0.69 \times 40 = 27.6$ ft. The normalized crack depth $r_d$ at 0.5 percent settlement is 90 percent, so $d_c = 0.90 \times 27.6 = 24.8$ ft, and
   the bottom of the crack lies 24.8 ft below the crest.

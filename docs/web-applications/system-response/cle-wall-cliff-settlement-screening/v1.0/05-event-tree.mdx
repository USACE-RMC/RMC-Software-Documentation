# Event Tree

The model computes the system response probability (SRP) at each headwater level from the eight-node event tree shared by the concentrated leak
erosion (CLE) screening models. The tree, its node questions, its relationship to the trees of the Typical Event Tree Database, and the headwater
grid on which it is evaluated are documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree">Event Tree</DocLink>
chapter of the CLE Common Methods manual. This chapter states how this model populates each node. Nodes 1 and 2 carry the results of this
model's flaw assessment; Nodes 3 through 8 follow the crest-crack conventions of the family without change.

## Node 1: Flaw

*Question:* Does a continuous transverse crack caused by differential settlement against the wall or cliff exist through the embankment?

The node probability is the flaw probability $P_{flaw}$ of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#raw-flaw-probability), the same at every
pool level. No flaw-continuity stage multiplies it and no observation override replaces it. It is zero when the cross-valley profile or the
abutment slope scores a likelihood factor of 1 (see
[Incompatible-Condition Factors](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#incompatible-condition-factors)).

## Node 2: Initiation

*Question:* Is the hydraulic shear stress on the walls of the crack large enough to initiate concentrated leak erosion?

The model applies the headwater gate of the crest-crack models (<EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-8" fallback="Equation 8" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="initiation" anchor="crest-crack-models-headwater-gate">Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual) at the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$ of
[Bottom-of-Crack Elevation](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#bottom-of-crack-elevation). The crack width plays no part, and neither does tailwater. The
crack depth is therefore the controlling quantity of initiation: a deeper crack lowers the pool level at which the SRP steps up from negligible.
In this model the crack depth is proportional to the wall or cliff height only at a fixed bench-width ratio. With a fixed bench width, a taller
wall or cliff also lowers the bench-width ratio and so raises the effective wall height ratio, or leaves it unchanged; wherever that ratio rises,
the crack deepens by more than in proportion to the height. The crack depth rises, or stays constant, as the normalized settlement increases, and
falls, or stays constant, as the bench widens or the abutment steepens. It stays constant at and above a normalized settlement of 1 percent, at
and above a bench-width ratio of 2, and at abutment slopes of 45° to 60° once the bench-width ratio reaches 1; with no bench, the abutment slope
has no effect on it (see [Crack Depth](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth)).

## Node 3: Continuation

*Question:* Does an unfiltered seepage exit exist that allows erosion to continue?

Continuation follows the crest-crack pathway convention (<EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-14" fallback="Equation 14" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-3-continuation">Node 3: Continuation</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual). Where a filter intersects the failure pathway, its top elevation $z_{tf}$ gates the node at each pool
level: at or below $z_{tf}$, or at every pool level for a filter that caps the pathway (one whose top reaches the crest, so that no full-pathway
materials are listed), the node takes the probability of continuing erosion $P_{ce}$, the node result of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation)
Screening model (written $P_{cont}$ in that manual; see its
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="output-and-interpretation" anchor="node-result">Node Result</DocLink>
section); above $z_{tf}$ it is 1.0. With no filter on the pathway, it is 1.0 at every pool level.

$P_{ce}$ is computed once, from the filter and the base soils upstream of it, and applies wherever the filter governs. The continuation method
selects, for each base soil, the most informative method its inputs support, and the worst base soil governs (see the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening" page="method-selection-and-governing-base-soil">Method Selection and Governing Base Soil</DocLink>
chapter of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening manual).

## Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

*Question:* Does a continuous stable roof form over the enlarging pipe, so that it stays open?

The node is the minimum roof-support probability over the materials that apply at the pool level, as documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-4-progression--roof-support">Node 4: Progression — Roof Support</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. The below-filter materials, taken at the bottom-of-crack elevation, apply at pool levels at or below
$z_{tf}$ (and at every pool level for a filter that caps the pathway); the full-pathway materials apply above $z_{tf}$, and at every pool level
when no filter is present. Roof support therefore switches at the top of the filter, in step with continuation.

## Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

*Question:* Does the enlarging pipe remain unfilled, rather than being filled and healed by material carried in from upstream?

The probability of no crack filling depends only on the embankment type and is the same at every pool level (see the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-5-progression--no-crack-filling">Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual).

## Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

*Question:* Does the flow into the enlarging pipe remain unrestricted by upstream seepage-limiting elements?

The node is the probability of no flow limitation of the upstream facing element times that of the governing wall (the most effective of the
walls listed), where an element that is absent or does not apply contributes 1.0, so the node is 1.0 when none applies. The walls are gated per
pool level at the top of a wall as documented in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="walls-that-stop-below-the-crest">Walls That Stop Below the Crest</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. At pool levels at or below the top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ the walls apply; above it the walls drop
out and only an upstream facing element still limits flow. With no top-of-wall elevation, the walls apply at every pool level. The upstream wall
of Node 6 is a seepage-limiting element on the upstream side of the embankment, distinct from the abutment wall or cliff whose settlement effect
the flaw assessment describes.

## Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

*Questions:* Do detection and intervention fail (Node 7)? Does an uncontrolled release of impounded water occur (Node 8)?

Both nodes are the shared intervention and breach inputs, documented with their defaults in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="nodes-7-and-8-unsuccessful-intervention-and-breach">Nodes 7 and 8</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. The model applies them without change.

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each headwater (pool) elevation $h$ is the product of the eight node probabilities, by <EquationReference doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" equationKey="cle-common-methods-screening-eq-1" fallback="Equation 1" /> in the
<DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="event-tree" anchor="srp-computation">SRP Computation</DocLink>
section of the CLE Common Methods manual. In this model $P_{init}$, $P_{cont}$, $P_{roof}$, and $P_{nl}$ vary with $h$, and the other nodes are
the same at every pool level.

Where the product is zero — at and below the bottom of the crack, or at every pool level when the flaw probability is zero — the SRP is reported
as a negligibly small value rather than zero. Above the bottom of the crack, with initiation equal to 1.0, the SRP at each pool level is the
product of the flaw probability and the progression, intervention, and breach nodes that govern there, and is constant between the steps at
$z_{tf}$ and $z_{tw}$.

## Elevations Where the Curve Changes

Three elevations can produce a step in the system response curve: the bottom of the crack $z_{bc}$, the top of the filter $z_{tf}$, and the top
of a wall $z_{tw}$. Because initiation is zero at and below $z_{bc}$, a filter or wall top affects the SRP only where it lies above the bottom of
the crack:

- **Filter top above the bottom of the crack** — between $z_{bc}$ and $z_{tf}$ the SRP carries $P_{ce}$ and the below-filter roof support; above
  $z_{tf}$ it carries certain continuation and the full-pathway roof support. The SRP steps at $z_{tf}$, up or down depending on how $P_{ce}$
  compares with the ratio of the full-pathway to the below-filter roof support (see the
  <DocLink doc="web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening" page="failure-pathway-and-progression" anchor="node-3-continuation">Node 3: Continuation</DocLink>
  section of the CLE Common Methods manual).
- **Filter top at or below the bottom of the crack** — at every pool level where initiation is possible the pool is above $z_{tf}$, so continuation
  is 1.0 and the full-pathway materials govern; $P_{ce}$ and the below-filter materials have no effect on the SRP. A filter that caps the
  pathway — one whose top reaches the crest, so that no full-pathway materials are listed — is the exception: it governs at every pool level.
- **Wall top above the bottom of the crack** — the governing wall's flow-limitation credit, multiplied with that of any upstream facing element,
  applies from $z_{bc}$ up to $z_{tw}$ and is lost above it.
- **Wall top at or below the bottom of the crack** — only an upstream facing element, if present, limits flow wherever initiation is possible.

Because the crack depth of this model can approach the full wall or cliff height, the bottom of the crack can lie well below the crest, and a
filter or wall that reaches only partway up the embankment can still lie above it.

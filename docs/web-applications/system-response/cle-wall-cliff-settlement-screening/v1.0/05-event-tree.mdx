# Event Tree

The model computes the SRP at each headwater level from the eight-node event tree shared by the CLE screening models. The tree, its node
questions, its relationship to the trees of the Typical Event Tree Database, and the headwater grid on which it is evaluated are documented in the
[Event Tree](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree) chapter of the common methods manual. This
chapter states how this model populates each node. Nodes 1 and 2 carry the results of this model's flaw assessment; Nodes 3 through 8 follow the
crest-crack conventions of the family without change.

## Node 1: Flaw

*Question:* Does a continuous transverse crack caused by differential settlement against the wall or cliff exist through the embankment?

The node probability is the flaw probability $P_{flaw}$ of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability), the same at every pool
level. No flaw-continuity stage multiplies it and no observation override replaces it. It is zero when the cross-valley profile or the abutment
slope scores a likelihood factor of 1 (see
[Incompatible-Condition Factors](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#incompatible-condition-factors)).

## Node 2: Initiation

*Question:* Is the hydraulic shear stress on the walls of the crack large enough to initiate concentrated leak erosion?

The model applies the headwater gate of the crest-crack models, documented with its basis in the
[Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
section of the common methods manual. With the bottom-of-crack elevation $z_{bc}$ of
<EquationReference equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-8" />,

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-9"
  equation="P_{init}(h) = \begin{cases} 1.0 & h > z_{bc} \\[4pt] 0 & h \le z_{bc} \end{cases}"
/>

at each pool level $h$. The crack width plays no part, and neither does tailwater. The crack depth is therefore the controlling quantity of
initiation: a deeper crack lowers the pool level at which the SRP steps up from negligible. In this model the crack depth is proportional to the
wall or cliff height only at a fixed bench-width ratio. With a fixed bench width, a taller wall or cliff also lowers the bench-width ratio and so
raises the effective wall height ratio, or leaves it unchanged; wherever that ratio rises, the crack deepens by more than in proportion to the
height. The crack depth rises, or stays constant, as the normalized settlement increases, and falls, or stays constant, as the bench widens or the
abutment steepens. It stays constant at and above a normalized settlement of 1 percent, at and above a bench-width ratio of 2, and at abutment
slopes of 45° to 60° once the bench-width ratio reaches 1; with no bench, the abutment slope has no effect on it (see
[Crack Depth](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth)).

## Node 3: Continuation

*Question:* Does an unfiltered seepage exit exist that allows erosion to continue?

Continuation follows the crest-crack pathway convention documented in the
[Node 3: Continuation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-3-continuation)
section of the common methods manual. Where a filter intersects the failure pathway, its top elevation $z_{tf}$ gates the node at each pool
level: at or below $z_{tf}$, or at every pool level for a filter that caps the pathway (one whose top reaches the crest, so that no
full-pathway materials are listed), the node takes the probability of continuing erosion $P_{ce}$ computed by the shared continuation method; above $z_{tf}$ it is 1.0. With no filter on the pathway, it is 1.0 at every pool level.

$P_{ce}$ is computed once, from the filter and the base soils upstream of it, and applies wherever the filter governs. The continuation method
selects, for each base soil, the most informative method its inputs support, and the worst base soil governs (see the
[Method Selection and Governing Base Soil](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/method-selection-and-governing-base-soil)
chapter of the Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening Technical Reference Manual).

## Node 4: Progression — Roof Support

*Question:* Does a continuous stable roof form over the enlarging pipe, so that it stays open?

The node is the minimum roof-support probability over the materials that apply at the pool level, as documented in the
[Node 4: Progression — Roof Support](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-4-progression--roof-support)
section of the common methods manual. The below-filter materials, taken at the bottom-of-crack elevation, apply at pool levels at or below
$z_{tf}$ (and at every pool level for a filter that caps the pathway, one whose top reaches the crest so that no full-pathway materials are
listed); the full-pathway materials apply above $z_{tf}$, and at every pool level when
no filter is present. Roof support therefore switches at the top of the filter, in step with continuation.

## Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling

*Question:* Does the enlarging pipe remain unfilled, rather than being filled and healed by material carried in from upstream?

The probability of no crack filling depends only on the embankment type and is the same at every pool level (see the
[Node 5: Progression — No Crack Filling](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-5-progression--no-crack-filling)
section of the common methods manual).

## Node 6: Progression — No Flow Limitation

*Question:* Does the flow into the enlarging pipe remain unrestricted by upstream seepage-limiting elements?

The node is the minimum probability of no flow limitation over the applicable upstream elements, or 1.0 when none applies, gated per pool level at
the top of a wall as documented in the
[Walls That Stop Below the Crest](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#walls-that-stop-below-the-crest)
section of the common methods manual. At pool levels at or below the top-of-wall elevation $z_{tw}$ every listed element applies; above it the
walls drop out and only an upstream facing element still limits flow. With no top-of-wall elevation, every listed element applies at every pool
level. The upstream wall of Node 6 is a seepage-limiting element on the upstream side of the embankment, distinct from the abutment wall or cliff
whose settlement effect the flaw assessment describes.

## Nodes 7 and 8: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

*Questions:* Do detection and intervention fail (Node 7)? Does an uncontrolled release of impounded water occur (Node 8)?

Both nodes are the user-specified probabilities $P_{int}$ and $P_{br}$, each one value at every pool level and each defaulting to 1.0 (see the
[Nodes 7 and 8](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#nodes-7-and-8-unsuccessful-intervention-and-breach)
section of the common methods manual).

## Where the Crack Meets the Pathway Gates

Three elevations can produce a step in the system response curve: the bottom of the crack $z_{bc}$, the top of the filter $z_{tf}$, and the top
of a wall $z_{tw}$. Because initiation is zero at and below $z_{bc}$, a filter or wall top affects the SRP only where it lies above the bottom of
the crack:

- **Filter top above the bottom of the crack** — between $z_{bc}$ and $z_{tf}$ the SRP carries $P_{ce}$ and the below-filter roof support; above
  $z_{tf}$ it carries certain continuation and the full-pathway roof support. The SRP steps at $z_{tf}$, up or down depending on how $P_{ce}$
  compares with the ratio of the full-pathway to the below-filter roof support (see the
  [Node 3: Continuation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression#node-3-continuation)
  section of the common methods manual).
- **Filter top at or below the bottom of the crack** — at every pool level where initiation is possible the pool is above $z_{tf}$, so continuation
  is 1.0 and the full-pathway materials govern; $P_{ce}$ and the below-filter materials have no effect on the SRP. A filter that caps the
  pathway — one whose top reaches the crest, so that no full-pathway materials are listed — is the exception: it governs at every pool level.
- **Wall top above the bottom of the crack** — the walls' flow-limitation credit applies from $z_{bc}$ up to $z_{tw}$ and is lost above it.
- **Wall top at or below the bottom of the crack** — only an upstream facing element, if present, limits flow wherever initiation is possible.

Because the crack depth of this model can approach the full wall or cliff height, the bottom of the crack can lie well below the crest, and a
filter or wall that reaches only partway up the embankment can still lie above it.

## SRP Computation

The SRP at each pool level is the product of the eight node probabilities, as documented in the
[SRP Computation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/event-tree#srp-computation) section of the common
methods manual:

<Equation
  equationKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-eq-10"
  equation="\mathrm{SRP}(h) = P_{flaw} \times P_{init}(h) \times P_{cont}(h) \times P_{roof}(h) \times P_{nf} \times P_{nl}(h) \times P_{int} \times P_{br}"
/>

Where the product is zero — at and below the bottom of the crack, or at every pool level when the flaw probability is zero — the SRP is reported as
a negligibly small value rather than zero. Above the bottom of the crack, with initiation equal to 1.0, the SRP at each pool level is the product
of the flaw probability and the progression, intervention, and breach nodes that govern there, and is constant between the steps at $z_{tf}$ and
$z_{tw}$.

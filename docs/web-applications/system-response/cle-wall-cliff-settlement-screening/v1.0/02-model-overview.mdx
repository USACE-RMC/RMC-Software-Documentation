# Model Overview

## Purpose

The wall or cliff settlement screening model computes a system response curve for concentrated leak erosion through a transverse crack caused by
differential settlement of the embankment adjacent to a steep wall or cliff. The SRP at each headwater level is the conditional probability of
breach of the embankment by concentrated leak erosion through such a crack, given that pool level. The curve of SRP values across the range of
headwater levels characterizes the embankment's vulnerability to the mechanism.

Where embankment fill is placed against a steep wall or cliff at an abutment, the fill settles after construction while the wall or cliff does
not. The resulting differential settlement can open a transverse crack in the embankment near the crest, alongside the wall or cliff. The
mechanism is a crest-crack mechanism: the crack is open at the crest and extends down to a bottom-of-crack elevation, and concentrated leak
erosion can begin once the pool rises above the bottom of the crack. The model takes three site characteristics as the controls on the crack:
the width of any bench at the base of the wall or cliff relative to its height, the post-construction settlement relative to the dam height, and
the slope of the abutment below the wall or cliff.

## Intended Use

The model is a screening-level assessment of concentrated leak erosion through a settlement crack adjacent to a wall or cliff. Like the other CLE
screening models, it makes simplifying and generally conservative assumptions with two goals (see the
[Intended Use](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#intended-use) section of the common
methods manual):

- **Preventing false negatives** — The model is designed to avoid underestimating risk at the screening level, so that embankments with a
  potential cracking concern at a wall or cliff are flagged for further analysis.
- **Reducing input burden** — The flaw assessment uses four geometric measurements and either a measured settlement or the year of construction.
  When the settlement is not known, a default settlement ratio based on the era of construction is used.

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments. A detailed analysis estimates the crack and its likelihood from the
totality of the site evidence (see [Comparison with Detailed Models](#comparison-with-detailed-models)).

## How the Model Works

The model executes the three stages common to the CLE screening models (see the
[How the Model Works](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#how-the-model-works) section of
the common methods manual):

1. **Flaw assessment** — The model forms two dimensionless ratios, the bench-width ratio (bench width to wall or cliff height) and the normalized
   settlement (post-construction settlement as a percentage of the dam height), and scores them, together with the abutment slope below the wall
   or cliff, as three likelihood factors. The weighted sum of the scores is converted to the probability of a continuous transverse crack. The
   same three quantities then set, through tabulated relationships, the crack width, the effective height of the wall, and the crack depth, and
   the crack depth fixes the bottom-of-crack elevation below the crest. The flaw probability and the bottom-of-crack elevation are the same at
   every pool level (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).

2. **Pathway resolution** — The user resolves the failure pathway along the crack: whether a drain or filter intersects it and where the
   filter's top lies, which materials must hold a roof over the enlarging pipe below and above the filter's top, and which upstream
   seepage-limiting elements are present and how high any wall reaches (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#shared-inputs)).

3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the model composes the eight node probabilities of the CLE event tree and multiplies them to
   produce the SRP. Initiation is certain once the pool rises above the bottom of the crack and zero at or below it. Continuation, roof support,
   and flow limitation each depend on where the pool stands relative to the top of the filter and the top of any wall (see
   [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx)).

Every node is deterministic except continuation, which the shared continuation method evaluates by Monte Carlo simulation when a filter
intercepts the pathway and the method's soil classification route applies.

## Physics Basis

The model combines the following engineering methods:

- **Risk-factor and likelihood-factor scoring** of the bench-width ratio, the normalized settlement, and the abutment slope for the probability of
  a continuous transverse crack, with the weighted sum mapped to a probability by a log-linear curve <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx) and the
  [Risk-Factor and Likelihood-Factor Scoring](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/flaw-conventions#risk-factor-and-likelihood-factor-scoring)
  section of the common methods manual)
- **Tabulated crack width and crack depth relationships** in terms of the same three quantities, in which the crack depth is a fraction of an
  effective wall height <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-depth))
- **A headwater gate** at the bottom of the open crack for initiation, shared by the crest-crack CLE models (see the
  [Crest-Crack Models: Headwater Gate](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/initiation#crest-crack-models-headwater-gate)
  section of the common methods manual)
- **Roof-support, crack-filling, and flow-limitation probabilities** from tables by soil classification, embankment type, and upstream
  element <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" /> (see the
  [Failure Pathway and Progression](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/failure-pathway-and-progression)
  chapter of the common methods manual)
- **Filter evaluation** for the probability that erosion continues past a filter intersecting the crack, applied through the shared continuation
  method (see the
  [Model Overview](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/model-overview) chapter of the Continuation (Filter
  Evaluation) Screening Technical Reference Manual)

## Relationship to the Shared CLE Methods

The model is one of the four crest-crack models of the CLE family, with cross-valley differential settlement, desiccation cracking, and frost
cracking (see
[The Two Model Groups](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/model-overview#the-two-model-groups) in the
common methods manual). It follows every convention of that group: the headwater gate at the bottom of the crack, the per-pool-level gates at
the top of the filter and the top of a wall, and the tabulated progression nodes. What sets it apart is its flaw assessment, which scores the bench-width
ratio, the normalized settlement, and the abutment slope, and derives the crack depth from an effective wall height. It is also one of two models
in the family with no observation override: an observation of cracking does not set its flaw probability (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#no-observation-override)).

The model calls no toolbox method. The continuation node is the only delegated calculation, handed to the shared continuation method whenever a
filter intersects the failure pathway (see [Event Tree](./05-event-tree.mdx#node-3-continuation)).

# Input Parameters

This chapter documents the inputs specific to the wall or cliff settlement model: the dam geometry, the geometry of the wall or cliff, and the
settlement information. These inputs drive the flaw assessment of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx). The model's remaining inputs —
hydraulic loading, the embankment type, the failure-pathway inputs, the upstream seepage-limiting elements, and the intervention and breach
probabilities — are shared with the other crest-crack CLE models and documented in the
[Common Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) chapter of the common
methods manual; [Shared Inputs](#shared-inputs) below lists them and notes how this model uses each.

All elevations are on one common datum, and every length is in the same unit as the elevations. Every relationship in the flaw assessment is
written in dimensionless ratios — the bench width to the wall or cliff height, and the settlement to the dam height — and the crack depth is a
fraction of the wall or cliff height, so the model gives the same result in any consistent length unit and needs no unit conversion. The one
quantity with a fixed unit is the crack width, reported in millimeters whatever the length unit
(see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#crack-width)).

## Dam and Wall or Cliff Geometry

- **Crest elevation** $z_c$ — the top of the embankment. The crack depth is measured down from it to give the bottom-of-crack elevation (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#bottom-of-the-crack)).
- **Dam height** $H$ — the height of the embankment. It enters the model only as the denominator of the normalized settlement, and only when a measured
  settlement is supplied; under the era default it has no effect.
- **Wall or cliff height** $H_w$ — the height of the steep wall or cliff against which the embankment fill is placed. It is the denominator of
  the bench-width ratio and the height of which the effective wall height, and so the crack depth, is a fraction.
- **Bench width** $W_b$ — the width of the bench at the base of the wall or cliff. A bench width of zero states that there is no bench: the fill
  rests directly against the full height of the wall or cliff.
- **Abutment slope below the wall or cliff** $\beta_1$ — the inclination, in degrees from horizontal, of the abutment below the wall or cliff. It
  is scored as one of the three flaw factors, and it also enters the crack width and effective wall height relationships. Where an embankment has
  walls or cliffs on both abutments, the slope supplied is the one below the wall or cliff being evaluated.

The model checks each geometric input against its own range and does not compare the geometric inputs with one another. In particular, it does not require the wall or
cliff height to be less than the dam height, or the bottom of the crack to lie above the base of the embankment.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-01 — Definition]
The geometric definitions of the wall or cliff height $H_w$, the bench width $W_b$, and the abutment slope below the wall or cliff $\beta_1$
require confirmation, ideally with a reference to the defining sketch. State (1) whether $H_w$ is the full height of the wall or cliff or only the
part in contact with the embankment fill, and whether it may exceed the dam height $H$; (2) where the bench width $W_b$ is measured — horizontally
from the base of the wall or cliff to the edge of the bench — and whether it is measured along or across the dam axis; and (3) which surface
$\beta_1$ describes — the abutment foundation surface between the bench and the valley floor — and how it is measured where that surface is not
planar; and (4) whether the top of the wall or cliff is assumed to be at or near crest level, since the crack depth is measured down from the
crest elevation and the model takes no top-of-wall or top-of-cliff elevation.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-02 — Definition]
The physical meaning of the dam height $H$ and the maximum post-construction settlement $s$ requires confirmation. State (1) whether $H$ is the
maximum height of the dam or the height of the embankment at the wall or cliff section; and (2) whether $s$ is the settlement of the crest, at the
wall or cliff section or the maximum along the crest, and over what period it is taken (for example, from the end of construction to the time of
the assessment, or the expected long-term settlement).
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-03 — Applicability]
State how an embankment with more than one wall or cliff — for example, one on each abutment, or several steps along one abutment — is to be
screened. The model evaluates one wall or cliff geometry per run.

Proposed (unconfirmed): each wall or cliff is evaluated in its own run with its own geometry and abutment slope, and the run giving the largest
SRP governs the mechanism. Confirm or replace.
:::

## Settlement Information

The normalized settlement is formed from a measured post-construction settlement when one is supplied, or from a default ratio selected by the
year of construction when it is not (<TableReference tableKey="cle-wall-cliff-settlement-screening-table-3" />). At least one of the two inputs
must be supplied.

- **Maximum post-construction settlement** $s$ — the settlement of the embankment after the end of construction, in the same length unit as the
  dam height. When it is supplied, the normalized settlement is computed from it and the year of construction is not used by the flaw assessment.
- **Year of construction** $Y$ — the year the dam was completed. It is used only when no settlement is supplied, to select the default normalized
  settlement for the era of construction: 0.5 percent for a dam completed before 1950, and 0.25 percent for a dam completed in 1950 or later (see
  [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#normalized-settlement)).

The continuation method takes its own year of construction among the continuation inputs, for the filter evaluation (see
[Shared Inputs](#shared-inputs)). The two inputs are separate: the year supplied here does not reach the continuation method.

## Shared Inputs

The following inputs are documented in full in the common methods manual. The notes give how this model uses each.

- **Hydraulic loading** — the headwater series and the optional tailwater series (see
  [Hydraulic Loading](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#hydraulic-loading)). The
  tailwater is reported with the results but does not enter any node of this model.
- **Embankment type** — homogeneous earthfill, zoned earthfill, or central-core earth and rockfill, which sets the crack-filling node (see
  [Embankment Description](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#embankment-description)).
  The model takes no slope-segment description of the embankment section; its flaw assessment uses only the inputs of this chapter.
- **Failure-pathway inputs** — the top-of-filter elevation, the full-pathway roof materials, the below-filter roof materials, and the
  continuation inputs, in the crest-crack form (see
  [Failure-Pathway Inputs](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#failure-pathway-inputs)).
  The below-filter materials are taken at the bottom-of-crack elevation that the flaw assessment computes, so they are listed after that
  elevation is known (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#bottom-of-the-crack)). The continuation inputs themselves are documented in
  the
  [Input Parameters](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/input-parameters) chapter of the Continuation (Filter
  Evaluation) Screening Technical Reference Manual.
- **Upstream seepage-limiting elements** — the elements present and, when one is a wall, the top-of-wall elevation (see
  [Upstream Seepage-Limiting Elements](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#upstream-seepage-limiting-elements)).
- **Intervention and breach** — the probabilities of unsuccessful intervention and of breach, each defaulting to 1.0 (see
  [Intervention and Breach](https://usace-rmc.github.io/RMC-Software-Documentation/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/input-parameters#intervention-and-breach)).

The model takes none of the initiation material inputs of the compaction models — no initiation soil classification, critical shear stress, initial
pipe diameter, or seepage path length — because its initiation node is the headwater gate, and it takes no observation of cracking (see
[Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#no-observation-override)).

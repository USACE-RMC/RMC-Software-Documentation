# Output and Interpretation

The model reports the SRP at each evaluated pool level together with the probability of each of the eight event-tree nodes, and the intermediate
results of its flaw assessment. The node results take the form common to the crest-crack CLE models, documented in the
[Output and Interpretation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) chapter of the
common methods manual. This chapter summarizes them and documents the flaw-assessment results and how they shape the curve.

## Node Results

**Pool-independent results:**

- **Flaw probability** — Node 1, the scored flaw probability of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability).
- **Crack-filling probability** — Node 5, the probability of no crack filling for the embankment type.
- **Flow-limitation probability** — the minimum across all listed upstream elements, or 1.0 when none is listed. It is the Node 6 value at pool
  levels at or below the top of a wall, and at every pool level when no top-of-wall elevation is given.
- **Roof-support probabilities** — the minimum across the full-pathway materials, not applicable for a filter that caps the pathway (one whose
  top reaches the crest, so that no full-pathway materials are listed), and the minimum across the below-filter materials, not applicable when no
  filter is present.

**Pool-dependent series**, evaluated at every pool level of the headwater grid (see the
[Pool-Dependent Series](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#pool-dependent-series)
section of the common methods manual):

- **Pool levels and tailwater** — the evaluated headwater elevations and the tailwater at each. Tailwater is reported for reference only; there is
  none when no tailwater is given.
- **Initiation probability** — Node 2: zero at and below the bottom of the crack, 1.0 above it.
- **Continuation probability** — Node 3: the continuation method's result at or below the filter's top, and 1.0 above it or where no filter is
  present.
- **Roof-support probability** — Node 4: the below-filter or full-pathway value that governs at each pool level.
- **Flow-limitation probability** — Node 6 after any wall has dropped out above its top.
- **Unsuccessful-intervention and breach probabilities** — Nodes 7 and 8, the user-specified values.
- **SRP** — the product of the eight nodes: the system response curve.

When a filter intersects the failure pathway, the continuation method also reports which method it applied to each base soil and the result of
each (see the
[Output and Interpretation](/docs/web-applications/system-response/continuation-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation) chapter of the
Continuation (Filter Evaluation) Screening Technical Reference Manual).

## Flaw-Assessment Results

The flaw assessment reports each intermediate quantity of [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx), so that the calculation can be followed
step by step:

- **Normalized ratios** — the bench-width ratio $r_b$ and the normalized settlement $r_s$ (in percent), whether measured or the era default.
- **Likelihood factors** — the scores of the cross-valley profile, the settlement, and the abutment slope.
- **Weighted sum** $\Sigma$ — the risk-factor-weighted sum of the three scores.
- **Crack width** $w$ — in millimeters, at the abutment slope of the site. It is a supporting result only and does not enter the event tree.
- **Effective wall height ratio and effective wall height** — $r_e = H_e/H_w$ and $H_e$.
- **Normalized crack depth and crack depth** — $r_d = 100\, d_c/H_e$ (in percent) and $d_c$.
- **Bottom-of-crack elevation** $z_{bc}$ — the initiation gate of the event tree.

The flaw probability is reported with the node results. It equals the scored value, since the model has no observation override.

## Interpreting the System Response Curve

The curve has the piecewise-constant form of every crest-crack model (see the
[Interpreting the System Response Curve](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#interpreting-the-system-response-curve)
section of the common methods manual). It is negligible at and below the bottom of the crack and steps up as soon as the pool rises above it.
Above the bottom of the crack, at each pool level,

<EquationNoRef equation="\mathrm{SRP} = P_{flaw} \times P_{cont} \times P_{roof} \times P_{nf} \times P_{nl} \times P_{int} \times P_{br}" />

with continuation, roof support, and flow limitation taking the values that govern at that pool level. The flaw assessment controls two features
of the curve:

- **The pool level of the first step** is the bottom of the crack, $z_{bc} = z_c - d_c$. It moves down as the wall or cliff height increases.
  It moves down, or stays where it is, as the normalized settlement increases, and moves up, or stays where it is, as the bench widens. It no
  longer changes once the normalized settlement reaches 1 percent, once the bench-width ratio reaches 2, or, at abutment slopes of 45° to 60°,
  once the bench-width ratio reaches 1. With no bench and a normalized settlement of 1 percent or more, the crack extends
  the full height of the wall or cliff below the crest.
- **The height of the curve** scales with the flaw probability, which ranges from negligible to 0.2 (see
  [Flaw Probability](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#flaw-probability)). A flaw probability of zero — a bench wider than 2.5 times the wall or cliff
  height, or an abutment slope of 25° or flatter — leaves the curve negligible at every pool level, and the lowest reachable weighted sum, 10, leaves it effectively negligible.

The bench-width ratio enters both features: a narrower bench raises, or leaves unchanged, the cross-valley profile score and so the flaw
probability, and it raises, or leaves unchanged, the effective wall height and so the crack depth. The normalized settlement likewise raises, or
leaves unchanged, both the settlement score and the crack depth. The abutment slope can act in both directions: a steeper slope raises its score
where it crosses a band limit, and where there is a bench it lowers the effective wall height, or leaves it unchanged, and so makes the crack
shallower or leaves it unchanged. The effective wall height does not change with slope between 45° and 60° at bench-width ratios of 1 or more,
nor beyond 60° or below 15°.

Further steps occur where the pool crosses the top of a filter or the top of a wall above the bottom of the crack (see
[Where the Crack Meets the Pathway Gates](./05-event-tree.mdx#where-the-crack-meets-the-pathway-gates)). The node series show which node changes
at each step; the common methods manual's
[Reading the Node Breakdown](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#reading-the-node-breakdown)
section describes the typical readings.

## Checking the Result

Every node except continuation is deterministic, so the curve can be reproduced from the reported values. The continuation probability itself may
be a Monte Carlo estimate when the continuation method uses its soil classification route. The following checks apply in addition to those of the
[Checking the Result](/docs/web-applications/system-response/cle-common-methods-screening/v1.0/output-and-interpretation#checking-the-result)
section of the common methods manual:

- **Likelihood factors** — each score can be read against the bands of
  [Likelihood Factors](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx#likelihood-factors) from the reported ratios and the abutment slope, and the weighted sum is
  $3\,LF_{xv} + 2\,LF_{s} + LF_{\beta}$.
- **Crack depth** — the crack depth equals $(r_d/100) \times r_e \times H_w$ and is no greater than the wall or cliff height; the
  bottom of the crack lies that distance below the crest.
- **Era default** — when no settlement was supplied, the normalized settlement is exactly 0.5 or 0.25 percent, according to the year of
  construction.
- **Bottom of the crack against the embankment** — the crack depth is a multiple of the wall or cliff height and does not depend on any datum.
  Because it is no greater than the wall or cliff height, a bottom of the crack more than the dam height below the crest indicates a wall or cliff
  height greater than the dam height; a bottom of the crack below a known foundation elevation can also indicate a dam height inconsistent with
  the crest elevation (see [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx#dam-and-wall-or-cliff-geometry)).

## Screening Results Relative to Detailed Analysis

The model is a screening tool. Its results indicate whether settlement cracking adjacent to a wall or cliff warrants detailed analysis; they are
not a substitute for a detailed assessment. The flaw probability and crack depth come from tabulated relationships in four measurements and a
settlement, the era default stands in for an unknown settlement, and an open crack is taken to initiate erosion as soon as the pool reaches it.
Where a screening result is significant to a decision, the crack likelihood and depth should be re-examined with site-specific information —
settlement records, crest inspections, and investigation at the wall or cliff — and the methods of Fell et al. (2024) <Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />
and the judgment of the risk team should replace the screening values.

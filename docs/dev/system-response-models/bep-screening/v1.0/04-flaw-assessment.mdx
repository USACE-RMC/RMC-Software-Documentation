# Flaw Assessment

The first event-tree node is the probability that a continuous path of fine to medium, uniform sand exists in the foundation — the flaw that
backward erosion piping requires. The screening model estimates this probability with a Bayesian assessment: a prior probability based on the
geologic depositional environment is updated by the available site evidence. The assessment is independent of pool level; the resulting
probability applies across the entire system response curve.

## Bayesian Framework

The assessment combines up to three pieces of information:

1. **Geologic prior** — The depositional environment of the foundation soils sets the prior probability $p_0$ that a laterally continuous,
   uniform fine sand layer was deposited.
2. **Subsurface-data evidence** — When borings, soundings, geophysics, or comparable data inform the judgment, the analyst characterizes the
   strength of that evidence for (or against) a continuous flaw, together with the quality of the data behind the judgment.
3. **Seepage-observation evidence** — When seepage has been observed downstream during past loading, the character of the observation serves
   as evidence that a continuous transmissive pathway already exists.

Each evidence source is expressed as a likelihood ratio $LR$ — the ratio of the probability of the observation given that a continuous flaw
exists to its probability given no flaw. Ratios above one make the flaw more probable; ratios below one make it less probable. The update is
performed in log-odds form:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-1"
  equation="\ell_0 = \ln\!\left(\frac{p_0}{1 - p_0}\right)"
/>

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-2"
  equation="\ell = \ell_0 + q \cdot \ln(LR_{sub}) + \ln(LR_{seep})"
/>

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-3"
  equation="P_{flaw} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\ell}}"
/>

where:

> $p_0$ = prior probability of a continuous flaw from the geologic environment  
> $\ell_0$, $\ell$ = prior and posterior log-odds  
> $LR_{sub}$ = likelihood ratio for the subsurface-data evidence  
> $q$ = subsurface data quality factor, which scales the weight given to the subsurface evidence  
> $LR_{seep}$ = likelihood ratio for the seepage observation  
> $P_{flaw}$ = posterior probability of a continuous flaw

When subsurface data is not available, the subsurface term drops out of <EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-2" /> (equivalently,
$LR_{sub} = 1$). When no seepage has been observed, the seepage term drops out ($LR_{seep} = 1$). The quality factor $q$ acts as an exponent on
the subsurface likelihood ratio: high-quality data receives full weight ($q = 1$), while lower-quality data is discounted toward neutral
($q < 1$).

## Uncertainty Quantification

Each categorical selection maps to a *range* of values rather than a single number, reflecting the imprecision of the underlying judgments.
The model propagates this uncertainty by Monte Carlo simulation: in each realization, the prior probability and each active likelihood ratio
and quality factor are drawn independently and uniformly from their ranges, and the posterior probability is computed from
<EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-3" />. Across realizations this produces a distribution of the posterior flaw probability.

The **mean posterior probability** populates the event-tree node. The 5th–95th percentile band of the posterior is reported alongside it as a
diagnostic, so the user can see how sharply the evidence constrains the flaw judgment (see
[Output and Interpretation](./09-output-and-interpretation.mdx)).

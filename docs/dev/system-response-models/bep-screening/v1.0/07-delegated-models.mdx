# Delegated Models

The screening model delegates its Monte Carlo computations to two established analysis methods, each documented in a dedicated technical
manual: blanket theory for the blowout and heave probabilities
([BEP Initiation](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/preface)) and the Schmertmann
critical-gradient method for progression
([BEP Progression](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann)). The theory
lives in those manuals; this chapter documents how the screening model invokes each method — the quantities passed in, the outputs consumed,
and every screening-level adjustment relative to the dedicated documentation.

## Blanket Theory — Blowout and Heave

Blanket theory<Citation citationKey="USACE1956" /> computes the excess head beneath the downstream confining layer and the vertical gradient
through it, from which the probability of a gradient-driven failure of the layer follows. The screening model routes the analysis to Case 2 or
Case 4 as documented in [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx), and runs it **twice with
identical sampled realizations** of the foundation thickness, confining-layer thickness, and downstream path length:

- **Blowout run** — the critical gradient is computed from the *confining layer's* saturated unit weight $\gamma_{bl}$, representing rupture
  of the layer's buoyant weight. Its result feeds the unfiltered-exit node.
- **Heave run** — the critical gradient is computed from the *foundation sand's* saturated unit weight $\gamma_{sand}$, representing
  fluidization of the sand column at a defect through the layer. Its result feeds the initiation node.

Because the two runs share their sampled realizations, the blowout and heave probabilities differ only through the unit weight entering the
critical gradient.

### Governing relationships

Within each Monte Carlo realization, and at each pool level with net head $H$ (defined in
[Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)):

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-15" equation="h_o = H \cdot \frac{L_3}{L_{total}}" />

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-16"
  equation="h_x = \begin{cases} h_o \cdot \dfrac{L_3 - x}{L_3} & x < L_3 \\[6pt] 0 & x \geq L_3 \end{cases}"
/>

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-17" equation="i_{cv} = \frac{\gamma_{sat} - \gamma_w}{\gamma_w}" />

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-18" equation="i_v = \frac{h_x}{z_{bl}}, \qquad FS = \frac{i_{cv}}{i_v}" />

where:

> $h_o$ = excess head above the exit datum at the embankment toe  
> $h_x$ = excess head at the exit evaluation point, offset $x$ from the toe  
> $L_3$, $L_{total}$ = downstream and total seepage path lengths ($L_3$ sampled per realization when the downstream extent is unknown)  
> $i_{cv}$ = critical vertical gradient  
> $\gamma_{sat}$ = saturated unit weight ($\gamma_{bl}$ for blowout, $\gamma_{sand}$ for heave)  
> $\gamma_w$ = unit weight of water (62.4 pcf)  
> $i_v$ = vertical exit gradient through the confining layer  
> $z_{bl}$ = sampled confining-layer thickness  
> $FS$ = factor of safety against the vertical gradient

The blowout or heave probability at each pool level is the fraction of Monte Carlo realizations in which $FS < 1$. Realizations with no exit
gradient (zero net head) contribute no failure.

### Screening-level adjustments

Relative to the dedicated blanket-theory documentation, the screening model fixes the following:

- **Finite-blanket head loss always applies** — the excess head at the toe is always reduced by the path-length ratio
  (<EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-15" />). The idealization of an infinite downstream blanket carrying the full net head at
  the toe is not used.
- **Downstream blanket length follows the resolved downstream path** — $L_3$ enters as the supplied downstream extent's single value, or is
  drawn per realization from the wide default range when the extent is unknown
  (see [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)).
- **Confining-layer thickness comes from the category band** — the sampled $z_{bl}$ follows the qualitative thickness category (or the custom
  distribution) documented in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx).
- **The unit weight is mechanism-specific** — $\gamma_{bl}$ for blowout and $\gamma_{sand}$ for heave, each defaulting per
  [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx).
- **Foundation permeability does not influence the screening result** — hydraulic conductivity affects only the seepage-quantity outputs of
  the blanket-theory analysis, which the screening model does not use; the exit-gradient computations are independent of it.

## Schmertmann Method — Progression

The Schmertmann method<Citation citationKey="Schmertmann2000" /> evaluates whether the average applied gradient along the seepage path exceeds
the critical gradient required to advance an erosion pipe through the foundation sand. The complete method — the laboratory basis and every
correction factor — is documented in the
[BEP Progression manual](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/schmertmann); this
section documents the screening invocation.

### Base gradient and critical gradient

The base critical gradient derives from laboratory piping-model tests, represented as a family of quantile curves linear in the coefficient of
uniformity:

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-19" equation="i_{pmt}(q) = m(q)\,C_u + b(q)" />

where $i_{pmt}(q)$ is the laboratory critical gradient at cumulative probability $q$, and $m(q)$ and $b(q)$ are the quantile regression
coefficients from the dedicated documentation. The screening model uses these built-in laboratory relationships without modification. The
field critical gradient scales the laboratory gradient by the correction factors:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-20"
  equation="i_{ch}(q) = i_{pmt}(q) \cdot \frac{C_D \, C_L \, C_S \, C_K \, C_\gamma \, C_Z}{C_R} \cdot C_\alpha \cdot C_{OF}"
/>

In the screening configuration, four factors are fixed at unity and five remain active
(<TableReference tableKey="bep-screening-table-22" />).

### Exit geometry and fines corrections

The overall field correction is:

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-21" equation="C_{OF} = C_{FC} \cdot C_{3d}" />

The exit-geometry factor $C_{3d}$ distinguishes concentrated (hole-type) exits from distributed (plane-type) exits. Van Beek et
al. (2015)<Citation citationKey="vanBeek2015" /> found that three-dimensional flow converging toward a single exit point produces critical
gradients roughly half those of the two-dimensional configuration, so a hole-type exit takes $C_{3d} = 0.5$ and a plane-type exit
$C_{3d} = 1.0$. The screening model assigns the exit type as follows:

- **Toe pathway** — hole-type ($C_{3d} = 0.5$) when the confining layer is confirmed, *unless* a berm is present and either the layer's
  downstream extent is unknown or the berm reaches or clears that extent (the exit then daylights beyond the layer and is plane-type). When
  the layer's presence is No or Unknown, the toe exit is plane-type.
- **Daylight pathway** — always plane-type ($C_{3d} = 1.0$); the exit is an exposed sand surface.

The fines-content correction is a step function of the foundation sand's fines content:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-22"
  equation="C_{FC} = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{fines} < 5\% \\[4pt] 1.4 & \text{fines} \geq 5\% \end{cases}"
/>

The higher factor applies at exactly 5% fines and above; a fines content below 5% takes no correction.

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-16 — Engineering Basis]
The screening fines-content correction — a step from 1.0 to 1.4, with the higher factor applying at exactly 5% fines and above — requires an
engineering basis. It replaces the continuous fines-content relationship in the dedicated Schmertmann documentation (which increases the
correction progressively with fines content). Provide the source or engineering-judgment attribution for the step form, the 5% threshold,
and the 1.4 value.
:::

### Probability of advancement

At each pool level, the applied gradient is the net head over the pathway's seepage path length:

<Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-23" equation="i_{adv}(h) = \frac{H(h)}{L_{prog}}" />

where $L_{prog}$ is the toe or daylight progression path length from
[Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx) (sampled per realization on the daylight pathway when the
downstream extent is unknown). Within each realization, the correction factors scale the entire family of laboratory quantile curves
(<EquationReference equationKey="bep-screening-eq-20" />), and the probability of advancement is the cumulative probability that the corrected
critical gradient is at or below the applied gradient — interpolated from the scaled quantile family, and clamped to 0 and 1 beyond its ends.
A pool producing no positive net head yields no probability of advancement.

The progression node probability at each pool level is the mean probability of advancement across the Monte Carlo realizations, subsequently
constrained to be non-decreasing with pool level as described in [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx).

# Event Tree

The BEP screening model computes the system response probability at each headwater level by multiplying probabilities from a seven-node event
tree. The tree follows the structure defined in the
[Typical Event Tree Database](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/risk-calculations-suite/typical-event-tree-database/v1.5/backward-erosion-piping)
for the backward erosion piping failure mode; every database node maps one-to-one onto a screening-model node.

## Node 1: Flaw — Continuous Path

The probability $P_{flaw}$ that a continuous path of erodible foundation sand exists is the mean posterior probability from the Bayesian flaw
assessment documented in [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx). The flaw is a geologic condition, so this probability is constant across
all pool levels and is shared by both exit pathways.

## Node 2: Flaw — Unfiltered Exit

### Toe pathway

When a confining layer may be present and the toe exit remains beneath it — no berm, or a berm ending within the layer's known downstream
extent — seepage can reach the surface unfiltered at the toe through either of two mechanisms:

- **Pre-existing defect** — a discontinuity that already penetrates the layer (desiccation crack, root channel, animal burrow, or similar).
  The probability that a defect passes completely through the layer is evaluated as a survival function on the layer thickness:

  <Equation equationKey="bep-screening-eq-10" equation="P_{defect} = \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{z_{bl}}{t_c}\right)^{k}\,\right]" />

  where $z_{bl}$ is the confining-layer thickness, $t_c = 3.5$ ft is the characteristic defect penetration depth, and $k = 1.25$ is the shape
  parameter. The expression is the probability that a defect's penetration depth equals or exceeds the layer thickness under a Weibull
  distribution of defect depths — thinner layers are more likely to be fully penetrated. The probability is evaluated for each sampled layer
  thickness and averaged across the Monte Carlo realizations; it does not depend on pool level.

- **Hydraulic blowout** — rupture of the layer when uplift at its base exceeds its buoyant weight. The blowout probability $P_{blowout}(h)$ at
  each pool level is computed by the blanket-theory analysis using the confining layer's saturated unit weight
  (see [Delegated Models](./07-delegated-models.mdx)); it increases with pool level. The probability is evaluated at the exit evaluation
  point — the toe by default, offset beyond a berm that ends within the layer's known extent
  (see [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)).

The two mechanisms are combined as the union of independent pathways:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-11"
  equation="P_{ue}(h) = P_{defect} + \bigl(1 - P_{defect}\bigr)\, P_{blowout}(h)"
/>

Three site conditions override the composition and force $P_{ue} = 1.0$ at every pool level:

1. **No confining layer** — with the layer's presence answered No, nothing filters the exit.
2. **The exit daylights at the berm toe** — for a confirmed layer, a berm that reaches or clears the supplied downstream extent, or overlies
   a layer of unknown extent: the berm toe then sits at or beyond the layer's termination, so nothing filters the exit there. In these cases
   the berm toe is itself the daylighting exit and no separate daylight pathway is evaluated (see Governing SRP below). For a layer of
   unknown presence, any berm forces this override — a daylighting exit at the berm toe is assumed rather than established — and both
   pathways are retained.
3. **Observed seepage through the layer** — sand boils or concentrated seepage have been observed emerging through the confining layer,
   demonstrating that an unfiltered exit already exists. The override applies to any toe exit that remains beneath the layer, with or
   without a berm.

A berm ending within a confirmed layer's known extent triggers none of these overrides: the through-layer composition above is retained, with
the blowout probability evaluated at the offset exit point beyond the berm.

The Weibull defect-survival parameters — characteristic penetration depth $t_c = 3.5$ ft and shape parameter $k = 1.25$ — require an
engineering basis. These are empirical values with no published-literature provenance recorded. Provide the data source, calibration
rationale, or engineering-judgment attribution to state here.
:::

:::warning[INPUT NEEDED IN-14 — Engineering Basis]
Two judgments in the berm treatment require an engineering basis to state here:

1. No berm is ever credited with filtering the exit: where the berm end remains beneath the layer, the exit resistance is carried entirely
   by the layer's defect and blowout composition (evaluated at the offset exit point), and where the berm daylights the exit, the unfiltered
   exit is certain.
2. When the layer's presence is unknown and a berm is present, a daylighting exit at the berm toe is assumed (forcing the unfiltered exit to
   certainty) rather than evaluated.

### Daylight pathway

At the daylight exit the confining layer has terminated; there is nothing to filter the seepage. The unfiltered-exit probability on the
daylight pathway is 1.0 at every pool level. The pathway itself is evaluated only when the daylighting exit is distinct from the toe exit
(see Governing SRP below).

## Node 3: Initiation

### Toe pathway

Initiation at a confined exit requires the vertical gradient through the layer (or through a defect in it) to fluidize the underlying sand.
The initiation probability $P_{init}(h)$ at each pool level is the blanket-theory heave probability, computed with the foundation sand's
saturated unit weight (see [Delegated Models](./07-delegated-models.mdx)). Four site conditions override the computed value and force
$P_{init} = 1.0$:

1. **No confining layer** — with no overburden to resist heave, initiation accompanies the unfiltered exit.
2. **A berm overlies a confirmed layer of unknown downstream extent** — a daylighting exit is assumed at the berm toe, beyond the
   characterized layer.
3. **The berm reaches or clears a supplied downstream extent** — the berm is at least as long as the layer's extent, so the exit sits at or
   beyond the layer's termination. The override applies whether the layer's presence is confirmed or unknown.
4. **The pool reaches the trigger elevation** — sand boils have historically activated at or above this pool, demonstrating initiation:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-12"
  equation="P_{init}(h) = \begin{cases} 1.0 & h \geq E_{trigger} \\[4pt] P_{heave}(h) & h < E_{trigger} \end{cases}"
/>

where $E_{trigger}$ is the trigger elevation and $P_{heave}(h)$ the blanket-theory heave probability.

Over a confirmed layer, the second and third conditions are the berm-toe daylighting cases: they force both this node and the unfiltered-exit
node to certainty, and the separate daylight pathway is not evaluated (see Governing SRP below). Under a layer of unknown presence, the third
condition forces this node alone and both pathways remain.

### Daylight pathway

At the daylight exit the sand is exposed at the surface with no confining layer to resist heave; the initiation probability is 1.0 at every
pool level.

## Node 4: Progression — Mechanical Condition

A pipe can only progress if the material above it sustains a stable roof. When the confining layer's presence is confirmed (answered Yes), the
layer itself provides the roof and the probability is 1.0. Otherwise, the probability is evaluated from the embankment materials: each USCS
class maps to a roof-support probability per <TableReference tableKey="bep-screening-table-21" />, following Fell et
al. (2024)<Citation citationKey="Fell2024" />, and the minimum across the listed materials governs — every material along the pipe path must
hold a roof, so the least capable material controls. The probability is constant across pool levels.

## Node 5: Progression — Hydraulic Condition

The probability that the applied gradient is sufficient to advance the pipe from the exit toward the reservoir is the mean probability of
advancement from the Schmertmann analysis, evaluated at each pool level for the pathway's seepage path length
(see [Delegated Models](./07-delegated-models.mdx)). The toe pathway uses the path to the embankment or berm toe; the daylight pathway uses
the longer path to the daylight exit. The resulting probability is constrained to be non-decreasing with pool level, reflecting that a higher
pool cannot make progression less likely.

## Nodes 6 and 7: Unsuccessful Intervention and Breach

The final two nodes are the user-specified scalar probabilities documented in [Input Parameters](./03-input-parameters.mdx). Both default to
1.0 — no successful intervention and certain breach — a conservative screening posture.

## SRP Computation

The system response probability for each pathway at each pool level is the product of its seven node probabilities:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-13"
  equation="\mathrm{SRP}(h) = P_{flaw} \times P_{ue}(h) \times P_{init}(h) \times P_{roof} \times P_{prog}(h) \times P_{int} \times P_{br}"
/>

where:

> $P_{flaw}$ = probability of a continuous flaw (Node 1)  
> $P_{ue}(h)$ = probability of an unfiltered exit (Node 2)  
> $P_{init}(h)$ = probability of initiation (Node 3)  
> $P_{roof}$ = probability of roof support (Node 4)  
> $P_{prog}(h)$ = probability of hydraulic progression (Node 5)  
> $P_{int}$ = probability of unsuccessful intervention (Node 6)  
> $P_{br}$ = probability of breach (Node 7)

On the daylight pathway, $P_{ue}$ and $P_{init}$ are both 1.0 and $P_{prog}$ reflects the longer daylight path.

## Governing SRP

A daylight pathway is evaluated only where the daylighting exit is distinct from the toe exit. When a berm over a confirmed layer reaches or
clears the layer's downstream extent — or overlies a confirmed layer of unknown extent — the berm toe is itself the daylighting exit: the toe
pathway then carries a certain unfiltered exit and certain initiation over the berm-extended path, which are exactly the daylighting-exit
conditions, so a separate daylight pathway would repeat the same exit node for node and is not evaluated. A layer of unknown presence retains
both pathways even with a berm, because its toe pathway keeps the heave-gated initiation node and the two pathways genuinely differ.

When both pathways are evaluated, the governing SRP at each pool level is the larger of the two pathway values:

<Equation
  equationKey="bep-screening-eq-14"
  equation="\mathrm{SRP}_{gov}(h) = \max\bigl(\mathrm{SRP}_{toe}(h),\; \mathrm{SRP}_{day}(h)\bigr)"
/>

When only the toe pathway exists — no confining layer, or a berm-toe daylighting exit — it governs directly.

## Pool-Dependent Series

Every pool-dependent result is reported as a series over the evaluated pool levels, which span the supplied headwater rating
(<TableReference tableKey="bep-screening-table-25" />). The daylight-pathway series are present only when a confining layer may be present
(its presence is Yes or Unknown) *and* the daylighting exit is distinct from the toe exit. When the berm toe is itself the daylighting exit —
a berm over a confirmed layer that reaches or clears its downstream extent, or overlies a layer of unknown extent — the daylight series are
absent and the toe series carry the governing curve (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx)).

## Analysis Audit Record

Alongside the numerical results, the analysis reports an audit record documenting how the model resolved the analysis configuration, so a
reviewer can verify the routing, geometry, and inputs without re-deriving them. The record is observation-only — assembling it never alters
the numerical results — and contains:

- **Selected blanket-theory configuration** — Case 2 or Case 4, per the routing rule in
  [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx); not reported when no confining layer is present (the
  blowout and heave analyses do not run).
- **Effective seepage path lengths** — the upstream path length $L_1$ and embankment base width $L_2$ actually used; the low and high
  bounds of the resolved downstream path length $L_3$ (equal when $L_3$ resolved to a single value), together with the resolution rule that
  produced each of $L_1$ and $L_3$; and the toe and daylight progression path lengths.
- **Resolved hydraulic geometry** — the toe and daylight reference elevations, the exit-evaluation offset, whether the toe exit was treated
  as a concentrated (hole-type) exit, and whether the daylighting exit resolved to the berm toe (collapsing the daylight pathway into the
  toe pathway).
- **Resolved inputs with their origins** — every distribution the analysis used, each marked as supplied by the user, filled from a
  screening default, derived from a qualitative category, or overridden by the model (for example, the thickness band forced to Unknown when
  the layer presence is unverified), with an explanatory note wherever the resolution needs one; and the per-material roof-support
  probabilities behind the governing minimum.
- **Delegated-analysis records** — one record per delegated analysis (the flaw assessment, the blowout and heave analyses, the defect
  evaluation, and the toe and daylight progression analyses) documenting the exact quantities passed in and a digest of the outputs
  consumed, including the net head series and the initiation probabilities at the toe and at the offset exit point.
- **Node provenance** — which rule produced the toe unfiltered-exit and initiation values: computed from the underlying mechanisms, or
  forced to certainty by a specific override, with the trigger elevation echoed so per-pool forcing is readable off the series.

Each analysis also carries a version identifier for the screening model, so reported results can be tied to the model revision that computed
them.

## Interpreting the System Response Curve

The governing SRP series is the system response curve — the conditional probability of breach by backward erosion piping as a function of pool
level. The curve is non-decreasing with pool level: the flaw, roof-support, intervention, and breach nodes are constant; the blowout and heave
probabilities rise with net head; and the progression node is explicitly constrained to be non-decreasing. Pools at or below the governing exit
reference elevation carry effectively zero SRP because no net driving head exists there.

### Which Pathway Governs

The toe and daylight pathways trade off against each other:

- The **toe pathway** carries unfiltered-exit and initiation probabilities below one (the confining layer resists both), but its progression
  analysis benefits from a shorter seepage path — and, when the layer is confirmed, from the concentrated (hole-type) exit that halves the
  Schmertmann critical gradient.
- The **daylight pathway** carries certain unfiltered exit and initiation (nothing confines the exit there), but its progression analysis runs
  over the longer path to the layer's termination with a plane-type exit.

When the confining layer is thick and well characterized, its resistance suppresses the toe pathway and the daylight pathway tends to govern.
When the layer is thin — or its thickness band is wide because it is unknown — the defect pathway drives the toe unfiltered-exit probability
up and the toe pathway tends to govern. When both pathways are evaluated, both are reported, so the trade-off is visible directly in the
results. When the berm toe is itself the daylighting exit, only the toe pathway is reported — it *is* the daylighting exit, carrying certain
unfiltered-exit and initiation probabilities over the berm-extended progression path.

### Sensitivity to Key Inputs

The inputs with the greatest influence on the screening SRP are:

- **Flaw evidence selections** — the flaw probability scales the entire curve. Moving the geologic environment, strength of evidence, or
  observed-seepage selections shifts every pool level proportionally.
- **Confining layer thickness category** — thickness controls both the defect probability (steeply, at small thickness) and the vertical exit
  gradients in the blowout and heave analyses.
- **Downstream layer extent** — sets the daylight path length and the head remaining at the exit; the unknown-extent default spans a wide
  range and widens the result accordingly.
- **Relative density category** — denser sand raises the Schmertmann critical gradient and suppresses progression.
- **Embankment materials** — the minimum roof-support probability governs; a single clean sand or gravel in the list reduces the SRP sharply
  when no confining layer is confirmed.
- **Reference elevations** — the toe and daylight ground surfaces position the zero-head threshold and thereby the low-pool end of the curve.

### Screening Versus Detailed Analysis

A high screening SRP does not by itself indicate a high risk of failure — it indicates that the structure warrants analysis with the full
toolbox models
([BEP Initiation](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/preface),
[BEP Progression](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/preface)), where:

- the blanket-theory case matching the actual stratigraphy can be selected from all eight configurations;
- site-specific blanket geometry replaces the screening defaults and unknown-value ranges;
- the anisotropy, underlayer, and pipe-inclination corrections can be applied to the Schmertmann analysis;
- the Sellmeijer method is available as an alternative progression formulation; and
- the flaw judgment can be refined by targeted subsurface investigation rather than screening-level evidence categories.

A low screening SRP provides reasonable confidence that backward erosion piping is not a critical concern at the screening level, subject to
the assumptions and limitations documented in this reference.

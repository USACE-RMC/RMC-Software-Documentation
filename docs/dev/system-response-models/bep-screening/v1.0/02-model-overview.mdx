# Model Overview

## Purpose

The BEP screening model computes a system response probability (SRP) for the backward erosion piping failure mode as a function of headwater
elevation. The SRP represents the conditional probability of dam or levee breach due to backward erosion piping, given a specific pool level. The
model produces a system response curve — an array of SRP values across a range of headwater elevations — that characterizes the structure's
vulnerability to this failure mode.

Backward erosion piping is the progressive removal of cohesionless foundation soil by seepage, beginning at an unfiltered downstream exit and
eroding backward (upstream) beneath the embankment until a continuous pipe connects the impounded water to the exit. The failure mode requires a
continuous path of erodible material, an unfiltered exit, sufficient gradient to initiate erosion, a soil roof that keeps the developing pipe
open, and sufficient gradient to advance the pipe to the reservoir.

## Intended Use

The model is designed for the Dam Screening Tool (DST), where it serves as a screening-level assessment of backward erosion piping risk. It makes
simplifying and generally conservative assumptions with two goals:

- **Preventing false negatives** — The model is calibrated to avoid underestimating risk at the screening level, so that structures with
  potential BEP concerns are flagged for further analysis.
- **Reducing input burden** — The model requires fewer inputs than the full BEP toolbox models. Qualitative selections (confining layer
  thickness category, relative density category, geologic environment) substitute for site-specific distributions, and default distributions
  cover material properties that are typically unavailable at the screening stage. Where a site characteristic is genuinely unknown — the
  presence of a confining layer, or the extent of that layer beyond the embankment — the model accepts "unknown" as an answer and substitutes a
  deliberately wide characterization rather than requiring the user to guess.

The model is not intended for detailed or semi-quantitative risk assessments. For higher-level analyses, the full
[BEP Initiation (BT Case 1–8)](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-initiation/v1.0/preface) and
[BEP Progression (Schmertmann and Sellmeijer)](/docs/toolbox-technical-manuals/internal-erosion-suite/backward-erosion-piping-progression/v1.0/preface)
toolbox models provide the complete methodologies with full parameterization.

## How the Model Works

The screening model executes three stages:

1. **Flaw assessment** — A Bayesian analysis estimates the probability that a continuous path of fine to medium, uniform sand exists in the
   foundation. A prior probability based on the geologic depositional environment is updated with subsurface-data evidence and observed-seepage
   evidence when available. The mean posterior probability populates the first event-tree node and is independent of pool level
   (see [Flaw Assessment](./04-flaw-assessment.mdx)).

2. **Hydraulic and seepage-geometry resolution** — The model resolves the downstream reference elevations that control net driving head, the
   upstream seepage path length $L_1$, the embankment base width $L_2$, and the downstream seepage path length $L_3$ from the site inputs, and
   selects the appropriate blanket-theory configuration — Case 2 when an upstream seepage barrier is present, Case 4 otherwise
   (see [Hydraulic Loading and Seepage Paths](./05-hydraulic-loading-and-seepage-paths.mdx)).

3. **Event-tree assembly** — At each headwater level, the model composes seven node probabilities and multiplies them to produce the SRP. Two
   nodes are evaluated by Monte Carlo simulation delegated to established analysis methods: blanket theory computes the blowout and heave
   probabilities feeding the unfiltered-exit and initiation nodes, and the Schmertmann critical-gradient method computes the probability of pipe
   progression. A companion defect-survival model contributes the probability that a pre-existing defect already penetrates the confining layer
   (see [Event Tree](./06-event-tree.mdx) and [Delegated Models](./07-delegated-models.mdx)).

## Exit Pathways

The model evaluates up to two seepage exit pathways:

- **Toe pathway** — Seepage exits at the downstream toe of the embankment, or at the toe of the downstream seepage berm when one is present.
  This pathway is always evaluated. When a confining layer may be present and the toe exit remains beneath it — no berm, or a berm ending
  within the layer's known extent — the unfiltered-exit probability at the toe is composed from two mechanisms — a pre-existing defect through
  the layer, and hydraulic blowout (rupture) of the layer — and the initiation probability is the blanket-theory heave probability. Where a
  berm instead places the exit at or beyond the layer's downstream termination, the exit daylights at the berm toe and the unfiltered-exit
  and initiation probabilities are certain there.

- **Daylight pathway** — Seepage exits at the downstream termination of the confining layer, where the underlying sand daylights at the ground
  surface. This pathway is evaluated whenever a confining layer may be present (its presence is confirmed or unknown) *and* the daylighting
  exit is distinct from the toe exit. At the daylight exit there is no confining layer to penetrate, so the unfiltered-exit and initiation
  probabilities are both certain there, and the pathway is distinguished by its longer seepage path to the exit. When a berm over a confirmed
  layer reaches or clears the layer's downstream extent — or overlies a confirmed layer whose extent is unknown — the berm toe is itself the
  daylighting exit: the toe pathway then represents that exit directly, so a separate daylight pathway would repeat the same exit and is not
  reported. A layer of unknown presence always retains both pathways.

The governing SRP at each headwater level is the maximum of the two pathway SRPs, so the more critical exit condition controls the screening
result. When no confining layer is present — or the daylighting exit is the berm toe, so the toe pathway already represents it — only the toe
pathway is evaluated and it governs directly.

## Relationship to Toolbox Models

initiation cases and the Schmertmann progression method — but constructs their configurations internally from the screening inputs, applying

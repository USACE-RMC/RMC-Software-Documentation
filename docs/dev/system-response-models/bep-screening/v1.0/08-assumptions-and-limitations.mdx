# Assumptions and Limitations

The BEP screening model applies deliberate simplifications relative to the full toolbox models. They reduce input requirements and are designed
to avoid false negatives at the screening level. <TableReference tableKey="bep-screening-table-23" /> documents each assumption with its
engineering basis, its effect on results, and the conditions under which it may not hold. Where an engineering basis is still awaiting
confirmation, the table cross-references the input marker at the assumption's primary location in this document.

## Interpretation Cautions

Three aspects of the screening formulation deserve particular attention when reading results:

- **Unknown-driven breadth.** The Unknown selections (layer presence, thickness, extent) intentionally substitute wide characterizations. A
  high screening SRP at an unknown-heavy site reflects that breadth as much as the site physics; resolving the unknowns with field information
  and re-running is the intended response.

- **Defect-pathway dominance for thin layers.** Because the defect-survival probability rises steeply as the layer thins, the unfiltered-exit
  node for Very Thin and Unknown thickness bands is often controlled by the defect pathway rather than blowout, and it does not diminish at
  low pools (the defect probability is pool-independent). The pool dependence of the SRP then comes mainly from the initiation and progression
  nodes.

- **No driving head below the exit reference.** Pools at or below the governing exit reference elevation produce zero net head and therefore
  effectively zero SRP. The reference elevations — and the daylight ground surface in particular — directly position the low-pool end of the
  system response curve.
